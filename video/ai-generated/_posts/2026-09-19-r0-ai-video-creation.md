# Making AI Video Without Spending a Cent.

Artificial intelligence has made video creation considerably more accessible.

But there is an important question that often gets buried underneath the excitement:

**What can an ordinary person actually create without paying for an AI subscription?**

This article is a practical exploration of **R0 AI video creation**, with particular attention to tools that can be used from South Africa.

The goal is not to find the most expensive or most powerful video generator.

The goal is much simpler:

> **How much useful video can we create without spending money?**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The R0 Principle.

For PlebWare, "free" means something specific.

A service should not be described as R0 simply because it offers a free trial before demanding payment.

There is a difference between:

- A genuinely recurring free allowance.
- A limited free account.
- One-time promotional credits.
- A free hosted demonstration.
- Open-source software that can be run without a subscription.
- A service that is technically free but requires expensive hardware.

This article therefore separates those categories rather than pretending they are all the same thing.

## Google Vids: Ten Free Generations a Month.

Google Vids is one of the clearest examples of genuinely useful free AI video creation.

Google states that personal Google accounts receive **10 video generations every month at no cost**, using Veo 3.1. Videos can be generated from a text prompt or a photograph.

That makes Google Vids particularly interesting for someone who wants to experiment without entering a subscription.

Ten generations is not enough to produce an entire feature film.

It is, however, enough to experiment with:

- Short introductory shots.
- Animated illustrations.
- Technology demonstrations.
- PlebWare promotional clips.
- Article backgrounds.
- Short social-media videos.
- Image-to-video experiments.

Google Vids also provides conventional video-editing functions, screen recording through its Chrome extension, and the ability to publish directly to YouTube.

The important point is that the AI generation allowance is **monthly**, not ten generations every day.

For an R0 workflow, those ten generations should therefore be treated as a limited resource.

## Gemini Free: The Planning Department.

Gemini is useful in a different way.

The free Gemini experience can help with:

- Writing scripts.
- Developing story ideas.
- Creating shot lists.
- Writing narration.
- Developing video prompts.
- Turning an article into a storyboard.
- Breaking a long explanation into short scenes.

The important distinction is that we should **not automatically count Gemini Free as a free Veo video generator**.

Free AI features and free video-generation quotas are separate things, and Google's video-generation products and limits can change.

For an R0 workflow, Gemini can therefore act as the **writer and planning assistant**, while another service performs the actual video generation.

That division of labour is perfectly acceptable.

## Meta Muse: Interesting, But Not Yet in South Africa.

Meta's Muse is one of the more interesting developments in the AI world.

Meta describes Muse as a personal AI agent capable of taking actions rather than merely answering questions.

However, availability is regional.

At the time of writing, Muse is rolling out in the United States, while access elsewhere is still expanding.

For a South African user who receives the message:

> "Muse isn't available in your country or region yet."

the practical R0 allowance is therefore **zero for the moment**.

That does not mean Muse should be ignored.

It means it belongs on the **watch list**.

If Meta opens Muse to South African users, it can be tested again without assuming that a subscription is necessary.

## Wan2.2: The Open-Source Wild Card.

Wan2.2 is particularly interesting because it approaches the problem from an entirely different direction.

Instead of being primarily a subscription service, Wan2.2 is an open video-generation model.

The Wan2.2 TI2V-5B model supports text-to-video and image-to-video generation and is released under the Apache 2.0 licence.

That changes the economics completely.

With a conventional online service:

**Free credits → Credits run out → Pay.**

With an open model:

**Obtain the model → Provide suitable hardware → Generate locally.**

The catch is hardware.

The TI2V-5B model requires approximately **24 GB of GPU VRAM** for local generation.

That means Wan2.2 is not a realistic local video-generation solution for every ordinary Linux computer.

A modest computer without a suitable discrete GPU is not going to turn into a Wan2.2 workstation simply because the software is free.

## Hosted Wan2.2: The R0 Experiment.

This is where Wan2.2 becomes much more interesting for ordinary users.

Hugging Face hosts community-built Spaces that provide Wan2.2 through cloud GPUs.

Some of these Spaces use shared or ZeroGPU infrastructure.

That means the user's computer does not have to own the expensive GPU.

The basic idea becomes:

**Ordinary computer.**

↓

**Web browser.**

↓

**Hosted Wan2.2 model.**

↓

**Remote GPU performs the generation.**

↓

**Video returns to the user.**

This can provide an R0 way of experimenting with a model that would otherwise require a powerful graphics card.

There are limitations.

Hosted Spaces can have:

- Queues.
- Usage restrictions.
- Changing availability.
- Slow generation.
- Community-maintained interfaces.
- Different output limits.
- Spaces that disappear or change configuration.

Therefore, a hosted Wan2.2 Space should be considered an **R0 experiment**, not an unlimited free video-production service.

## Other Free Video Generators Worth Investigating.

The AI video landscape changes quickly.

Other services worth investigating include:

- Pika.
- Kling.
- PixVerse.
- Hailuo.
- Luma Dream Machine.
- Runway.
- Dreamina.
- Other Hugging Face video-generation Spaces.

The important rule is to check the current free allowance before designing a workflow around any particular service.

A service may offer:

**Recurring free credits.**

Or:

**A one-time trial.**

Or:

**A limited daily allowance.**

Or:

**Free access to a demonstration but not unrestricted generation.**

Those are very different propositions.

PlebWare therefore treats the free allowance as part of the tool's specifications, not as an assumption.

## The R0 Video Laboratory.

The most interesting discovery is that we do not necessarily need one AI service to do everything.

A practical R0 workflow can divide the work between several tools.

### Step One — Write.

Use a free AI assistant to develop:

- The idea.
- Script.
- Narration.
- Shot list.
- Prompts.

### Step Two — Create Images.

Create or collect still images that can become starting points for video.

### Step Three — Generate Motion.

Use the available free video-generation allowance.

For example:

**Google Vids:** ten free monthly generations.

Or:

**Hosted Wan2.2:** experimental cloud generation when a suitable Space is available.

### Step Four — Record.

Use ordinary screen recording for demonstrations.

This is particularly useful for:

- PlebMachine.
- Linux tutorials.
- Software demonstrations.
- Website walkthroughs.
- PlebWare articles.

### Step Five — Edit.

Use a normal video editor to combine:

- AI-generated clips.
- Screen recordings.
- Still images.
- Narration.
- Music.
- Titles.
- Subtitles.

The AI does not have to generate the entire finished movie.

It only needs to provide the pieces that are difficult or expensive to create manually.

## Ten Short Clips Can Become Something Bigger.

An eight-second AI clip may sound insignificant.

It isn't.

Suppose a video contains:

1. An opening title.
2. A five-to-eight-second establishing shot.
3. A screen recording.
4. Another AI-generated transition.
5. A still illustration.
6. Another short AI sequence.
7. Narration.
8. Subtitles.
9. A closing graphic.

The AI-generated material may only represent a small part of the finished production.

That is the important lesson.

**AI does not need to make the whole video.**

It can provide the difficult little pieces around which a human-built production is assembled.

## R0 Does Not Mean Zero Work.

There is a temptation to think that free AI video means:

> "Type one sentence and receive a finished film."

Reality is rather different.

Good results still require:

- Ideas.
- Prompt writing.
- Selection.
- Editing.
- Timing.
- Narration.
- Sound.
- Captions.
- Human judgement.

The cost may be R0.

The work is not R0.

And that is perfectly acceptable.

PlebWare has never been about removing humans from the process.

It is about giving ordinary people useful tools.

## The PlebWare R0 Video Philosophy.

The experiment has produced a simple philosophy:

> **Don't ask what the most expensive AI can do. Ask what an ordinary person can create with what is already available.**

A free Google account can provide a limited amount of Veo-powered video generation.

A free AI assistant can help write the script.

Open-source models such as Wan2.2 can provide another route to video generation.

Hosted AI services can sometimes provide access to powerful models without requiring the user to own a powerful GPU.

And ordinary Linux video editors can assemble the pieces.

The result is not unlimited AI video.

It is something more useful:

**A starting point.**

## What We Have Learned.

The biggest lesson from this experiment is not that AI video has become free.

It hasn't.

The more accurate statement is:

> **Useful pieces of AI video creation can now be accessed for R0.**

That distinction matters.

Free quotas are limited.

Hosted services can change.

Open models can require expensive hardware.

Regional availability can prevent access.

But none of those limitations prevents an ordinary person from experimenting.

And that is exactly where PlebWare begins.

**Experiment first. Spend money only when spending money becomes necessary.**

We started this investigation with a simple question:

**Can we make useful AI video without paying for an AI subscription?**
