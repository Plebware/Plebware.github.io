# Lesson 3 — Setting Up Text-to-Speech on a Samsung Galaxy.

<img src="/assets/images/samsung-text-to-speech.webp" alt="A Samsung Galaxy phone with text-to-speech settings" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">

Welcome back to PlebWare Tuition.

In Lesson 2, we set up Text-to-Speech on a general Android phone.

Now we are going to look specifically at **Samsung Galaxy** devices.

Samsung has its own version of Android called **One UI**, and Samsung sometimes places its speech settings in a different part of the Settings menu.

Don't worry.

The principle is exactly the same:

**Find the speech settings. Choose an engine. Choose a language and voice. Test it.**

## 🔑 Before We Begin — Your Galaxy May Look Different

Samsung Galaxy phones come in many models.

You may have a Galaxy A-series, S-series, M-series, Note, or another Galaxy device.

The version of One UI on your phone can also change where particular settings appear.

So if your screen doesn't look exactly like this lesson, **don't panic**.

The easiest solution is often the Settings search box.

Open **Settings** and search for:

**Text-to-Speech**

You can also try:

**Speech**

or:

**Text to speech output**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 1 — Open Settings

Open the **Settings** app on your Samsung Galaxy.

It normally has a gear-shaped icon.

You don't need to change anything yet.

We are simply going to find the correct controls.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 2 — Find Text-to-Speech

On many Samsung Galaxy devices, you can find Text-to-Speech through:

**Settings → General management → Text-to-speech**

On some Galaxy versions, you may instead find it under:

**Settings → Accessibility → Text-to-speech**

Samsung can change the location of settings between software versions.

That is why the **Settings search** is your friend.

Search for:

**Text-to-Speech**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 3 — Choose Your Speech Engine

Inside the Text-to-Speech settings, you may see an option for the **preferred engine**.

Samsung commonly provides its own speech engine, while Google's speech engine may also be available.

The engine is the software that turns written words into spoken words.

For now, don't worry about which engine is technically "best."

Choose an available engine and test it.

We will discuss voices and choosing between options in **Lesson 5**.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 4 — Choose Your Language

Look for the **Language** setting.

You may see options such as:

- English — South Africa
- English — United Kingdom
- English — United States
- English — Australia

The exact choices depend on the speech engine and the voice data installed on your Galaxy.

If your preferred language isn't available, look for an option such as:

**Install voice data**

This may allow you to download additional voices or languages.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 5 — Choose A Voice

Your Samsung Galaxy may offer several voices.

Try them.

You are looking for a voice that is:

- Clear
- Easy to understand
- Comfortable to listen to
- Suitable for the way you intend to use your phone

Don't worry about choosing the "perfect" voice.

You can always change it later.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 6 — Adjust The Speech Rate

The **speech rate** controls how quickly the voice speaks.

If your Galaxy sounds like it is racing through the words, slow it down.

If it sounds painfully slow, increase the speed.

For reading articles, messages, books, or study material, choose a speed that you can comfortably follow.

Remember:

**Fast is not necessarily better.**

Understanding what your phone is saying is the important part.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 7 — Adjust The Pitch

The **pitch** controls how high or low the voice sounds.

You can leave this at the default setting if you are happy with it.

If the voice sounds uncomfortable or unnatural, make a small adjustment and test it again.

There is no magic setting.

Your ears get the final vote.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 8 — Test The Voice

Samsung provides a way to hear a sample of the selected voice.

Look for **Play**, **Preview**, or a similar option.

Press it.

You should hear your Galaxy speak a sample sentence.

If you hear the voice:

**Congratulations. Your Galaxy's TTS engine is working.**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 🔑 No Sound?

Before deciding that something is broken, check the simple things.

### Check Your Volume

Turn up the phone's media volume.

### Check Bluetooth

Your Galaxy might be connected to:

- Bluetooth headphones
- A Bluetooth speaker
- Your vehicle

The sound may be going there instead of through the phone speaker.

### Check The Engine

Return to the Text-to-Speech settings and make sure a speech engine is selected.

### Check The Language

Make sure the selected language has suitable voice data installed.

### Try The Test Again

Press **Play** again.

Sometimes the simplest solution really is the correct one.

## Samsung Speech Engine Or Google Speech Engine?

Some Galaxy phones provide more than one speech engine.

You may see a **Samsung** engine and a **Google** engine.

You don't need to make this complicated.

Try one.

Listen to the voice.

Then try the other if it is available.

Choose the one that gives you the voice and pronunciation you prefer.

This is your phone.

You get to decide what sounds best.

## 🔑 TTS And TalkBack — What Is The Difference?

This is one of the most important things to understand.

**Text-to-Speech (TTS)** is the **speech engine**.

It takes written words and converts them into spoken words.

Think of TTS as the **voice inside the phone**.

For example, an app may use TTS to read:

> "Your appointment is tomorrow at ten o'clock."

TTS is doing the speaking.

---

**TalkBack** is different.

TalkBack is an **Android screen reader**.

It uses speech, including TTS, to help a person **navigate and operate the entire phone**.

It can tell you:

- What is on the screen.
- Which button you are touching.
- What menu item you have selected.
- Where you are moving on the screen.
- Whether an item is switched on or off.
- What actions are available.

TalkBack also changes the way you interact with the touchscreen.

Instead of simply tapping an icon to open it, TalkBack uses special gestures and spoken feedback to help you explore and control the device.

### The Easy Way To Remember It

**TTS = The voice.**

**TalkBack = The screen reader using the voice.**

So TalkBack can **use TTS**, but TTS does not mean that TalkBack is switched on.

---

### Do I Need TalkBack To Make My Phone Read Text?

**No.**

If you simply want your phone to read an article, selected text, a message, or other written material aloud, you generally do **not** need to turn on TalkBack.

Android provides other features for reading text aloud, depending on the device and Android version.

TalkBack is primarily for **screen reading and navigation**, particularly for people who need spoken assistance to operate their device.

### One More Important Point

If you accidentally turn on TalkBack while trying to find TTS, your phone may suddenly behave very differently.

Don't panic.

You have not broken your phone.

You have simply switched on a screen-reader feature.

We will cover TalkBack properly in:

**Lesson 7 — TalkBack and VoiceOver.**

## The PlebWare Principle

Technology should serve the person using it.

A phone is supposed to make life easier — not turn a simple task into a treasure hunt.

Find one setting.

Change one thing.

Test it.

If it works, keep it.

If it doesn't, change it back and try something else.

That's how we learn technology without being intimidated by it.
