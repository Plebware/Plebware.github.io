# Lesson 4 — Text-to-Speech on iPhone and iPad.

<img src="/assets/images/iphone-text-to-speech.webp" alt="An iPhone and iPad using text-to-speech features" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">

## Welcome To The Apple Side

If you use an iPhone or iPad, you already have powerful speech features built into your device.

You do not have to start by installing another app.

The basic idea is simple:

**Your device can speak the words that appear on the screen.**

In this lesson, we are going to find those features and get them working.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔑 First — TTS Is Not VoiceOver

This is an important distinction.

**Text-to-Speech, or TTS,** is the technology that turns written words into spoken words.

Apple provides several ways to use spoken content. Features such as **Speak Screen** and **Speak Selection** are useful when you simply want your device to read text aloud.

**VoiceOver** is different. VoiceOver is Apple's screen reader. It helps a person navigate and operate the entire device using spoken information and gestures.

An easy rule to remember is:

> **TTS = The voice.**  
> **VoiceOver = The screen reader using the voice.**

You do not need to turn on VoiceOver simply because you want your iPhone or iPad to read an article to you.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 1 — Open Settings

Start with the **Settings** app.

Look for the familiar gear icon.

Open it.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 2 — Open Accessibility

Inside Settings, find **Accessibility**.

Apple puts many helpful tools here for people who need assistance with seeing, hearing, movement, or reading information on the screen.

Tap **Accessibility**.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 3 — Find Read & Speak

On newer versions of iOS and iPadOS, look for **Read & Speak**.

On some older versions, this area may be called **Spoken Content**.

The exact wording can change when Apple updates the operating system.

If you cannot find the exact words, look inside Accessibility for settings related to **speech**, **spoken content**, or **reading**.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 4 — Turn On Speak Screen

Look for **Speak Screen**.

This feature allows your iPhone or iPad to read the contents of the screen aloud.

It can be useful for:

- Web pages.
- Articles.
- Documents.
- Messages.
- Notes.
- Other screens containing lots of text.

Turn **Speak Screen** on.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 5 — Try The Two-Finger Gesture

Now let us test it.

Open a page containing some text.

Place **two fingers near the top of the screen** and swipe downward.

Your iPhone or iPad should begin reading the screen aloud.

If it works, congratulations.

Your Apple device is now talking to you.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 6 — Try Speak Selection

There is another useful feature called **Speak Selection**.

This allows you to select some text and have the device read only that selected text.

For example, you might select:

- A sentence.
- A paragraph.
- A quotation.
- Part of an email.
- A section of a document.

Select some text and look for the **Speak** option.

This is particularly useful when you do not want the device to read an entire page.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔑 Speak Screen Or Speak Selection?

Here is the easy way to remember the difference:

**Want the whole page?**  
Use **Speak Screen**.

**Want only some words?**  
Use **Speak Selection**.

That is all you really need to remember for now.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 7 — Choose Your Voice

Your device can provide different voices and languages.

Inside the speech settings, look for the voice options.

Depending on your device and language, you may be able to choose another voice or download additional voices.

Do not worry about finding the perfect voice immediately.

Choose one that sounds comfortable to you.

You can always come back and change it later.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 8 — Adjust The Speaking Rate

You can also change how quickly the device speaks.

If the voice is too fast, slow it down.

If it is too slow, speed it up.

The best setting is the one that allows you to understand the words comfortably.

There is no prize for making your phone speak at maximum speed.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 9 — Test It

Let us perform a simple test.

1. Open a page with text.
2. Use the two-finger swipe from the top.
3. Listen to the voice.
4. Stop the reading.
5. Select a short sentence.
6. Use **Speak**.

If both tests work, your basic spoken-text setup is working.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔑 No Sound?

Do not panic. Your phone is probably not broken.

Check these things first:

- Is the volume turned up?
- Is the sound being sent to Bluetooth headphones, AirPods, a car, or another device?
- Is **Speak Screen** turned on?
- Is a voice selected and available?
- Can you hear other sounds from the device?

If necessary, disconnect Bluetooth temporarily and test again using the phone or tablet's own speaker.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## What About Siri?

Siri and TTS are not the same thing.

**Siri** listens to spoken requests and can respond to you.

**TTS** takes written text and turns it into spoken words.

A simple way to remember it is:

> **You speak → Siri can listen.**  
> **The device has text → TTS can speak it.**

They can work together, but they are different technologies.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## What About VoiceOver?

VoiceOver is much more than an article-reading tool.

It can read buttons, menus, controls, text, notifications, and other information on the screen.

It also changes the way you interact with the device by using special gestures.

That makes VoiceOver extremely useful for people who need a full screen reader, but it can also be confusing if it is switched on accidentally.

If your iPhone or iPad suddenly seems to behave differently after VoiceOver has been enabled, the device is not necessarily broken. The way you control it has changed.

We will look at VoiceOver properly in **Lesson 7 — TalkBack and VoiceOver**.

## The PlebWare Principle

You do not need to understand every technical term before you can use the technology.

Start with one useful feature.

Test it.

Use it.

Then learn the next feature.

That is how PlebWare Tuition works.

Your phone or tablet already contains many powerful accessibility tools. The trick is learning where to find them and what they actually do.
