# Lesson 2 — Setting Up Text-to-Speech on Android.

<img src="/assets/images/android-text-to-speech.webp" alt="An Android phone with text-to-speech settings" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">

Welcome back to PlebWare Tuition.

In Lesson 1, we learned what Text-to-Speech, or TTS, actually is. Now we are going to teach an Android phone how to speak.

The good news is that most modern Android phones already have built-in text-to-speech support. The exact menus can differ between phones, so we are going to keep this lesson simple.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔑 Before We Begin — Don't Panic.

Android is not identical on every phone.

A Samsung Galaxy may put TTS in a different place from a Motorola, Xiaomi, Nokia, or another Android device. Your Android version and the phone manufacturer can change the menu names too.

So don't worry if your screen does not look exactly like someone else's.

Look in **Settings** for words such as:

- **Text-to-Speech**
- **Speech**
- **Accessibility**
- **Read aloud**

If your phone has a search box in Settings, use it. Search for **Text-to-Speech** first.

That is often the quickest way to find it.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 1 — Open Settings

Open your Android phone's **Settings**.

You are looking for the place where your phone controls its speech output.

Don't change anything yet. We are simply finding our way around.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 2 — Find Text-to-Speech

On many Android devices, Text-to-Speech can be found under:

**Settings → Accessibility → Text-to-Speech output**

On some phones it may be somewhere else.

If you cannot find it, use the Settings search function and type:

**Text-to-Speech**

You may also find the words **TTS** or **Speech**.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 3 — Choose The Speech Engine

You may see an option called **Preferred engine** or **Text-to-Speech engine**.

The engine is the software that turns written words into spoken words.

Your phone may offer Google's speech engine, the manufacturer's engine, or another installed engine.

For now, choose the engine you already have available and continue.

We will look at choosing and installing voices in Lesson 5.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 4 — Choose Your Language

Choose the language you want your phone to speak.

For example, you may see choices such as:

- English — South Africa
- English — United Kingdom
- English — United States
- English — Australia

Your available choices depend on the speech engine and installed voice data.

If the language you want is missing, look for an option to **Install voice data** or download additional voice data through the speech engine's settings.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 5 — Choose A Voice

Some Android phones allow you to choose between different voices.

Try the available voices and find one that is clear and comfortable to listen to.

There is no single "correct" voice.

The best voice is the one you can understand easily.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 6 — Adjust The Speech Rate

**Speech rate** controls how quickly the phone speaks.

If the voice is too fast, move the slider towards slower speech.

If it is too slow, move it towards faster speech.

For learning, reading articles, or listening while doing other things, a comfortable speed is more useful than simply choosing the fastest setting.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 7 — Adjust The Pitch

**Pitch** controls how high or low the voice sounds.

You can normally leave this at its default setting at first.

If the voice sounds uncomfortable, experiment with the pitch until it sounds natural to you.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 8 — Press Play

Most Android TTS settings provide a **Play** or **Listen** button.

Press it.

Your phone should now speak a sample sentence.

If you hear the voice, congratulations — your Android TTS engine is working.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔑 No Sound?

Don't immediately assume that TTS is broken.

Try these simple checks:

1. Turn up the phone's media volume.
2. Check whether Bluetooth headphones or a speaker are connected.
3. Press **Play** again.
4. Check that the correct language is selected.
5. Check that a speech engine is selected.
6. Try another available voice if one is listed.

Very often, the phone is working perfectly — the sound is simply going somewhere else.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Step 9 — Make Android Read Something Useful

Getting the TTS engine working is only half the job.

Android also provides accessibility features that can read text aloud. Depending on your device and Android version, you may find features such as **Select to Speak** or **Read aloud**.

These let you move from testing the voice to actually using it.

We will spend more time on practical reading in **Lesson 6 — Making Your Phone Read Text**.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## TTS And TalkBack Are Not The Same Thing

This is important.

**Text-to-Speech** is the voice technology that turns written words into speech.

**TalkBack** is a full screen reader that helps a person navigate and operate an Android device using spoken feedback and gestures.

You do not need to turn on TalkBack simply because you want ordinary text-to-speech.

We will deal with screen readers properly in **Lesson 7**.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## Android TTS Checklist

Before moving on, see how many of these you can tick off:

- [ ] I found the TTS settings.
- [ ] I selected a speech engine.
- [ ] I selected my language.
- [ ] I selected or installed a voice.
- [ ] I adjusted the speech rate.
- [ ] I tested the voice.
- [ ] I can hear my phone speaking.

If you can tick all seven, your Android phone is ready for the next lesson.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## What If My Android Phone Looks Different?

That's perfectly normal.

Android phones are made by many different manufacturers, and menu names and locations can change between devices and software versions.

Don't get stuck trying to make your phone look like the example in this lesson.

Use **Settings search** and look for **Text-to-Speech**.

The goal is not to follow one particular set of screens.

The goal is to find the speech controls on **your** phone.

## The PlebWare Principle

Technology should work for ordinary people.

You should not need to be a technician to make your phone speak.

Start with what the phone already provides. Change one setting at a time. Test it. If it works, keep it.

And if something goes wrong, don't panic.

We troubleshoot one step at a time.
