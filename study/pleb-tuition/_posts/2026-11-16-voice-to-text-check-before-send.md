# 🎙️ Your Voice, Your Words — Check Before You Send!

**By Otto Brinkmeier**

Welcome to the first practical lesson in using Artificial Intelligence.

One of the easiest ways to communicate with an AI assistant is through voice dictation. Instead of typing everything on a keyboard, you simply speak, and your device converts your speech into written text.

It is convenient, quick, and particularly useful when you have a lot to say.

But there is one small step that can make an enormous difference.

**Always check what the AI has heard before sending your message.**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔑 1. Start by Speaking Naturally.

You don't need to speak like a newsreader or a professional broadcaster.

Simply speak naturally and clearly.

A few useful suggestions:

- Speak at a comfortable pace.
- Avoid rushing your sentences.
- Pause briefly between different thoughts.
- Pronounce names and unusual words as clearly as possible.
- Try to minimise background noise.

Your AI assistant will attempt to convert your spoken words into text.

However, speech recognition is not perfect. It makes mistakes, sometimes without us even noticing.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## ⏹️ 2. The Important Little Square Button.

This is the most important part of today's lesson.

When dictating a message, you will generally see a small square button while the recording is taking place.

**Press that square button to finish your dictation.**

The application then processes your speech and places the recognised words into the message area, depending on the application you are using.

Now comes the important part.

**Do not immediately press the Send button!**

Take a few seconds to examine the text that has appeared on your screen.

The square button ends the recording. It is the opportunity to review the resulting transcription that makes the difference.

Think of it as a little quality-control checkpoint between your voice and the AI.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🧐 3. Why Checking Your Words Matters.

Let's use a practical example from the PlebWare project.

During one of my recent dictations, I mentioned three unusual words:

- PlebFormers.
- PlebTunes.
- PlebVox.

These are not ordinary dictionary words. They are names associated with our own projects and terminology.

When I examined the transcription after pressing the square button, I discovered that **all three words had been incorrectly transcribed.**

Had I simply pressed Send, the AI would have received three incorrectly written names.

That might not sound particularly serious, but imagine doing the same thing when dictating:

- A person's name.
- A business name.
- A street address.
- A technical command.
- A computer filename.
- A medication name.
- An important instruction.

One incorrectly recognised word can change the meaning of an entire sentence.

This is why checking your transcription is so important.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## ✍️ 4. Correct the Mistakes Before Sending.

Once you have examined your transcription:

1. Check the spelling of unusual words.
2. Check names, numbers and important details.
3. Look for missing or duplicated words.
4. Check whether the punctuation makes sense.
5. Correct anything that does not accurately represent what you intended to say.

If you are unsure about a word, don't simply assume the AI got it right.

Correct it yourself before sending.

You can also explain an unfamiliar name to the AI. For example:

"This is the correct spelling of our project name: PlebVox. Please use that spelling throughout this conversation."

This gives the assistant useful context for the discussion.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🤖 5. Remember That AI Is Not a Mind Reader.

Artificial Intelligence can understand an extraordinary amount of ordinary language.

It can help with spelling, grammar, sentence structure, explanations and even creative writing.

But it cannot automatically know every unusual name, personal expression, invented word or project-specific term.

It may make a very convincing guess.

And that is precisely why human supervision remains important.

**The AI interprets your message, but you remain responsible for what you actually send.**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🎓 6. The Five-Second Rule.

Here is a simple routine worth remembering whenever you dictate a message to an AI assistant.

**Speak → Press Square → Read → Correct → Send.**

Five seconds of attention can save several minutes of confusion and corrections later.

You don't have to proofread every ordinary sentence like a professional editor.

Just pay particular attention to the words that matter.

Names, numbers, instructions and unusual terminology deserve extra attention.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 💡 The Lesson to Remember.

Voice dictation is one of the easiest ways to communicate with Artificial Intelligence.

But convenience should never replace accuracy.

The square button is not merely something you press to finish talking. It marks the point where you can examine what the software has understood and make sure your intended message is actually the message being sent.

**Never let a speech-recognition mistake become an AI instruction simply because you were in a hurry.**

Your voice provides the message.

Your eyes check the message.

Your judgement approves the message.

And then you press Send.

Happy dictating!
