# 🎙️ Using Speech-to-Text Without the Hiccups

**By Otto Brinkmeier**

Speech-to-text has come a long way from the days of Dragon NaturallySpeaking. Modern phones and AI assistants can understand ordinary conversation surprisingly well.

But they are not perfect.

The quality of the final transcription depends on **how we speak, where we speak, and how carefully we check what the device has heard.**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🗣️ Speak Clearly and Take Your Time

There is no need to speak like a robot.

Speak naturally, but give your words a little space. Avoid rushing several sentences together.

Taking short pauses between thoughts gives the speech-recognition system a better opportunity to recognise where one idea ends and another begins.

**A good rule is:**

> Speak → pause → breathe → continue.

Taking proper breaks also helps *you* maintain accuracy and concentration during a longer dictation.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 👀 Check Before You Send

This is one of the easiest mistakes to make.

After dictating, **read the transcription before pressing Send**.

If you immediately press the blue Send arrow, you may be sending whatever the speech-recognition system guessed you said — including incorrect names, places, technical terms and punctuation.

A few seconds of checking can prevent a much bigger correction later.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔊 Watch Out for Background Noise

Speech recognition has to separate your voice from everything happening around you.

A passing trolley, traffic, music, people talking or machinery can interfere with transcription.

If possible, find a quieter place before starting an important dictation.

And remember: **the louder you speak does not necessarily make the transcription better.**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🤖 Different AI Assistants Work Differently

Not every AI assistant handles voice input in exactly the same way.

Some applications allow you to pause or review your speech before sending it. Others may record while you hold a button and process the recording when you release it.

Controls can also change between different versions of an app.

**Learn how your particular assistant works before relying on it for important dictation.**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 💡 The PlebTuition Rule

Don't expect speech-to-text to be perfect.

Instead:

1. **Speak clearly.**
2. **Pause between thoughts.**
3. **Take proper breaks during longer dictation.**
4. **Reduce background noise where possible.**
5. **Read the transcription.**
6. **Correct obvious mistakes.**
7. **Then press Send.**

A few seconds of checking can save several minutes of repairing a badly transcribed message.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🎓 PlebTuition Conclusion

Speech-to-text is a powerful tool, but **the human still has the final responsibility for what gets sent or published**.

Learn to speak to the technology, learn how the particular app handles voice input, and — most importantly — learn to check what it heard.

**Don't let the blue Send arrow outrun your brain.** 😄
