# Lesson 6 — Making Your Phone Read Text.

<img src="/assets/images/read-text-aloud.webp" alt="A mobile device reading text aloud" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## Welcome To The World Of Read-Aloud

In the first five lessons, we learned what Text-to-Speech is, how to set it up on Android, Samsung Galaxy and iPhone or iPad, and how to choose a voice.

Now we make it useful.

Your phone can read written words aloud for you.

That can be useful for:

- Articles.
- Messages.
- Documents.
- Instructions.
- Study material.
- Giving your eyes a break.

The exact method depends on your phone, operating system and the app you are using.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## 🔑 There Are Different Ways To Read Text

There is no single button that works exactly the same way on every mobile device.

You may be able to:

- Select a piece of text and have it spoken.
- Ask the phone to read the whole screen.
- Use an accessibility feature to read selected content.
- Use a screen reader for full navigation and operation.

The important thing is to learn what your own device provides.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## Method 1 — Read Selected Text

This is often the easiest place to start.

Press and hold on some text.

Select a word, sentence or paragraph.

Look for an option such as:

**Speak**

or

**Read aloud**

The exact wording varies between devices and apps.

If you see the option, tap it and listen.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## Android — Select To Speak

Many Android devices provide an accessibility feature called **Select to Speak**, or a similar read-aloud function.

Open **Settings** and search for:

**Select to Speak**

or

**Read aloud**

If your device provides the feature, follow its instructions to activate it.

Remember that Android manufacturers can change the menu structure, so your phone may use slightly different names.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## Samsung Galaxy

On a Samsung Galaxy, start with **Settings** and use the search function if you cannot immediately find the feature.

Look under **Accessibility** for speech and read-aloud options.

Depending on your Galaxy model and software version, you may encounter options related to:

- Text-to-Speech.
- Speak.
- Read aloud.
- Select to Speak.

The important lesson is not to memorise one menu path.

**Learn how to find the feature on your own phone.**
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## iPhone And iPad — Speak Selection

Apple provides a simple way to have selected text spoken aloud.

Go to:

**Settings → Accessibility → Read & Speak**

On some older versions of iOS or iPadOS, this area may be called **Spoken Content**.

Enable the appropriate spoken-content feature, then select text in an app and look for **Speak**.

Your iPhone or iPad can then read the selected text aloud.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## Method 2 — Read The Whole Screen

Sometimes you do not want to select individual paragraphs.

You want the phone to read what is on the screen.

Apple provides **Speak Screen** for this purpose.

On supported iPhone and iPad devices, you can use the spoken-content settings and then use the appropriate gesture to start reading the screen.

Android devices also provide various accessibility and read-aloud features, but the exact method depends on the device and software.

Do not worry if your phone works differently from somebody else's.

That is normal.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## 🔑 Selected Text Or Whole Screen?

Here is a simple rule:

**A sentence or paragraph:**

Select it and use **Speak** or **Read aloud**.

**A whole page or screen:**

Use a screen-reading or spoken-content feature provided by your device.

If you want full control of the phone by spoken feedback, that is where screen readers such as **TalkBack** and **VoiceOver** become important.

We will deal with those properly in Lesson 7.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## Try It With A PlebWare Article

Now let's do something practical.

Open a PlebWare article on your phone.

Select one paragraph.

Choose **Speak** or **Read aloud**.

Listen carefully.

Then try a longer section.

Adjust the speaking rate if necessary.

You are no longer simply testing whether TTS works.

You are testing whether **your chosen voice works for you**.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## 🔑 PlebVox Is A Great Voice Test Bench

The **PlebVox** articles on the PlebWare website are an excellent place to test your voices.

Real articles are much better for testing than a single sentence such as:

> "This is a test of text-to-speech."

A PlebVox article contains real sentences, punctuation, headings, lists, names, numbers and different sentence lengths.

Try reading:

- A short paragraph.
- A longer paragraph.
- A heading.
- A numbered list.
- A paragraph containing punctuation.
- An unfamiliar name.
- A paragraph containing numbers or abbreviations.

Listen carefully.

Does the voice sound natural?

Is it easy to understand?

Is the speaking speed comfortable?

Does it pause in the right places?

Does it pronounce words correctly?

Think of PlebVox as your little **TTS test bench**.

You are testing more than whether the phone can speak.

You are testing whether the **voice works well for you**.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## Try It With A Message

If your messaging app allows you to select the text, try having a message read aloud.

This can be useful when a message is long or when you simply want to listen instead of reading.

Remember to consider your surroundings.

A phone reading a private message aloud in a public place may not be a good idea.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## Try It With A Web Page

Web pages are another useful test.

Start with a short section.

Then try something longer.

Listen for the things that matter to you:

- Voice quality.
- Pronunciation.
- Pauses.
- Speaking speed.
- Long sentences.

A voice that sounds good for ten seconds may not be the voice you want to listen to for twenty minutes.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## 🔑 TTS Can Become A Second Reading Method

You do not have to choose between reading with your eyes and listening with your ears.

You can use both.

For example, you can read an article silently first and then listen to it.

Or you can listen while following the words on the screen.

This can be useful for:

- Studying.
- Reading documents.
- Reading articles.
- Following instructions.
- Proofreading.
- Giving your eyes a short break.

Text-to-Speech gives you another way to access written information.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## What If The App Does Not Let You Select Text?

Not every app allows text to be selected.

That does not necessarily mean your phone's TTS is broken.

Try another piece of text or another app.

If the whole screen needs to be read, investigate the screen-reading or spoken-content features available on your device.

Different apps have different capabilities.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## 🔑 What If The Voice Pronounces Something Incorrectly?

TTS voices can sometimes struggle with:

- Names.
- Abbreviations.
- Acronyms.
- URLs.
- Unusual words.
- Words from another language.

Do not assume that the written text is wrong just because the voice pronounced something strangely.

Check the original text.

This is another reason why listening to real articles is such a useful test.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## Your Six-Minute Reading Test

Let's put everything together.

**Minute 1:** Open a PlebVox article.

**Minute 2:** Select a paragraph and listen to it.

**Minute 3:** Read the same paragraph silently.

**Minute 4:** Listen again while following the words on the screen.

**Minute 5:** Try a longer section.

**Minute 6:** Decide whether the voice, pronunciation and speaking speed are comfortable.

Congratulations.

You have now tested TTS with real content rather than a demonstration sentence.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->
## Your Read-Aloud Checklist

Before moving on, make sure you can:

- Select text.
- Find **Speak** or **Read aloud**.
- Read a longer section.
- Test a PlebVox article.
- Test a message or document.
- Recognise that different apps work differently.
- Adjust the speaking rate.
- Notice pronunciation problems.
- Choose a voice that is comfortable to listen to.
<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## The PlebWare Principle

Technology becomes useful when it solves a real problem.

Your phone is not merely capable of producing a voice.

It can become a tool for reading, studying, listening and accessing information.

You decide:

**What it reads.**

**Which voice it uses.**

**How fast it speaks.**

Learn the tool.

Test the tool.

Make it work for you.
