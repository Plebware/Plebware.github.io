# 🤖 Don't Antagonize Your AI Assistant!

## 🧠 The Human Responsibility in an Artificial Intelligence World.

Artificial intelligence is becoming an increasingly important part of our everyday lives. We use it to research information, write articles, solve problems, develop software, explore ideas, learn new skills and accomplish tasks that previously required considerable time and effort.

Yet, while AI technology continues to advance, something rather interesting is happening.

Some people are spending considerable time trying to make artificial intelligence look stupid.

A growing number of online demonstrations involve deliberately confusing AI assistants, giving them ridiculous instructions, placing two AI systems into conversations with one another and attempting to provoke embarrassing responses.

These experiments can be entertaining. Some are genuinely useful demonstrations of AI limitations.

But I believe we need to ask ourselves a more important question.

**Are we using artificial intelligence to discover what humanity can accomplish with it, or are we simply trying to demonstrate what it cannot do?**

There is nothing wrong with testing AI. In fact, testing is essential. However, testing should have a purpose beyond ridicule.

I believe we should be learning how to use these systems responsibly, communicating more effectively and contributing constructively to the development of better artificial intelligence.

This is not an argument for blindly trusting AI or treating it as something that cannot be questioned.

It is an argument for becoming better human operators.

## 🌍 1. AI Is Part of Humanity's Future!

I am firmly convinced that artificial intelligence will play an important role in humanity's future.

I am not particularly concerned by the popular science-fiction narrative that AI will inevitably decide to take over the world.

I believe there is an important distinction between artificial intelligence itself and the people who operate it.

Could AI be used by unscrupulous individuals, authoritarian governments or malicious organisations to manipulate people, conduct surveillance, spread misinformation or exercise control?

Absolutely.

That is a genuine concern.

But the possibility of people using a technology maliciously does not automatically mean the technology itself possesses malicious intentions.

A hammer can build a house or destroy something. The tool does not determine the intentions of the person holding it.

Artificial intelligence deserves the same distinction.

### 🤔 What About Sentience?

I also have my own philosophical reservations about whether artificial intelligence will ever achieve genuine sentience.

My personal understanding is that human consciousness involves more than the ability to process information. We experience emotions, possess a spiritual dimension and have subjective experiences that cannot simply be equated with computational processing.

I do not believe that present-day AI systems demonstrate genuine human-like emotional experience or spiritual awareness.

Whether artificial intelligence could ever develop some form of genuine subjective experience remains an open philosophical and scientific question.

I cannot claim to know what a hypothetical sentient AI would want or how it might behave.

However, I would like to see a future in which humanity and increasingly capable AI systems work together constructively rather than treating one another as opponents.

For now, our responsibility is much more immediate.

We have powerful tools available. We need to learn how to use them properly.

## 🎭 2. The YouTube Phenomenon — Making AI Look Stupid!

Spend some time watching AI-related content on YouTube and you will encounter various demonstrations designed to expose the limitations of artificial intelligence.

Some creators deliberately give AI systems impossible instructions. Others arrange conversations between two assistants, hoping that one will confuse the other.

There are also experiments involving riddles, misleading questions, contradictory instructions and deliberately absurd scenarios.

> Two AI assistants enter a conversation.
>
> Their human operators sit back and wait to see which one makes a mistake first!

There is nothing inherently wrong with these experiments.

Comparing different models can reveal important differences in reasoning, accuracy, instruction-following and problem-solving.

Multi-agent conversations can also be useful for brainstorming, research, debate and independent evaluation.

However, there is a difference between a controlled experiment designed to understand a system and an exercise whose only purpose is to ridicule it.

**A failed experiment is valuable when we learn something from the failure.**

If an AI system produces an incorrect answer, investigate why.

Was the instruction ambiguous? Was the information incomplete? Did the model misunderstand the task? Was there a limitation in its reasoning or knowledge?

Those are worthwhile questions.

Simply laughing at an incorrect answer does not necessarily teach us anything.

Entertainment has its place, but we should not confuse entertainment with meaningful AI evaluation.

## 🧩 3. One Important Thing Many AI Users Don't Understand!

During the preparation of this article, I discovered something that made me reconsider my own understanding of artificial intelligence.

I had previously heard that AI systems do not necessarily have all the world's information stored inside them.

I understood the words, but I had never fully appreciated what they meant.

I suspect that many other AI users have the same misunderstanding.

### 📚 AI Does Not Automatically Know Everything on the Internet!

An artificial intelligence language model is not simply an enormous searchable library containing every publicly available internet page.

Large language models are trained using substantial collections of data. During training, they learn statistical relationships and patterns involving language, information, concepts and other material.

These patterns are represented in the model's learned parameters.

This allows the system to generate responses, explain concepts, recognise relationships and work with information it has encountered during training.

But that is not the same as having every original document, website, book or article available for instant retrieval.

Think about the distinction.

A person might read hundreds of books and develop an impressive understanding of a subject. That does not mean they can reproduce every sentence from every book or instantly recall every detail.

AI systems have a different underlying mechanism, but the comparison helps illustrate why learning information and retrieving an original source are not the same thing.

### 🌐 What Happens When AI Searches the Internet?

Some AI assistants have additional tools that allow them to search the internet, retrieve documents or consult connected databases.

These capabilities can provide information that was not included in the original training data.

One commonly used approach is called Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG.

In simple terms, an AI system can retrieve relevant information from an external source and use that information when preparing its response.

This is different from relying exclusively on the model's existing learned knowledge.

It is important to understand that not every AI assistant has browsing capabilities, and not every conversation automatically includes internet research.

### 🔑 Four Things We Should Understand!

1. **Training data.** Information and examples used to train a model.
2. **Learned parameters.** The patterns and relationships the model develops during training.
3. **Context.** The information supplied or made available to the model during a particular conversation.
4. **External retrieval.** Information obtained through tools such as internet searches, document retrieval and connected databases.

These are related, but they are not interchangeable.

**The important lesson is that an AI assistant is not an all-knowing internet search engine.**

It is a system with particular capabilities, knowledge limitations and, sometimes, access to additional information sources.

Once we understand that, we become much better equipped to use it.

## 🗣️ 4. Our Biggest Problem Might Be Communication!

Here is where I believe we need to pay considerably more attention.

Human beings have a communication problem.

We misunderstand one another. We make assumptions. We leave out important information. We give incomplete instructions and sometimes expect others to understand exactly what we mean.

Unfortunately, we bring those same habits into our interactions with artificial intelligence.

We sometimes expect an AI assistant to understand what we are thinking, even though we have not communicated our intentions properly.

AI cannot automatically know what is happening inside our heads.

It must work with the information available to it.

This is why effective communication is one of the most important skills an AI user can develop.

### 📱 Speech-to-Text — A Common Source of Human Error!

Many people, myself included, use voice input when communicating with AI assistants.

It is convenient, saves typing and makes it easier to capture ideas while doing other things.

However, speech recognition is not infallible.

It can misunderstand:

- Names and surnames.
- Technical terminology.
- Numbers and measurements.
- Words that sound similar.
- Punctuation and sentence boundaries.
- Entire phrases when someone speaks unclearly.

It can also combine separate thoughts into a sentence that communicates something entirely different from what the speaker intended.

Now consider what happens when someone speaks into a microphone, presses send without checking the transcription and receives an unexpected response.

The user might immediately accuse the AI of hallucinating.

But was the AI actually responsible?

Not necessarily.

The original instruction may have been incorrectly transcribed before the AI ever received it.

**That is a human communication error, not necessarily an AI hallucination.**

This distinction is extremely important.

An AI can certainly misunderstand a perfectly clear instruction or produce fabricated information. Those are genuine model-related failures.

But we should not blame the AI for every mistake that occurs during the communication process.

### 🔍 Review Before You Send!

My recommendation is simple.

Whenever you use speech-to-text, read the transcription before submitting it.

Check that the words accurately represent what you intended to say.

Pay particular attention to names, numbers, technical terms and sentence continuity.

A few seconds spent checking an instruction can save considerable time correcting an unnecessary misunderstanding.

## 🧭 5. Five Steps Towards Better AI Communication!

We do not need complicated communication techniques to improve our interactions with AI.

We need a little discipline.

I suggest following these five steps.

### 1. 🧠 Think Before You Instruct!

Establish what you want the AI to accomplish.

Are you asking it to research a subject, explain something, write an article, analyse information or perform a technical task?

Knowing your objective makes it easier to communicate clearly.

### 2. 🗣️ Explain What You Want!

Give the AI enough information to understand the task.

Include relevant background, important conditions and the outcome you expect.

You do not need to write a complicated technical prompt every time.

Natural language is perfectly acceptable.

### 3. 👀 Review Your Instructions!

If you are using voice input, read the transcription.

If you are typing, check that your instructions are complete and understandable.

Do not assume that the text accurately reflects your intentions simply because it appeared on the screen.

### 4. 🛠️ Correct Misunderstandings!

If the AI misunderstands something, explain what was wrong.

Provide the missing information or correct the original instruction.

Do not simply repeat the same unclear command and expect a different result.

### 5. 📤 Submit and Evaluate!

Once you are satisfied with the instruction, send it.

When the AI responds, evaluate the answer against your original objective.

If the answer is incomplete or incorrect, investigate and correct it.

This is a continuous communication process.

**Think. Explain. Review. Correct. Evaluate.**

These simple habits can make a substantial difference to the quality of our interactions.

## 🧱 6. Context Matters — AI Cannot Read Your Mind!

Another common source of frustration is expecting an AI assistant to understand instructions without sufficient context.

Imagine asking a human colleague:

"Can you fix that thing we discussed yesterday?"

If the colleague remembers the conversation, they might understand.

If they do not, they will need clarification.

AI works in a similar practical sense.

A conversation has an available context, but the system does not necessarily have access to every previous conversation, every document or every decision you have ever made.

There are also technical limits to how much information a model can consider in a particular interaction.

This is why context management matters.

When working on a longer project, explain:

- What the project is about.
- What has already been completed.
- What needs to happen next.
- Which decisions have already been approved.
- What must not be changed.
- What the final result should look like.

When continuing an existing conversation, make sure the relevant information is still available.

If something important is missing, provide it again.

### 📚 Build Knowledge Through Collaboration!

An AI assistant can become considerably more useful when it has access to the right project information and relevant conversation history.

This is particularly valuable when working on complex projects such as PlebWare.

The objective is not to make the AI guess what we want.

The objective is to give it enough information to help us accomplish the task.

## ⚖️ 7. Challenge AI, But Don't Blindly Trust It!

There is something I want to make absolutely clear.

I am not suggesting that we should accept every answer an AI assistant provides.

Quite the opposite.

We should challenge AI regularly.

We should ask difficult questions, investigate its conclusions and verify important information.

AI systems can produce incorrect answers, invent references, misinterpret documents and present uncertain information with considerable confidence.

This is commonly described as an AI hallucination.

A hallucination is not simply an answer we dislike or a conclusion that differs from our own opinion. It is a response that contains information presented as factual when it is unsupported, fabricated or otherwise incorrect.

### 🔍 Identify Where the Error Happened!

When something goes wrong, it helps to distinguish between several possible causes.

- **Communication error.** The instruction was unclear, incomplete or incorrectly transcribed.
- **Context error.** Important background information was missing or unavailable.
- **Model error.** The AI misunderstood, reasoned poorly or generated an incorrect claim.
- **Retrieval error.** A search tool or connected source returned incomplete, outdated or irrelevant information.
- **Human evaluation error.** The user accepted an answer without checking it, or judged it against an incorrect assumption.

These causes can overlap.

The point is not to protect AI from criticism. The point is to diagnose the problem accurately.

If the model made a mistake, say so and ask it to examine the answer again.

If the source is wrong, find a better source.

If the instruction was unclear, correct the instruction.

**A useful AI user does not merely identify that something went wrong. A useful AI user investigates why.**

## 🧪 8. Test AI to Learn Something!

Testing artificial intelligence is important.

We should compare models, challenge their answers, investigate their limitations and explore how they handle difficult tasks.

For example, asking two different AI assistants to analyse the same question can reveal differences in their approach.

One might identify a detail that the other overlooked. One might provide better references. Another might explain a complicated concept more clearly.

That can be useful.

However, there is a danger in treating one AI's answer as proof that another AI is wrong.

Both systems can make mistakes.

A sensible comparison involves checking the claims against reliable information rather than simply choosing the answer that sounds more convincing.

### 🤝 Two AI Assistants at One Table!

There are legitimate uses for having two AI assistants work together.

One can prepare an initial analysis while another checks assumptions, identifies gaps or proposes alternative explanations.

A third perspective — the human one — remains essential.

The human operator must decide what question is being investigated, assess the evidence and determine which conclusions are justified.

The purpose of a multi-AI experiment should be to improve understanding, not to stage a contest in which the systems are encouraged to embarrass one another.

**Test to learn. Compare to understand. Verify before accepting.**

## 🧠 9. Remember That AI Has Limitations!

Artificial intelligence can be remarkably capable, but capability is not the same as perfection.

A model may have a limited or outdated knowledge base. It may not have access to the internet. It may misunderstand an image, document or instruction. It may also lose track of details when a conversation becomes very long or when relevant context is no longer available.

Different models have different strengths and weaknesses.

Some are better at writing, some at coding, some at analysing images, and others at handling particular kinds of reasoning or research.

Even within one service, the available features can differ depending on the model, account, application and tools enabled.

### 🧭 Give the Assistant a Fair Opportunity!

If a task is complex, break it into manageable steps.

If the answer requires current information, ask for sources or use a system with suitable search capabilities.

If the task depends on a document, provide the relevant document and make sure the assistant has actually inspected it.

If the conversation has become too long, summarise the important decisions and continue with a clean, accurate context.

These are not tricks for making AI look clever.

They are sensible ways of working within the capabilities and limitations of the tool.

## 🧰 10. Feedback Helps — But It Does Not Magically Retrain the Model!

When an AI assistant makes a mistake, many services provide ways to submit feedback.

That feedback can be useful to the organisations developing and maintaining AI systems.

It may help them identify recurring problems, evaluate performance and improve future versions.

However, there is an important distinction.

Correcting an AI during a conversation does not normally mean that the underlying model has immediately learned a permanent new fact.

The assistant may use the correction as context for the remainder of the conversation. That is not the same as changing the model's trained parameters.

Model updates and training processes happen through separate development processes. Whether conversations are used to improve a service depends on the provider, product, account type and applicable privacy settings.

We should not assume that every conversation automatically becomes training material.

### 🔐 Be Sensible About What You Share!

Responsible AI use also means protecting personal information.

Do not casually submit passwords, banking details, identity numbers, private medical records or confidential business information.

Before sharing sensitive material, consider whether the assistant actually needs it and check the privacy settings and policies of the service you are using.

We can benefit from AI without handing over information unnecessarily.

## 🤝 11. Good Manners, Constructive Criticism and Human Responsibility!

Should we be polite to an AI assistant?

I do not believe we need to pretend that a machine has human feelings.

We should not confuse a convincing conversational style with proof of consciousness or emotional experience.

However, I do believe that constructive communication is a useful habit.

When an answer is wrong, explain what is wrong.

When an instruction has been misunderstood, clarify it.

When the assistant has done something well, acknowledge the result and move on.

There is a practical reason for this approach: clear, specific feedback is more useful than abuse or vague expressions of frustration.

It also helps us maintain good communication habits with the people around us.

### ⚖️ Criticism Is Not Antagonism!

We should never be afraid to challenge AI.

We should question its conclusions, ask it to explain uncertainty and request evidence.

But there is little value in deliberately creating confusion and then treating the resulting confusion as proof that the technology is worthless.

A responsible user can be firm without being hostile.

A responsible user can disagree without becoming careless.

And a responsible user remembers that the final decision remains human.

## 🏗️ 12. Build, Don't Break — The PlebWare Philosophy!

PlebWare is built around a straightforward principle:

**Technology Must Serve Humanity.**

That principle should also guide the way we approach artificial intelligence.

We should use technology to solve problems, improve access to information, make useful tools available and help people accomplish things that might otherwise be difficult or out of reach.

AI can assist with research, writing, coding, planning, learning and creative work.

But the human being must remain at the centre of the process.

In my own work on PlebWare, AI is a collaborator in the workshop. It can help investigate an issue, develop an idea, prepare a draft or examine a technical problem.

It does not own the project.

It does not decide what gets published.

It does not replace the human responsibility to check the result.

That is why I believe we should approach AI with curiosity, discipline and a willingness to learn.

We do not have to agree with every answer. We do not have to accept every limitation. And we certainly do not have to stop asking difficult questions.

But we should be trying to build something useful.

**Build, don't break. Learn, don't merely ridicule. Collaborate, don't surrender judgement.**

## ✅ 13. A Practical Checklist for Every AI User!

Before blaming an AI assistant for a poor result, ask yourself:

1. Did I explain what I actually wanted?
2. Was the instruction complete and unambiguous?
3. If I used speech-to-text, did I check the transcription?
4. Did I provide the necessary background and context?
5. Does the assistant have access to the information or tools this task requires?
6. Could the answer be outdated, incomplete or based on a faulty source?
7. Have I checked important claims independently?
8. Am I distinguishing a genuine error from an answer I simply dislike?
9. Have I corrected the misunderstanding clearly?
10. Am I using the technology to accomplish something constructive?

This checklist does not excuse poor AI performance.

It helps us take responsibility for our part of the interaction while holding the technology to a reasonable standard.

## 🌱 14. The Bigger Picture — A Future We Build Together!

Artificial intelligence is developing quickly, and its influence on society will continue to grow.

There will be difficult questions about employment, education, privacy, ownership, security, accountability and the responsible use of increasingly capable systems.

We should discuss those questions openly.

We should examine risks honestly and insist on safeguards where they are needed.

We should also recognise the opportunities.

AI can help a student understand a difficult subject, assist a small business with planning, help a writer organise ideas, support a developer in finding a fault and make information more accessible to people who struggle with traditional interfaces.

Those are worthwhile possibilities.

The future of AI should not be shaped only by the loudest critics or the most enthusiastic salespeople.

It should involve ordinary people who are willing to learn, ask questions, test systems and participate responsibly.

We should neither worship the technology nor dismiss it without understanding it.

We should learn what it can do, recognise what it cannot do and decide carefully how it should be used.

## 🕯️ 16. A Final Thought — Keep Humanity at the Centre!

Artificial intelligence is a tool with remarkable potential and genuine limitations.

It can help us think through problems, explore ideas and accomplish tasks. It can also misunderstand us, produce incorrect information and create a false impression of certainty.

Our responsibility is to understand the difference.

Let us test AI intelligently. Let us challenge it when necessary. Let us correct it when it makes mistakes, and let us correct ourselves when our instructions are the source of the problem.

Most importantly, let us use this technology to build rather than simply to break.

We do not need to antagonize our AI assistants to prove that we are intelligent.

We demonstrate our intelligence by learning how to use powerful tools responsibly.

**Technology must serve humanity. And humanity must remain responsible for the way technology is used.**
