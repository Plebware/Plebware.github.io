# Soro and PlebWare: A Good Tool That Is Not Our Tool.

A friend recently suggested that I try **Soro**, an AI-powered SEO and content automation service designed to research keywords, write articles, optimise them for search, and publish them to a connected website.

So, naturally, we brought the suggestion to the PlebWare conference table.

The conclusion is not that Soro is bad.

Quite the opposite.

Soro is solving a real problem for people who want a largely automated content-marketing operation. The interesting question is simply whether that problem is the problem PlebWare is trying to solve.

At the moment, it is not.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔑 First, What Is Soro?

Soro describes itself as an **AI-powered SEO platform**. Its service combines keyword research, article generation, search optimisation, internal linking, image generation, and publishing automation.

Its basic promise is straightforward: connect your website, let Soro study the site and its audience, and then let the system continually create and publish search-oriented content.

Soro says it supports major website and publishing platforms, including WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Notion, HubSpot, Ghost, Wix, Next.js, and custom integrations through webhooks.

In other words, Soro is not really another blogging home in the traditional sense.

It is better understood as a **content-production and SEO automation layer** that sits on top of an existing website.

That distinction matters when comparing it with PlebWare.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 💡 What Soro Does Well.

There is a lot to like about the idea behind Soro.

For a business owner who knows that the company needs search traffic but does not have the time or expertise to produce content regularly, automation can be valuable.

Soro brings several jobs together:

- keyword research;
- content planning;
- long-form article generation;
- SEO optimisation;
- internal and external linking;
- image generation;
- brand-voice configuration;
- and automated publishing.

Soro also supports more than 50 languages and says its system is built around large-scale analysis of content that already performs in search.

For someone running a commercial website where publishing consistently is itself a major bottleneck, that can be a compelling proposition.

There is no reason for PlebWare to pretend otherwise.

A useful tool can be useful even when it is not useful to us.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 💰 The First PlebWare Question: What Does It Cost?

This is where the PlebWare philosophy starts to matter.

Soro's current published pricing starts at **US$39 per month**. Its public pricing page does not advertise a permanent free tier, and Soro's own money-back-guarantee page explicitly says that there is **no free trial**.

Instead, first-time subscribers can qualify for a 14-day money-back guarantee under the stated conditions.

That is a perfectly reasonable commercial model for a commercial service.

But PlebWare is built around a different idea.

PlebWare is intended for the **common person**: people who may have an old computer, limited resources, limited technical knowledge, or simply no spare money for another monthly subscription.

US$39 a month is not pocket change in that environment.

At the time of writing, that is approximately **US$468 per year** before considering exchange rates, taxes, banking charges, or other costs.

For a business already earning enough from its website to justify an SEO automation subscription, that may be an investment.

For somebody still trying to build something from very little, it is a recurring expense.

Those are two very different situations.

## 🧑‍💻 PlebWare Already Has a Publishing Machine.

Here is the biggest reason Soro does not immediately make sense for PlebWare.

We already have a system.

PlebWare uses **GitHub Pages, Markdown, Jekyll, and PlebVox** to turn ordinary text files into a functioning knowledge website.

The workflow is deliberately simple:

**Write → Markdown → GitHub → Jekyll → Website → PlebVox.**

There is no separate blogging subscription required to keep the system alive.

The content remains in the repository.

The structure remains under our control.

The publishing process is visible.

And, perhaps most importantly, the human being remains in the loop.

Soro's great strength is automation.

PlebWare's great strength is **ownership, simplicity, transparency, and control**.

Those are not necessarily competing values.

They are simply different priorities.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 🧠 The Human Being Is Part of the PlebWare System.

PlebWare is not trying to produce the maximum possible number of articles every day.

It is trying to produce useful knowledge.

That distinction is important.

An automated SEO system can be excellent at answering the question:

**"What should I publish today to pursue this search opportunity?"**

PlebWare often starts with a different question:

**"What have we learned that is worth sharing with another ordinary person?"**

Sometimes those questions lead to the same article.

Sometimes they do not.

A human writer brings experience, mistakes, experiments, humour, opinions, observations, and context that cannot be reduced to a keyword calendar.

That does not make automation useless.

It means automation should serve the writer rather than automatically becoming the writer.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 📈 What About the Traffic Question?

This is the strongest argument for Soro, and it should not be dismissed.

Soro's purpose is not simply to give someone another place to post a blog. Its purpose is to help a business **find search opportunities and publish content designed to attract organic traffic**.

Soro also says its content is designed to appear in Google and AI-powered search environments.

That is potentially valuable.

PlebWare absolutely wants more people to discover its material.

But there is an important difference between needing more traffic and needing an automated content factory.

PlebWare can work on discoverability without handing its entire publishing strategy to another service.

We can improve:

- article titles;
- descriptions and excerpts;
- internal linking;
- useful categories;
- relevant tags;
- pillar articles;
- links between related articles;
- search-friendly explanations;
- and the overall structure of the knowledge base.

The goal should be to make the **existing PlebWare library easier to discover**, rather than simply making the library bigger.

More pages do not automatically mean more value.

## 🏗️ Why Not Just Create a Second Blog?

This is another question worth asking.

If Soro can generate content, why not create another blog, point it at PlebWare, and use it as a traffic feeder?

Because another website also means another property to maintain, another publishing identity to manage, another stream of content to monitor, and another place where attention can become fragmented.

PlebWare already has a growing footprint.

It contains technical material, everyday advice, study resources, AI discussions, PlebMachine development, PlebVox, cooking, writing, journalism, devotions, and other parts of the PlebWare ecosystem.

The better strategy is to strengthen that ecosystem before creating another one.

A second blog only becomes interesting when it has a clear job that the main site cannot perform.

At present, Soro does not provide a sufficiently compelling reason to create that second property.

## ✍️ Where Soro Might Fit the Othello Cody Verrocchio Side.

There is, however, a future scenario where a service like Soro could make more sense.

My author identity, **Othello Cody Verrocchio**, is a different proposition from PlebWare.

A commercial author platform could eventually have a stronger reason to invest in search automation, audience acquisition, book promotion, and content marketing.

But there is a simple rule behind that possibility:

**The income should come before the expense.**

If the author business eventually generates dollars, then paying dollars for a service that saves substantial time or produces measurable traffic may become a sensible business decision.

Until then, there is no reason to manufacture an expense simply because a tool exists.

That is not anti-business.

That is budgeting.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🪙 The PlebWare Rule: Spend When the System Can Pay for It.

One of the central ideas behind PlebWare is that ordinary people should be able to build useful things without first having to become wealthy.

That applies to software as much as it applies to everything else.

If a free tool can accomplish the job, use the free tool.

If an open system can provide the required control, use the open system.

If a paid service eventually becomes worthwhile because the project is earning money, then consider paying for it.

That is a much healthier relationship with technology than subscribing to every shiny new service that promises to make life easier.

Soro may be worth US$39 a month to someone whose time is worth more than that and whose website can convert the resulting traffic into revenue.

That is a legitimate business calculation.

It is simply not the calculation PlebWare needs to make today.

## ⚖️ A Fair Verdict on Soro.

So, is Soro good?

**It appears to be a serious and thoughtfully constructed service aimed at a real business problem.**

Is it useful?

**For businesses and creators who need large-scale SEO content production and can justify the recurring cost, it may be.**

Is it a free blogging platform?

**No.**

Does it replace GitHub Pages and the PlebWare publishing workflow?

**No.**

Does PlebWare need it right now?

**No.**

Could it make sense for a future commercial author or business operation?

**Possibly.**

Would PlebWare reconsider it if a genuinely useful free tier became available?

**Certainly.**

That is not a rejection of Soro.

It is simply choosing the right tool for the job.

## 🔑 The PlebWare Conference Table Decision.

After putting Soro on the conference table, the decision is surprisingly simple.

**Soro is a good tool for a different stage of the journey.**

PlebWare is currently concentrating on building the journey itself.

We have GitHub.

We have Markdown.

We have Jekyll.

We have PlebVox.

We have a growing knowledge base.

And we have something that no SEO autopilot can manufacture on its own:

**a reason for the website to exist.**

So Soro can remain on the radar without becoming another monthly bill.

If PlebWare eventually becomes a revenue-generating ecosystem, the conference table can always be reconvened.

Until then, the money stays where it belongs:

**in the project, not in subscriptions.**
