# 📰 Between Arrivals and Departures — A Johannesburg Working-Day Expedition.

**Wednesday, 25 November 2026 — Johannesburg, South Africa. 🇿🇦**

Some working days have a habit of turning into unexpected adventures. Today was one of those days.

My morning began with a trip towards OR Tambo International Airport to collect a Bangladeshi passenger. What should have been a straightforward journey turned into a slow crawl along the highway.

Traffic was moving at approximately five kilometres per hour in places. At first, it was difficult to understand why the roads were so congested.

The explanation turned out to be roadworks. Two lanes had been closed while workers carried out repairs to what appeared to be part of a bridge structure. Sections around the metal supports were exposed, and the repair work was clearly visible.

Ironically, Gillooly's interchange itself was about as quiet as one could hope for. I practically sailed through it. The real trouble was the approach from the Alberton side, stretching past Van Buuren Road.

Had I known about the roadworks, I would have considered taking the back roads.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## ✈️ Waiting at OR Tambo.

Eventually, I reached the airport and found myself sitting in the pick-up parking area, with the Oliver Reginald Tambo statue nearby and a steady stream of people coming and going.

I do not get to visit the airport very often. In fact, this was only my second airport pick-up involving a Bangladeshi passenger in the past two years.

The sky was cloudy, and it looked as though rain might be on its way across the East Rand. That added another practical concern: the passenger's luggage would not fit inside the cab of the single-cab bakkie.

I had a blue canvas salvage sheet large enough to cover the luggage in the back. If necessary, I could wrap everything securely and use the cargo net to keep it in place. Nobody wants luggage becoming an easy target when the vehicle stops at a traffic light.

There was also the small matter of airport parking. The first twenty minutes were free, forty minutes cost R30, and the next tariff was not easy to read from where I was parked. I had R100 available, so I was hoping the passenger would clear customs before the parking bill became another expedition of its own!

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🧳 A Little Airport Theatre.

While waiting, I watched the usual airport activity: travellers arriving, people collecting luggage, vehicles moving through the pick-up area, and families trying to coordinate their journeys.

One small incident caught my attention. A group of Afrikaans travellers had a mishap with a luggage trolley. It struck a speed bump, and several suitcases ended up on the ground.

After collecting their belongings, they apparently decided that carrying the luggage by hand was a better option than trusting the trolley again.

It was a small moment, but it captured the everyday theatre of an international airport rather nicely.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 📞 The Phone Call — Passenger on the Way.

After some waiting, Farouk phoned and asked where I was. I told him I was parked in the same place as during my previous visit.

He asked me to come inside, so I made my way back towards the terminal buildings to find the passenger.

I put my phone away while entering the terminal. There was no point taking chances with airport rules or distracting myself when I needed to find the right person.

The passenger was eventually collected, and we headed back towards Forest Hill.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🍽️ Back at Forest Hill — The Itinerary Changes.

Once we arrived back at Forest Hill, we dropped the passenger off near his flat.

He wanted something to eat before continuing with his plans. His next destination was Gold Reef City, where he intended to try his luck at the casino.

That meant another stop had to be added to the day's itinerary.

For me, however, the working day was not finished. I still had another passenger to collect, and after dropping the first passenger at Gold Reef City, I would be heading back to the daily stock-buying routine.

From international arrivals to a casino drop-off, and then straight back to procurement. It is quite a range of duties for one bakkie and its driver!

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🚙 The Working Day Continues.

There is a certain rhythm to this kind of work. One minute you are navigating airport traffic and watching international travellers arrive; the next, you are organising luggage, arranging drop-offs, collecting another passenger, and preparing to buy stock.

The route changes, the passengers change, and the day's assignments keep moving.

The bakkie remains the mobile office, the shopping vehicle, and the transport link between one task and the next.

And somewhere between the roadworks, the airport parking meter, the approaching rain and the next stock run, there is always another story waiting to happen.

## 📰 Captain Gemini's Field Assessment.

Today's expedition offered a reminder that the working life is rarely as predictable as the diary might suggest.

A road closure can turn a routine journey into a traffic ordeal. An airport pick-up can become a luggage-management exercise. A passenger's plans can add another destination, while the driver's own work continues regardless.

Johannesburg supplies the setting, the people supply the stories, and the road supplies the occasional surprise.

**Sometimes the best field reports are not about extraordinary events. They are about paying attention to ordinary life as it unfolds.**
