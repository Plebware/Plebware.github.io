## The Beginning: 0tronix Developments.

My first formal business venture into computing was **0tronix Developments** — and yes, it is spelt with a **ZERO**.

**0-TRO-NIX Developments.**

I registered it as a CK in 2003, and eventually closed it in 2010.

It was my first serious attempt at making a living in the computer business.

It was also a hard education.

There were difficult times, mistakes, financial pressure, and a very steep learning curve. But looking back, I can see something important:

**The idea never really died.**

It simply went dormant.

## The Website Era: GreenGeeks.

After 0tronix came the website years.

I built and operated a website using GreenGeeks hosting for almost ten years. The project was called **PlebWear.com**, which eventually evolved into the name **PlebWare**.

For years, the website represented another attempt to build something of my own.

Then came 2020.

Just before COVID hit, I was already struggling financially. I needed approximately R3,000 to renew three years of hosting.

I couldn't get the loan.

The hosting expired.

I lost **PlebWear.com**.

That hit me hard.

For a while, I was in a very bad state.

But sometimes losing something forces you to rethink how you are building it.

## The Reset: GitHub.

I had actually created a GitHub account back in 2016.

The original intention was to put PlebMachine there.

But I never really used GitHub properly.

Years later, I came back to it.

This time the thinking was different.

Instead of paying for hosting that could disappear the moment I could no longer afford the bill, I could build around **free, open technologies**.

GitHub became the workshop.

Markdown became the publishing language.

And PlebWare began evolving into something bigger than the website I had lost.

It became an ecosystem.

The philosophy became simple:

**Build with what ordinary people can afford.**

**Use open technologies where practical.**

**Don't create unnecessary dependencies on expensive services.**

And most importantly:

**Keep building.**

## PlebMachine: Version One.

The early PlebMachine wasn't an operating system.

It wasn't even really a Linux distribution.

Version One was built around **Rainmeter on Windows**.

It created layers over the Windows desktop — an additional interface intended to make the computer more productive and easier to organise.

Looking back, some of it was admittedly a little ridiculous.

But that was V1.

And V1 mattered.

Because underneath the strange-looking desktop was an idea:

**The computer should adapt itself to the person using it.**

That idea eventually became much more sophisticated.

## From Windows to Linux.

Over time, my relationship with Windows changed.

My concerns about privacy, telemetry, vendor lock-in, and the increasing amount of information collected by large technology companies pushed me toward alternatives.

Ironically, I am currently using **Windows 11 Pro** on one of my machines.

That machine was gifted to me earlier this year after my Proline laptop running MX Linux suffered a GPU failure.

So this isn't a religious war against Windows.

It is about **choice**.

Use the tool that works for you.

Understand what it does.

Understand what it costs.

And, where possible, retain control over your own computing environment.

For PlebMachine, that eventually led me to:

**Debian + XFCE.**

Lightweight.

Efficient.

Modular.

And capable of running on hardware that would otherwise be considered obsolete.

## What PlebMachine Is Today.

Today, **PlebMachine is no longer just a desktop experiment.**

It is becoming a modular productivity layer built on top of Debian and XFCE.

The objective is not to reinvent Linux.

The objective is to organise it.

PlebMachine provides a structured environment for different kinds of work — from writing and research to graphics, music, video, development, accounting, AI assistance, and everyday computing.

The computer becomes a workspace rather than merely a collection of applications.

And that is where the original idea from V1 finally starts making sense.

## PlebWare: From Website to Knowledge Platform.

PlebWare has travelled a similar road.

It began as a name attached to a website.

Today it is evolving into an **online learning and publishing platform**, using GitHub and open technologies as its foundation.

The website is no longer the entire idea.

The idea is the ecosystem of knowledge, tools, experiments, articles, documentation, and projects surrounding it.

PlebWare is the publishing side.

PlebMachine is the computing side.

And AI has now become another tool within that ecosystem.

## The AI Question.

This brings me to one of the most important parts of the experiment.

**Artificial intelligence is powerful.**

But powerful tools can be used intelligently or irresponsibly.

I don't believe AI should replace human thinking.

I believe it should **assist human thinking**.

I use AI assistants to research, brainstorm, troubleshoot, write, analyse, experiment, and learn.

The human still has to make the decisions.

That distinction matters.

We shouldn't create a generation of people who simply ask a machine what to think.

We should create people who know how to **think with machines**.

That is one of the principles I want PlebWare and PlebMachine to promote.

## Affordability Is Part of the Mission.

There is another reason this matters to me.

Computing has become incredibly powerful, but access to that power isn't equal.

There are people who cannot afford expensive computers, expensive software subscriptions, expensive hosting, or corporate AI services.

That doesn't mean they shouldn't have access to technology.

It is my opinion — and yes, this is partly tongue-in-cheek — that sometimes we are asked to pay millions for services that don't seem to do very much for ordinary people.

Some commercial services are absolutely worth paying for.

I'd happily buy many of them myself if I could afford them.

But **affordability matters**.

When the cost of technology becomes too high, people become shackled by it.

My experiment is an attempt to find another way.

Use free and open-source software where it makes sense.

Reuse older hardware.

Use free hosting where practical.

Learn rather than simply consume.

And use AI as an assistant rather than a replacement for human ability.

## The Road Continues.

From **0tronix Developments in 2003**, through the PlebWear website years, the loss of that website in 2020, the rediscovery of GitHub, and the development of PlebMachine on Debian and XFCE, the journey has taken more than twenty years.

The technology changed.

The names changed.

The computers changed.

The internet changed.

But the basic objective remained remarkably consistent:

**Find a way to use technology to improve ordinary people's lives without making technology itself the obstacle.**

That is what I am still experimenting with.

PlebMachine is not finished.

PlebWare is not finished.

And neither, apparently, am I.

The road continues.
