# 🚢 When the Flagship Loses Its Compass, Must We Follow?

*An independent opinion piece about leadership, personal responsibility, Ubuntu, and the strange habit of allowing somebody else's bad decisions to become our own.*

📰 **CAPTAIN GEMINI — OPINION AND REFLECTION.**

There is a word that deserves a little attention: *kakistocracy*.

It comes from Greek roots meaning, broadly, government by the least suitable or least qualified people. It is a sharp word, and an uncomfortable one. People sometimes use it when they believe the leadership of a country has become dysfunctional, incompetent, or morally compromised.

But here's a more uncomfortable question.

**When leadership loses its moral compass, why should everybody else throw theirs overboard?**

Does poor leadership give every individual permission to become dishonest? Does corruption in public life make dishonesty acceptable in private life? And when the flagship starts sailing towards the rocks, why are all the smaller ships apparently expected to follow?

Ladies and gentlemen, the ocean is not flat. There is no edge of the world waiting to catch us. Yet sometimes we behave as though independent thought has been declared illegal.

😂 Perhaps somebody should check whether the national compass has been sold as scrap metal.

## ⚓ The Captain May Be Lost, but You Still Have a Steering Wheel!

Leadership matters. There is no denying it.

A country needs responsible leadership. Businesses need ethical management. Communities need people who understand that authority is a responsibility, not a licence to exploit.

When those responsibilities are neglected, trust begins to disappear.

People become cynical. Promises become punchlines. Accountability becomes an endangered species.

And eventually, a dangerous attitude starts spreading:

"If they can do it, why can't I?"

Well, perhaps because you are still responsible for your own actions.

A dishonest leader does not force a shopkeeper to cheat a customer. A corrupt executive does not force an employee to mistreat a pensioner. A dysfunctional institution does not require every person connected to it to abandon decency.

We may not control the flagship, but we can still steer our own vessels.

🚢 **A bad captain does not automatically turn every passenger into a pirate.**

## 🤝 Ubuntu: A Beautiful Word Looking for Its Missing Owners.

We have a word in South Africa that carries extraordinary meaning: **Ubuntu**.

"I am because we are."

It speaks of humanity, compassion, mutual respect, and the recognition that our lives are connected.

It is a beautiful philosophy.

But what happens when Ubuntu becomes a word printed on banners, mentioned in speeches, and forgotten in everyday encounters?

What happens when the person standing opposite you is no longer a human being deserving of patience and dignity, but merely an inconvenience or an opportunity?

When did goodwill become a business expense? When did compassion become something we advertise rather than something we practise?

Perhaps Ubuntu has not disappeared. Perhaps we have simply become very good at displaying the word while forgetting to demonstrate its meaning.

😂 We have the slogan, the logo, the corporate presentation, and possibly a commemorative mug. All we need now is the actual humanity!

## 🎄 Christmas Is Coming. Please Keep Your Wallets Ready!

And here we are, approaching the Christmas season.

The season of peace, goodwill, generosity, forgiveness, and kindness.

At least, that is what the greeting cards tell us.

Meanwhile, the commercial calendar has apparently declared war on our bank accounts.

Black Friday! Christmas Specials! Black December! Festive Deals! Mega Discounts! Limited-Time Offers!

🎄 Somewhere between the first Christmas advertisement and the seventeenth special offer, the baby in the manger has been surrounded by promotional banners.

😂 I half expect to see a nativity scene advertised as:

**"Three Wise Men. One Stable. Zero Deposit. Terms and Conditions Apply!"**

There is nothing wrong with buying presents, supporting businesses, or enjoying a festive meal. Commerce is not the enemy.

But surely we can distinguish between celebrating a season of goodwill and exploiting the season's commercial opportunities?

A kind word should not require a minimum purchase. A little patience should not be available only to premium customers. Treating another human being with dignity should not be considered an optional festive upgrade.

## 💰 When Did Making Money Become More Important Than Being Human?

Businesses need to make money. People work hard. Businesses have expenses. Employees need salaries. Products and services have costs.

Profit is not inherently immoral.

But there is a difference between making a profit and treating people as though profit is the only thing that matters.

A customer is not merely a transaction number. A pensioner is not an opportunity to collect another fee. A struggling family is not a festive-season revenue target.

A person who asks a reasonable question deserves a reasonable answer.

And when something goes wrong, accountability should not require an ordinary person to become a detective, an accountant, a lawyer, and an amateur forensic scientist just to understand what happened.

🔎 **Transparency should be ordinary practice, not a special service unlocked after three complaints and a blood sacrifice.**

We should expect honesty, fairness, and clear communication throughout the year — not only when somebody is trying to sell us something.

## 🧭 The Dangerous Excuse: "Everybody Does It!"

This is perhaps the most worrying part.

Once poor behaviour becomes normalised, people stop questioning it.

They shrug. They laugh. They say, "That's just how things work."

And slowly, standards are lowered until the unacceptable becomes ordinary.

But who decided that?

Who gave us permission to stop expecting better?

If a person sees dishonesty and chooses honesty, that person has made a decision. If a business sees an opportunity to exploit someone but chooses fairness, that business has made a decision. If a leader sees an opportunity to abuse authority but chooses responsibility, that leader has made a decision.

Those decisions matter.

We cannot demand integrity from the people above us while excusing the absence of integrity in ourselves.

**The moral standard of a society is not measured only by the people sitting in the highest offices. It is also measured by the ordinary decisions made by ordinary people every day.**

## 🌊 If the Lead Ship Sails Towards the Edge, Must We Follow?

Let's return to our ships.

Imagine a fleet travelling across the ocean.

The flagship changes course. The captain insists that the horizon is wrong, the maps are outdated, and the crew should stop asking questions.

The smaller ships begin following.

One sailor notices the rocks. Another sees the warning signs. A third knows perfectly well that the ocean does not end where the captain says it does.

But nobody wants to be the first to question the order.

And so the fleet sails on.

Now, here's the question:

**Why?**

Are we no longer capable of thinking independently? Have we surrendered personal responsibility to whoever happens to be holding the loudest microphone? Are we so accustomed to following instructions that we have forgotten how to recognise a dangerous direction?

A captain can command a ship. A captain cannot command reality to change.

And no leader, institution, organisation, or fashionable public opinion should have the power to switch off an individual's conscience.

We are not passengers without steering wheels. We are not children waiting for permission to do what is right. And we certainly do not have to sail over the edge simply because somebody at the front of the fleet insists that it is a perfectly sensible route.

😂 Besides, the insurance premiums would be absolutely ridiculous.

## 🪞 Perhaps the Change We Want Starts Much Closer to Home.

It is easy to demand better leadership. It is easy to criticise institutions and organisations. Sometimes that criticism is entirely justified.

But perhaps we should also ask ourselves a few questions.

- Do we treat people fairly when nobody is watching?
- Do we tell the truth when a lie would be more convenient?
- Do we respect people who cannot offer us anything in return?
- Do we honour our agreements?
- Do we take responsibility when we make mistakes?
- Do we show kindness without expecting a reward?

These are not complicated questions.

They do not require a parliamentary committee, a corporate strategy, or a million-rand advertising campaign.

They require something much simpler.

Character.

Perhaps the first step towards a better society is not waiting for somebody else to become a better person. Perhaps it begins when we decide not to become part of the problem.

And perhaps, this Christmas, we could try something revolutionary.

Be kind without advertising it. Help without calculating the return. Give without expecting applause. Treat people fairly even when there is no financial incentive.

Imagine that!

🎁 **Goodwill. No subscription required. No hidden charges. No expiry date.**

## ❤️ One Last Question Before Christmas.

We cannot always choose who leads us. We cannot always control the institutions around us. We cannot always change the direction of the flagship.

But we can choose the direction of our own ship.

So, when leadership loses its way, must humanity follow?

When dishonesty becomes fashionable, must honesty become foolish?

When greed becomes normal, must generosity become extinct?

And when the Christmas season arrives, will we remember the people around us — or merely the products we have not yet purchased?

Maybe the world does not need another spectacular Christmas promotion.

Maybe it needs a little more patience. A little more honesty. A little more compassion. A little more Ubuntu.

And perhaps a few people prepared to stand up and say:

**"I don't care what everybody else is doing. I still know the difference between right and wrong."**

Because the flagship may be lost. The captain may be confused. The navigation system may be making noises that sound suspiciously like a blender full of marbles.

But you still have a steering wheel.

Use it.

*Are we becoming the people we criticise, simply because we have convinced ourselves that everybody else is doing it?*

What do you think? Have we lost our sense of goodwill, or is it still there, waiting for ordinary people to bring it back?

**Let's have that conversation.**
