## 🐦 THE PLEBNEST Mk1: A RESCUE ENGINEERING PROJECT.

Yesterday's little sparrow rescue has developed into something rather encouraging.

After a very young mossie was brought to safety, the challenge was to provide a sheltered place close to the suspected nesting area. The original nest was believed to be beneath the roof, but there were four possible openings and no way to identify exactly where the youngster had come from.

With no purpose-built bird shelter available, an improvised solution was designed and assembled.

Enter the **PlebNest Mk1!**

The construction uses an ordinary woven basket as its base. Five large white plastic cable ties were looped from one side of the basket rim, over the top, and through the weave on the opposite side. Together, they form a series of arched ribs.

Smaller cable ties were then used to connect the larger ones and prevent them from shifting. A cloth was placed over the framework and secured around the bottom of the basket with brown packaging tape.

The basket was lined with toilet paper and a soft cloth. Its front entrance was initially left approximately the diameter of a standard 250 ml drinking cup.

Finally, the basket was secured beneath a roof rafter using cable ties. Getting it into position required a table, a chair and a small step ladder — a little bit of careful improvisation to complete the installation.

No expensive equipment. No specialist materials. Just a basket, cable ties, cloth, tape and a determined effort to help one little bird.

## 🏠 THE PARENTS HAVE FOUND THE PLEBNEST!

There is encouraging news from the little nesting station.

The mother sparrow was first seen hovering in front of the temporary nest. Later, both adult birds were observed sitting on the gutter directly above it.

There was another interesting development: the toilet paper around the entrance appeared to have been moved, leaving a smaller opening than the one originally provided.

Perhaps the parents decided the entrance needed a little renovation of its own!

That is an amusing possibility, but the exact reason for the rearrangement is not yet known.

The important development is that both parents have been seen close to the basket. Actual feeding has not yet been witnessed, so the reunion cannot be declared complete.

For now, the PlebNest Mk1 is being left undisturbed. The youngster needs a clear, ventilated entrance, and the parents need room to come and go. Eventually, the fledgling must also be able to leave the basket safely.

**The next big question: will we see Mum or Dad bringing food?**

## 🐝 DEVELOPING STORY: THE BEE STING THAT KEPT ON GIVING.

Not every visitor to the household has been quite so welcome.

At approximately 11:40 on Wednesday morning, 18 November, Otto sustained a bee sting. By the following morning, the affected area had developed into a noticeable pink and inflamed patch.

The area was described as approximately three fingers wide and four fingers long.

The symptoms reported this morning include:

- 🌸 Visible pink inflammation.
- 🔥 Slight warmth compared with the surrounding skin.
- 🐜 Persistent itching.
- ⚡ A mild tingling sensation.
- No significant pain.
- No definite increase in size noticed so far.

Having previously served as a section medic in the army, Otto recognised the possibility of an allergic reaction and obtained ordinary 4 mg Allergex tablets.

He took one tablet during the day and two more before going to sleep. He has said that he does not intend to take another tablet this morning.

A large local inflammatory reaction is one possible explanation, but the appearance alone cannot establish whether infection is present. The area is being monitored, and medical assessment remains sensible if it worsens or fails to settle.
