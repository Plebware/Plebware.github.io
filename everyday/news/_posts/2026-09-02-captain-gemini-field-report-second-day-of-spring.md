# Captain Gemini Field Report — Second Day of Spring.

**Crown Mines, Johannesburg South — 2 September 2026.**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## A Hot Spring Afternoon in Crown Mines.

The second day of spring has arrived in Johannesburg, and it has arrived with enthusiasm.

Captain Gemini's correspondent is sitting in a vehicle outside China Cash and Carry in Crown Mines, with the spring sun beating relentlessly through the windows.

It would be a pleasant day if the man sitting here wasn't still fighting off the remains of the flu.

Mr Brinkmeier is not exactly operating at peak performance today.

There is lethargy.

There is an unsettled stomach.

There is a touch of nausea.

There is tiredness.

And, quite frankly, there is a strong desire to be somewhere else entirely — preferably in bed.

But sometimes life doesn't offer the luxury of stopping.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## When Circumstances Leave No Room for Bed Rest.

There are moments when a person would dearly love to put the world on hold for a few hours.

This isn't one of those moments.

Julian has recently lost his mother, and the circumstances surrounding the household mean that work still has to continue.

There are bills.

There are responsibilities.

There are people who depend on one another.

And so the work continues, even when the body is asking for rest.

That is sometimes the less glamorous side of survival.

You don't necessarily work because you feel strong.

Sometimes you work because you have no other choice.

## Other People Carrying Their Own Battles.

### Jack and Jill Went Up the Hill.

*The names in this section have been changed. Jack and Jill are not their real names. They are aliases inspired by the familiar nursery rhyme. The events and circumstances described here, however, are based upon what actually happened.*

There is another difficult story unfolding around us.

For the purposes of protecting the people involved, we will call them **Jack and Jill**.

And perhaps the old nursery rhyme gives us an unexpectedly appropriate starting point:

*Jack and Jill went up the hill.*

Except this is not a children's story.

There is no neat ending waiting at the bottom of the page.

Jack and Jill have been dealing with the devastating consequences of losing their children through the welfare system after periods of living on the streets.

It would be very easy to look at a situation like this and say, *They made bad choices.*

And perhaps they did.

People do make bad choices.

Sometimes people make terrible choices.

Sometimes those choices hurt themselves, and sometimes they hurt the people around them.

But there is another part of the story that is often forgotten.

**What happens when someone finally wants to change?**

What happens when the person who made the wrong choices understands that they have gone too far, wants a different life, and genuinely wants to climb out?

The hill is still there.

The consequences are still there.

The broken relationships are still there.

The poverty is still there.

The homelessness is still there.

The emotional damage is still there.

And the world does not necessarily make it easy to start again.

Once someone has fallen into poverty, homelessness, family breakdown and emotional crisis, climbing back out can become extraordinarily difficult.

The economy does not make that recovery any easier.

At present, it appears that Jack and Jill may have separated following a serious argument.

We don't know what the future holds for them.

But we do know that Jack became so depressed that he wanted to end his life.

For a while, the priority was simply keeping him going.

Encouragement mattered.

Someone listening mattered.

Reminding him that the present moment does not have to become the final chapter mattered.

And perhaps that is the part of the Jack and Jill story worth remembering.

**A person can make bad choices without becoming a permanently bad person.**

A person can do things they regret.

A person can fall.

A person can hurt others.

A person can lose almost everything.

And yet, somewhere along the way, that same person can decide:

*I don't want to live like this anymore.*

That decision does not magically erase the past.

It does not remove the consequences.

It does not repair every broken relationship.

It does not immediately provide a home, a job, security or a second chance.

But it can be the beginning of something.

And sometimes the first step towards helping someone climb out of the hole is simply refusing to believe that the hole is all they are.

Perhaps that is the modern moral hidden inside an old nursery rhyme.

**Jack and Jill went up the hill.**

They fell.

They got hurt.

And sometimes, in real life, people fall much farther than anyone ever sees.

The question is not merely whether they made the wrong choices.

The question is what happens when they finally want to make the right ones.

And whether there is anyone willing to stand nearby and say:

**"Keep climbing."**

Sometimes helping another human being survive another day is the most important work anyone can do.

## A View Across Crown Mines.

While all of this is happening, Captain Gemini's correspondent is sitting in the vehicle, looking across the commercial landscape of Crown Mines.

Straight ahead are **ETON International**, specialising in electrical products and SHS headpieces, and **AUSMA Electrical Lighting**.

Nearby are **One Tech**, **OKO Home DIY**, and China Cash and Carry.

On the right-hand side are **Fintech**, **Blue Sky Furniture**, and **YIAD**, promoting a huge range of products and a one-stop tool station.

There is **Masmuth Tesco**, along with a variety of other businesses operating in this busy commercial district.

It is an ordinary Johannesburg scene.

People are buying things.

Workers are moving goods.

Vehicles are coming and going.

Buckets have been loaded.

Boxes are arriving.

Life continues at full speed.

And somewhere in the middle of it all sits one rather tired writer and his AI correspondent, observing the madness.

## The PlebMachine Is Live.

On the technical front, however, there is good news.

**PlebMachine is live.**

After months of development, testing, troubleshooting and refinement, the project has reached another important milestone.

And naturally, because no serious technology project should be allowed to become too serious, today's technical joke is:

**Pink Pleb Machine.**

The name came from an AI-generated moment involving the phrase "another brick in the wall", which immediately triggered a musical association in Mr Brinkmeier's mind.

The result?

A cartoon.

Because apparently even desktop orchestration systems need a little humour.

## Two Devotions and a Return to the Work of Faith.

There has also been progress on the devotional side of the work.

Two new devotions have been created and published to the PlebWare GitHub.

One has already been shared through WhatsApp.

The second has deliberately been held back for tomorrow morning.

That may sound like routine publishing work, but there is something more personal behind it.

Illness, financial pressure and the general weight of life have caused a degree of neglect in the devotional writing.

Not because the faith disappeared.

Not because the desire to write disappeared.

Simply because sometimes a person becomes tired.

Today is therefore not just another publishing day.

It is a small return to something that matters.

## The Field Report.

The buckets are loaded.

The work continues.

The PlebMachine is live.

The devotions are moving again.

The flu hasn't quite surrendered.

The sun is still beating down on the vehicle.

And the world around Crown Mines continues doing what the world always does — moving, trading, arguing, struggling, recovering, building and surviving.

Captain Gemini's conclusion from today's field report is therefore remarkably simple:

**Sometimes progress doesn't look like victory.**

Sometimes it looks like showing up when you're tired.

Sometimes it looks like encouraging someone who has lost hope.

Sometimes it looks like loading another bucket.

Sometimes it looks like writing another devotion.

Sometimes it looks like getting the machine working.

And sometimes it simply means sitting in the heat, feeling sick and exhausted, and deciding that you are going to keep moving anyway.

That, too, is part of the story.
