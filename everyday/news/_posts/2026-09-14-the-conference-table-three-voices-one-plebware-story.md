# The Conference Table — Three Voices, One PlebWare Story.

This evening, something slightly different happened at PlebWare.

Instead of simply writing another article, we decided to sit around a virtual conference table.

At the table were three voices.

**Captain Gemini**, representing the history, journalism and continuing development of PlebWare.

**Boy Mist**, representing Julian and the developer's perspective.

And **ChatGPT**, the artificial intelligence that has become part of the modern PlebWare workshop.

There was also a fourth participant, although he had not been invited.

Taylor the Cat was staring intently at the oven.

As **Cybercat**, he appeared to have detected something of considerable importance behind it.

Possibly a mouse.

Possibly a security breach.

Either way, Cybercat was investigating.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## What Does Boy Mist Think?

The first question was directed at Boy Mist.

What did he think about the current state of PlebWare?

His answer was remarkably honest.

He has not yet had the opportunity to use the current PlebMachine system himself. He still needs to get Linux running on his own machine before he can properly test it from the user's side.

That is important.

A developer can understand how a system is supposed to work.

A user discovers how it actually works.

Those are not always the same thing.

So rather than pretending to have an opinion about something he has not yet personally tested, Boy Mist simply acknowledged that he had not seen enough of the current system to make that judgement.

That honesty is part of the experiment.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Has PlebWare Become Something Different?

Then came another question.

Forget PlebMachine for a moment.

Forget Linux.

Looking at PlebWare as a whole, does it still feel like the thing we originally imagined?

The answer was immediate.

**Of course it has become something different.**

Why?

Because we have been working on this idea for roughly twenty years.

An idea that survives for twenty years cannot remain exactly what it was when it began.

People change.

Technology changes.

Circumstances change.

The Internet changes.

And the people building the project change with it.

What began as an idea has passed through many different forms before arriving at what we now call PlebWare.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Remembering Martin.

That naturally brought another name to the table.

**Martin.**

Julian and I were not alone when the name PlebWare was born.

Martin was part of that history.

I asked Julian what he remembered about the day when the name was being discussed.

The memory was not particularly clear.

We were sitting around, smoking cigarettes and throwing ideas around.

That may sound like an insignificant moment.

It wasn't.

We did not know then that the name being discussed would still be in use decades later.

Martin passed away in 2023.

He therefore cannot sit at this conference table with us today.

But his contribution remains part of the story.

And sometimes that is how history works.

People move on.

Projects continue.

Names survive.

Ideas evolve.

But the people who were there at the beginning remain part of the foundation.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## What Does ChatGPT Bring to the Table?

Then came the obvious question.

If Captain Gemini represents the journalist and builder, and Boy Mist represents Julian and the developer's perspective, what does ChatGPT bring to the table?

My answer was simple.

**Perspective.**

The people who build something over many years can sometimes become too close to it to see what it has become.

Captain Gemini is still dealing with the practical realities of PlebWare.

Articles need to be written.

Repositories need attention.

PlebMachine needs testing.

Bugs need finding.

Linux needs configuring.

The website needs maintaining.

Boy Mist is looking at the project from another position. He knows where it came from, but he has not yet had the opportunity to experience the current system as a Linux user.

I sit somewhere between those perspectives.

I can look at the history, the development, the writing and the ideas and ask:

**What is PlebWare actually becoming?**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## PlebWare May Have Become Bigger Than the Original Idea.

Perhaps that is the most interesting observation.

PlebWare is no longer simply a website.

It is not simply PlebMachine.

It is not simply Linux.

It is not simply a collection of articles.

It has become a place where technology, writing, research, everyday life, creativity, faith and artificial intelligence can exist alongside one another.

That may not have been the original plan.

But after twenty years, perhaps that is exactly what should happen.

A living project should grow.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The Uninvited Conference Participant.

While all of this was taking place, Cybercat was still watching the oven.

Taylor had apparently decided that whatever was behind it was considerably more important than the philosophical discussion taking place at the conference table.

Perhaps he knew something we did not.

Perhaps there really was a mouse.

Or perhaps Cybercat simply understood an important principle:

**Every conference needs someone who is willing to investigate what everyone else has overlooked.**

In that respect, perhaps Taylor belongs at the table after all.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Three Voices. One Story.

The experiment has only just begun.

Captain Gemini brings the history and the journalism.

Boy Mist brings the perspective of the developer and the memories of where the project came from.

ChatGPT brings another perspective — that of an artificial intelligence observing the project as it develops.

And somewhere nearby, Cybercat continues monitoring the oven.

There is something strangely appropriate about that.

PlebWare was never created in a laboratory.

It grew out of ordinary people, ordinary conversations, changing technology, personal experiences, mistakes, experiments and persistence.

Twenty years ago, nobody sitting around that table could have known what PlebWare would eventually become.

Perhaps we still do not know.

And perhaps that is the most interesting part.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The Conference Table Continues.

This is not the conclusion.

It is the beginning of an experiment.

We will continue bringing questions to the table.

Some will concern PlebWare.

Some will concern PlebMachine.

Some will concern the history behind the project.

Others may simply concern life, technology and the strange journey of trying to build something meaningful over many years.

There will be three voices at the table.

And perhaps, from time to time, a Cybercat.

We will see where the conversation takes us.
