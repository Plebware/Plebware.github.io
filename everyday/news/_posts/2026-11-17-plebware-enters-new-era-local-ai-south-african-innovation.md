Our philosophy remains straightforward: technology should expand human capability, not eliminate human responsibility.

## 🤖 PlebAI Chatbot — A Major Technological Breakthrough!

Yesterday marked another significant achievement: the completion of the PlebAI chatbot.

Following extensive experimentation with locally hosted artificial intelligence, we now have a working chatbot environment that allows us to interact with multiple AI models.

Three AI models are currently available, each offering different capabilities and approaches to handling requests.

We can select between quicker responses for everyday writing and more detailed processing for research, extended articles and complex assignments.

Coding assistance is another important part of the development.

We are also investigating the integration of a DeepSeek module to expand the capabilities available within the PlebAI environment.

The objective is to create a flexible AI workspace rather than rely on one model for every task.

Local AI also introduces an important advantage: we can experiment with and develop our own working environment without making every interaction dependent on a remote commercial chatbot.

PlebAI represents an important step towards greater independence, experimentation and technological self-reliance.

## 🇿🇦 President Ramaphosa Takes South Africa Into the AI Conversation!

There is some particularly interesting news developing on the national front.

President Cyril Ramaphosa is currently leading a South African delegation on a working visit to Silicon Valley in California, scheduled for 15 to 21 November 2026.

The delegation includes representatives from government, business, universities and research institutions.

The purpose is to engage with leading technology companies, investors, researchers and artificial intelligence specialists to establish how South Africa can participate more meaningfully in the global AI revolution.

In his presidential newsletter on 16 November, Ramaphosa made his position clear: South Africa must become a producer of artificial intelligence innovation rather than remain merely a consumer of technology developed elsewhere.

This is a position we wholeheartedly support.

We do not necessarily agree with every decision made by our government, but encouraging South African technological development, research, innovation and skills creation is something we can certainly get behind.

### 🔬 What Does the President Want to Achieve?

Several important objectives have emerged from the visit.

**1. 💻 Develop South African Technological Capabilities.**

South Africa must develop its own businesses, intellectual property, research capacity and technological expertise.

**2. 💰 Encourage Investment.**

The country wants to attract investment into data centres, digital infrastructure, cloud computing and AI development.

**3. 🎓 Develop Local Skills.**

Universities, researchers, young people and businesses must be equipped to participate in the new technological economy.

**4. 🏥 Improve Public Services.**

Artificial intelligence could contribute to better healthcare, education, government administration, agriculture, transport and energy management.

**5. 🛡️ Protect South Africa's Digital Sovereignty.**

The country must embrace technological progress while retaining appropriate control over its digital future.

One particularly interesting development is the President's proposal that a focused programme of practical initiatives should be identified within 90 days of the delegation's return.

These initiatives are expected to include investment opportunities, research partnerships, skills development and practical AI applications.

There is, however, still work to do on South Africa's national AI policy. The earlier draft was withdrawn in June 2026 for revision, so a final national policy should not be assumed to be in place.

For those of us working in technology, these developments are worth following closely.

South Africa has an opportunity to become more than a customer of international technology companies. We can develop our own solutions, cultivate local talent and contribute to the future of artificial intelligence.

And perhaps that is where smaller independent projects such as PlebWare have a role to play.

We may not have the resources of Silicon Valley, but innovation does not have to begin with a multinational corporation.

Sometimes it begins with a computer, a determined individual and an idea that refuses to go away. 💡
