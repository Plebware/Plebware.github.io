# 🌐 PlebWare — A Living Knowledge Library and Human-Centred Ecosystem.

**Technology Must Serve Humanity.**

PlebWare is an independent publishing, learning and knowledge-sharing platform. It brings together practical guidance, research, technical documentation, creative work and ideas intended to help ordinary people learn, create, understand technology and make informed decisions.

It is not simply a collection of articles. It is a growing knowledge library supported by a publishing structure, editorial conventions, accessibility features and a wider ecosystem of related projects.

This overview explains what PlebWare is, how its content is organised, how its quality is checked and how it connects with PlebMachine and Pleb Circle.

## 💙 Our Guiding Philosophy.

PlebWare is built around one central principle:

**Technology Must Serve Humanity.**

Technology should help people learn, create, communicate, solve problems and make informed decisions. It should not make people feel excluded or dependent simply because they are not specialists.

We value practical usefulness over unnecessary complexity, understanding over hype, and human judgement over blind reliance on automated systems.

PlebWare is a work in progress. Its content, tools and structure will continue to develop as we learn, test, correct and improve.

## 📚 Twelve Principal Categories.

The website uses twelve principal categories to make its growing library easier to explore.

1. **Everyday** — practical information for daily life, household organisation and everyday computing.
2. **Author** — fiction, non-fiction, poetry, journalism and other forms of written expression.
3. **Study** — lessons, educational resources and explanations that support learning.
4. **Research** — investigation, comparisons, source evaluation and evidence-informed exploration.
5. **Graphics** — visual design, illustration, image creation and editing.
6. **Music** — musical creativity, composition, recording and sound production.
7. **Video** — moving images, visual storytelling and video production.
8. **Broadcast** — podcasts, streaming, radio, live presentation and audience communication.
9. **AI Helpers** — practical artificial-intelligence tools, prompts, workflows and responsible use.
10. **Developer** — programming, Linux, automation, web development and technical projects.
11. **Accounting** — budgeting, bookkeeping, financial organisation and related tools.
12. **Leisure** — hobbies, games, recreation and interests pursued for enjoyment.

Subcategories help visitors move from broad subjects to more specialised material. Classification should follow the actual purpose and subject of an article, rather than a keyword in its title.

The live repository remains the authority for current category names, subcategory paths and published content.

## 🧭 Publishing With Consistent Standards.

A growing website needs more than new articles. It needs repeatable conventions that help contributors prepare content correctly and make the site easier to maintain.

PlebWare uses valid Jekyll front matter, a clear distinction between metadata titles and visible article headings, consistent Markdown, meaningful links and image descriptions, and established PlebVox markers for suitable spoken content.

The shared website theme provides common presentation and technical features. These include canonical page addresses, page-description handling, social-sharing metadata and structured data appropriate to the page layout.

The site also uses a generated sitemap and a robots.txt reference to help search engines discover its pages. Google Search Console ownership verification has been completed and the sitemap submitted. Search Console processing and indexing reports are monitored separately; submission does not guarantee that every page will be indexed or achieve a particular ranking.

The objective is to keep the site useful, understandable, maintainable and discoverable without duplicating functions already provided by the shared theme.

## 🛡️ The Three-Stage Editorial Quality Process.

PlebWare distinguishes between the initial review that accompanies publication, the second review represented by the Human Reviewed badge, and the third review represented by PlebVox listening verification.

These are three separate stages.

### 1. Initial Editorial Review.

Otto reviews every article as it is published. This is the first editorial check. It is a normal part of the publishing workflow and does not automatically earn the Human Reviewed badge.

### 2. Human Reviewed — Second Review and Polish.

The Human Reviewed badge is added only after a second review. This pass double-checks the article, corrects errors, improves wording and polishes the overall presentation.

It is a deliberate second check, not simply a record that the article was published.

### 3. PlebVox Verified — Third Review Through Listening.

PlebVox verification follows a third review: listening to the complete narrated version of the article.

Otto may listen to the narration more than once—twice, three times or as often as necessary—to check the spoken result. This includes text-to-speech mistakes, pronunciation, awkward phrasing, punctuation and other issues that may affect clarity or accessibility.

Only after the complete audio has been listened to and approved does Otto add the PlebVox verification marker.

The process can be summarised as:

**First reviewed. Double-checked and polished. Then triple-checked through listening.**

The markers are deliberately distinct:

- `<!-- REVIEWED -->` represents the second, Human Reviewed editorial pass.
- `<!---VOXED--->` represents the complete PlebVox narration having been listened to and approved.
- `<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->` and `<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->` identify text intended for narration. They are not verification badges.

This system gives visitors a meaningful indication of the checks an article has passed. It also protects the integrity of the badges: they are used only when the corresponding human review has actually taken place.

## 🔊 PlebVox — Making Written Knowledge Easier to Access.

PlebVox is PlebWare's read-aloud feature for suitable articles. It provides another way to engage with written material and can be useful for visitors who prefer listening or find extended screen reading difficult.

Narration requires editorial attention of its own. A sentence can look perfectly acceptable in writing but sound unnatural when spoken. Technical terms, names, abbreviations, punctuation and sentence length can all affect the listening experience.

PlebVox section markers are placed so that section headings are included in the narration. Tables and code-heavy material are handled according to the established conventions rather than being read indiscriminately.

The final listening check is therefore an accessibility and quality-control measure. It evaluates the spoken article as a complete experience.

## 🤖 PlebMachine — The Practical Technology Environment.

PlebMachine is a related project with a different role from the website.

Where PlebWare publishes and organises knowledge, PlebMachine is being developed as a context-aware desktop and technology environment for Linux. Its broader aim is to help people organise their computing around their activities and needs, while keeping the user in control of the system.

PlebWare supports this work through documentation, tutorials, research and development notes. PlebMachine, in turn, gives practical form to ideas explored and documented through the wider project.

The two projects share a philosophy, but they are not interchangeable: one is the knowledge and publishing centre; the other is the practical desktop environment.

## 🤝 Pleb Circle — The Human Community.

Pleb Circle is the community and communication environment associated with the wider PlebWare project.

Its purpose is meaningful interaction: people introducing themselves, exchanging ideas, asking questions, sharing experience and helping one another. It is intended to be a place for participation rather than a passive announcement channel.

Relevant PlebWare and PlebMachine developments are being mirrored into the Pleb Circle WhatsApp community so members can follow the work and remain connected.

The community has its own role. It is not meant to reproduce the entire website or become a second article directory. PlebWare is where knowledge is published; PlebMachine is where practical computing workflows are developed; Pleb Circle is where people connect and participate.

## 🔄 One Ecosystem, Three Complementary Environments.

The relationship can be expressed simply:

- **PlebWare — Knowledge and Publishing.**
- **PlebMachine — Tools and Productivity.**
- **Pleb Circle — People and Participation.**

The twelve principal categories provide a shared organisational language across the ecosystem, while each environment serves its own purpose.

A subject such as Linux may have explanatory articles in PlebWare, practical tools and workflows in PlebMachine, and a discussion space in Pleb Circle. A writing subject may have published work and guidance on the website, suitable tools in the desktop environment, and a community conversation for writers.

Consistency does not mean forcing every environment to behave identically. It means preserving a recognisable purpose and structure while allowing each part to do what it does best.

## 🔍 Discoverability and the Road Ahead.

PlebWare has taken the step of verifying its website through Google Search Console and submitting its sitemap. This provides a way to monitor discovery, indexing reports and technical issues as Google processes the site.

Search visibility is useful because it helps people find relevant information. But rankings are not the purpose of the project. The purpose is to publish material that is useful, accurate, understandable and worth finding.

The ongoing work includes maintaining existing articles, improving internal links, checking navigation, reviewing technical reports, developing PlebMachine and keeping the Pleb Circle community informed.

We will continue to work in manageable stages, verify changes and document what has actually been completed.

## 🌱 The PlebWare Commitment.

PlebWare does not claim to have every answer. It aims to make it easier to ask better questions, find useful information, develop practical skills and share what we learn.

Its value lies not only in the number of pages it contains, but in the care taken to organise, review, maintain and make that knowledge accessible.

PlebWare, PlebMachine and Pleb Circle are three complementary environments built around one shared belief:

**Technology Must Serve Humanity.**

Learn. Build. Create. Share. And never stop asking why.
