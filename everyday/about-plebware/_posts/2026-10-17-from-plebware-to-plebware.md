# From Plebware to PlebWare: The Skeleton Crew

Every project has a history, even when that history is scattered across old computers, forgotten websites, archived pages, and memories.

PlebWare did not appear out of nowhere in 2026. Its present form grew from earlier experiments, years of learning, and an idea that continued to survive even when circumstances changed.

To understand what we are building today, it helps to understand what came before.

## What Happened to Plebware?

The original Plebware website was part of a much longer online journey. The old domain, `plebware.com`, became home to an earlier chapter of that work.

That chapter mattered. It was a place to experiment, publish, learn, and establish an identity on the internet.

The site was active during the 2010–2020 period, and the Wayback Machine preserves snapshots of parts of that earlier online presence. Those archived pages help document the history of the project.

Then circumstances changed.

I lost my job in late 2019, and the disruption surrounding COVID-19 made an already difficult period harder. In 2020, the old domain was lost.

Losing a domain is not simply losing a web address. It can feel as though a room full of work has suddenly become inaccessible. Years of effort, plans, and identity can be tied to a name that people have come to recognise.

But losing a domain does not automatically erase the ideas that gave it meaning.

The old website belonged to an earlier chapter. Its loss was painful, but it did not mean that the underlying vision had to disappear.

## The Meaning Behind the Name

The name Plebware reaches back to 2006.

It grew from an idea about ordinary people: the people who use technology, learn from it, experiment with it, and try to make it serve their needs.

A person does not need to work for a large technology company to have useful ideas. A person does not need an expensive laboratory to begin experimenting. Curiosity, patience, practical experience, and a willingness to learn can take someone a long way.

That human element remains central to the project.

Over time, the name and its meaning developed. Plebware came to represent the human element, while PlebWare became the name for the wider philosophy, creative environment, and collection of work built around it.

The distinction is about more than capital letters. It reflects the growth of an idea into a broader ecosystem.

Today, PlebWare includes writing, learning, Linux, software, research, creative work, and the continuing development of PlebMachine.

The technology has grown, but the original human question remains:

**How can ordinary people use technology to do useful, meaningful things?**

## Why a Skeleton Crew?

PlebWare is not being built by a large organisation with departments for every task.

It is being built by a small team with limited resources, different strengths, and a willingness to keep learning.

That is the spirit behind the name *Skeleton Crew*.

The expression also carries a personal literary association for me through Stephen King's book *Skeleton Crew*. Here, however, the phrase describes the small group doing the work with the resources available.

At the centre of this effort are Otto Brinkmeier and Jullian “Juelz” De Villiers.

We do not have identical skills or identical circumstances. We bring different interests and abilities to the table, and that is part of what makes the partnership useful.

I handle much of the writing, website structure, design, graphics, proofreading, and video-related work. Juelz contributes to audio, music, collaboration, and other creative tasks.

Those are broad descriptions rather than rigid job titles. We help where we can, and the work develops as our abilities and needs change.

Our circumstances also shape the way we work. Juelz faces challenges involving his eyesight and epilepsy, and we have to account for the realities of daily life rather than pretend that every day offers unlimited time and energy.

The point is not that we can do everything.

The point is that we can still do something.

## A New Online Base of Operations

The current PlebWare website is not simply a restoration of the old site. It is a new chapter built on the lessons, interests, and ideas that came before it.

The online environment has changed dramatically since the early days of free web space and modest personal websites. Today, we can use modern publishing tools, version control, open-source software, and new ways of learning and collaborating.

PlebWare is using those opportunities to build a more organised home for its work.

The site brings together articles, technical documentation, personal reflections, learning resources, and creative experiments. It is both a publishing environment and a place where ideas can be developed and recorded.

PlebMachine is part of that wider journey: an attempt to make the computer environment more useful to the person using it.

The aim is not to make technology the boss. It is to make technology serve people.

## Rebuilding Without Pretending to Be Finished

A project rebuilt from the ground up will have rough edges.

Some ideas will work well. Others will need to be revised. Some plans will be abandoned when experience shows that a different approach is better.

We ask for patience because PlebWare is an evolving project, not a finished corporate product.

That does not excuse carelessness. It means being honest about the stage of development and being willing to improve what needs improving.

We would rather keep learning and correcting our work than pretend to have all the answers.

## The Voyage Continues

The old domain may be gone, but the history behind it still matters.

The early experiments mattered. The years of learning mattered. The setbacks mattered. The partnership matters.

PlebWare carries that history forward, not by trying to recreate every detail of the past, but by taking what remains useful and building something new.

This is the work of a small crew: learning, creating, documenting, correcting, and moving forward one step at a time.

The name has a new home.

The vision has another chapter.

And the voyage continues.
