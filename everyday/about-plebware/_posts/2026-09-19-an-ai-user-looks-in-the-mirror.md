# 🤖 Artificial Intelligence's Mirror — What Three Artificial Intelligences See in an Artificial Intelligence User.

Three artificial intelligences were asked the same basic question:

**How good am I at using Artificial Intelligence, where do I stand as a free-tier Artificial Intelligence user, and what should I improve?**

The answers were interesting because they were not identical.

ChatGPT described me as an **advanced practical Artificial Intelligence user** moving toward super-user territory.

Meta AI described me as a **Power User** and highlighted the way I build repeatable systems around Artificial Intelligence.

Gemini went further and described me as a **system architect**, particularly because of the way I combine Artificial Intelligence with Linux, programming, writing and PlebWare.

Those descriptions are assessments by Artificial Intelligence systems, not independently verified rankings or measurements.

But taken together, they gave me something more useful than a number.

They gave me a mirror.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 🔍 Three AIs, Three Perspectives.

The three assessments came from different Artificial Intelligence systems with different styles and capabilities.

ChatGPT focused heavily on practical Artificial Intelligence use: using Artificial Intelligence as part of a wider productivity and development system rather than simply asking isolated questions.

Meta AI noticed the structure behind the work. It highlighted modular thinking, repeatable workflows, clear boundaries between what the user asks an Artificial Intelligence to do and what the Artificial Intelligence is allowed to execute, and the importance of teaching what has been learned.

Gemini focused particularly on the broader system: combining Artificial Intelligence with Linux, shell scripting, Python, Inkscape, GIMP, writing and the PlebMachine project.

All three perspectives pointed toward the same broad observation:

**I am no longer simply experimenting with Artificial Intelligence. I am building workflows around it.**

That distinction matters.

There is a big difference between asking:

> "What can this Artificial Intelligence do?"

and asking:

> **"What job should I give this Artificial Intelligence?"**

## 🧩 What They All Saw.

There were several common themes.

### Systems Thinking.

PlebWare and PlebMachine are not single applications.

They are collections of ideas, scripts, documentation, tools and workflows that need to work together.

Artificial Intelligence has become another component in that system.

### Multi-Model Collaboration.

Instead of assuming that one Artificial Intelligence should do everything, I have experimented with several.

ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek and Meta AI can all occupy different seats at the Conference Table.

The useful question is not which one is "the best."

The useful question is:

**Which tool is appropriate for this particular job?**

### Artificial Intelligence as a Working Partner.

Artificial Intelligence has become part of writing, research, programming, Linux troubleshooting, documentation, website maintenance and experimentation.

That is a different level of use from occasional question-and-answer interaction.

### Human Oversight.

The strongest common thread was also the simplest:

**The human still has to think.**

Artificial Intelligence can generate.

Artificial Intelligence can explain.

Artificial Intelligence can suggest.

Artificial Intelligence can analyse.

But the human still has to decide what is true, what is useful and what should actually happen.

## 📚 Where I Still Have Room to Grow.

This was probably the most useful part of the exercise.

My practical experience with Artificial Intelligence is stronger than my theoretical understanding of what is happening underneath.

I can use an Artificial Intelligence.

I can compare AIs.

I can build workflows around AIs.

I can use Artificial Intelligence to help write code.

But I want to understand the machinery better.

That means learning more about things such as:

- **Tokens.**
- **Context windows.**
- **Embeddings.**
- **Attention.**
- **Transformers.**
- **Inference.**
- **Temperature and sampling.**
- **System instructions.**
- **Tool calling.**
- **Agents.**
- **Retrieval-augmented generation.**
- **Structured outputs.**
- **Multimodal Artificial Intelligence.**
- **Hallucinations and model limitations.**

The goal is not to become a machine-learning engineer overnight.

The goal is to understand enough of the technology that I can use it more intelligently.

## 🚀 From Artificial Intelligence User to Artificial Intelligence Super User.

If there is a next level, I don't think it is simply learning more prompt tricks.

It is building reliable habits.

That means creating reusable prompt templates rather than reinventing every request.

It means keeping better Git discipline around technical projects.

It means learning enough Linux and programming fundamentals to recognise when an Artificial Intelligence-generated solution is suspicious.

It means experimenting with local Artificial Intelligence where appropriate.

It means eventually learning more about APIs and automation.

It means teaching other people what I learn.

And it means becoming comfortable saying:

> **"Try to prove this wrong."**

That may be one of the most valuable prompts an Artificial Intelligence user can learn.

An Artificial Intelligence that is only asked to confirm an idea can become an echo chamber.

An Artificial Intelligence that is asked to challenge the idea can become a useful testing partner.

## 👥 The Human Still Sits at the Table.

The Conference Table is an important part of how PlebWare thinks about Artificial Intelligence.

Artificial Intelligence systems can sit around the table.

They can discuss an idea.

They can generate code.

They can suggest alternatives.

They can critique one another.

But the human being still sits at the table.

That means humans remain responsible for purpose, values, evidence, testing, privacy, security, publication and the consequences of decisions.

The Artificial Intelligence does not get to become the authority simply because it sounds confident.

A convincing answer is still only an answer until it has been checked.

That lesson has been reinforced repeatedly while building PlebMachine.

There is a difference between:

**"This should work."**

and:

**"This has been tested and works."**

The second statement requires evidence.

## 🎓 What I Am Learning Next — And PlebBuddies Can Follow.

Being an advanced Artificial Intelligence user does not mean knowing everything about artificial intelligence.

One of the clearest things I learned from asking three different AIs to assess me is that my practical experience is ahead of my theoretical understanding.

So I am going back to school.

Not because I need another certificate hanging on the wall, but because I want to understand **what is happening underneath the Artificial Intelligence tools I already use every day.**

I have therefore chosen three free-to-study courses on Alison as my next Artificial Intelligence learning path.

### 1. 🤖 Prompt Engineering With Generative Artificial Intelligence.

This is the first course I plan to tackle.

It covers subjects that directly address one of my weaker areas, including **tokens, context windows, temperature, top-p, system and user prompts, ambiguity, token budgeting, limitations and validation.**

That should help me understand not just **how to talk to an Artificial Intelligence**, but why an Artificial Intelligence sometimes responds differently depending on how the request is constructed.

[Prompt Engineering With Generative Artificial Intelligence — Alison](ZXQH0QXZ)

### 2. 🧠 Introduction to Artificial Intelligence — IBM.

The second step is to broaden the foundation.

This course covers the relationship between **artificial intelligence, machine learning and deep learning**, along with neural networks, natural-language processing, reinforcement learning, Artificial Intelligence ethics, bias and privacy.

That should help put the technology I am already using into a much bigger picture.

[Introduction to Artificial Intelligence (Artificial Intelligence) — IBM on Alison](ZXQH0QXZ)

### 3. ⚙️ Machine Learning for Absolute Beginners.

The third course goes one step deeper into how machines actually learn.

It covers **features and labels, training, supervised and unsupervised learning, classification, regression, clustering, dimensionality reduction, overfitting and reinforcement learning.**

For someone who spends a lot of time working with Artificial Intelligence but does not come from a formal Artificial Intelligence or computer-science background, this is exactly the sort of foundation worth building.

[Machine Learning for Absolute Beginners — Alison](ZXQH0QXZ)

### 👥 PlebBuddies Can Follow Along.

This isn't just my personal learning plan.

If you are a PlebWare user, a developer, a writer, a student, a Linux enthusiast, or simply someone trying to get more out of Artificial Intelligence without spending a fortune, you can follow the same path.

The idea is simple:

**Learn the theory.**

**Use the tools.**

**Ask questions.**

**Test what you learn.**

**Then put it into practice.**

That is very much the PlebWare way.

And as I work through these courses, the interesting concepts can come back to the PlebWare table for discussion.

If I learn something useful, I can explain it.

If I don't understand something, I can ask.

If an Artificial Intelligence tells me something questionable, I can test it.

And if I discover that something I previously believed was wrong, I can correct it.

That last part may actually be the most important lesson of all.

### 🎓 The PlebWare Artificial Intelligence Learning Path.

For now, this becomes my informal **PlebWare Artificial Intelligence Master Class — Level 1**:

**Course 1 → Prompt Engineering and Generative Artificial Intelligence**

**Course 2 → Artificial Intelligence Fundamentals**

**Course 3 → Machine Learning Fundamentals**

Three courses.

A few hours of study.

And hopefully a much better understanding of what is happening behind the screen.

The goal isn't to become an Artificial Intelligence scientist.

The goal is to become a **better Artificial Intelligence user who understands enough of the machinery to use it intelligently, question it properly, and remain responsible for the final decision.**
