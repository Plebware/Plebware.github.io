# Welcome Aboard the Journey

Every journey has a beginning. Some begin with a grand plan. Others begin with curiosity, a little opportunity, and the determination to see what is possible.

Mine began with computers, a desire to learn, and a growing interest in what ordinary people could create with the technology available to them.

This is not the story of a corporation with a large budget or a team of professionals working behind closed doors. It is the story of an ordinary man who kept learning, experimenting, making mistakes, and trying again.

Welcome aboard.

If you would like to understand the idea behind the name, start with [Who or What Is PlebWare?](/everyday/about-plebware/2026/09/17/who-or-what-is-plebware.html). This personal introduction tells another part of the story: the people, experiences, and values behind the project.

## No Free Lunch — The Beginning

In 1999, I discovered the possibilities of having a website on the internet. The opportunity came with a modest allowance of approximately five megabytes of free web space.

Five megabytes might sound almost laughably small today. At the time, however, it was an opportunity.

I began learning how websites worked, experimenting with the tools available to me, and discovering how much could be done by someone prepared to teach himself.

There was no guarantee that I would become an expert. There was simply something I wanted to understand, and I was willing to put in the time to learn.

By then, computers were not entirely new to me.

My father bought me a Commodore VIC-20 in 1982. That little machine helped introduce me to a world in which a person could interact with technology, experiment, and discover what happened when an idea was put into practice.

Years later, in the backyard at Turffontein, Johannesburg, my mother helped me take another step forward by buying me my first PC. I was around 36 years old.

That gift mattered. It opened a door at a time when I was ready to explore what personal computers and the internet could offer.

The equipment changed over the years, but the curiosity remained.

The VIC-20 gave way to PCs. My experiments expanded. Websites became projects, projects became ideas, and ideas gradually developed into something much bigger.

PlebWare grew out of that continuing journey.

## Out of Necessity and Love

I have never pretended that I am a perfect writer.

Over the years, people have criticised my grammar, spelling, and way of expressing myself. Some of those criticisms have been uncomfortable, but I have continued learning and improving.

I have also pursued formal study, including a diploma in journalism. Yet a qualification does not remove every weakness, and being self-taught does not mean a person has nothing left to learn.

What matters to me is the willingness to keep working.

My circumstances are not those of a wealthy entrepreneur with unlimited time and money. I am approaching 63, living on a pension, and taking local driving work when the vehicle and circumstances allow. I have had to make practical choices about what I can afford, what I can maintain, and what I can realistically accomplish.

Juelz has his own challenges, including difficulties with his eyesight and epilepsy. Those realities affect how we work and what is possible for us.

We continue anyway.

PlebWare is built from necessity as well as love: the need to make useful things with the resources available to us, and the love of learning, creating, sharing knowledge, and working together.

We do not have to be wealthy to begin something worthwhile. We do not have to know everything before we start. We do, however, have to be prepared to learn from our mistakes.

Sometimes progress is a matter of taking one small step, solving one problem, writing one article, or getting one stubborn piece of software to work.

That is still progress.

## Words Are Good. Integrity Is Better.

I have spent more than 27 years exploring and working with the internet in one form or another. During that time, I have learned that putting words on a screen is easy compared with earning someone's trust.

I want PlebWare to be a place where honesty, integrity, and the desire to learn matter.

My Christian faith is an important part of who I am. I identify as a born-again, Spirit-filled follower of Jesus Christ, and I want my conduct to reflect the values I profess.

That does not mean claiming to be perfect. It means recognising that character matters, that truth matters, and that we should be willing to acknowledge our mistakes.

A website can contain thousands of pages and still mean very little if its words are not backed by integrity.

Equally, a small project created by ordinary people can have genuine value when it is built with care, honesty, and a willingness to serve others.

That is the standard I want to keep working towards.

## An Invitation, Not a Claim to Perfection

PlebWare is a work in progress. So am I.

There will be things we get right, things we need to improve, and ideas that will change as we learn more. We will sometimes need to correct ourselves and try a different approach.

I ask visitors to approach this place with curiosity and patience. Explore what interests you, question what needs questioning, and take what is useful to you.

You do not have to agree with everything here to be welcome.

The aim is to learn, build, create, and share — not to pretend that we have already reached the end of the road.

If you have ever wanted to make something but felt that you lacked the money, qualifications, equipment, or confidence, perhaps some part of this journey will encourage you to begin.

Start with what you have.

Learn what you need.

Keep your integrity.

And take the next step.

**Welcome aboard. The journey continues.**

— Otto Brinkmeier
