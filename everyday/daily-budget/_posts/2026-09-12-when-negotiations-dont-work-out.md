# When Negotiations Don't Work Out.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

There comes a point in any negotiation when you have to stop asking the same question and start looking at the situation for what it actually is.

For the past couple of months, I have been talking to my bosses about getting an increase.

The answer has essentially been that I am not really buying the vehicle.

Fair enough.

But that is not what I am doing at all.

I am buying **stock on behalf of the business**.

That can mean going to the butcher for forequarters, or visiting different outlets to buy coffee, rice and other supplies. Today alone, I was given R13,000 to work with. Then another R3,000 was added. Then another R600.

That meant I was handling **R16,600 in business cash** for today's purchases.

So the question is not whether I am buying a vehicle.

I am not.

The question is what happens when a driver is entrusted with R16,600 to purchase stock, while his own daily wage remains R150.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The R16,600 Question.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

Today's shopping list was not small.

There was chicken. There were forequarters. There were kidneys. There were four bales of Nojiwan basmati rice. And there were other goods that had to be collected from various outlets.

This is purchasing work on behalf of the business.

The money is not mine. I am trusted to carry it, spend it according to instructions, collect the stock, and make sure the goods get where they need to go.

That is a responsibility I take seriously.

But it makes the wage question even more difficult to ignore.

R16,600 was entrusted to me today.

My own day's earnings are R150.

Those are two very different numbers.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## What Other Drivers Are Earning.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

There is another part of this that bothers me.

I know other drivers. I speak to some of them regularly.

I know of at least two cases where drivers are earning around R200 a day, which works out to approximately R4,700 a month.

I am not writing this to say that their situation is better or worse than mine.

I am asking why this seems to be the sort of employment that is available to people who are already struggling.

And that brings me to another question.

Why are so many of the people doing this work older men?

Why pensioners?

Why people who are already desperate for additional income?

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Why Older Men?

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

I don't want to make an accusation that I cannot prove.

That is not the purpose of this article.

But I can ask questions.

Is it because older men are considered more reliable?

Is it because pensioners are willing to accept work that younger people might refuse?

Is it because older people are grateful for the opportunity to earn something?

Or is there another reason?

I have even wondered whether age makes a difference when dealing with traffic police.

Perhaps an older driver is perceived as less likely to be stopped.

Perhaps that is completely wrong.

I honestly don't know.

And that is precisely why I am asking rather than making a declaration.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## When Negotiation Reaches Its Limit.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

I have tried negotiating.

I have explained my position.

I have asked about an increase.

The response has not changed enough to change my circumstances.

Eventually, a person has to recognise that negotiation only works when both sides are prepared to move.

If one side keeps asking and the other side keeps saying no, then eventually the negotiation is over.

That doesn't necessarily mean that somebody is evil.

It doesn't mean that somebody is being exploited.

It simply means that the arrangement has reached a point where the numbers have to be examined honestly.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The Question Behind the Question.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

Perhaps the biggest question isn't actually about my increase.

Perhaps it is this:

What is the real value of the work being performed?

If somebody is prepared to spend their day driving, purchasing stock, collecting goods, dealing with traffic, fuel, vehicles, customers, responsibility and risk, what should that person's time actually be worth?

And if that person is a pensioner who has already spent a lifetime working, should desperation determine the value of his labour?

I don't have an easy answer.

But I think it is a question worth asking.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## I Am Not Looking for a Fight.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

This isn't an attack on my employers.

It is a record of where I find myself.

I have bills.

I have responsibilities.

I have limited working years left.

And I am trying to make the numbers work.

If negotiation doesn't work, then I have to look at the situation differently.

Perhaps I need to accept the arrangement.

Perhaps I need to find another arrangement.

Perhaps I need to create another source of income.

Or perhaps I simply need to stop expecting the situation to change because I have asked for it to change.

That last possibility is probably the hardest one to accept.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The Everyday Budget Lesson.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

A budget is not only about adding up money.

Sometimes a budget tells you when a working arrangement is no longer making sense.

R16,600 in business cash doesn't mean that I am financially secure. It is not my money. It is money I have been entrusted to use for purchasing stock.

R200 a day doesn't automatically mean that somebody else is being treated unfairly.

But both numbers tell us something.

They tell us that there are real people trying to make real lives work with real money.

And when the negotiations don't work out, the only responsible thing to do is look at the numbers honestly and decide what comes next.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## So, Why?

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

Why pensioners?

Why older men?

Why R200 a day for some drivers?

Why does somebody who can be entrusted with R16,600 to purchase chicken, forequarters, kidneys, rice and other stock still feel as though he needs to negotiate for a better income?

Why do some working arrangements make sense to the person paying, but leave the person doing the work wondering whether it makes sense at all?

I don't know.

I genuinely don't know.

But I think these are fair questions.

And sometimes asking the question is the first step toward discovering the answer.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Final Thought.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

There is a difference between complaining and recording reality.

I have complained.

I have negotiated.

I have asked.

Now I am recording what I see.

The numbers are what they are.

The questions are still unanswered.

And tomorrow, I still have to get up and make the budget work.

That is everyday life.
