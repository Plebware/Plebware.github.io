# 💾 Two WD Blue SSDs, One Adapter, and a Data-Transfer Plan

Sometimes useful computer equipment is sitting quietly in a cupboard, waiting for another job. In this case, there are two Western Digital WD Blue solid-state drives, each with a capacity of 500 GB, that need to be connected and checked before their contents can be copied.

The drives are currently stored away rather than installed in a computer. The plan is to use an adapter obtained through Takealot to connect them when the equipment is ready.

This is a preparation and data-transfer project. The drives have not yet been connected, examined, or copied as part of this work.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔌 Connecting the Drives

The adapter should make it possible to connect each SSD to a computer without installing it internally. Before connecting anything, the exact connector and drive compatibility need to be checked against the adapter.

The two drives should be handled carefully and connected one at a time. That makes it easier to identify which drive is being examined and reduces the chance of mixing up their contents.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🛡️ Protecting the Existing Data

The priority is to preserve the information already stored on the drives.

When a drive is first connected, the sensible approach is to inspect it before making changes. Do not format, initialise, repartition, or erase a drive simply because a computer displays a prompt. Those actions can make existing data harder to recover.

Once the contents and condition of each SSD have been checked, the required files can be copied to a suitable destination. The original drives should remain unchanged until the copied data has been checked.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 📂 The Next Step

The next stage is to connect the first WD Blue SSD using the adapter, identify its partitions and files, and determine what needs to be transferred. The same process can then be followed for the second drive.

The actual transfer results, the files recovered, and any problems encountered can be documented after the work has been carried out. For now, the important thing is to prepare properly and avoid risking data that may not exist anywhere else.
