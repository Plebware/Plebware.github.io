# 🧠✍️ From Thought to Publication — The Human Behind My AI-Assisted Writing Process.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 📝 Introduction — Where Does an Article Actually Begin?

When visitors read an article on PlebWare, they see the finished result. They may see the words, illustrations, formatting, references and perhaps the PlebVox audio controls.

What they do not necessarily see is the process that brought that article into existence.

I think that process deserves an explanation.

Artificial intelligence is becoming an increasingly common part of writing and publishing. However, there is a considerable difference between asking an AI system to produce an article and using AI as an assistant within a human-directed editorial process.

I want to explain how I work.

This is not a claim that my method is the only correct way to use artificial intelligence. It is an account of my own practical workflow, including the decisions, checks and responsibilities that remain mine.

The central principle is straightforward:

**AI assists with the work. The human remains responsible for the published result.**

This is consistent with the philosophy behind PlebWare: technology should serve humanity, not replace human judgement.

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 💡 1. Every Article Begins With a Thought.

The starting point is usually an idea.

It might come from something I have experienced, a conversation, a technical problem, a question, a discovery, something I have read, or simply a thought that develops while I am going about my day.

I do not always have the opportunity to sit down at a computer and write immediately.

That is why I carry a small notebook in my pocket.

When an idea comes to mind, I jot down the important points. Sometimes it is only a few words. Sometimes it is a short list. Occasionally, it develops into a more substantial outline.

The notebook is my first point of capture.

It preserves the original thought before I start developing it with an AI assistant.

The idea belongs to the person who had it. The technology helps take it further.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 📱 2. From Notebook to Voice Dictation.

Later, when I have an opportunity, I dictate those notes to an AI assistant.

I use voice input because it is practical, particularly when I am away from my computer. It also saves time compared with typing everything manually on a mobile phone.

I use different assistants for different circumstances. Meta AI is convenient for quick work within WhatsApp, while ChatGPT is generally my preferred working partner for more detailed PlebWare articles, research and technical tasks.

The assistant helps transcribe what I say, organise the points, improve grammar and turn rough notes into something easier to work with.

There is an important distinction here.

**Dictation is not the same as authorship, and transcription is not the same as publication.**

Speech recognition can misunderstand names, technical terminology and even the name PlebWare itself. The resulting text must therefore be checked.

I also recognise that voice input has an accessibility benefit. It provides another way of working for people who find typing difficult or who have eyesight limitations.

For me, it is a practical combination of accessibility, convenience and efficiency.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## ♿ 3. Why I Use AI Assistants.

There are several reasons.

First, efficiency. I can capture an idea and begin developing it without having to type every sentence manually.

Second, accessibility. Voice interaction makes it easier to work from a phone and provides an alternative to conventional keyboard input.

Third, accuracy and structure. An AI assistant can help identify unclear sentences, organise information, suggest improvements and point out possible gaps in an explanation.

Fourth, research. AI can help investigate a subject, compare information and identify questions that deserve further attention. Important claims still need to be checked against appropriate sources.

I do not expect one AI system to be perfect at everything. Different systems can provide different perspectives, and I can use those perspectives to improve the work.

I have written more about this practical approach in [How I Leverage Free-Tier AI as a Pensioner Builder of PlebWare](https://plebware.github.io/ai-helpers/ai-use/2026/09/22/how-i-leverage-free-tier-ai/).

The important thing is not which AI assistant produces the most impressive paragraph.

It is whether the tool helps me achieve the purpose of the work without taking responsibility away from me.

## 🔍 4. The Editorial Workshop — Developing the Article.

Once the initial notes have been transcribed, the actual writing process begins.

For PlebWare articles, ChatGPT is generally my primary assistant for detailed development.

Before preparing an article, I expect the assistant to work within the current PlebWare conventions. These include the repository's AI-INSTRUCTIONS.md, the Housekeeping reference and the Personal Copy-and-Paste Codes reference.

These documents help establish how articles should be structured, categorised, formatted and prepared for publication.

The assistant must also consider the existing PlebWare website.

That means checking whether similar material already exists, identifying suitable categories and looking for relevant articles that can be linked to the new work.

I do not want PlebWare to become a collection of disconnected pages. I want it to become an interconnected knowledge library.

The assistant can help with:

- Organising my original notes.
- Researching relevant information.
- Identifying gaps or inconsistencies.
- Improving grammar and readability.
- Suggesting headings and section structure.
- Checking Markdown and front matter.
- Identifying appropriate PlebWare categories.
- Finding relevant existing articles for internal links.
- Preparing the article for my review.

The assistant may suggest changes, but suggestions are not automatically accepted.

I remain involved in the decisions.

## 📚 5. PlebWare Articles Should Connect to Other PlebWare Articles.

There is another important part of the publishing process that I want to make increasingly consistent.

Where relevant material already exists, new PlebWare articles should link to it.

This is useful for readers because they can follow a subject beyond the page they are currently reading. It helps them discover related explanations, projects, tutorials and background information.

It also helps search engines understand the relationships between pages within a website.

For example, a reader interested in this publishing workflow might also want to explore:

These are not random links. Each provides additional context for a reader who wants to understand how PlebWare works.

My intention is to make contextual internal linking a normal part of suitable PlebWare articles.

There is no artificial link quota. A few genuinely useful links are better than a long list of unrelated pages.

Every link must point to an existing resource and should make sense in the context in which it appears.

**The goal is not simply to keep people clicking. It is to help people learn about PlebWare.**

## 🧐 6. Proofreading, Questions and Human Approval.

When the assistant has prepared a draft, I review it.

I read through the material, consider the explanations, examine the structure and question anything that does not seem right.

If something is inaccurate, unclear, incomplete or simply not expressed the way I intended, I ask for corrections.

Sometimes this takes several rounds.

I may ask for a source. I may challenge an explanation. I may correct a name, change a heading or ask the assistant to rewrite an entire section.

I do not regard an AI-generated answer as automatically correct.

The assistant must be able to explain its reasoning and provide supporting information where appropriate. I must then decide whether that information is adequate.

When I am satisfied with the draft, I explicitly approve it for publication.

That approval is important.

The AI does not independently decide that an article is ready to go live.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 🛠️ 7. GitHub — The Publishing Workshop.

Once I have approved an article, the technical publishing work can begin.

PlebWare uses GitHub as its workshop and Markdown as one of its principal publishing languages.

The assistant can prepare the Markdown file, check the front matter, follow the repository conventions and use the authorised GitHub workflow to add the approved article.

The publishing process must still be checked.

A successful file update is not necessarily proof that the website has built correctly or that every link and page element works as intended.

The published result must be examined.

This is one reason I value having a clear separation between preparing an article, approving it and actually publishing it.

Each stage has its own purpose.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 📲 8. The First Published Review — My Vivo Phone.

After publication, I open the article on my Vivo phone.

The phone is an important part of my working environment. It allows me to inspect the live page as an ordinary visitor would see it, rather than relying only on the Markdown file in the repository.

I check the presentation and, where PlebVox is available, I listen to the article.

This is particularly useful because an article can look perfectly acceptable on a screen but sound awkward when read aloud.

A sentence may be too long. A technical term may be pronounced incorrectly. A name may be misunderstood. A heading or punctuation mark may interrupt the natural flow.

If I find a problem, I ask for a correction.

If everything is satisfactory, the article remains published.

This first listening session is part of my normal post-publication checking process. It is not, by itself, the same as applying the formal VOXED verification marker.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 💻 9. The Second Review — Back at the Laptop.

When I return home, I review the day's work again on my laptop.

This gives me another opportunity to examine the actual published content and consider whether anything needs improvement.

I check the wording, punctuation, factual details, formatting and general presentation.

Once I have personally reviewed an article and am satisfied with it, I can add the PlebWare human review marker:

`<!-- REVIEWED -->`

This marker has a specific meaning.

It indicates that a human has reviewed the article.

It does not mean that the article has necessarily been listened to in its entirety.

That distinction matters because editorial review and audio review are two different activities.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔊 10. PlebVox — Listening Is a Separate Approval.

If I have the time, I listen to the article again.

Sometimes I listen twice. Occasionally, I listen three times.

I listen to the complete PlebVox rendition, not merely a short sample.

I pay attention to pronunciation, awkward phrasing, missing or misplaced words and the overall listening experience.

If something needs correction, it goes back into the editing process.

Only after I have listened to the complete audio and am satisfied with it do I add the separate PlebVox listening-verification marker:

`<!---VOXED--->`

This marker has a different meaning from the Human Reviewed marker.

It confirms that the article has received a complete human listening review and approval.

It is not simply an indication that PlebVox is available or that the article has audio.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 🔑 11. Three Different Things — Three Different Meanings.

It is important that readers understand the distinction between the three elements involved.

**PlebVox section markers.**

The PLEBVOX START and END markers identify the sections intended for PlebVox narration. They define what the audio system should read.

**Human editorial review.**

The REVIEWED marker identifies an article that has received a human editorial review.

**Human listening approval.**

The VOXED marker identifies an article whose complete PlebVox audio rendition has been listened to and approved by a human moderator.

These indicators are not interchangeable.

An article can have PlebVox narration markers without having received a human editorial review.

An article can be human-reviewed without having received a complete listening review.

An article receives the VOXED marker only after the full listening process has been completed and approved.

This separation helps readers understand what has actually been checked rather than assuming that one indicator means everything has been verified.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 🌍 12. Transparency for Readers, Search Engines and AI Systems.

I want PlebWare visitors to understand how its content is produced.

I also want search engines and AI systems examining the website to encounter a clear explanation of the human and technological roles involved.

AI-assisted writing should not be confused with an absence of human involvement.

Equally, human involvement should not be presented as a guarantee that every statement is infallible.

The purpose of this article is to describe the process honestly.

The original ideas, direction, decisions, corrections and publication approval remain human responsibilities. AI tools assist with transcription, research, organisation, editing and technical work.

The verification markers communicate specific stages of human review. They do not claim that mistakes are impossible.

Clear explanations and relevant internal links also help readers and automated systems understand how different PlebWare resources relate to one another.

This is part of building a website that is not merely searchable, but understandable.

## 🤝 13. A Partnership, Not a Transfer of Responsibility.

I regard the AI assistant as a working partner around the PlebWare Conference Table.

It can research, suggest, organise, edit, explain and help carry out authorised technical tasks.

But it does not replace the person who decides what PlebWare should publish.

The human supplies the purpose and direction. The AI helps with the work. The human examines the result and accepts responsibility for the final decision.

This is how I want to use the technology.

Not blindly.

Not carelessly.

And certainly not by handing over my judgement.

The same principle applies to the wider PlebWare ecosystem: technology should make people more capable, not make them less involved.

## 🧭 Final Thoughts — The Human Still Holds the Pen.

My process begins with a thought.

It moves into a notebook.

The notebook becomes spoken notes.

The notes are transcribed and developed with AI assistance.

The draft is researched, questioned, corrected and reviewed.

I approve the article.

The approved material is published through GitHub.

I inspect it on my phone, listen to it through PlebVox and later review it again on my laptop.

If the work passes the relevant checks, I apply the appropriate human verification markers.

That is the process.

It is not an attempt to hide the use of artificial intelligence. It is an explanation of how I use it responsibly.

I believe AI can be an extremely useful tool for writers, researchers, developers and ordinary people. But usefulness should never be confused with authority.

The machine can help with the words.

The machine can help with the research.

The machine can help with the publishing.

But the human still decides what those words mean, whether the work is acceptable and whether it should be published.

**The thought begins with a human. The technology helps bring it to life. The responsibility remains human.**

That is my publishing process.

And that is how I intend to keep building PlebWare.
