# 🚙 Bucky and Me — Back to Driving School!

There comes a time in life when even an old friendship needs a little rebuilding.

In my case, that friendship has four wheels, a steering wheel, a diesel engine, and a name.

**Bucky. My faithful Ford Ranger.**

Recently, Bucky underwent gearbox repairs. I was expecting to get my old companion back, ready for another adventure. Instead, I seem to have inherited a vehicle that looks familiar but feels like a stranger.

And that has presented me with an interesting challenge.

I need to learn to drive my own vehicle again!

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔧 When a Familiar Gearbox Becomes a Stranger.

Before the gearbox repair, I knew Bucky.

I knew the feel of the gear lever, the clutch, the accelerator, and the little habits that come with driving a vehicle regularly.

You eventually stop thinking about every movement. Your hands and feet seem to know what to do automatically.

That is muscle memory.

But something has changed.

Twice now, I have selected third gear instead of first.

Now, selecting the correct gear should not be a guessing game!

It is a little unsettling when something you have done countless times suddenly becomes uncertain.

I suspect that I need to become familiar with the gear selection all over again. However, I also want the repairer to check whether anything in the gear-selection mechanism has changed or needs adjustment following the gearbox installation.

I don't want to blame myself for a mechanical problem, nor do I want to blame the mechanics without evidence.

There is a difference between adapting to a vehicle and compensating for a fault.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🚦 The Starting Problem — Another Mystery.

There is another little niggle.

When I start Bucky, I have developed an unusual routine.

I switch on the ignition and wait for the dashboard warning lights to go out.

However, when I attempt to start the engine, a transmission or gear symbol with an exclamation mark appears, and the starting process fails.

I then have to switch the ignition off and repeat the procedure.

On the second attempt, Bucky starts.

That is not something I want to accept as normal.

A warning symbol is there for a reason, and the fact that the problem appeared after gearbox work makes it particularly important to investigate.

Possible areas for the repairer to examine include:

- Electrical connectors and wiring disturbed during gearbox removal and installation.
- The clutch starter-interlock switch.
- Neutral or gear-position detection, if fitted to this particular model.
- Electrical grounds and transmission-related connections.
- Stored diagnostic fault codes.

These are investigation points, not confirmed diagnoses.

I also want to photograph the actual dashboard warning so that the symbol can be identified correctly.

Repeatedly switching the ignition off and on is not a proper repair.

Bucky deserves a proper inspection, and so does his driver!

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 🎓 Back to Driving School — Starting From Scratch.

I have decided to approach this differently.

Instead of becoming frustrated with Bucky, I am going to treat this as an opportunity to refresh my driving skills.

Not because I have never driven before, but because confidence comes from knowing exactly what you are doing.

I want to rebuild that familiarity systematically.

My training will begin with the fundamentals:

**1. Understanding the controls.**

Reacquaint myself with the clutch, brake, accelerator, handbrake, and gear lever.

**2. Learning the gear positions.**

Understand the H-pattern and practise identifying first, second, third, fourth, fifth, and reverse without hesitation.

**3. Understanding the clutch.**

Learn the biting point again and develop smooth coordination between clutch and accelerator.

**4. Moving off and stopping.**

Practise controlled starts and stops in a suitable, supervised practice area.

**5. Changing gears.**

Develop a smooth rhythm when moving through the gears, without rushing or forcing the gear lever.

**6. Downshifting.**

Understand when and how to select a lower gear safely.

**7. Hill starts and manoeuvring.**

Rebuild confidence with hill starts, reversing, parking, and low-speed control.

**8. Returning to normal driving.**

Only once the mechanical issues are resolved and the fundamentals are comfortable will I progress to ordinary traffic conditions.

I will use a qualified driving instructor or experienced supervisor where appropriate.

There is no shame in starting again.

## 🛡️ Safety Before Confidence.

One thing I have learned over the years is that confidence should never replace common sense.

If the gearbox warning returns, if gears become difficult to select, or if the vehicle behaves unpredictably, I must stop and have it inspected.

I will also ask the gearbox repairer to investigate the starting problem before treating it as an ordinary driving issue.

A vehicle should not require its driver to develop a special ignition ritual just to start reliably.

And no amount of driving practice will repair an electrical connector, a faulty switch, or a misadjusted mechanical component.

**The driver and the machine must work together.**

## 🧠 A Lesson Beyond Driving.

There is something rather interesting about this experience.

We often become so accustomed to doing something that we forget how much learning originally went into it.

Driving becomes automatic.

Typing becomes automatic.

Using a computer becomes automatic.

Even operating complicated machinery eventually becomes second nature.

But when something changes, those familiar routines can suddenly become unreliable.

That is when we need to slow down, observe, ask questions, and learn again.

There is absolutely nothing wrong with returning to the basics.

In fact, sometimes that is the smartest thing we can do.

I have spent a considerable part of my life learning new skills, working with machinery, repairing things, and figuring out how systems operate.

Why should driving be any different?

## 🚙 The Road Ahead — Bucky and Me.

So, here we are.

An old driver and his faithful steed, preparing for a new chapter.

I will investigate the mechanical issues, refresh my driving knowledge, and gradually rebuild the confidence that comes from knowing a vehicle properly.

There will probably be a few frustrating moments.

Perhaps a laugh or two.

And hopefully, plenty of kilometres ahead.

One thing is certain:

**Bucky and I are going back to driving school!**

Because it is never too late to learn something again.

And sometimes, the road to moving forward begins by returning to the beginning.
