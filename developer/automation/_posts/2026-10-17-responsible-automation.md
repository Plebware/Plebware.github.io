# ⚙️ Automation Should Serve Humanity, Not Replace Human Judgement.

Automation is becoming an increasingly important part of modern life. It helps us manage information, simplify repetitive tasks, operate machinery, maintain websites, organise our working environments, and make better use of our time.

Artificial intelligence adds another dimension by helping computers interpret information, recognise patterns, generate content, and support decisions.

But technological progress raises an important question.

**🤔 Are we using technology to make people more capable, or are we gradually making people dependent on systems they no longer understand or control?**

At PlebWare, we believe technology should serve people. This article explores the meaning of automation, its benefits and risks, and the principles that can help us build technology that respects human choice, privacy, dignity, and responsibility.

## 🔧 What Is Automation?

Automation is the use of technology to perform tasks or processes with reduced repetitive human involvement. Depending on the application, a system might follow a simple rule, execute a scheduled task, respond to a sensor, or use artificial intelligence to interpret information.

Examples include:

- 💾 Automatically backing up important files.
- 🔗 Checking a website for broken links.
- 🖥️ Restoring a computer's preferred working environment.
- 🗓️ Scheduling routine maintenance.
- 💡 Adjusting lighting in response to changing conditions.
- 🐛 Identifying errors in documents or software.
- 🔔 Sending a reminder when a task needs attention.

Automation does not necessarily require artificial intelligence. A simple script can perform a predictable task reliably without a sophisticated model, an internet connection, or an external service.

**🔑 The best solution is not always the most complicated one.**

## 🧩 Digitalisation, Automation, and Artificial Intelligence.

These three concepts are related, but they describe different capabilities.

- **💻 Digitalisation:** Using digital technologies to improve how information is handled and how work is performed.
- **⚙️ Automation:** Making a task or process happen automatically, or with less repeated human intervention.
- **🧠 Artificial intelligence (AI):** Systems designed to perform tasks that may involve recognising patterns, interpreting language, generating content, or supporting decisions.

These capabilities can work together. A digital publishing system might store articles as Markdown files, automatically validate their metadata, and use an AI assistant to suggest improvements to their wording.

Each part has a different job.

**🎯 The important question is not simply whether a task can be automated. It is whether automation makes the process more useful, reliable, accessible, and responsible.**

## 🌟 The Benefits of Automation.

When thoughtfully implemented, automation can remove unnecessary repetition and give people more time to concentrate on work that benefits from human attention.

### 🔁 1. Reducing repetitive work.

Computers are well suited to performing clearly defined tasks repeatedly. They can check files, calculate values, organise information, and execute predictable procedures.

Automating these tasks can reduce mistakes caused by fatigue, distraction, or inconsistent manual processes.

### 🎯 2. Improving consistency.

A well-designed automated routine can apply the same rules every time. This is useful when maintaining a website, validating documents, testing software, or ensuring that a computer environment follows an agreed configuration.

Consistency, however, depends on the quality of the rules. An incorrect rule can repeat an error just as consistently as a correct one.

### ✍️ 3. Supporting creativity.

Automation can free people from some of the administrative work surrounding creative projects.

Writers can spend less time checking routine formatting. Developers can automate repetitive tests. Researchers can organise information more efficiently.

The purpose should be to create more room for human creativity, not to assume that creativity itself is an obstacle to efficiency.

### ♿ 4. Improving accessibility.

Automation can help people who find certain tasks difficult, repetitive, or physically demanding. Reminders, predictable workflows, simplified interfaces, and assistive technologies can make computing more approachable.

However, accessibility must be designed into a system. A complicated automated workflow that users cannot understand or override may create more problems than it solves.

### 💡 5. Making better use of limited resources.

Automation can reduce unnecessary work, improve the consistency of maintenance, and help people manage limited time and computing resources.

For community projects and individuals working with modest budgets, simple open-source tools and local scripts may provide substantial benefits without requiring expensive subscriptions or cloud services.

## ⚠️ The Risks We Must Not Ignore.

Automation is not automatically beneficial simply because it is technically impressive.

The United Nations has highlighted how digital technologies can expand access to education, public services, and economic opportunities while also creating risks involving inequality, privacy, and the misuse of personal information.

🌐 Reference:<a href="[The Impact of Digital Technologies — United Nations](https://www.un.org/en/un75/impact-digital-technologies)" target="_blank"</a>


### 🔒 Privacy and surveillance.

The ability to collect information does not establish a legitimate reason to collect it.

Systems can reveal details about people's behaviour, movements, interests, and habits. If this information is collected without sufficient justification or protection, technology intended to help people can instead become a means of monitoring them.

Responsible automation should collect only what is necessary, explain its purpose, and protect information appropriately.

### ⚖️ Bias and unfair outcomes.

Automated systems can reproduce or amplify biases present in their rules, training data, or surrounding institutions.

An automated decision is not necessarily an objective decision. It may simply apply existing assumptions more quickly and at a greater scale.

Important decisions therefore require appropriate scrutiny, meaningful accountability, and opportunities to challenge mistakes.

### 🔗 Overdependence on technology.

When people cannot understand or control the systems they rely upon, a technical failure can leave them unable to continue their work.

A reliable system should make its behaviour understandable, provide useful error messages, and offer recovery options wherever practical.

### 👷 Employment and changing skills.

Automation changes the nature of work. It can reduce the need for certain repetitive tasks while creating demand for new skills and different responsibilities.

The long-term effects will vary across industries, occupations, and communities. It would be misleading to claim that automation will eliminate all human work, or that every worker will benefit equally.

Education, retraining, accessibility, and opportunities to participate in the changing economy remain important.

### 🧭 The loss of human judgement.

Some tasks are straightforward enough to automate with little risk. Others involve values, context, relationships, or consequences that cannot be reduced to a simple technical rule.

Efficiency should not become an excuse for removing human responsibility from decisions that materially affect people's lives.

## 🤝 A PlebWare Philosophy of Responsible Automation.

PlebWare is a publishing and learning environment built around the idea that technology should be understandable and useful to ordinary people.

Our approach to automation should follow that same philosophy.

The following principles are proposed as a practical foundation for PlebWare and PlebMachine.

### 🧑‍💻 1. Human choice comes first.

Automation should help people accomplish their goals without unnecessarily restricting their choices.

Users should retain reasonable control over their working environment. Automated assistance should not become a barrier to ordinary computer use.

### 🔍 2. Transparency builds trust.

People should be able to understand what a system does, why an action occurs, and what changes are being made.

Where possible, automated operations should be documented, observable, and straightforward to troubleshoot.

### 🛡️ 3. Privacy should be protected by design.

Systems should avoid unnecessary collection of personal information and should not introduce surveillance merely because monitoring is technically possible.

Local processing and simple configuration can sometimes provide the required functionality without sending information to external services.

### 🪶 4. Simplicity is a strength.

A predictable script or configuration rule may be safer, faster, and easier to maintain than an AI-powered system.

Use the simplest approach that adequately solves the problem.

### ↩️ 5. Important actions should be recoverable.

Where feasible, automated changes should be reversible. Systems should protect existing work, explain failures, and provide a route back to a usable configuration.

### 🧑‍⚖️ 6. People remain accountable.

Automation can perform a task, but responsibility for how it is designed, deployed, and used remains with the people and organisations involved.

AI-generated suggestions and automated test results should be evaluated according to the task they actually perform.

### 🔄 7. Systems should improve through feedback.

No design is perfect. Software must be tested, reviewed, corrected, and adapted as users' needs and technology change.

Listening to users is part of responsible development, not an optional extra.

## 🖥️ PlebMachine: Automation With the User in Control.

PlebMachine applies these ideas to the Linux desktop.

Its purpose is to help people work within a more organised and context-aware computing environment. Different desktop states and working modes can support different levels of familiarity and different activities.

Automation can assist with preparing a workspace, presenting relevant tools, managing configuration, restoring a preferred environment, and providing reminders.

But assistance must not become control.

A user should still be able to access ordinary desktop facilities and launch applications outside the suggested workflow. A guided environment should help a person learn, not imprison that person inside a predetermined set of choices.

This distinction is especially important when designing beginner-friendly features.

A system can offer guidance and make advanced tools easier to discover. It should not confuse repeated use with demonstrated competence, or assume that every person learns at the same pace.

**🔑 PlebMachine should make computing more approachable while respecting the user's ability to make decisions.**

## 🌐 PlebWare: Automating the Work Around Knowledge.

The same philosophy applies to website publishing and maintenance.

A website can benefit from automated checks for broken links, missing metadata, malformed front matter, and other predictable errors. These checks help reduce avoidable mistakes and allow the author to concentrate on the substance of the work.

Structured metadata and search-engine optimisation can also benefit from automation. A system can verify whether required fields exist, while a human assesses whether an article is accurate, relevant, and useful.

AI assistants can contribute to research, outlining, editing, and technical problem-solving. Their output should still be checked, especially when it contains factual claims or references to external sources.

Automation should strengthen the publishing process, not replace the author's responsibility for what appears on the website.

## ✅ A Practical Test Before Automating Anything.

Before introducing an automated feature, ask the following questions.

1. 🎯 **Purpose:** What genuine problem does this automation solve?
2. 🧩 **Necessity:** Could a simpler solution achieve the same result?
3. 🕹️ **Control:** Can the user understand and influence its behaviour?
4. 🔒 **Privacy:** Does it collect or transmit information unnecessarily?
5. ⚠️ **Safety:** What happens if it fails or produces an incorrect result?
6. ↩️ **Recovery:** Can the user undo its changes or return to a working state?
7. ♿ **Accessibility:** Does it help the intended users, including those with limited resources or experience?
8. 🧑‍⚖️ **Accountability:** Who is responsible for reviewing its behaviour and correcting mistakes?

If these questions cannot be answered satisfactorily, the design needs more work.

Not every automation requires the same level of scrutiny. Automatically formatting a heading is different from automatically deleting files or making a consequential decision about a person.

**🛡️ The level of safeguards should reflect the possible consequences of failure.**

## 🚀 The Future Should Be Human-Centred.

Research into automation and artificial intelligence continues to examine their social, ethical, economic, and environmental effects.

Khogali and Mekid, in their 2023 review of automation and AI, emphasise the importance of ethical considerations, human values, openness, and continued collaborative research.

📚 Reference: Khogali, H. O., and Mekid, S. (2023). *The blended future of automation and AI: Examining some long-term societal and ethical impact features.* Technology in Society, 73, 102232. [https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techsoc.2023.102232](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techsoc.2023.102232)

These questions will not be settled by technology alone. They require thoughtful design, informed users, responsible organisations, and a willingness to reconsider assumptions when evidence shows that something is not working.

At PlebWare, we do not need to automate everything to benefit from automation.

We need to identify the tasks where it genuinely helps, implement those tasks responsibly, and retain the human judgement necessary to ensure that technology remains a tool rather than a master.

**💙 Our guiding principle is simple:**

- 🧑 The computer should serve the person.
- ⚙️ Automation should make that service better.
- 🕊️ The person should remain in control.
