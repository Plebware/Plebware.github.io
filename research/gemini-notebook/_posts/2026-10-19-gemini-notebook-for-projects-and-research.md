# 🧠 Your Own Artificial Intelligence Research Assistant — Without an Enterprise Budget.

Imagine having a research assistant who can help you understand your documents, organise your ideas, compare information from different sources, and turn rough notes into a useful plan.

Now imagine using that assistant to help manage a Linux software project, develop a website, or write an educational article — without immediately committing yourself to another monthly subscription.

That is the opportunity offered by **Google Gemini Notebook**, formerly known as NotebookLM.

For people living on a limited income, independent developers, students, writers, and lifelong learners, this technology is worth exploring. It can help make information easier to understand and more useful, even when money, time, or computing resources are limited.

But there is an important distinction: an Artificial Intelligence research assistant is not a replacement for your own judgement, your original files, or proper technical testing.

In this article, we will explore how to use it sensibly, what it can do for a real project, and where its limitations matter.

## 📓 1. What Is Gemini Notebook?

Google renamed NotebookLM to **Gemini Notebook in July 2026**. According to Google's official website, the change reflects its wider effort to integrate notebooks into the Gemini Artificial Intelligence experience. Existing notebooks remain accessible.

Official website: [Gemini Notebook](ZXQH0QXZ).

Think of a notebook as a workspace built around information you choose to collect. Depending on the features available to your account, you can add documents and other supported sources, ask questions about them, and generate useful summaries or other research materials.

Instead of repeatedly searching through a large pile of notes, you can ask questions in ordinary language.

For example:

- 📚 “Summarise the main ideas in these documents.”
- 🔍 “What differences exist between these two proposals?”
- 📝 “Turn these notes into a step-by-step guide.”
- 🧩 “Which important questions have not yet been answered by my sources?”
- 📖 “Explain this technical subject in language a beginner can understand.”

A particularly useful feature is that answers can include citations pointing back to source material. This gives you a way to check whether an answer is supported by the documents you supplied.

That does not make every answer automatically correct. The sources might be incomplete, outdated, contradictory, or misunderstood by the Artificial Intelligence. You should follow the citations and verify important claims.

**The central idea is simple:** give the Artificial Intelligence relevant information, ask a clear question, inspect its answer, and use your own judgement.

## 💰 2. Can You Use It Without Paying?


For someone trying to make every rand count, free access is an important consideration.

Gemini Notebook offers standard access, with higher limits and additional features available through certain eligible paid plans. The exact limits and available features can change, so check Google's current [plans and usage limits](ZXQH0QXZ) before making plans around a particular feature.

The free offering can be useful for individual research, learning, organising project information, and preparing written material. You do not necessarily need an expensive enterprise subscription to start experimenting.

However, **free access does not mean unlimited access**. Usage quotas, supported features, file limits, and availability may differ according to the account and plan.

Nor does “free” mean that the entire workflow costs nothing. You still need a suitable device, an internet connection, electricity, and time to review the results.

### 🔑 A sensible approach for a tight budget.

1. Start with the free access available to your account.
2. Choose one real problem you want to solve.
3. Upload a small, relevant collection of documents.
4. Test whether the answers save you meaningful time.
5. Consider paying for additional features only if you have a clear reason to do so.

Do not buy a subscription merely because a feature looks impressive. First establish whether it solves a problem you actually have.

## 🐧 3. Managing a Linux Software Project Like PlebMachine.

A software project can accumulate an enormous amount of information: source code, configuration files, design decisions, bug reports, installation instructions, test results, and ideas for future improvements.

PlebMachine is a useful example because a context-aware desktop environment involves more than writing scripts. It also involves maintaining a consistent philosophy, protecting user choice, documenting behaviour, and ensuring that components work together.

A notebook can help make that information easier to understand.

### 📂 Build a project knowledge base.

Start by collecting selected project documents, such as:

- The project overview and design philosophy.
- Architecture notes and approved design decisions.
- Relevant README and Markdown documentation.
- Descriptions of important scripts and configuration files.
- Bug reports, test results, and known limitations.
- Installation instructions and user guides.

Use clear filenames and explain the purpose of each source. Avoid dumping thousands of unrelated files into a notebook and expecting the Artificial Intelligence to understand the entire project automatically.

For a large project, divide the material into manageable collections according to purpose.

### 🔍 Example: check a proposed design change.

Suppose you want to separate a desktop environment switcher from another component.

You could ask:

> I am considering separating the workspace environment switcher from the main control script. Using the architecture notes and source files supplied, identify three lightweight design options. For each option, explain the benefits, disadvantages, dependencies, and possible risks on a low-spec MX Linux computer. Distinguish facts supported by my sources from assumptions that need testing.

This encourages the Artificial Intelligence to reason about your actual project rather than produce a generic recommendation.

### 🛠️ Example: investigate a bug.

> Examine the supplied Python function and relevant design notes. Explain what the function is intended to do, identify possible failure conditions, and suggest the smallest practical correction. Do not assume that the proposed correction works. List the tests I should run on my target MX Linux and XFCE environment.

The result can help you understand a problem and prepare a sensible test plan.

However, there is a crucial limitation: **reading source code is not the same as executing it**.

A notebook review does not prove that a script runs, that a desktop integration works, or that a proposed fix is safe. You must still inspect the actual code, test it in the target environment, and examine the results.

### 📖 Turn development notes into documentation.

Another valuable use is converting technical notes into accessible documentation.

Try this prompt:

> Using the supplied project notes, prepare a beginner-friendly installation guide for MX Linux with XFCE. Explain prerequisites, installation steps, expected results, common problems, and recovery procedures. Do not invent commands, filenames, dependencies, or options. Mark anything that needs verification against the actual project.

You can then compare the draft against the real software and correct anything that is missing or inaccurate.

The notebook becomes a documentation assistant, not the final authority on your software.

## 🌐 4. Improving a Website Like PlebWare.

A website is more than a collection of articles. It is a system that helps visitors find information, understand ideas, learn new skills, and discover related material.

PlebWare combines research, learning resources, software development, creative writing, and practical information. As a site grows, keeping its content understandable and logically connected becomes an ongoing task.

Gemini Notebook can help with that work when you provide it with the right material.

### 📝 Improve an article without losing its meaning.

Supply a draft and the editorial instructions that govern your website.

Then ask:

> Review this article for an ordinary reader who may have little technical knowledge. Identify jargon, unexplained assumptions, long or confusing sentences, and missing context. Suggest clearer wording while preserving the author's meaning. Do not invent facts or remove important qualifications. Return a list of recommended changes and explain why each one would help.

This is especially useful when an article began as technical research and needs to be rewritten for a general audience.

### 🧭 Improve navigation and content organisation.

You can supply a site map, category descriptions, selected index pages, and examples of existing articles.

Ask:

> Examine the supplied PlebWare site structure. Identify topics that appear difficult for a newcomer to locate, potentially overlapping categories, and opportunities for useful internal links. Cite the supplied pages supporting each recommendation. Do not invent URLs or assume that an article exists unless the source material confirms it.

The notebook can help you develop a list of questions and potential improvements. You should verify every suggested destination against the live website and repository before changing anything.

### 🔎 Improve SEO and readability.

Search engine optimisation is not simply about repeating keywords. A useful page needs a clear subject, an accurate title, descriptive headings, helpful links, and content that answers the reader's question.

Ask:

> Evaluate this article for clarity, search intent, descriptive headings, introductory paragraphs, and opportunities for relevant internal links. Suggest a concise page description that accurately reflects the content. Do not promise higher Google rankings or claim that search performance has improved without supporting evidence.

A notebook can review the content you provide, but it cannot establish actual search rankings, visitor behaviour, or click-through rates from article text alone.

Those questions require appropriate tools and live data, such as [Google Search Console](ZXQH0QXZ).

### 🔗 Connect related articles.

Suppose you have research about Linux, digital literacy, artificial intelligence, and website development.

Ask:

> Compare these article summaries and identify useful connections between them. Recommend internal links only where they help a reader understand the subject or continue learning. Explain the relationship between each pair of articles and identify the exact source pages that support the recommendation.

The goal is not to link everything to everything else. It is to help readers discover genuinely relevant information.

## ✍️ 5. A Research and Writing Partner.

Research often begins with scattered notes, multiple documents, and questions that are not yet clearly defined.

A notebook can help you move from that initial collection towards a structured article.

A practical workflow looks like this:

1. **Collect reliable sources.** Start with official documentation, credible research, and relevant primary material.
2. **Ask questions.** Identify the main claims, disagreements, unanswered questions, and important definitions.
3. **Prepare an outline.** Organise the findings into a logical sequence suitable for your intended readers.
4. **Draft the article.** Ask for plain language, useful examples, and appropriate headings.
5. **Verify the claims.** Follow citations, consult original sources, and correct unsupported statements.
6. **Edit and publish.** Apply your website's editorial conventions and retain responsibility for the final work.

### 🧪 Example: turn research into an article plan.

> Using the supplied sources, prepare an outline for an article about the impact of artificial intelligence on ordinary people. Separate established facts from interpretations and predictions. Identify areas where sources disagree, list important limitations, and provide citations to the relevant source material. Do not fill gaps with invented facts.

This approach is useful for research articles, technical explainers, educational resources, and project documentation.

It is also valuable for writers who need help organising large amounts of background material before beginning the actual writing.

The author's voice and judgement still matter. Artificial Intelligence-generated writing should be edited for accuracy, originality, tone, and relevance rather than published automatically.

## 🔐 6. Privacy, Security, and Accuracy.

A tool that can read documents deserves careful consideration, especially when those documents contain personal information or confidential project material.

Google states that Gemini Notebook content is not used to train its foundational generative Artificial Intelligence models unless you provide feedback. Google also explains that feedback may include your prompts, uploads, responses, and other associated material, which can be reviewed to improve its products.

Account type matters: eligible Google Workspace and education accounts have additional data-handling protections. Read the current terms that apply to your own account rather than assuming every account receives identical treatment.

Official reference: [Privacy and Terms of Use in Gemini Notebook](ZXQH0QXZ).

### 🛡️ Protect sensitive information.

Before uploading documents:

- Remove unnecessary identity numbers, account numbers, addresses, and personal details.
- Do not upload passwords, private keys, access tokens, or credentials.
- Avoid supplying confidential information belonging to another person without appropriate permission.
- Review the sharing settings before sharing a notebook.
- Check the data-handling terms for your account and organisation.

For financial documents, a notebook may help explain terminology or compare figures you have deliberately provided. It should not be treated as a qualified financial adviser or as a substitute for checking statements and calculations yourself.

### ⚖️ Remember that Artificial Intelligence can be wrong.

Even when an answer includes citations, the interpretation may be incomplete or incorrect.

A source can be outdated. Two sources can contradict each other. A document may omit an important fact. The Artificial Intelligence can misunderstand a technical requirement or produce a plausible-sounding answer that the evidence does not support.

For important decisions, return to the original sources and verify the relevant details.

**A useful rule:** the more serious the consequences of an error, the more carefully the result must be checked.

## 🎬 7. What About Artificial Intelligence-Generated Videos and Landscape Formats?

Artificial Intelligence video generation is an attractive feature, particularly for people creating educational content, presentations, or social media material.

However, availability can differ by product, plan, account, and the particular generation feature being used. The existence of a portrait video option does not, by itself, prove that Google has deliberately removed landscape video for all free users.

Likewise, it would be misleading to claim that resource costs or a mobile-first strategy explain a particular restriction without a reliable source confirming that explanation.

If a landscape option is missing from your account, check the current official feature documentation, the controls available in your interface, and the limits applicable to your plan.

For PlebWare, the practical lesson is to choose the output format according to its intended use. A vertical video may suit a phone screen, while a landscape video may be more appropriate for a desktop presentation or a conventional educational video.

Do not build a workflow around a feature until you have confirmed that it is available to you.

## 🚀 8. Your First Notebook — A Simple Starting Exercise.

You do not need to organise an entire project on the first day. Begin with a small experiment.

### Step 1. Open Gemini Notebook.

Visit [notebook.google](ZXQH0QXZ) and sign in with your Google account. Check that the service and features you need are available to your account.

### Step 2. Choose one subject.

For example, create a notebook for a software project, a research topic, or a collection of writing notes.

Avoid mixing private financial documents, unrelated creative work, and technical project files in one enormous collection.

### Step 3. Add a few useful sources.

Start with three to five relevant documents. These might be a project overview, a design note, and a user guide.

Check that the sources are current and that you have permission to use them.

### Step 4. Ask three questions.

Try these:

- “Summarise the main purpose of these documents.”
- “Identify any contradictions or important unanswered questions.”
- “Suggest five practical next steps, explaining which source supports each recommendation.”

### Step 5. Check the answers.

Open the citations. Compare the responses with your actual documents. Correct errors and note anything the sources do not establish.

### Step 6. Save what is useful.

Transfer approved decisions, corrected documentation, and useful notes into your normal project files. Keep a clear record of what has been verified and what remains a proposal.

This final step matters. A notebook is a place to think and investigate; it should not silently become the authoritative record of a changing project.

## 💡 10. The Bigger Lesson — Technology Should Expand Opportunity.

The real value of an Artificial Intelligence research assistant is not that it can produce a large quantity of text. It is that it can help people make better use of information they already possess.

An independent developer can use it to understand architecture notes. A writer can organise research. A student can ask questions about reading material. A website maintainer can review content for clarity and consistency.

These are practical uses that do not require an enterprise-sized organisation.

But the best results come from combining Artificial Intelligence assistance with reliable sources, organised files, sensible privacy practices, and human judgement.

For a project such as PlebMachine, the software must still be tested. For a website such as PlebWare, proposed changes must still be checked against the live repository. For a research article, factual claims must still be verified.

Artificial Intelligence can help us work through the information. It should not take away our responsibility for the decisions.

You do not need to spend a fortune to begin learning, researching, developing, and publishing. Start with the resources you have, use free tools thoughtfully, and invest your time where it produces genuine value.

**The goal is not to let technology think for us. It is to use technology to help us think more clearly.**
