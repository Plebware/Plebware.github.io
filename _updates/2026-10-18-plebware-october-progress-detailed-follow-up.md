# 🔑 Looking Beneath the Surface of PlebWare.

It has been a turbulent few months since PlebWare's revamp. But the results have been deeply satisfying, and the work has become much more substantial than I initially imagined.

My earlier update dated 18 October 2026 provided an overview of our recent progress. This article is a **detailed follow-up**: a closer look at the milestones, decisions and working principles behind that progress.

The achievements below are arranged by importance, rather than simply by the order in which they happened. Some are visible to visitors; others are part of the structure that helps the project remain maintainable as it grows.

The central purpose remains straightforward:

**PlebWare exists to make technology, knowledge and creative work more accessible to ordinary people.**

## Milestone 1 — Establishing PlebWare's Identity.

One of the most important developments has been establishing a clear distinction between **Plebware** and **PlebWare**.

Plebware represents the human element: the user, the person whose needs, choices and creativity should remain at the centre.

PlebWare represents the brand, the philosophy, the website and the wider ecosystem that brings our work together.

The difference may look like a small change in capitalisation, but it gives us a more coherent way to explain what we are building. The [PlebWare Lexicon](https://plebware.github.io/about/the-plebware-lexicon/) provides a more detailed explanation of the language and concepts behind the project.

In August, I also developed the PlebWare Manifesto to put the project's evolving philosophy into words. The roots go back much further. As far back as 2006, I was experimenting with the idea of creating a PlebMachine for Windows 7, using Rainmeter as a layered platform.

The concept has since evolved into a broader environment for writing, education, research, Linux, creativity, software, storytelling and artificial intelligence integration.

For me, the important question has always been: **What is PlebWare actually for?**

It is for bringing these different activities together without forcing them to become identical. My fiction remains fiction. My Christian writing retains its own purpose. Technical experiments can become documentation and tutorials. Practical discoveries can be shared with other people.

As I have put it, PlebWare has developed an identity capable of holding its different ambitions together. That is something Juelz and I have been working towards from the beginning.

## Milestone 2 — From Publishing to Building.

On 3 May 2026, the PlebWare revamp began a period of rebuilding, experimentation, testing, correction and publication. Our September report recorded 142 days of work since that starting point. That figure is a historical snapshot from the earlier report, rather than a claim about the current number of days.

Each month brought a different emphasis.

### May — The Rebuild.

The priority was establishing a workable environment: the website, the publishing workflow and the supporting tools needed to turn ideas into repeatable work.

### June — Creating the Structure.

The work expanded into developer documentation, Linux tutorials, GitHub Pages guidance, PlebMachine guides, manuals and technical papers.

PlebMachine software development was progressing alongside its documentation. This connection matters: PlebWare explains, teaches and preserves knowledge, while PlebMachine explores ways of putting some of the project's principles into practice.

### July — Making the Workflow Repeatable.

Housekeeping conventions, image handling, copy-and-paste instructions and practical publishing guides became increasingly important.

I particularly wanted these conventions to help Juelz continue the work without unnecessary difficulty if I were no longer able to do it myself. They can also help future contributors and other creators, especially authors, establish useful workflows of their own.

The aim is not to make everyone work exactly as I do. It is to give people a tested starting point that they can understand and adapt.

### August — Giving the Ecosystem a Shared Language.

The Manifesto, PlebWare Lexicon, PlebVox documentation and discussions about the relationship between PlebWare and PlebMachine helped articulate the wider purpose.

The project was no longer simply accumulating features. It was developing a clearer explanation of why those features belonged together.

### September — Connecting the Pieces.

Attention increasingly turned to PlebMachine architecture, collaboration between multiple AI systems, project history, documentation and continuity of work.

Our articles began to explain not only what had been built, but also how the people and tools around PlebWare worked together.

### October — Refinement and Discoverability.

The October work has focused on clearer progress reports, SEO conventions, structured data, navigation, article organisation and better publishing standards.

The aim is to keep improving the experience for me as the developer, and ultimately for every PlebVisitor, PlebBuddy and PlebPartner.

## Milestone 3 — A Publishing Platform, Not Just a Blog.

I never set out to create another conventional blog.

A traditional blog often depends heavily on chronology: an article appears, newer posts push it down the list, and older material can become difficult to discover even when it remains useful.

PlebWare has deliberately moved beyond that model.

The website now brings together multiple knowledge modes, dedicated sections, technical documentation, personal writing, practical resources, devotional material and tools that help readers find relevant information.

The subcategory dashboard is one example. It brings together article counts, recent titles and publication dates across the twelve knowledge modes, using information generated from the article library rather than requiring a separate list to be maintained by hand.

That is a small but meaningful piece of infrastructure. It reduces repetitive maintenance and helps the site explain itself as it grows.

The same principle applies to reusable snippets, Markdown conventions, metadata and image insertion. Standardising repeatable tasks helps reduce inconsistencies and makes the next article easier to publish than the previous one.

### The scale of the library.

One historical homepage-search snapshot displayed 447 published articles, of which 424 were enabled for PlebVox. Those figures belong to that particular snapshot; they should not be mistaken for verified live totals today.

Numbers are useful, but they are not the whole story. The more important questions are whether readers can find relevant information, whether articles are organised sensibly, whether older work remains useful, and whether new contributors can understand the publishing conventions.

There is still more to do to improve discovery, but the foundation is in place.

Our ambition is to maintain a growing knowledge library without creating an ever-increasing burden of manual administration.

## Milestone 4 — PlebVox Gives Written Knowledge a Voice.

PlebVox is one of the most important features in the PlebWare ecosystem. On a page, it may appear to be a simple Play button. In practice, it changes how people can use the material.

A visitor can read an article, listen to it, or use the spoken presentation when reading on-screen text is inconvenient. Long articles can be divided into readable sections instead of becoming one enormous block of speech.

This is especially meaningful to me because Juelz has eyesight difficulties. Accessibility is not an abstract consideration for us; it affects people we know and work with.

PlebVox also offers a practical proofreading benefit. Authors and collaborators can listen to their own words and sometimes notice awkward phrasing, missing words or transcription mistakes that are easy to overlook when reading silently.

Voice dictation has become another part of the workflow. I can speak an idea, review the transcription, correct errors and turn the approved material into Markdown. The transcription still needs checking — we have encountered enough amusing misinterpretations to know that the machine occasionally has a mind of its own! 😂

We have also learned to make practical engineering decisions. When read-aloud text highlighting caused compatibility problems across browsers and platforms, we removed the unreliable highlighting while retaining the voice functionality.

That was the sensible choice: preserve the feature that provides value rather than insist that every planned feature must survive unchanged.

PlebVox reflects a principle at the heart of PlebWare: knowledge should be accessible in different ways, while the author retains ownership and responsibility for the work.

Read the [PlebWare Manifesto](https://plebware.github.io/about/manifesto/) for more about the philosophy behind this approach.

## Milestone 5 — PlebMachine Has Become an Architectural Project.

PlebMachine is not merely another topic covered by PlebWare. It is an attempt to put some of the project's philosophy into practice.

PlebWare is the online publishing and knowledge environment. PlebMachine explores how a context-aware desktop environment might help people work according to their experience, needs and chosen activities.

The work has increasingly moved beyond asking whether a feature would be useful. It now involves examining how components should interact, how the system should behave and how users should remain in control.

The four PlebMachine states are:

- **AEGIS** — beginner assistance.
- **NEXUS** — intermediate assistance, including automatic breaks.
- **APEX** — advanced assistance with more direct control.
- **ZENITH** — super-user operation with minimal interruption.

The twelve modes organise activities such as Everyday computing, Authoring, Study, Research, Graphics, Music, Video, Broadcast, AI Helpers, Development, Accounting and Leisure.

The architecture also considers startup behaviour, restoration, launcher management, state changes and the boundary between system logic and user configuration.

### The less visible work.

Good software development involves deciding what should happen before writing the code that makes it happen.

- What happens when the program starts?
- What happens when the user switches modes?
- What happens when a feature is disabled?
- What happens if a component fails?
- What happens to the user's original desktop?

These questions are less glamorous than adding a new feature, but they are essential to building something maintainable.

I also want to preserve a clear distinction between a documented design, an implemented component and a component that has passed real-world testing. They are different stages of maturity, and we should not claim more than the evidence supports.

Much of this work takes place on Linux, including the PlebMachine development environment, and through the Android Vivo phone as a portable working and communications terminal.

The guiding principle remains consistent:

**The computer should help people think through decisions, not make those decisions on their behalf.**

Automation can prepare a workspace, offer a reminder or suggest a useful action without taking control away from the user. Familiar desktop access and user-directed operation must remain available.

That is not merely a technical preference. It is part of the philosophy.

## Milestone 6 — Developing a Responsible Way to Work With AI.

PlebWare has become a practical environment in which multiple AI systems can contribute to writing, research, technical analysis, software development and constructive criticism.

Different systems can offer different strengths. Gemini can help investigate an idea. DeepSeek can contribute to coding. ChatGPT can help structure a discussion, examine documentation or challenge an assumption. Other systems can offer additional perspectives.

But an answer does not become correct simply because an AI produced it.

The working method we are developing is straightforward:

1. A human identifies a problem or proposes an idea.
2. AI helps explore possible solutions.
3. The proposed solution is examined and challenged.
4. The result is checked against evidence or actual system behaviour.
5. The human decides what to accept, change or reject.

We have had to correct mistaken assumptions, catch incomplete drafts and distinguish between a proposed change and a completed publication. Those experiences have taught us that an AI-assisted workflow needs checks and balances.

The conference table has become a useful way of organising this process. I can bring an unfinished idea to the conversation without pretending that I already know the answer. We can examine it, debate it, refine it and decide what should happen next.

Sometimes the answer is to build something. Sometimes it is to write an article. Sometimes it is to test a claim. And sometimes the right decision is to wait until better evidence is available.

That last option is just as important as the others.

This represents a shift from using AI primarily to obtain answers towards using it as part of a working process.

The human remains the project owner. AI contributes assistance, analysis and ideas; it does not take ownership of the work or final authority over what gets published.

## Milestone 7 — Stronger Publishing Standards and SEO.

Some of our most important improvements are things visitors may never notice directly. They are the conventions that help articles remain consistent, accessible, understandable and discoverable.

Recent work includes:

- More consistent article metadata and descriptions.
- Better alignment between page information and search-engine descriptions.
- Structured data for articles, including BlogPosting JSON-LD.
- Canonical URLs and clearer author identification.
- Improved internal linking and navigation.
- More consistent Markdown and PlebVox conventions.
- Better housekeeping instructions for future contributors.
- Human-reviewed article indicators.
- Testing of links and site behaviour after changes.

I have tested structured data with Google's Rich Results tools and received successful validation for the markup tested. We have also checked article links and examined how index pages behave.

It is important to understand what those checks mean. A page being crawled does not mean it qualifies for a rich result, and passing a structured-data test does not guarantee a particular search ranking.

The purpose is to give search engines clearer information about our content and provide a better foundation for discovery.

### Why this matters.

PlebWare is intended to remain free for its readers. Its value should not depend on charging people to access knowledge.

Instead, we need to make that knowledge useful, findable, trustworthy and worth returning to.

Good SEO, clear navigation, reliable articles and a consistent identity all help. None guarantees traffic or generates income by itself, but together they make the platform better prepared to reach people.

The Flag Counter provides another way to observe international interest, although a visitor counter is only one indicator and should not be mistaken for a complete analytics system.

We are now considering the entire publishing chain: from the original idea through writing, metadata, validation, publication, navigation and discovery.

That is a substantial step beyond simply putting an article online.

## Milestone 8 — Changing How I Work.

Perhaps the most important change is not a feature or a line of code. It is how I approach the work itself.

After months of working with artificial intelligence, I increasingly treat ideas as things that need to be captured, examined, documented, tested and carried forward.

A conversation can become an article. An article can become a documented principle. A principle can influence software architecture. A technical discovery can become a tutorial. A mistake can lead to a correction in the publishing conventions.

That is a productive cycle.

It has also helped connect my different activities — writing, research, Linux experimentation, software development, education and Christian writing — without requiring them all to become the same thing.

My work as an author remains my work as an author. My Christian writing retains its own purpose. Technical experiments can be documented for others. Practical experiences can become useful material for readers.

PlebWare provides a common publishing environment without erasing those differences.

This is why the housekeeping conventions matter so much to me. They are not simply rules about Markdown or image paths. They are a way of preserving knowledge, reducing avoidable mistakes and helping other people continue the work.

I particularly want Juelz and future contributors to be able to understand the process without having to rediscover every lesson from scratch.

The aim is not perfection. It is to build a working method that learns from experience and becomes more dependable over time.

## The Conference Table — Where Ideas Become Work.

The PlebWare Conference Table is a practical metaphor for the way these activities now fit together.

The Vivo phone serves as a communication terminal and, in our concept, a holographic projector. At the virtual conference table, AI appears as a holographic collaborator.

GitHub is our workshop. Markdown is one of our principal publishing languages. PlebVox gives suitable written content a voice. PlebWare is the public knowledge platform, while PlebMachine explores how some of our human-centred principles can be applied to the desktop environment.

The table is where unfinished ideas can be discussed, where problems can be examined and where different perspectives can challenge one another.

But the metaphor also reminds us of a boundary: the human being remains the author, decision-maker and project owner.

A suggestion is not a decision. A draft is not an approved article. A code change is not proof that a feature works. A successful commit is not, by itself, proof that the live page behaves as intended.

Our process should therefore remain:

**Research. Discuss. Build. Test. Correct. Approve. Publish. Verify.**

That is how we want to work — with curiosity, practical evidence and clear responsibility.

## What Comes Next?

PlebWare is still evolving. The twelve knowledge modes give the site a stronger structure, while the publishing conventions make it easier to maintain. PlebVox broadens access to written content, and ongoing SEO and navigation work helps readers find what they need.

PlebMachine continues to explore a desktop environment that adapts to the user's chosen work without taking control away from them.

We will keep refining the publishing workflow, checking the site after changes, improving discoverability and documenting what we learn. We must continue to distinguish between what has been proposed, what has been implemented and what has actually been tested.

That discipline is part of building a platform people can trust.

PlebWare has come a long way since the revamp began in May 2026. What started as a developing website has grown into a connected environment for publishing, learning, creativity, research and software development.

The achievement is not measured only in articles, pages or lines of code. It is also measured in the systems we improve, the mistakes we catch, the knowledge we preserve and the barriers we remove for the next person who wants to learn.

Our continuing promise is to put people before technology, usefulness before fashion, knowledge before hype and truth before popularity.

**Learn. Build. Create. Share. And never stop asking why.**
