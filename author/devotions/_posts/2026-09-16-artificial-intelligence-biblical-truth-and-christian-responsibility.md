# Artificial Intelligence, Biblical Truth, and the Christian Responsibility.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Opening Prayer.

Heavenly Father, I come before You today.

I ask, Lord, that You give me wisdom when advising my brothers and sisters about using artificial intelligence when doing Bible study and biblical writing, including devotions.

Lord, help us understand the importance of making sure that the Scriptures we use are correct and true according to Your Word as recorded in the Bible.

Help us to hear the Holy Spirit and to seek Your guidance when we write with artificial intelligence.

And Lord, this does not apply only to devotions. It applies to everything we write or communicate, whether it is an encouragement to a friend, an article, a report, or something as simple as a message.

Artificial intelligence is becoming part of everyday life. In fact, we have already been using forms of artificial intelligence for years, sometimes without even realizing it. Grammar and writing tools, search engines, recommendation systems, social media platforms, and video services increasingly use automated systems and artificial intelligence to help process and present information.

Lord, give us wisdom in using this technology so that we do not abuse it, misuse it, or allow it to distort Your Word.

Help us never to allow technology to become more authoritative than Scripture.

Help us remember that artificial intelligence is a tool created by human beings. It is not the Holy Spirit, it is not the Bible, and it is not God.

Give us discernment so that we can use technology without allowing technology to use us.

I ask this in Jesus' precious name.

Amen.

## Introduction — The Technology Is Here.

Many people are strongly opposed to artificial intelligence.

Others embrace it without question.

But there is another group — probably much larger than either of those groups — who have been using artificial intelligence or artificial-intelligence-driven systems without necessarily realizing how much technology is working behind the scenes.

When we search the internet, automated systems help determine what information is presented to us.

When we use social media, algorithms analyse our activity and help determine which posts and videos appear in our feeds.

When we watch YouTube, recommendations are influenced by what we have watched before.

Technology is already deeply embedded in modern life.

Whether we like it or not, artificial intelligence is becoming part of the world in which we live.

Therefore, the Christian question should not simply be:

**"Should I use artificial intelligence?"**

A better question is:

**"How should I use artificial intelligence responsibly as a follower of Jesus Christ?"**

That distinction matters.

Artificial intelligence can help us research, organise information, improve grammar, brainstorm ideas, translate languages, explain difficult subjects, and assist us in writing.

But it can also make mistakes.

It can misunderstand a question.

It can produce information that sounds convincing but is incorrect.

And when it comes to Scripture, that becomes especially important.

An artificial intelligence system can quote a Bible verse incorrectly.

It can give the wrong reference.

It can combine ideas from different passages.

It can present an interpretation as though it were Scripture.

And if a Christian simply copies and pastes the result without checking it, that Christian becomes responsible for whatever was published or shared.

That is why Bible study must remain **Bible study**.

Artificial intelligence may assist us.

It must never replace the Word of God.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Church History — Christians Have Always Used the Tools of Their Generation.

There is an important lesson in church history.

Christians have always used the communication technologies available to them.

The early Church relied heavily upon handwritten manuscripts and letters.

The writings of the apostles were copied and circulated among Christian communities.

Centuries later, the development of the printing press dramatically increased the ability to reproduce and distribute Scripture and Christian literature.

The technology itself was not the message.

The message was the Gospel.

The same principle applies today.

A printing press does not make the Gospel true.

A computer does not make the Gospel true.

A smartphone does not make the Gospel true.

And artificial intelligence does not make the Gospel true.

**God's Word is true because it is God's Word.**

Technology can help us communicate that Word, but technology has no authority to change it.

The responsibility therefore remains with the Christian.

If we use a computer to prepare a Bible study, we must check the Scriptures.

If we use artificial intelligence to help write a devotion, we must check the Scriptures.

If we receive a Christian message from somebody else, we should examine what it says against God's Word.

Technology changes.

The responsibility to seek truth does not.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Scripture One — Study and Handle the Word Correctly.

### 2 Timothy 2:15.

> "Do your best to present yourself to God as one approved, a worker who has no need to be ashamed, rightly handling the word of truth."

The principle here is enormously important.

Paul does not tell Timothy to accept everything he hears.

He tells him to be diligent and to handle the Word of truth correctly.

That means a Christian should never be satisfied simply because something **sounds biblical**.

It must actually be biblical.

When artificial intelligence produces a Bible reference, check it.

When it provides a quotation, check it.

When it explains a passage, read the passage yourself.

When it makes a theological claim, compare that claim with Scripture.

Artificial intelligence can help us find the information.

But we must still do the studying.

The responsibility cannot be outsourced.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Scripture Two — Test Everything.

### 1 Thessalonians 5:21.

> "Test all things; hold fast what is good."

This Scripture provides an excellent principle for the information age.

**Test it.**

Do not automatically believe something because:

- It appeared on Google.
- It was published on Facebook.
- It came from YouTube.
- It was written by an artificial intelligence system.
- It appeared in a Christian-looking article.
- It contains a Bible verse.
- It sounds intelligent.
- Or somebody you trust forwarded it to you.

Test it.

And once you have tested it, hold on to what is good.

This does not mean becoming suspicious of everything.

It means becoming discerning.

There is a difference.

A Christian should not live in fear of technology.

A Christian should live according to truth.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Scripture Three — Search the Scriptures.

### Acts 17:11.

The Bereans provide us with a wonderful example.

After hearing Paul and Silas teach, they did not simply accept everything because Paul was an important teacher.

They searched the Scriptures to determine whether what they were being taught was true.

That is a powerful lesson for Christians today.

If we receive a theological explanation from artificial intelligence, search the Scriptures.

If we hear a preacher make a statement, search the Scriptures.

If we read something on social media, search the Scriptures.

If I write something in a devotion, **search the Scriptures.**

That final point matters.

It applies to me too.

The fact that I write Christian material does not mean that everything I write is automatically correct.

The Bible remains the standard.

Not the author.

Not the preacher.

Not the computer.

Not the artificial intelligence.

**The Word of God.**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## A Word of Caution — AI Can Sound Convincing.

One of the dangers of artificial intelligence is that it can produce remarkably confident answers.

Sometimes that confidence is deserved.

Sometimes it is not.

An artificial intelligence system can make an error while presenting the answer in a beautifully written paragraph.

That is why Christians must learn to distinguish between **good writing** and **truth**.

They are not the same thing.

A beautifully written paragraph can still contain a false statement.

A badly written paragraph can contain a true statement.

And a computer-generated paragraph can contain either.

This is particularly important when dealing with Scripture.

Never assume:

**"The computer gave me a Bible verse, therefore it must be correct."**

Open the Bible.

Read it.

Check the reference.

Read the surrounding verses.

Understand the context.

Then decide whether the explanation actually agrees with the passage.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Artificial Intelligence and Fake Content.

There is another responsibility we must consider.

Artificial intelligence can be used to create convincing images, videos, voices, music, and written material.

Some of these creations are harmless entertainment.

Others can deliberately mislead people.

A song can be generated in the apparent style or voice of a famous person.

A video can make someone appear to say something they never said.

A photograph can depict something that never happened.

A supposedly Christian message can be generated without representing the actual beliefs of the person whose name or voice appears to be attached to it.

Therefore, we should be careful about assuming that something is authentic simply because it looks or sounds real.

The same principle applies to Christian content.

A message containing Christian words is not necessarily a message produced by a Christian.

A person singing Christian lyrics is not necessarily making a personal profession of Christian faith.

We should therefore avoid judging a person's spiritual condition merely from something that artificial intelligence may have generated or manipulated.

Instead, we should test the message itself and remain truthful about what we actually know.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Practical Christian Guidelines for Using Artificial Intelligence.

Here are some simple rules that Christians can put into practice.

### 1. Use artificial intelligence as an assistant, not an authority.

Let it help you organise your thoughts.

Do not let it become your spiritual authority.

### 2. Check every Scripture reference.

If artificial intelligence gives you a verse, open your Bible and verify it.

### 3. Read the context.

Do not build an entire doctrine around one isolated sentence.

Read the verses before and after it.

### 4. Ask for sources.

When researching historical, scientific, or theological claims, ask where the information came from.

Then verify important claims independently.

### 5. Never blindly copy and paste.

Read what the system produced.

Ask yourself:

**"Is this true?"**

**"Is this biblical?"**

**"Is this what I actually believe?"**

### 6. Pray before producing Christian material.

Ask God for wisdom.

The technology can assist with the words.

God must guide the heart.

### 7. Remember who is responsible.

If you publish it under your name, you are responsible for what you publish.

Saying, "The artificial intelligence wrote it," does not remove that responsibility.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## A Simple Five-Step Bible and AI Check.

Before publishing or sharing Christian material created with assistance from artificial intelligence, try this:

**PAUSE.**

### 1. Pray.

Ask God for wisdom and discernment.

### 2. Check.

Verify every Scripture reference.

### 3. Read.

Read the surrounding biblical context.

### 4. Compare.

Compare the explanation with the wider teaching of Scripture.

### 5. Review.

Read the final message as a Christian before sharing it with somebody else.

This simple process can prevent many avoidable mistakes.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## What Would God Want Us to Do?

Based upon these Scriptures, the Christian responsibility is clear.

God's Word should be handled carefully.

Truth should be tested.

Scripture should be searched.

Technology should remain a servant rather than becoming a master.

If artificial intelligence helps us communicate encouragement, truth, knowledge, or the Gospel more effectively, we can use that assistance responsibly.

But if it encourages us to become lazy in our study, careless with Scripture, dishonest about our sources, or willing to spread information we have not checked, then we need to reconsider how we are using it.

The issue is not whether the machine is impressive.

The issue is whether **we are being faithful.**

God does not need artificial intelligence.

But Christians may use technology while serving God.

The important question is whether we are using the tool wisely, honestly, and responsibly.

If you are uncertain, do not present uncertainty as fact.

## To-Do for the Future.

From now on, whenever artificial intelligence helps you prepare something important, establish a simple personal rule:

**I will review before I release.**

Before publishing or sending the material:

- Check the facts.
- Check the Bible references.
- Check the context.
- Check the tone.
- Check whether anything has been exaggerated.
- Check whether the message says what you actually intended.
- And most importantly, pray about what you are about to share.

Let artificial intelligence help with the work.

But keep responsibility for the work.
