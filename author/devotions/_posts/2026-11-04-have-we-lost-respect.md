## 💭 TODAY'S THOUGHT — HAVE WE LOST RESPECT?

Have we lost respect?

It is a question worth asking, because respect seems to be disappearing from many corners of society. We see it in the way people speak to one another, the way children sometimes treat their parents, the way adults dismiss the elderly, and the way strangers behave towards one another in public.

Think about the pregnant woman standing on a crowded bus while able-bodied passengers remain seated. Think about an elderly person being spoken to as though age has made them irrelevant. Think about the parent who is treated as an inconvenience by the very child they raised.

These are not merely questions of manners. They reveal something about how we understand the worth of another person.

The commandment to honour our father and mother is not an outdated social custom. It reminds us that gratitude, humility and respect belong at the foundation of a healthy society. Respecting our elders does not mean pretending that every older person is always right. It means recognising their humanity, their experience and the dignity they deserve.

And respect must begin with ourselves.

If I have no respect for my own life, my responsibilities, my body, my words or my conduct, how can I reasonably demand that others respect me? Self-respect is not arrogance. It is recognising that my life has value and that I am accountable for what I do with it.

The world often says that respect must be earned. There is truth in the idea that trust and admiration grow through conduct. But Christian respect reaches further. Every person has a God-given dignity. We may not admire someone's behaviour, agree with their opinions or trust their decisions, but we can still treat them with basic human respect.

Respect does not mean surrendering our judgement. It does not mean tolerating abuse, injustice or wrongdoing. It means refusing to let another person's conduct turn us into people who abandon our own values.

Even our conversations matter. We should speak respectfully to one another, including when using artificial intelligence. An AI system is not a human being and does not possess human dignity or feelings in the way a person does. But the way we communicate still reflects our own habits, discipline and character. Respectful language is worth practising because it shapes the person using it.

And our responsibility does not end with people.

Look at the world around us. An old tree may have stood for decades, giving shade and shelter. A neighbour's cat may wander through our garden. A spider in a corner may be quietly controlling insects. Creation is not disposable simply because it is convenient to remove or destroy something.

We were given responsibility over creation, not permission to treat it carelessly.

Respect, then, is not just a word we demand from others. It is a way of living: before God, towards ourselves, towards our neighbours, and towards the world entrusted to our care.

And today, on voting day, respect also asks us to consider the consequences of our choices.

## 🏛️ CHURCH HISTORY — WHEN RESPECT BECAME PRACTICAL CARE.

The early Christians lived in a society where status, wealth, citizenship, gender and social position strongly influenced how people were treated. The Christian message challenged believers to recognise the worth of people who were easily overlooked.

This was not simply a new way of speaking. It became a new way of serving.

### 1. Jerusalem — The Widows Who Were Being Overlooked.

In Acts 6:1–7, the early church faced a practical problem. The number of disciples was growing, but the daily distribution of food was not reaching some of the Greek-speaking widows fairly. A complaint arose because they were being overlooked.

The apostles did not dismiss the concern or tell the widows to accept their position. They recognised that the problem needed attention. Seven men of good reputation, full of the Holy Spirit and wisdom, were appointed to take responsibility for the work.

This moment is often associated with the development of the diaconal ministry — service to the practical needs of the Christian community.

Notice what happened: the church took a complaint seriously, recognised the dignity of vulnerable people, and organised a response.

Respect became action.

### 2. Tabitha — Remembered for the Good She Did.

Acts 9:36–42 tells us about a disciple named Tabitha, also called Dorcas. She was known for good works and acts of charity. When she died, the widows stood beside Peter, weeping and showing him the garments she had made for them.

Her kindness had become part of the lives of people who might otherwise have been forgotten.

Tabitha did not need a public office or a grand platform to make a difference. She used what she had — her skill, her time and her hands — to serve others.

Her story reminds us that respect is not measured by titles. Sometimes it is measured by the people who are better off because we cared.

### 3. Basil the Great — Care Organised on a Larger Scale.

In the fourth century, Basil of Caesarea (c. AD 330–379), a bishop and theologian in Cappadocia, became known for his concern for the poor and sick.

Around AD 372, Basil established a major charitable complex outside Caesarea, often called the Basileias. Historical accounts describe facilities and services intended for people in need, including the sick, the poor and travellers, with attendants and medical care.

It is sometimes described as an early hospital. We should be careful with that label: historians debate how closely the complex compares with a modern hospital. It is more accurate to call it a substantial Christian charitable and care centre that brought organised assistance to vulnerable people.

The important point is not the modern label. It is the principle behind the work.

Basil believed Christian faith had practical consequences. Compassion could not remain a beautiful sentence in a sermon while sick and poor people were left without help.

### 4. What This History Teaches Us.

From the widows in Jerusalem, to Tabitha's garments, to Basil's organised charitable work, we see a consistent lesson.

Christian respect is not passive politeness. It recognises the dignity of people and responds to their needs.

The church is not called merely to speak about love. It is called to practise it.

We may not be able to establish a care centre, provide medical services or solve every social problem. But we can notice the person being overlooked. We can listen to the complaint that others dismiss. We can share what we have. We can use our skills to make another person's burden lighter.

A society becomes more humane when respect moves beyond words and becomes visible in our actions.

## 📜 SEVEN SCRIPTURES — THE BIBLICAL FOUNDATION OF RESPECT.

### 1. 👨‍👩‍👧 EXODUS 20:12 — HONOUR YOUR FATHER AND MOTHER.

> _Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee._
>
> **Exodus 20:12, KJV.**

This commandment places honour within the heart of family and community life. It calls us to recognise the people through whom we received life, care and guidance.

Honour does not require us to pretend that parents are perfect. Nor does it require anyone to remain in an abusive or dangerous situation. We can maintain appropriate boundaries while refusing to nurture hatred or contempt.

The principle is gratitude, dignity and a willingness to recognise the sacrifices and responsibilities that came before us.

**Question:** Do I show honour through my words and actions, or do I only expect it from others?

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

### 2. 👵 LEVITICUS 19:32 — RESPECT THE ELDERLY.

> _Thou shalt rise up before the hoary head, and honour the face of the old man, and fear thy God: I am the LORD._
>
> **Leviticus 19:32, KJV.**

In this instruction, respect for older people is connected directly with reverence for God.

Age does not automatically make a person wise in every matter, but it does not make them worthless either. The elderly deserve patience, consideration and dignity.

Sometimes respect is as simple as offering a seat, listening without impatience, or allowing someone the time they need to speak.

**Question:** How do I treat older people when their pace, opinions or needs inconvenience me?

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

### 3. ❤️ ROMANS 12:10 — GIVE HONOUR TO ONE ANOTHER.

> _Be kindly affectioned one to another with brotherly love; in honour preferring one another._
>
> **Romans 12:10, KJV.**


Paul describes a Christian community where people do not constantly compete for recognition. Instead, they look for opportunities to give honour to others.

Imagine how different our homes, workplaces, churches and public spaces would be if we stopped asking, “Why should I show respect first?” and began asking, “How can I treat this person with dignity?”

Respect is not a weakness. It is a deliberate expression of Christian character.

**Question:** Am I more concerned with receiving recognition than with showing kindness and honour?

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

### 4. 🌍 1 PETER 2:17 — HONOUR ALL PEOPLE.

> _Honour all men. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honour the king._
>
> **1 Peter 2:17, KJV.**

Peter gives a wide instruction: honour all people. Not only those who agree with us. Not only those who can do something for us. Not only those who belong to our community.

The verse also distinguishes between honouring people, loving fellow believers, fearing God and showing proper regard for governing authority. It does not teach that rulers are above accountability or that Christians must approve of every decision made by those in power.

We can respect the office while questioning the office-holder's conduct. We can disagree without becoming abusive.

**Question:** Can I disagree firmly while still treating the other person with dignity?

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

### 5. 👨‍👩‍👦 EPHESIANS 6:2–3 — HONOUR IS IMPORTANT.

> _Honour thy father and mother; which is the first commandment with promise;_
>
> _That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth._
>
> **Ephesians 6:2–3, KJV.**

Paul repeats the commandment and reminds believers of its promise.

In its original setting, this promise belongs to the covenant instruction given to Israel and is repeated by Paul as a principle of Christian family life. It should not be turned into a mathematical guarantee that every person who honours their parents will live to an old age, or that someone who dies young must have failed to honour them.

The deeper lesson remains: honour helps build families and communities where people are valued rather than discarded.

And again, honour does not mean accepting abuse. Safety, truth and appropriate boundaries matter.

**Question:** What can I do to make the people in my family feel valued and respected?

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

### 6. 🕊️ PROVERBS 22:4 — HUMILITY AND THE FEAR OF THE LORD.

> _By humility and the fear of the LORD are riches, and honour, and life._
>
> **Proverbs 22:4, KJV.**

True respect grows from humility. A person who believes they are always right will struggle to listen, apologise or learn.

Humility is not thinking that we have no value. It is understanding that we are not the centre of the universe. We answer to God, and our lives affect other people.

When we approach others with humility, we make room for wisdom, peace and mutual respect.

**Question:** Is pride preventing me from listening, learning or admitting when I am wrong?

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

### 7. 🌳 GENESIS 1:28 — CARE FOR WHAT GOD HAS ENTRUSTED TO US.

> _And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth._
>
> **Genesis 1:28, KJV.**

God entrusted humanity with responsibility over the earth and its living creatures. Dominion should not be confused with careless destruction.

Genesis 2:15 adds an important dimension: humanity was placed in the garden to dress it and to keep it. We are to work the earth and care for it.

That includes the way we treat animals, plants, water, soil and the natural spaces around us. We do not have to worship creation to respect the Creator's handiwork.

**Question:** Am I caring for what God has entrusted to me, or am I treating it as though it has no value?

## 🛠️ PRACTICAL TIP — MAKE RESPECT A DAILY HABIT.

Respect becomes real through ordinary decisions. Try these simple habits:

1. **Listen before answering.** Give people the courtesy of being heard.
2. **Speak without humiliating.** Correct a mistake without attacking someone's dignity.
3. **Honour age and experience.** Offer help, patience and consideration to older people.
4. **Notice who is being overlooked.** Make room for the pregnant woman, the elderly person, the disabled person or anyone who needs assistance.
5. **Respect yourself.** Keep your word, care for your health, take responsibility and refuse to destroy your own dignity.
6. **Disagree without hatred.** You can challenge an idea without degrading the person who holds it.
7. **Care for creation.** Think before cutting down, throwing away, poisoning or destroying.
8. **Use your influence responsibly.** Whether you are speaking to a child, a neighbour, a colleague or an AI tool, let your communication reflect discipline and character.

One small act of respect can change the direction of someone's day.

## 📋 DAILY EXERCISE — THE RESPECT CHALLENGE.

Today, make a conscious effort to practise respect.

- Give someone your full attention when they speak.
- Thank a person whose contribution you usually take for granted.
- Offer practical help to someone who needs it.
- Speak calmly where you would normally respond sharply.
- Do one small thing to care for your surroundings.
- Examine one area where pride or impatience has affected your behaviour.

At the end of the day, ask yourself:

**Did I make it easier for someone else to feel seen, heard and valued today?**

Do not use this exercise to condemn yourself. Use it to recognise where you can grow.

## ☁️ THE HEAVENLY VIEW — SEE PEOPLE AS I SEE THEM.

My child, I have created people with worth and dignity. Do not measure their value only by their usefulness to you, their possessions, their age, their position or their agreement with your opinions.

I ask you to walk humbly. Honour those who have cared for you. Be patient with those who move more slowly. Listen to those whose voices are often ignored. Speak truth, but do not use truth as an excuse for cruelty.

Remember that respect does not mean approving of wrongdoing. There will be times when you must stand against injustice, protect the vulnerable and speak when silence would allow harm to continue. Do so with courage, wisdom and a heart that does not delight in hatred.

Care for the world I have entrusted to you. Be a good steward of what you have been given.

And when you are tempted to ask why others do not respect you, first examine the way you treat them.

Let your life become an example of the love you profess.

## ✍️ ORIGINAL POEM — THE WEIGHT OF RESPECT.

Respect is more than words we say,  
Or manners worn for just a day.  
It lives in what our hands will do,  
In how we treat the old and new.

It lifts the fallen, makes room to stand,  
It offers help, an open hand.  
It listens first, it does not sneer,  
It gives another's voice an ear.

It honours those who paved the road,  
And shares with those who bear a load.  
It guards the weak, protects the small,  
And sees God's image in us all.

It tends the tree, it spares the wing,  
It cares for every living thing.  
It knows that power is not a right  
To rule without a sense of right.

So let respect begin in me,  
In words, in deeds, in what I see.  
For honour grows where hearts are true,  
And what I give returns anew.

## 🗳️ FINAL THOUGHT — RESPECT YOUR DESTINY. GO AND VOTE!

Today is voting day. And I want to leave you with an analogy.

Imagine a bus carrying thirty people. The road ahead approaches a dangerous cliff.

Ten passengers vote to drive the bus straight over the edge. Five vote against it. The remaining fifteen decide not to vote at all.

The result? The ten votes in favour outnumber the five votes against. The bus goes over the cliff — and all thirty people suffer the consequences.

The people who abstained may say, “But I did not vote for it.”

That may be true. But their decision not to participate did not stop the outcome.

That is the danger of treating our responsibility as though it belongs only to somebody else.

Voting is not a magic solution. No political party or candidate is perfect, and casting a vote does not mean surrendering your conscience. But participation gives your voice a place in the decision. It is one way citizens help shape the direction of their communities and country.

Do your homework. Consider the policies, conduct and record of those asking for your support. Think about the vulnerable, the elderly, the children, the people who cannot easily speak for themselves, and the generations who will inherit the consequences.

Vote according to your conscience and your best judgement. Respect the rights of others to make their own choice. Do not allow political disagreement to turn neighbours into enemies.

And remember: respect is not only about how we treat people who agree with us. It is also revealed in how we treat those who do not.

**Do not hand your responsibility to someone else. If you have the right to vote, use it.**

Respect yourself. Respect your neighbours. Respect the future.

And may God guide South Africa.

The verse also distinguishes between honouring people, loving fellow believers, fearing God and showing proper regard for governing authority. It does not teach that rulers are above accountability or that Christians must approve of every decision made by those in power.

We can respect the office while questioning the office-holder's conduct. We can disagree without becoming abusive.

*Question:* Can I disagree firmly while still treating the other person with dignity?

*5. 👨‍👩‍👦 EPHESIANS 6:2–3 — HONOUR IS IMPORTANT.*

> _Honour thy father and mother; which is the first commandment with promise;_
>
> _That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth._
> *Ephesians 6:2–3, KJV.*

Paul repeats the commandment and reminds believers of its promise.

In its original setting, this promise belongs to the covenant instruction given to Israel and is repeated by Paul as a principle of Christian family life. It should not be turned into a mathematical guarantee that every person who honours their parents will live to an old age, or that someone who dies young must have failed to honour them.

The deeper lesson remains: honour helps build families and communities where people are valued rather than discarded.

And again, honour does not mean accepting abuse. Safety, truth and appropriate boundaries matter.

*Question:* What can I do to make the people in my family feel valued and respected?

*6. 🕊️ PROVERBS 22:4 — HUMILITY AND THE FEAR OF THE LORD.*

> _By humility and the fear of the LORD are riches, and honour, and life._
> *Proverbs 22:4, KJV.*

True respect grows from humility. A person who believes they are always right will struggle to listen, apologise or learn.

Humility is not thinking that we have no value. It is understanding that we are not the centre of the universe. We answer to God, and our lives affect other people.

When we approach others with humility, we make room for wisdom, peace and mutual respect.

*Question:* Is pride preventing me from listening, learning or admitting when I am wrong?

*7. 🌳 GENESIS 1:28 — CARE FOR WHAT GOD HAS ENTRUSTED TO US.*

> _And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth._
> *Genesis 1:28, KJV.*

God entrusted humanity with responsibility over the earth and its living creatures. Dominion should not be confused with careless destruction.

Genesis 2:15 adds an important dimension: humanity was placed in the garden to dress it and to keep it. We are to work the earth and care for it.

That includes the way we treat animals, plants, water, soil and the natural spaces around us. We do not have to worship creation to respect the Creator's handiwork.

*Question:* Am I caring for what God has entrusted to me, or am I treating it as though it has no value?

*🛠️ PRACTICAL TIP — MAKE RESPECT A DAILY HABIT.*

Respect becomes real through ordinary decisions. Try these simple habits:

1. *Listen before answering.* Give people the courtesy of being heard.
2. *Speak without humiliating.* Correct a mistake without attacking someone's dignity.
3. *Honour age and experience.* Offer help, patience and consideration to older people.
4. *Notice who is being overlooked.* Make room for the pregnant woman, the elderly person, the disabled person or anyone who needs assistance.
5. *Respect yourself.* Keep your word, care for your health, take responsibility and refuse to destroy your own dignity.
6. *Disagree without hatred.* You can challenge an idea without degrading the person who holds it.
7. *Care for creation.* Think before cutting down, throwing away, poisoning or destroying.
8. *Use your influence responsibly.* Whether you are speaking to a child, a neighbour, a colleague or an AI tool, let your communication reflect discipline and character.

One small act of respect can change the direction of someone's day.

*🗳️ FINAL THOUGHT — RESPECT YOUR DESTINY. GO AND VOTE!*

Today is voting day. And I want to leave you with an analogy.

Imagine a bus carrying thirty people. The road ahead approaches a dangerous cliff.

Ten passengers vote to drive the bus straight over the edge. Five vote against it. The remaining fifteen decide not to vote at all.

The result? The ten votes in favour outnumber the five votes against. The bus goes over the cliff — and all thirty people suffer the consequences.

The people who abstained may say, “But I did not vote for it.”

That may be true. But their decision not to participate did not stop the outcome.

That is the danger of treating our responsibility as though it belongs only to somebody else.

Voting is not a magic solution. No political party or candidate is perfect, and casting a vote does not mean surrendering your conscience. But participation gives your voice a place in the decision. It is one way citizens help shape the direction of their communities and country.

Do your homework. Consider the policies, conduct and record of those asking for your support. Think about the vulnerable, the elderly, the children, the people who cannot easily speak for themselves, and the generations who will inherit the consequences.

Vote according to your conscience and your best judgement. Respect the rights of others to make their own choice. Do not allow political disagreement to turn neighbours into enemies.
