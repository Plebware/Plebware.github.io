# **🌿 Planning to Survive — When Life Becomes Overwhelming.**

**📅 Friday, 13 November 2026.**

**A Daily Christian Devotion.**

_When life takes more than we have to give, where do we find the strength to carry on?_

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

**🙏 OPENING PRAYER.**

_Heavenly Father,_

_Today I come before You with an honest heart. Sometimes life becomes so complicated that I struggle to see how everything is going to work out._

_The financial pressures, the physical exhaustion, the emotional burdens, and the dreams that seem to keep moving further away can become overwhelming._

_Lord, You know the circumstances of every person reading these words. You know the bills that cannot be paid, the food that must somehow stretch until the next payday, the loved ones we have lost, and the worries we carry silently._

_Teach us that survival is not simply about having enough money, strength, or energy. It is also about having faith when we cannot see the road ahead._

_Give us wisdom to plan, courage to persevere, patience when things take longer than expected, and the humility to accept help when we need it._

_Remind us that we are never outside Your sight, never beyond Your compassion, and never too insignificant for Your attention._

_In the precious name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Saviour._

_Amen. 🙏_

**🌅 TODAY'S THOUGHT — SURVIVAL IS MORE THAN GETTING THROUGH THE DAY.**

There is a difference between living and merely surviving.

When the cost of living keeps increasing, when unexpected expenses destroy an already carefully balanced budget, and when circumstances change without warning, even the simplest things can become difficult.

Sometimes it is not one enormous disaster that brings us to our knees.

It is a collection of smaller problems.

One unexpected vehicle repair. One missed day of income. One additional expense. One sleepless night. One more family crisis.

And suddenly, the entire balance of our lives is disturbed.

We often speak about planning for the future, but how do we plan when we are struggling to get through today?

How do we plan when we are physically exhausted, emotionally overwhelmed, and financially stretched?

And what happens when the things we dream about seem to be permanently postponed?

There is something important I want us to understand today.

**God does not promise that we will never encounter difficult circumstances. He promises that we do not have to face them alone.**

Faith does not remove the responsibility of planning.

Faith gives us a reason to keep planning when circumstances make planning difficult.

Sometimes the greatest victory of the day is not accomplishing something extraordinary.

Sometimes it is simply getting up, doing what needs to be done, and trusting God for tomorrow.

❤️ That, too, is perseverance.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

**💰 PART 1 — WHEN ONE SMALL THING KNOCKS EVERYTHING OVER.**

Have you ever noticed how quickly one small problem can become five?

Over the past couple of months, my employer's motor vehicle, affectionately known as Bucky, has been in for repairs several times.

It wasn't one enormous expense all at once. It was little bits here and there.

But when I calculated the consequences, the repairs and associated disruptions had cost me almost R5,000 in lost income.

When you are living on a carefully balanced budget, that is a serious amount of money.

Then something unexpected happened.

A friend overseas saw my circumstances and generously sent me some money. It covered what I had lost, with a little extra.

That kindness helped carry me through the following two months.

But now that money is almost finished again.

And Christmas is approaching.

Even if we don't celebrate Christmas in the traditional commercial sense, the festive season brings increased expenses, travelling, shopping, and other pressures.

People go away to spend time with their families.

Julian and I will probably spend Christmas together, as we have done before.

But this year will be different.

His mother, Rochelle, passed away earlier this year. She was someone who enjoyed planning the family Christmas gatherings.

Her absence will leave an enormous gap.

And sometimes the financial pressures and emotional pressures arrive together.

That is the reality of survival.

It is not always one problem. It is the accumulation of many problems.

**But here is something worth remembering: God's provision does not always arrive in the way we expect.**

Sometimes it comes through an unexpected opportunity.

Sometimes through the generosity of another person.

Sometimes through a friend who remembers us when everyone else is busy with their own lives.

And sometimes it comes through the strength to make another plan.

We must never become so focused on what we do not have that we overlook the blessings that have already reached us.

**🌡️ PART 2 — WHEN YOUR BODY IS EXHAUSTED AND YOUR MIND NEEDS REST.**

Financial survival is only one part of the picture.

There is also the physical battle.

Here in Southern Africa, the summer heat has been particularly uncomfortable.

Personally, I have never been particularly fond of hot weather.

Give me a cool winter morning, a warm cup of coffee, and a comfortable blanket, and I am a happy man! ☕😄

But when the temperatures rise, the mosquitoes arrive, and the nights become uncomfortable, sleep becomes another challenge.

Over the past few weeks, I have often managed only about five hours of sleep.

There was a recent night when I slept from 22:57 until 04:40.

That is five hours and forty-three minutes.

And the following morning, I still had to get up and go to work.

Now combine that with longer working hours, travelling, responsibilities at home, and the ordinary demands of everyday life.

It becomes exhausting.

We sometimes forget that our bodies are not machines.

Even the strongest person needs rest.

Even the most determined person has physical limitations.

And even someone with tremendous faith can become tired.

**Rest is not a weakness. It is part of God's design for human beings.**

We cannot solve every problem while running on empty.

Sometimes the most responsible thing we can do is recognise that we need to stop, breathe, rest, and allow our bodies to recover.

We should not confuse perseverance with destroying ourselves.

**💔 PART 3 — WHEN YOUR EMOTIONS ARE CONSTANTLY BEING ASSAULTED.**

There are some battles that cannot be measured in money or working hours.

They are emotional battles.

The loss of a loved one can leave an emptiness that does not disappear simply because the funeral is over.

Julian lost his mother unexpectedly.

I sometimes still find myself wondering why she had to leave us.

Then there are the family difficulties.

Young children whose circumstances have become so complicated that social services have had to intervene.

Family members struggling with the consequences of past substance abuse.

And a young woman who disappeared following a frightening series of events involving alleged threats and criminal activity.

Her family has searched hospitals, checked the morgues, and tried to establish whether she has been arrested.

There has been no confirmed answer.

That uncertainty is heartbreaking.

We must be careful not to judge people whose circumstances we do not fully understand. But we can certainly pray for those who are hurting, those who are missing, and those who are desperately searching for answers.

Then Christmas approaches.

And the person who once helped bring the family together is no longer here.

Grief has a way of making certain dates particularly difficult.

A birthday.

An anniversary.

A family gathering.

Christmas lunch.

A familiar chair that remains empty.

And while all of this is happening, ordinary life continues.

Work still needs to be done.

Bills still need to be paid.

People still expect you to get up in the morning and carry on.

But sometimes your heart is carrying things that nobody else can see.

**God understands the weight of an invisible burden.**

You do not have to pretend to be happy when you are hurting.

You do not have to pretend to be strong when you need encouragement.

And you do not have to carry everybody else's problems as though you alone are responsible for fixing the world.

There is a difference between caring about people and believing that you must personally rescue everyone.

Sometimes love means praying, helping where possible, establishing healthy boundaries, and trusting God with what lies beyond our control.

**⏳ PART 4 — WHEN YOUR DREAMS AND BASIC NEEDS KEEP GETTING POSTPONED.**

This is perhaps one of the most frustrating aspects of survival.

There are things we genuinely need.

There are things we would love to do.

And there are dreams that remain important to us, even when circumstances make them difficult to pursue.

For example, I need new shoes.

I have not bought myself a new pair in more than three years.

I am using safety boots to drive, which is uncomfortable in the summer heat.

When I get home, I take them off, wash my feet, and apply some cream.

Even something as simple as foot powder becomes an expense that must be considered.

Then there are the technological challenges.

Earlier this year, I received a second-hand Lenovo laptop as a gift after my previous computer developed a graphics failure.

That gift was an enormous blessing.

I was able to return to my work, use Linux, and continue developing my projects.

But the computer had limited storage.

I had to find a way to make better use of the equipment already available to me.

Eventually, I used a USB-to-SATA adapter and an existing Western Digital SSD to clone the smaller drive onto a larger one.

It was not a glamorous upgrade.

It was a practical solution using resources already available.

Now I need to create a proper backup before adjusting the partitions and giving Linux more working space.

Even that requires planning, time, and patience.

And then there is my writing.

I have a science-fiction series planned consisting of twenty-one books.

Twenty-one!

I still intend to write them.

But work, financial pressures, family responsibilities, and the loss of loved ones have repeatedly pushed that dream into the background.

In 2024, we lost Brandon to cancer.

Earlier this year, we lost Rochelle.

Life has changed considerably.

Sometimes I wonder when I will finally get the opportunity to sit down and concentrate on the things I have wanted to accomplish for so long.

But perhaps there is a lesson here.

**A postponed dream is not necessarily an abandoned dream.**

We should not allow our present circumstances to convince us that our future has already been decided.

Sometimes we must put certain ambitions aside temporarily.

Sometimes we must work with what we have.

Sometimes we must take one small step rather than attempting to complete the entire journey.

But we should never assume that because something has not happened yet, it can never happen.

God is not limited by our present circumstances.

**📜 CHURCH HISTORY — JOHN WESLEY AND THE CHRISTIAN RESPONSE TO POVERTY.**

During the eighteenth century, England experienced enormous social and economic hardship.

Poverty, poor working conditions, illness, unemployment, and overcrowded living conditions affected ordinary families.

Into this environment came John Wesley, one of the most influential figures in the Methodist movement.

Wesley did not believe that Christianity should be limited to attending church services and listening to sermons.

He believed that genuine faith should express itself through practical compassion.

His ministry included visiting prisoners, helping poor families, supporting education, providing medical assistance, and encouraging Christians to care for people who were struggling.

He also encouraged organised Christian fellowship through Methodist class meetings, where ordinary believers could support and encourage one another.

One of his well-known teachings was the principle:

**"Do all the good you can."**

Wesley's approach reminds us of something important.

Christianity is not merely about telling struggling people that God loves them.

It is also about demonstrating that love through our actions.

Sometimes that means providing food.

Sometimes it means helping somebody find work.

Sometimes it means visiting a lonely person.

Sometimes it means helping someone repair something they cannot afford to replace.

And sometimes it simply means listening without judging.

The Church is not meant to be a gathering place for people who have perfect lives.

It should also be a place where struggling people find compassion, encouragement, and practical support.

❤️ Faith becomes visible when love becomes action.

**📖 THREE SCRIPTURES FOR THE JOURNEY OF SURVIVAL.**

**1. 🌾 GOD KNOWS WHAT WE NEED.**

> "But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus."

**Philippians 4:19 — King James Version (KJV).**

There is something deeply comforting about this promise.

God knows our needs.

He knows when the money is running short.

He knows when we need food, clothing, transport, shelter, and the resources to continue our daily responsibilities.

But we must understand that this Scripture is not a guarantee that every financial difficulty will disappear overnight.

Paul wrote these words from a position of experience. He understood both abundance and hardship.

The passage teaches us to trust God regardless of our circumstances.

It also encourages us to remain generous and faithful, even when life is difficult.

**Reflection:**

Sometimes God's provision comes through people.

Sometimes through opportunities.

Sometimes through unexpected assistance.

And sometimes through the wisdom to manage what we already have.

**Ask yourself:**

Am I so worried about what I lack that I have stopped recognising the ways God has already provided for me?

**2. 🕊️ GOD INVITES THE WEARY TO FIND REST.**

> "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

**Matthew 11:28 — King James Version (KJV).**

Jesus did not invite only the successful, the wealthy, or the people who had everything under control.

He invited the weary.

Those who were carrying burdens.

Those who were struggling.

Those who had reached the point where life had become too heavy.

Notice that Jesus does not say we must first fix everything before coming to Him.

He says:

"Come unto me."

That invitation is still available today.

When you are exhausted, you can pray.

When you are grieving, you can pray.

When you cannot find the answers, you can pray.

You do not need to manufacture beautiful words.

Sometimes the most honest prayer is simply:

"Lord, I am tired. Please help me."

**Reflection:**

Rest is not only about sleeping.

It is also about allowing our hearts and minds to stop carrying every burden as though everything depends on us.

**Ask yourself:**

What burden am I carrying today that I need to place in God's hands?

**3. 🌅 GOD GIVES US STRENGTH FOR THE NEXT STEP.**

> "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me."

**Philippians 4:13 — King James Version (KJV).**

This is one of the most frequently quoted Scriptures in Christianity.

But its context is particularly important.

Paul was explaining that he had learned how to live through both abundance and hardship.

He understood what it meant to have plenty and what it meant to experience need.

His confidence was not based on having comfortable circumstances.

It was based on Christ.

That is a powerful lesson for anyone struggling to survive.

Sometimes we cannot change our circumstances immediately.

We cannot bring back someone who has passed away.

We cannot always control the behaviour of other people.

We cannot guarantee that tomorrow will be financially easier.

But through Christ, we can find strength to face the next responsibility, the next challenge, and the next day.

**Reflection:**

Faith does not mean that we will never become tired.

It means that tiredness does not have to become the end of our journey.

**Ask yourself:**

Am I trying to face tomorrow using only my own strength, or am I allowing Christ to strengthen me?

**🛠️ PRACTICAL TIPS — FAITH MUST ALSO WORK IN EVERYDAY LIFE.**

Spiritual encouragement is important, but faith should also influence the decisions we make.

Here are seven practical ways to approach difficult circumstances.

**1. 💰 Plan for the unexpected.**

Even a small emergency reserve can make a difference.

If you cannot save a large amount, start with whatever is realistic.

A little preparation is better than none.

**2. 📝 Separate necessities from wishes.**

Write down what must be paid, what must be purchased, and what can wait.

Do not confuse everything you would like to have with everything you need immediately.

**3. 🔧 Look after what you already own.**

Maintain your vehicle, equipment, clothing, and household items where possible.

Repairing something may be more affordable than replacing it.

But recognise when a repair is no longer economical.

**4. 😴 Protect your physical health.**

Do not treat exhaustion as a badge of honour.

Make time for sleep, hydration, cooling down, and recovery.

If persistent sleep problems or severe exhaustion continue, seek appropriate medical advice.

**5. ❤️ Establish emotional boundaries.**

You can love people without carrying every problem they create.

Help where you can.

Pray for those who need help.

But recognise that some situations require professional assistance and cannot be solved by one family member.

**6. 🙏 Build a daily relationship with God.**

Even five minutes of sincere prayer and Scripture reading can help restore perspective.

Do not wait until everything goes wrong before speaking to God.

**7. 🌱 Keep one dream alive.**

You may not have the money or time to accomplish everything today.

But choose one small action that keeps your dream moving.

Read one page.

Write one paragraph.

Repair one item.

Learn one new skill.

Small steps still count.

The things we have lost.

Bills still need to be paid.

People still expect you to get up in the morning and carry on.

But sometimes your heart is carrying things that nobody else can see.

*God understands the weight of an invisible burden.*

You do not have to pretend to be happy when you are hurting.

You do not have to pretend to be strong when you need encouragement.

And you do not have to carry everybody else's problems as though you alone are responsible for fixing the world.

There is a difference between caring about people and believing that you must personally rescue everyone.

Sometimes love means praying, helping where possible, establishing healthy boundaries, and trusting God with what lies beyond our control.

⏳ *PART 4 — WHEN YOUR DREAMS AND BASIC NEEDS KEEP GETTING POSTPONED.*

This is perhaps one of the most frustrating aspects of survival.

There are things we genuinely need.

There are things we would love to do.

And there are dreams that remain important to us, even when circumstances make them difficult to pursue.

For example, I need new shoes.

I have not bought myself a new pair in more than three years.

I am using safety boots to drive, which is uncomfortable in the summer heat.

When I get home, I take them off, wash my feet, and apply some cream.

Even something as simple as foot powder becomes an expense that must be considered.

Then there are the technological challenges.

Earlier this year, I received a second-hand Lenovo laptop as a gift after my previous computer developed a graphics failure.

That gift was an enormous blessing.

I was able to return to my work, use Linux, and continue developing my projects.

But the computer had limited storage.

I had to find a way to make better use of the equipment already available to me.

Eventually, I used a USB-to-SATA adapter and an existing Western Digital SSD to clone the smaller drive onto a larger one.

It was not a glamorous upgrade.

It was a practical solution using resources already available.

Now I need to create a proper backup before adjusting the partitions and giving Linux more working space.

Even that requires planning, time, and patience.

And then there is my writing.

I have a science-fiction series planned consisting of twenty-one books.

Twenty-one!

I still intend to write them.

But work, financial pressures, family responsibilities, and the loss of loved ones have repeatedly pushed that dream into the background.

In 2024, we lost Brandon to cancer.

Earlier this year, we lost Rochelle.

Life has changed considerably.

Sometimes I wonder when I will finally get the opportunity to sit down and concentrate on the things I have wanted to accomplish for so long.

But perhaps there is a lesson here.

*A postponed dream is not necessarily an abandoned dream.*

We should not allow our present circumstances to convince us that our future has already been decided.

Sometimes we must put certain ambitions aside temporarily.

Sometimes we must work with what we have.

Sometimes we must take one small step rather than attempting to complete the entire journey.

But we should never assume that because something has not happened yet, it can never happen.

God is not limited by our present circumstances.

📜 *CHURCH HISTORY — JOHN WESLEY AND THE CHRISTIAN RESPONSE TO POVERTY.*

During the eighteenth century, England experienced enormous social and economic hardship.

Poverty, poor working conditions, illness, unemployment, and overcrowded living conditions affected ordinary families.

Into this environment came John Wesley, one of the most influential figures in the Methodist movement.

Wesley did not believe that Christianity should be limited to attending church services and listening to sermons.

He believed that genuine faith should express itself through practical compassion.

His ministry included visiting prisoners, helping poor families, supporting education, providing medical assistance, and encouraging Christians to care for people who were struggling.

He also encouraged organised Christian fellowship through Methodist class meetings, where ordinary believers could support and encourage one another.

One of his well-known teachings was the principle:

*"Do all the good you can."*

Wesley's approach reminds us of something important.

Christianity is not merely about telling struggling people that God loves them.

It is also about demonstrating that love through our actions.

Sometimes that means providing food.

Sometimes it means helping somebody find work.

Sometimes it means visiting a lonely person.

Sometimes it means helping someone repair something they cannot afford to replace.

And sometimes it simply means listening without judging.

The Church is not meant to be a gathering place for people who have perfect lives.

It should also be a place where struggling people find compassion, encouragement, and practical support.

❤️ Faith becomes visible when love becomes action.

📖 *THREE SCRIPTURES FOR THE JOURNEY OF SURVIVAL.*

*1. 🌾 GOD KNOWS WHAT WE NEED.*

> _"But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus."_ *Philippians 4:19 — King James Version (KJV).*

There is something deeply comforting about this promise.

God knows our needs.

He knows when the money is running short.

He knows when we need food, clothing, transport, shelter, and the resources to continue our daily responsibilities.

But we must understand that this Scripture is not a guarantee that every financial difficulty will disappear overnight.

Paul wrote these words from a position of experience. He understood both abundance and hardship.

The passage teaches us to trust God regardless of our circumstances.

It also encourages us to remain generous and faithful, even when life is difficult.

*Reflection:*

Sometimes God's provision comes through people.

Sometimes through opportunities.

Sometimes through unexpected assistance.

And sometimes through the wisdom to manage what we already have.

*Ask yourself:*

Am I so worried about what I lack that I have stopped recognising the ways God has already provided for me?

*2. 🕊️ GOD INVITES THE WEARY TO FIND REST.*

> _"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."_ *Matthew 11:28 — King James Version (KJV).*

Jesus did not invite only the successful, the wealthy, or the people who had everything under control.

He invited the weary.

Those who were carrying burdens.

Those who were struggling.

Those who had reached the point where life had become too heavy.

Notice that Jesus does not say we must first fix everything before coming to Him.

He says:

"Come unto me."

That invitation is still available today.

When you are exhausted, you can pray.

When you are grieving, you can pray.

When you cannot find the answers, you can pray.

You do not need to manufacture beautiful words.

Sometimes the most honest prayer is simply:

"Lord, I am tired. Please help me."

*Reflection:*

Rest is not only about sleeping.

It is also about allowing our hearts and minds to stop carrying every burden as though everything depends on us.

*Ask yourself:*

What burden am I carrying today that I need to place in God's hands?

*3. 🌅 GOD GIVES US STRENGTH FOR THE NEXT STEP.*

> _"I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me."_ *Philippians 4:13 — King James Version (KJV).*

This is one of the most frequently quoted Scriptures in Christianity.

But its context is particularly important.

Paul was explaining that he had learned how to live through both abundance and hardship.

He understood what it meant to have plenty and what it meant to experience need.

His confidence was not based on having comfortable circumstances.

It was based on Christ.

That is a powerful lesson for anyone struggling to survive.

Sometimes we cannot change our circumstances immediately.

We cannot bring back someone who has passed away.

We cannot always control the behaviour of other people.

We cannot guarantee that tomorrow will be financially easier.

But through Christ, we can find strength to face the next responsibility, the next challenge, and the next day.

*Reflection:*

Faith does not mean that we will never become tired.

It means that tiredness does not have to become the end of our journey.

*Ask yourself:*

Am I trying to face tomorrow using only my own strength, or am I allowing Christ to strengthen me?

🛠️ *PRACTICAL TIPS — FAITH MUST ALSO WORK IN EVERYDAY LIFE.*

Spiritual encouragement is important, but faith should also influence the decisions we make.

Here are seven practical ways to approach difficult circumstances.

*1. 💰 Plan for the unexpected.*

Even a small emergency reserve can make a difference.

If you cannot save a large amount, start with whatever is realistic.

A little preparation is better than none.

*2. 📝 Separate necessities from wishes.*

Write down what must be paid, what must be purchased, and what can wait.

Do not confuse everything you would like to have with everything you need immediately.

*3. 🔧 Look after what you already own.*

Maintain your vehicle, equipment, clothing, and household items where possible.

Repairing something may be more affordable than replacing it.

But recognise when a repair is no longer economical.

*4. 😴 Protect your physical health.*

Do not treat exhaustion as a badge of honour.

Make time for sleep, hydration, cooling down, and recovery.

If persistent sleep problems or severe exhaustion continue, seek appropriate medical advice.

*5. ❤️ Establish emotional boundaries.*

You can love people without carrying every problem they create.

Help where you can.

Pray for those who need help.

But recognise that some situations require professional assistance and cannot be solved by one family member.

*6. 🙏 Build a daily relationship with God.*

Even five minutes of sincere prayer and Scripture reading can help restore perspective.

Do not wait until everything goes wrong before speaking to God.

*7. 🌱 Keep one dream alive.*

You may not have the money or time to accomplish everything today.

But choose one small action that keeps your dream moving.

Read one page.

Write one paragraph.

Repair one item.

Learn one new skill.

Small steps still count.
