# 🔑 When The Children Are In The Storm.

<img src="/assets/images/children-in-the-storm.webp" alt="A Christian devotional about protecting children, seeking justice, wisdom, and prayer during family crisis." style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">

## 🔑 Opening Prayer.

Heavenly Father,

Today we bring before You families who are hurting, children who are vulnerable, and parents who are frightened and uncertain about tomorrow.

Lord, You see what we cannot see.

You know every fact. You know every heart. You know every conversation that took place behind closed doors.

Where there is confusion, bring clarity. Where there is fear, bring peace. Where there is injustice, bring truth. Where there is genuine danger, bring protection. Where there is hardness of heart, bring compassion.

Most of all, Lord, protect the children. Let them be surrounded by people who genuinely seek their good.

In the name of Jesus Christ, Amen.

## 🔑 Introduction: When We Do Not Know The Whole Story.

There are times when something happens to another family and we immediately want to choose sides.

We hear one person's story. We become angry. We assume we know what happened. We decide who is guilty.

But Scripture teaches us something different.

God calls us to seek truth. God calls us to show mercy. God calls us to protect the vulnerable. And God calls us to pray.

When we encounter a family facing homelessness, poverty, separation, or intervention by authorities, we may not know the complete picture.

Perhaps children have been removed from their parents. Perhaps accusations have been made. Perhaps the parents are telling one story while officials have another.

What should Christians do?

We pray. We listen. We refuse to spread rumours. We seek truth. We encourage people to use lawful processes. And we place the people involved into the hands of God.

Because God sees the entire picture.

## 🔑 Church History: The Church Has Always Cared For The Vulnerable.

From the earliest days of Christianity, caring for vulnerable people was not considered an optional extra. It was part of Christian discipleship.

The early Christians cared for widows, orphans, the poor, prisoners, strangers, and people who had nobody else to help them.

James later described pure and undefiled religion in terms that included caring for orphans and widows in their distress.

The Christian response to suffering has therefore never been merely, "I will pray for you."

Christian prayer should often lead to Christian action.

Sometimes the answer to somebody else's prayer is the willingness of another Christian to open a door. Sometimes it is food. Sometimes it is accommodation. Sometimes it is transportation. Sometimes it is legal assistance. Sometimes it is simply somebody saying, "You are not alone."

## 🔑 Seven Scriptures For The Storm.

### 🔑 Scripture One: Protect The Vulnerable.

> "Defend the weak and the fatherless; uphold the cause of the poor and the oppressed." — Psalm 82:3.

God does not ask His people to ignore vulnerable people. He commands us to defend them.

That does not mean blindly taking someone's side. It means making sure that vulnerable people are not forgotten.

### 🔑 Scripture Two: Seek Justice.

> "Learn to do right; seek justice. Defend the oppressed." — Isaiah 1:17.

Justice requires truth. Sometimes truth requires patience.

Before we condemn somebody, we need to understand what actually happened.

Christian justice is not mob justice. It is not gossip. It is not revenge. It is the careful pursuit of what is right.

### 🔑 Scripture Three: Care For Children.

> "Let the little children come to me, and do not hinder them." — Matthew 19:14.

Jesus had a special tenderness toward children.

Children are not political arguments. They are not bargaining chips. They are not weapons in adult conflicts.

They are precious human beings who need love, stability, protection, and care.

### 🔑 Scripture Four: Ask God For Wisdom.

> "If any of you lacks wisdom, you should ask God." — James 1:5.

When we do not know what to do, we should not manufacture an answer. We should ask God for wisdom.

That applies to parents. It applies to social workers. It applies to lawyers. It applies to family members. And it applies to Christians watching from the outside.

Sometimes the wisest thing we can say is, "I don't know all the facts yet, so I am going to pray."

### 🔑 Scripture Five: Carry One Another's Burdens.

> "Carry each other's burdens, and in this way you will fulfil the law of Christ." — Galatians 6:2.

We cannot solve every problem. But we can help carry the burden.

A meal can carry a burden. A telephone call can carry a burden. A ride to court can carry a burden. A place to sleep can carry a burden. Prayer can carry a burden.

Sometimes the smallest act of kindness becomes enormous to someone who has nothing.

### 🔑 Scripture Six: Do Not Judge Hastily.

> "Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak and slow to become angry." — James 1:19.

We hear a story. We become angry. We share it. Then we discover that we did not know the whole story.

Christians must be different. Listen first. Pray first. Investigate carefully. Speak responsibly.

### 🔑 Scripture Seven: God Sees The Children.

> "For he will command his angels concerning you to guard you in all your ways." — Psalm 91:11.

We cannot physically stand beside every vulnerable child. But God can.

We cannot see every room. God can. We cannot hear every conversation. God can. We cannot know every danger. God can.

That does not mean we become passive. It means that our action is accompanied by prayer.

We work. We pray. We trust.

## 🔑 Daily T.I.P.: Truth, Intercession, Practical Help.

Today, remember the word, T.I.P.

**T — Truth.** Do not spread information you cannot verify.

**I — Intercession.** Pray for the people involved.

**P — Practical Help.** Where appropriate, do something useful.

A Christian should not merely ask, "What do I think about this?"

Ask instead, "What can I do that honours Christ?"

## 🔑 Practical Application.

If you encounter a family facing homelessness or child-protection intervention, consider these principles.

- **Listen before judging.**
- **Encourage parents to obtain legal advice.**
- **Encourage cooperation with lawful authorities.**
- **Help them find legitimate support services.**
- **Do not spread accusations without evidence.**
- **Pray for the children.**
- **Pray for the parents.**
- **Pray for the officials involved.**
- **Ask God whether you can provide practical assistance.**

And if you are personally in the middle of such a crisis, do not disappear, run, threaten people, destroy evidence, or sign documents you do not understand.

Seek help. Seek wisdom. Seek lawful assistance. And seek God.

## 🔑 Heaven's View.

Imagine looking at this situation from Heaven.

God does not see labels. He does not simply see, "Homeless family," "welfare case," "police incident," "parents," or "children."

God sees people.

He sees frightened children. He sees struggling parents. He sees officials trying to make decisions. He sees relatives wondering whether they can help. He sees the person who might open a door. He sees the person who might close one.

And He sees the truth.

Nothing is hidden from Him.

That should change the way we pray.

We do not need to tell God who is guilty. He already knows.

We do not need to tell God which version is true. He already knows.

We can simply say, "Father, let truth prevail. Protect the children. Give wisdom to the parents. Give wisdom to those responsible for the children. Open the right doors. Close the wrong ones. And let Your will be done."

That is prayer with humility.

## 🔑 The Christian Lesson.

The lesson is not, "Always believe the parents."

Nor is it, "Always believe the authorities."

The lesson is:

**Seek truth.**

**Protect children.**

**Show mercy.**

**Pursue justice.**

**Help the vulnerable.**

**Pray for wisdom.**

And never forget that behind every difficult case are human beings created in the image of God.

Sometimes God puts us close to somebody else's crisis because He wants us to become part of the answer.

Maybe the answer is prayer. Maybe it is accommodation. Maybe it is food. Maybe it is legal assistance. Maybe it is a listening ear. Maybe it is a family member opening their home.

We do not always know what part God wants us to play. But we can always ask.

## 🔑 Closing Prayer.

Father God,

We place this entire situation into Your hands.

Protect the children. Guard their minds. Guard their hearts. Keep them safe wherever they are.

Give their parents wisdom, humility, courage, and strength. Give them the ability to make good decisions under tremendous pressure.

Give wisdom to every social worker, police officer, court official, lawyer, and family member involved.

Let truth come into the light.

Where there has been misunderstanding, bring understanding. Where there has been fear, bring peace. Where there has been hardness, bring compassion.

Where there is a door that needs to open, open it. If there is a family member who can safely provide a home, give that person courage and compassion to consider doing so. And if there is another solution that we cannot yet see, Lord, reveal it.

Most importantly, let the best interests of these children remain at the centre of every decision.

Teach Your Church not merely to talk about compassion, but to practise it.

Teach us not merely to quote Scripture, but to live it.

Teach us to defend the vulnerable without abandoning truth. Teach us to pursue justice without becoming cruel. Teach us to show mercy without becoming blind. And teach us to trust You when we cannot see the road ahead.

In the mighty name of Jesus Christ, Amen.
