# 🙏 **When Gratitude Leaves Us Speechless.**

**A Journey of Grace, Faith, and Unexpected Blessings.**

**Saturday, 31 October 2026.**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🙏 **Opening Prayer.**

_Heavenly Father,_

_I come before You this morning with a heart that is so full that I struggle to find the words._

_You have been so good to me, Lord. Looking back over my life, I see Your hand in so many places. Through my younger years, my mistakes, my disappointments, my darkest moments, and the many people You have placed along my journey._

_Today, Father, I simply want to say thank You._

_Thank You for never abandoning me. Thank You for the people who have prayed for me, encouraged me, and helped me when I needed it most._

_I pray that this devotion will encourage somebody who is struggling, somebody who has perhaps forgotten how much You have already done in their life._

_Open our hearts, Lord. Help us to recognise Your goodness, even when it arrives through the hands of another person._

_Bless everyone who reads these words, and may all the honour and glory belong to You._

_In the precious name of Jesus Christ._

_Amen._

## 📖 **Introduction — Almost Five Decades of Grace.**

There are moments when we simply run out of words.

Not because we have nothing to say, but because our hearts are so overwhelmed with gratitude that ordinary language seems inadequate.

This morning, I experienced one of those moments.

I have been walking a journey of faith for approximately forty-seven to forty-eight years.

My spiritual journey began taking shape in 1978, when I received confirmation in the Methodist Church. I remember walking home that day feeling as though I were walking on clouds. I experienced an extraordinary peace, and I believe the Holy Spirit was already working in my life.

Around 1979, following a conversation with a Christian acquaintance who challenged me about my relationship with Christ, I found myself examining my faith more seriously.

I remember being rather stubborn about it. I told him I was already a Christian, that I had been confirmed and was born again.

Yet that evening, I prayed and reaffirmed my commitment to Christ in a deeply personal way.

I cannot give you an exact date, but those two years were significant milestones in my spiritual journey.

I was young, inexperienced, and still had much to learn about what it meant to follow Jesus.

And then came my military service.

### 🪖 **The Day God Spoke to Me.**

There was a period during my military service when I experienced some exceptionally dark moments.

After being removed from a driver's course, I became so overwhelmed with despair that I contemplated ending my life.

I had a loaded rifle positioned beneath my chin, my thumb in the trigger mechanism, ready to pull the trigger and end everything.

But in that moment, the Holy Spirit spoke to me.

Not vaguely. Not as an uncertain feeling. Not as something I simply imagined afterwards.

**I heard the Holy Spirit clearly.**

The words were:

"You really want to commit yourself to damnation? Do you really want to leave your mother in this position? What will your mother think?"

Those words broke through the darkness.

I burst into tears.

I removed the live round from the chamber, returned it to the magazine, put the weapon aside, and prayed.

I believe with all my heart that God intervened in my life that day.

It was not simply a moment of emotional reflection. It was an encounter that I have never forgotten.

My life could have ended that day.

But God spoke, and I listened.

During those military years, I also developed a close friendship with another Christian soldier. We would pray together, sing Christian songs, and encourage one another while on guard duty.

I particularly remember one occasion when we were guarding the headquarters building at 8 South African Infantry Battalion in Upington.

There we were, two young soldiers, singing Christian songs and praying together while carrying out our duties.

Looking back, I realise how important that fellowship was.

God was teaching me that we were never intended to walk this journey alone.

### 🏡 **Finding a Spiritual Home.**

After leaving the military, I eventually found employment and began rebuilding my life.

Then came another significant moment.

A letter from someone who cared about me challenged me to reconsider my relationship with Christ. It prompted me to reaffirm my faith and return to regular church attendance.

That person also introduced me to a congregation that would eventually become my spiritual home.

There, I experienced considerable spiritual growth.

I learned more about Scripture, fellowship, Christian responsibility, and the importance of making my faith part of everyday life.

In 1988, my mother and I decided to receive water baptism together.

It remains one of the special memories of my spiritual journey.

I became a member of that congregation, and for a season, I experienced a wonderful sense of belonging and spiritual development.

Life, however, is not always straightforward.

Years later, personal circumstances and disagreements resulted in my separation from that church.

I made mistakes, encountered disappointments, and found myself wrestling with aspects of my Christian walk.

But separation from a congregation does not necessarily mean separation from God.

Eventually, around 2012 to 2014, I found my way back to that spiritual community for a time.

Looking back, I realise that God used many different people and circumstances to shape my understanding of faith.

Some walked with me for years. Others appeared for only a season.

Some encouraged me. Others challenged me.

But each experience became part of the journey.

And through all those years, God remained faithful.

### ❤️ **A Morning I Will Not Easily Forget.**

Now, after all these years, I find myself in another difficult chapter of life.

There have been personal losses, financial pressures, household responsibilities, unexpected expenses, and the continuing challenges of keeping my little world together.

There have been mornings when I have wondered how I would manage the next day.

And then something happened that left me almost speechless.

A longstanding friend, someone whose family has been part of my life for many years, quietly reached out and offered financial assistance.

There was no publicity, no expectation of recognition, and no desire for the world to know.

Just a genuine act of kindness.

When I opened my banking application this morning, I saw that the money had arrived.

Only a short while earlier, my account had been overdrawn.

Now there was provision in front of me.

I sat looking at that screen, trying to process what had happened.

I wanted to thank my friend, but I struggled to find the words.

Then I realised something.

If I was struggling to express my gratitude to another human being, how often had I stood before God with the same difficulty?

How many times had I received His mercy, protection, provision, and patience without fully appreciating the magnitude of it?

I could only pray:

"Father, I don't have the words. All I can say is thank You."

And perhaps that was enough.

The generosity of another person does not replace God as our provider. Rather, it reminds us that God often works through willing hearts and compassionate hands.

I cannot repay every kindness I have received throughout my life.

But I can acknowledge it, appreciate it, pray for those who have blessed me, and endeavour to extend that same kindness to someone else when the opportunity arises.

**Sometimes God's answer to a prayer arrives through the hands of another person.**

## 🏛️ **Church History — The Fellowship of Giving.**

The early Christian church understood something that we sometimes overlook today.

Faith was never intended to be an isolated experience.

In the Book of Acts, we find a community of believers who shared their lives, their resources, and their possessions according to the needs of others.

Acts 2:44–45 describes how the believers were together and shared what they had.

This was more than charitable giving.

It was an expression of their understanding that they belonged to one another in Christ.

The early church did not measure Christian love merely by the words people spoke.

They demonstrated it through practical action.

They fed those who were hungry.

They supported those who were struggling.

They cared for widows and vulnerable members of their communities.

They prayed for one another.

And they recognised that the blessings they had received could become a blessing to somebody else.

Throughout Christian history, this spirit of generosity has continued through acts of hospitality, charity, practical assistance, and mutual encouragement.

The buildings, denominations, and traditions may differ, but the underlying principle remains:

**We are called to love one another.**

Sometimes that love looks like a meal.

Sometimes it looks like a prayer.

Sometimes it looks like somebody quietly helping another person through a difficult financial season.

And sometimes it is simply somebody reminding us that we are not forgotten.

## ✍️ **An Original Poem — When Words Are Not Enough.**

```
I searched for words to say thank You, Lord,
But every sentence disappeared.
My heart was full, my eyes were wet,
Your goodness made Your presence near.

Through youthful days and troubled years,
Through every failure, every tear,
You placed kind souls along my road,
To remind me You were always near.

Some prayed with me beneath the skies,
Some helped me find my way again.
Some stood beside me in my trials,
And helped me rise above the pain.

And now, when I have little left,
When tomorrow seems uncertain too,
You send a hand to lift my load,
And once again I think of You.

I cannot see the road ahead,
Nor know what waits beyond today.
But I have seen Your faithfulness,
And that is why I choose Your way.

So take these words, however small,
This grateful heart, this humble plea.
I cannot repay the love I've known,
But let my life reflect Your mercy.

Thank You, Father. Thank You, Lord.
For every blessing, great and small.
When gratitude leaves me without words,
My heart still knows You gave it all.
```

## 📜 **Scripture One — Psalm 107:1. Give Thanks for His Goodness.**

> "O give thanks unto the LORD, for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever."
>
> **Psalm 107:1 — KJV.**

### 💭 **Reflection.**

God's goodness is not determined by whether our circumstances are comfortable.

He remains good in seasons of abundance and in seasons of hardship.

Sometimes we become so focused on the problems in front of us that we forget the many occasions when God has already carried us through difficult circumstances.

We forget the prayers He has answered.

We forget the dangers from which we have been protected.

We forget the people He has placed in our lives.

And sometimes we forget to say thank You.

Gratitude is not merely a response to receiving something.

It is an acknowledgement of who God is.

### ❓ **Question for Today.**

*When last did I stop looking at my problems long enough to thank God for everything He has already done in my life?*

## 📜 **Scripture Two — 2 Corinthians 9:8. God's Provision.**

> "And God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work."
>
> **2 Corinthians 9:8 — KJV.**

### 💭 **Reflection.**

Paul reminds us that God's grace is sufficient and that He is able to provide what we need to fulfil the good works He places before us.

There is something important here.

God's provision is not always delivered in the way we expect.

Sometimes it comes through employment.

Sometimes through an opportunity.

Sometimes through an unexpected solution to a problem.

And sometimes through the generosity of another human being.

When somebody opens their heart and helps another person, that kindness can become an instrument through which God's provision reaches someone in need.

We should never underestimate what a willing heart can accomplish.

And when we are the recipients of kindness, we should never allow pride to prevent us from receiving it with gratitude.

There is no shame in needing help.

There is something beautiful about being able to accept a helping hand with humility.

### ❓ **Question for Today.**

*Am I willing to recognise God's provision, even when it arrives through an unexpected person or an unexpected circumstance?*

## 📜 **Scripture Three — Romans 8:38–39. Nothing Can Separate Us from His Love.**

> "For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come,

> Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

> **Romans 8:38–39 — KJV.**

### 💭 **Reflection.**

When I look back over almost five decades of faith, I see a journey that has not always been straightforward.

There have been wonderful experiences.

There have been difficult experiences.

There have been moments of spiritual growth, periods of uncertainty, mistakes, disappointments, and occasions when I needed to find my way back.

But through it all, God's love has remained.

Our journey may include mistakes, disappointments, periods of spiritual uncertainty, and even separation from a church community.

But God's love is not something we earn through perfect attendance or flawless behaviour.

His grace continually calls us back to Him.

We should never confuse our imperfections with God's inability to love us.

Nor should we confuse a difficult season with God's absence.

**God's faithfulness is greater than the difficulties of our journey.**

### ❓ **Question for Today.**

*Have I allowed the difficulties of my journey to make me forget the enduring love and faithfulness of God?*

## 🛠️ **Practical Tip — Remember the Hands That Helped You.**

Take a moment to reflect on the people God has placed along your journey.

- Who prayed with you when you were struggling?
- Who encouraged you when your faith was young?
- Who challenged you to return to God?
- Who helped you when you had nothing left to give?
- And whose life might God be asking you to encourage today?

You don't have to publish their names or announce their generosity.

Some acts of kindness are best honoured through private gratitude and sincere prayer.

Remember, too, that generosity is not measured only by the amount of money somebody gives.

A listening ear, a meal, a telephone call, a prayer, a lift, or a few words of encouragement can make an enormous difference.

**Never underestimate the value of a kindness offered at precisely the moment somebody needs it.**

## 🏃 **Daily Exercise — Become Someone Else's Blessing.**

Today's exercise is simple.

Think of one person who has made a difference in your life.

Pray for that person.

Thank God for placing them in your journey.

If appropriate, let them know that their kindness has not been forgotten.

Then ask yourself:

**What can I do today to become a blessing in somebody else's life?**

You do not need to have money to be generous.

You can give somebody your time.

You can offer encouragement.

You can listen.

You can pray.

You can forgive.

You can share what you have.

And sometimes, the greatest gift you can offer another person is simply letting them know that they are not alone.

## 🌅 **Heavenly View — God Sees the Entire Journey.**

We tend to remember our lives in chapters.

There are the happy chapters.

There are the painful chapters.

There are the chapters we would rather forget.

And there are those moments that we only understand years later.

But God sees the entire journey.

He sees the young person who received confirmation.

He sees the inexperienced believer trying to understand what faith really means.

He sees the soldier praying on guard duty.

He sees the person who nearly gave up, but heard the Holy Spirit speak.

He sees the mother and son entering the water together for baptism.

He sees the years of spiritual growth, the disappointments, and the returns.

And He sees the person sitting in front of a banking application, overwhelmed by an unexpected act of kindness.

Nothing in our journey is hidden from Him.

Sometimes we cannot understand why certain things happen.

Sometimes we cannot see how a difficult situation will resolve itself.

And sometimes, when an answer finally arrives, we struggle to find the words to express our gratitude.

Perhaps that is when we should remember that our relationship with God is not built on our ability to produce beautiful prayers.

It is built on His grace, His love, and our willingness to keep turning towards Him.

Today, I am reminded that the God who carried me through my younger years is still the God I can turn to today.

He has not changed.

And I have another reason to say:

**Thank You, Father.**

You do not need an eloquent prayer to express genuine gratitude.

You do not need a perfect vocabulary.

You do not need to explain every emotion.

Sometimes the most meaningful prayer is simply:

**"Thank You, Father."**

And when life becomes difficult again, remember the moments when God carried you through.

Remember the people who stood beside you.

Remember the prayers that were answered.

Remember His faithfulness.

**Almost five decades of grace. One more reason to be grateful. And another opportunity to pass that kindness on.**
