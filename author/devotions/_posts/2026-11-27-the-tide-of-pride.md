**📖 Key Scripture — Proverbs 16:18 (KJV).**

> "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall."

**💭 Today's Thought — The Rising Tide.**

There is perhaps nothing the human heart falls into as easily as pride, and yet there is no sin that Scripture condemns more frequently, more emphatically, and more eloquently.

Pride is a dangerous tide. It can rise quietly, almost unnoticed, until it begins to cover the very ground upon which we stand.

It convinces us that we are greater than we are, wiser than we are, and more deserving than those around us. It whispers that our accomplishments are entirely our own, that our knowledge makes us superior, and that our position places us above others.

But pride is a terrible foundation upon which to build a life.

Pride stands upon sand. Worse still, it puts its foot upon the waves of the sea, which give way beneath every step. And worse even than that, it stands upon bubbles that must soon burst beneath its feet.

Of all things, pride has perhaps the worst foothold.

It is unjustified, foolish, and inconstant. It promises elevation but prepares humiliation. It promises greatness but produces emptiness.

And when the tide finally withdraws, the proud discover that they have been standing upon nothing.

🌊 **The question we must ask ourselves is this: Are we building our lives upon the solid foundation of Christ, or are we attempting to stand upon the shifting waters of our own importance?**

**📜 1. Church History — The First Fall.**

Pride is not a modern invention. Scripture's account of human rebellion reaches back to the beginning, and the language of Isaiah 14:12–15 has long been read by Christians as reflecting the spiritual pattern of Satan's self-exaltation.

The immediate passage is a taunt against the king of Babylon. Its imagery describes a ruler brought low after aspiring to greatness. Across Christian history, many readers have also understood the passage as pointing beyond that earthly ruler to the pride and rebellion traditionally associated with Satan.

The five declarations are striking:

1. "I will ascend into heaven."
2. "I will exalt my throne above the stars of God."
3. "I will sit also upon the mount of the congregation."
4. "I will ascend above the heights of the clouds."
5. "I will be like the most High."

Five declarations. Five expressions of self-exaltation. Five attempts to place the self above the Almighty.

Yet the passage ends with the declaration that the one who sought to rise would be brought down (Isaiah 14:15).

The lesson is not that ambition, diligence, or using our God-given abilities is wrong. The danger is the desire to claim God's place, His glory, or authority that does not belong to us.

Human history repeatedly shows the damage that follows when people believe themselves beyond correction, accountability, or the authority of God.

Pride begins when the creature forgets the Creator.

⚔️ **No created being can rightfully claim the glory that belongs to the Creator.**

**📖 3. Three Scriptures for the Examination of Pride.**

**🔑 Scripture One — The Warning of a Fall.**

> "Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall."
>
> *Proverbs 16:18, King James Version (KJV).*

Pride makes us careless. When we believe that we cannot be wrong, we stop listening. When we believe that we cannot fall, we stop watching our steps.

The warning in Proverbs is not an invitation to live in fear. It is a call to humility, wisdom, and honest self-examination. We should never become so confident in ourselves that we forget our dependence upon God.

*💭 Reflection:* Have I become so certain of my own judgement that I no longer listen when someone offers wise correction?

**🔑 Scripture Two — What Have We That We Did Not Receive?**

> "For who maketh thee to differ from another? and what hast thou that thou didst not receive? now if thou didst receive it, why dost thou glory, as if thou hadst not received it?"
>
> *1 Corinthians 4:7, King James Version (KJV).*

Our abilities, opportunities, knowledge, strength, and resources are not reasons to look down upon others. They are things for which we should give thanks and which we should use responsibly.

There is nothing wrong with being pleased about a job well done. The problem begins when gratitude becomes superiority, or when we forget the grace and opportunities that helped us along the way.

*💭 Reflection:* Do I thank God for what I have received, or do I behave as though I achieved everything entirely by myself?

**🔑 Scripture Three — The Example of Christ.**

> "Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus:"
>
> *Philippians 2:5, King James Version (KJV).*

Paul goes on to describe Christ taking the form of a servant and humbling Himself in obedience, even unto death on the cross (Philippians 2:6–8).

The Son of God gives us the opposite example to self-exaltation. Jesus did not use His position to pursue selfish glory. He came to serve and to give Himself for us.

Humility is not weakness. It is the willingness to submit our lives to God, to serve others, and to place obedience above our own vanity.

*💭 Reflection:* When people encounter me, do they see something of the humility and servant-hearted character of Jesus?

**🛠️ 4. Practical Tip — Recognise Pride in Its Disguises.**

Pride does not always look like boasting. It can wear many different garments.

It can appear as:

- **Arrogance:** believing that our opinions are always superior.
- **Stubbornness:** refusing to admit that we are wrong.
- **Envy:** resenting another person's success or recognition.
- **Self-righteousness:** seeing everybody else's faults while excusing our own.
- **Attention-seeking:** needing constant praise or recognition.
- **False humility:** speaking about how humble we are in order to receive admiration.
- **Unforgiveness:** refusing to make peace because apologising feels beneath us.

Pride is not restricted to the wealthy, powerful, educated, or successful. It can be found in the beggar's rags as easily as in the rich man's garments. It can occupy any heart that places itself above others.

Try this simple practice:

1. 🙏 **Pause before reacting.** Ask whether your response is driven by truth or wounded pride.
2. 👂 **Listen to correction.** Consider whether there is something useful you need to hear.
3. ❤️ **Give credit.** Acknowledge the contribution and abilities of others.
4. 🤝 **Apologise when necessary.** Saying "I was wrong" is not defeat.
5. 🌱 **Practise gratitude.** Remember the people, opportunities, and grace that helped you.
6. ✝️ **Give God the glory.** Use your gifts as a faithful steward, not as a reason for superiority.

Humility does not require us to deny our abilities or pretend that we have achieved nothing. It asks us to recognise our gifts honestly, use them wisely, and never make them an excuse to consider ourselves better than another person.

**📝 5. Today's Exercise — Examine the Heart.**

Take a few quiet minutes today and ask yourself these questions:

- Do I find it difficult to admit when I am wrong?
- Do I look down upon people who have less than I do?
- Do I become resentful when somebody else receives recognition?
- Do I insist on having the last word?
- Do I believe that my knowledge makes me superior?
- Do I seek praise from people more than approval from God?
- Can I accept correction without becoming defensive?

Do not answer these questions on behalf of somebody else. Answer them honestly for yourself.

Choose one attitude that needs attention. Perhaps you need to apologise, listen more carefully, acknowledge another person's contribution, or stop comparing yourself with others.

Then pray about it and take one practical step.

This exercise is not intended to condemn us. It is intended to help us recognise what needs to change. Conviction should lead us towards repentance and God's grace, not towards hopelessness.

*The first step towards humility is being willing to admit that we still need to grow.*

**👑 6. The Heavenly View — What Does God See?**

We often measure ourselves by what we own, what we know, what we have achieved, or how much recognition we receive.

But God sees beyond outward appearances. He sees the heart and knows the motives behind our actions.

He sees whether we serve only when somebody is watching, whether we give only when praise is expected, and whether we treat people differently because of their wealth, position, education, or influence.

He also sees the quiet moments when we choose to listen instead of argue, apologise instead of defend our pride, and encourage another person instead of competing for attention.

These actions do not purchase salvation. We are saved by God's grace through faith, not by earning His favour. But a heart being transformed by grace should increasingly produce the fruit of humility, love, patience, and service.

James 4:6 reminds us:

> "God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble."
>
> *James 4:6, King James Version (KJV).*

What a sobering statement.

Let us not spend our lives trying to appear important before people while neglecting the condition of our hearts before God.

The applause of the world is temporary. Wealth changes hands. Positions disappear. Strength diminishes. Recognition fades.

But God's grace remains our hope.

Let us build our lives upon Jesus Christ, not upon the shifting waters of our own importance.

**🌿 7. Final Thought — When the Tide Goes Out.**

The tide of pride may carry a person to what appears to be great heights, but the tide eventually turns.

Wealth disappears. Positions change. Strength diminishes. Recognition fades. The applause of the world is temporary.

Pride offers no lasting foundation. It cannot save us, and it cannot place us above the God who made us.

Jesus Christ showed us another way: the way of humility, service, obedience, and love.

Let us not allow pride to become the tide that carries us away from God. Let us remain humble in our hearts, grateful in our lives, and obedient in our walk with Christ.

The greatest person is not the one who stands above everyone else. It is the one who understands that without God, we can do nothing.

**Let pride fall, so that Christ may rise within us.** ✝️
