_Even buying food is difficult when the nearest large stores are several kilometres away, and prices seem to rise constantly. Ordering online brings delivery fees on top of already expensive prices._

_Sometimes I do not even know what to say, Lord._

_I do not know what to do._

_I do not even want to tell people publicly when we are struggling with food or finances because it seems to be the same story again and again._

_So, Lord, I hand this over to You._

_Father God, please help us. Help everybody who is struggling._

_I know many people who are struggling. I think of James Kirby, Francois van Aswegen, and many others._

_Lord, please help us. Help everyone who is struggling._

_I ask this in Jesus' precious name._

_Amen._

**📖 Introduction — When the Fire Just Keeps Burning.**

There are times when trials and tribulations become so thick that you feel as though you are simply walking around the same mountain again and again.

You feel as though you have somehow undercut the mountain and now it is going to collapse on top of you.

The grooves become so deep that there seem to be sheer cliffs on either side.

There appears to be no way out unless God reaches down from heaven with His mighty hand and pulls you free.

We are often told that trials are good for us.

They build character.

They develop perseverance.

They teach us dependence upon God.

And Scripture certainly teaches us that God can use suffering to produce endurance and spiritual maturity.

But there is something we sometimes forget.

**Knowing that suffering can produce something good does not mean that suffering feels good.**

When the trial goes on for months, then years, and another problem arrives just when you thought you had finally reached solid ground, encouragement can begin to sound hollow.

You can quote Proverbs 3:5–6:

> “Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.”

And yet sometimes, when you are exhausted, those words can feel strangely distant.

You can remind yourself of Romans 8:1:

> “There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus.”

You know that you have been forgiven.

You know that Christ has paid the price.

You know that His blood has washed you clean.

And yet the circumstances around you can still hurt.

That is the difficult truth about faith.

**Faith does not mean that we never become discouraged.**

Faith means that even in our discouragement, we keep turning toward God.

There have been times when I have asked the Lord, “How much longer?”

There have even been moments when, exhausted by everything, I have cried out, “Lord, I am tired. I cannot carry this anymore.”

Perhaps you have prayed something similar.

If you have, you are not uniquely broken.

You are human.

And Scripture contains the cries of many people who reached the end of their own strength.

Elijah wanted to die.

David cried out from the depths of despair.

Job questioned everything around him.

Jeremiah wept.

Paul spoke openly about pressures that were beyond his own strength.

Yet their stories did not end with their despair.

And perhaps that is the message somebody needs this morning:

**The fact that you are exhausted does not mean that God has abandoned you.**

Sometimes perseverance looks heroic.

Sometimes perseverance looks like getting out of bed, making another cup of coffee, praying one more prayer, and saying:

**“Lord, I am still here. Please carry me.”**

**🏛️ Church History — Faithfulness in the Prison.**

John Bunyan, the English preacher and author of *The Pilgrim's Progress*, spent years imprisoned because of his refusal to stop preaching outside the restrictions imposed upon him.

His circumstances were not comfortable.

His imprisonment separated him from his family and placed severe limitations upon his life.

Yet from that difficult period came one of the most influential Christian allegories ever written.

Bunyan's circumstances did not magically become easy.

Instead, something remarkable happened **within** the hardship.

The prison became a place from which his faith and imagination continued to work.

His circumstances were restrictive, but his witness was not extinguished.

There is something important for us here.

Sometimes we pray:

**“Lord, get me out of this fire.”**

And sometimes God does.

But sometimes the immediate prayer God answers is:

**“Lord, keep me faithful while I am still in the fire.”**

We should never romanticise suffering.

But neither should we assume that suffering makes our lives meaningless.

God can still work in the place where we desperately wish we were somewhere else.

**💡 Practical Application — What Do I Do When I Cannot Fix Anything?**

Sometimes we make our spiritual lives unnecessarily complicated.

When everything is going wrong, we think we need some enormous act of faith.

Perhaps the next step is much smaller.

**1. Pray honestly.**

Do not manufacture a religious voice for God.

Tell Him where you hurt.

**2. Separate what you can control from what you cannot.**

You cannot control every person, company, bill, circumstance, or tomorrow.

But you can control the next faithful thing you do.

**3. Do the next right thing.**

Not the next twenty things.

Just the next one.

Make the phone call.

Wash the dishes.

Prepare the meal.

Send the message.

Read the Scripture.

Take the walk.

Rest when you need to rest.

Pray again.

**4. Refuse the lie that hardship equals worthlessness.**

Being poor does not make you worthless.

Being exhausted does not make you faithless.

Being confused does not mean God has abandoned you.

**5. Keep one promise of God in front of you.**

Today, let it be:

> **“The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart.”**

**🏃 Today's Exercise — The Five-Minute “Carry Me” Prayer.**

Find five quiet minutes today.

You do not need fancy words.

Sit somewhere quiet.

Take a slow breath.

Then pray:

**“Father, I cannot carry everything I am carrying. I give You what I cannot control. Give me strength for today. Give me wisdom for the next step. Keep my heart from bitterness. Keep my faith alive. And when I cannot hold on to You, hold on to me.”**

Then sit quietly for a moment.

Do not demand an immediate answer.

Simply remind yourself:

**God is here.**

**👑 God's View — The Fire Is Not the Whole Story.**

We see the fire.

God sees the person inside it.

We see today's empty cupboard.

God sees tomorrow.

We see the unanswered email.

God sees the entire story.

We see the mountain blocking the road.

God sees beyond the mountain.

That does not mean that every problem will immediately disappear.

It means that our present circumstances are not the complete story of our lives.

The Christian hope is larger than today's struggle.

And when we speak about the return of Christ and the difficult things Scripture teaches about the end times, we should remember something equally important:

**The Christian story does not end with persecution.**

It does not end with poverty.

It does not end with suffering.

It does not end with death.

It ends with Christ.

It ends with resurrection.

It ends with the defeat of death.

It ends with God's people dwelling with Him.

Whatever tomorrow brings, Christ remains the centre of our hope.

The prayer we nearly gave up praying.

The kindness we showed when we ourselves were struggling.

We may not understand the whole story now.

But one day, we will see more clearly.

Until then, we walk by faith.

Not because the road is easy.

Not because we understand everything.

But because **Jesus walks with us.**
