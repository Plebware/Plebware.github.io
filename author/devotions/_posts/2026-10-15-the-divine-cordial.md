## **🌿 Introduction — A Cordial for the Weary Soul.**

I am writing this special devotion in the hope that, by the blessing of God, it will lift despondent hearts and encourage readers who feel overwhelmed by trials and tribulations to look up with a more cheerful outlook.

Together with our brother Thomas Watson, I would prescribe that they take, now and then, a little of this cordial, this healthful spiritual tonic:

> *“And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.”* **Romans 8:28, KJV.**

There are seasons when life presses upon us from every direction. Financial difficulties, broken relationships, sickness, disappointment, loneliness, and the consequences of our own past decisions can leave us wondering how much more we are expected to endure.

Sometimes we pray and the answer seems delayed. Sometimes we encourage others with Scripture while quietly struggling to apply those same words to our own circumstances. Yet God's Word remains true even when our feelings fluctuate.

Thomas Watson's teaching reminds us that God is able to bring spiritual good out of suffering. This does not mean suffering is good in itself, that every painful event is punishment for a particular sin, or that every Christian will receive immediate earthly restoration.

Consider Job. He suffered profound loss, physical affliction, and the disappointment of friends who often misunderstood his circumstances. Job 42:10 records his restoration by the Lord, but his story must not be turned into a formula promising that every believer will experience the same earthly outcome.

Our hope is not that every storm will end the way we want it to. Our hope is that no storm can place the faithful child of God beyond the reach of His power, wisdom, goodness, and grace.

### **🔑 Practical tip.**

When suffering becomes overwhelming, do not demand that you understand the entire road ahead. Concentrate on the next faithful step.

### **📝 Practical to-do.**

Write down one burden weighing heavily upon your heart. Pray honestly about it and identify one practical action you can take today. If the burden is too heavy to carry alone, ask a trusted Christian brother or sister to stand with you.

## **❤️ A Personal Testimony — The Medicine I Also Need.**

There is something I want to make clear before I go any further. This devotion is not written only for the benefit of other people. **It is medicine for my own soul, too.**

Like many things I have done in life, including PlebMachine, this work began with a personal need. I created PlebMachine to serve me, to help me work more effectively with my computer, and to build an environment that suited the way I needed to work. I later recognised that what helped me might also prove useful to others.

This devotion has a similar beginning.

I have been going through trials and afflictions of my own. There have been difficult days, moments of discouragement, questions that remain unanswered, and circumstances that have tested my faith. I have needed encouragement. I have needed to return to Scripture. I have needed to remember God's strength, examine my heart, and remind myself that feeling overwhelmed does not mean that I have been abandoned by Him.

These struggles do not all come upon me at once. They come and go. Some days one concern weighs more heavily than another. Sometimes I find encouragement and peace, only to discover that a different difficulty needs my attention the next day.

So when I write about repentance, I am not merely giving somebody else an instruction. I, too, need to examine my life honestly before God. When I write about encouragement in trouble, I am reminding my own heart where to turn. When I write about God's strength, I am acknowledging that my own strength has limits. And when I write about feeling overwhelmed or fearing abandonment, I am addressing struggles that I know personally.

I do not write these words because I have mastered every lesson. I write them because I need to learn, practise, and return to them myself.

Perhaps that is one of the ways God uses the work of our hands. We begin by addressing a need in our own lives. As we learn, reflect, and build something useful, we may discover that the same work can encourage another person who is facing a similar difficulty.

I therefore offer this devotion honestly. It is written from within the struggle, not from a position of pretending that I have never struggled. I pray that the Lord will use it to help me grow in faith and that it will also bring encouragement to anyone else who reads it.

If you recognise yourself in these words, please know that you do not need to pretend to be stronger than you are. You can bring your real concerns before God, seek help when you need it, and take one faithful step at a time.

**I am writing this for myself, and I pray that it will be helpful to you, too.**

### **🔑 Practical tip.**

Do not wait until you have mastered a lesson before allowing God's Word to encourage you. Sometimes we need to hear and practise the same truth repeatedly.

### **📝 Practical to-do.**

Choose the one struggle that is most relevant to you today. Do not try to solve every issue at once. Find the corresponding Scripture in this devotion, read it slowly, and write down one action you can take in response.

## **🔥 Scripture One — God's Power Sustains Us in Weakness.**

**Scripture: 2 Corinthians 12:9, KJV.**

> *“My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness.”* **2 Corinthians 12:9.**

### **Reflection — The Bruised Reed That Still Stands.**

Thomas Watson teaches that God's glorious power works for the good of His people, particularly by supporting them in trouble.

Deuteronomy 33:27 speaks of the everlasting arms of God. Consider Daniel in the lions' den, Jonah in the belly of the great fish, and the three Hebrew men in the fiery furnace. These accounts remind us that human strength has limits, but God's power is not confined by those limits.

How can a believer continue to pray after disappointment, or encourage another person while carrying a heavy burden? The answer is not that Christians never become frightened, weary, or discouraged. It is that God's grace can sustain us when our own resources are insufficient.

Paul asked for relief from his thorn in the flesh. The answer he received was a promise of sufficient grace. His experience reminds us that God's help does not always take the form of immediate removal of the difficulty.

Watson also points to God's power to subdue sin, drawing upon Micah 7:19 and Psalm 74:14. Our struggles need not become excuses for surrendering to destructive habits. We can seek God's help, practise repentance, and take practical steps towards change.

God's grace does not forbid us from seeking practical assistance, either. Rest, wise counsel, medical attention when needed, and the support of fellow believers can all be part of receiving help.

### **🔑 Practical tip.**

Replace the question, “How will I survive everything ahead?” with the prayer, “Lord, give me the grace and strength I need for the next step.”

### **📝 Practical to-do.**

Name the difficulty that is draining your strength. Ask God for endurance and wisdom. Identify one burden you can share with someone trustworthy instead of carrying it alone.

### **☁️ Heavenly view — God's perspective.**

Our weakness does not surprise God. We may not know every reason He permits a trial, but we can depend upon His sufficient grace. Our weakness does not make His power inadequate.

**Question for reflection:** Where do I need to depend upon God's strength instead of pretending I can carry everything alone?

## **🕊️ Scripture Two — God's Wisdom Guides Us Through Affliction.**

**Scripture: Isaiah 9:6, KJV.**

> *“For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given: and the government shall be upon his shoulder: and his name shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, The mighty God, The everlasting Father, The Prince of Peace.”* **Isaiah 9:6.**

### **Reflection — When We Cannot See the Whole Picture.**

Thomas Watson reminds us that the God who possesses mighty power also possesses perfect wisdom. Isaiah's prophecy directs our attention to Christ, the Wonderful Counsellor.

When we suffer, we naturally want answers. Why did an opportunity disappear? Why has the financial pressure continued? Why does one prayer seem to receive an immediate answer while another remains unanswered?

The Bible does not require us to pretend that we understand what we cannot see. Job asked difficult questions, and the Psalms contain honest cries of distress. Questions are not automatically evidence of unbelief.

Sometimes hardship exposes habits that need to change, pride that needs to be humbled, or decisions that require greater care. Where we have done wrong, we should confess our sins and make amends where possible. But we must not assume that every hardship is punishment for a particular sin. Jesus challenged simplistic assumptions about the relationship between suffering and personal wrongdoing.

We can trust God's wisdom without claiming to know His secret purposes. We can remain teachable while taking responsibility for the decisions that are ours to make.

### **🔑 Practical tip.**

Separate what you know from what you fear or assume. Let Scripture guide your response instead of allowing uncertainty to become the final measure of God's character.

### **📝 Practical to-do.**

Make two short lists. Under “What I know,” record the facts and the biblical truths you can rely upon. Under “Where I need wisdom,” record the questions that still require prayer, advice, or further information. Then choose one wise next step.

### **☁️ Heavenly view — God's perspective.**

God sees more than we can see, but that does not give us permission to invent His reasons for every painful event. We can trust His wisdom without claiming to understand everything He permits.

**Question for reflection:** Am I allowing uncertainty to determine what I believe about God's character, or am I seeking His wisdom while acknowledging what I do not know?

## **🌱 Scripture Three — God's Goodness and Promises Nourish Our Faith.**

**Primary Scripture: Romans 8:28, KJV.**

> *“And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose.”* **Romans 8:28.**

**Supporting Scriptures:** Romans 2:4; 2 Peter 1:4; Hosea 14:4; Isaiah 44:3; Psalm 91:15; Psalm 37:39; Genesis 32:7, 12; John 10:29; Psalm 119:92.

### **Reflection — The Promises Are Food for Faith.**

Thomas Watson teaches that God's goodness works for the good of His people. Romans 2:4 reminds us that God's goodness and patience lead us towards repentance. His mercy is not an excuse to continue carelessly in sin; it is an invitation to turn towards Him.

Watson also describes God's promises as nourishment for faith. Faith draws strength from what God has revealed about His character and purposes.

When we struggle with sin, Hosea 14:4 speaks of God's willingness to heal the backsliding of His people. Isaiah 44:3 speaks of God pouring out His Spirit. Psalm 91:15 records the assurance of God's presence in trouble, while Psalm 37:39 identifies the Lord as the strength of His people in the time of trouble.

Consider Jacob in Genesis 32. He was afraid and distressed as he prepared to meet Esau. In verse 12, Jacob recalled God's promise to do him good. His circumstances had not yet become easy, but he brought God's promise into his prayer.

The promises are nourishment for faith, not a means of controlling God. They do not guarantee that every earthly difficulty will disappear. They remind us of what God has actually revealed, so that we can pray, obey, and endure with hope.

John 10:29 reminds believers of the security of Christ's sheep in the Father's hand. Psalm 119:92 records the psalmist's testimony that delight in God's law sustained him in affliction.

Romans 8:28 does not ask us to call evil good. It assures us that suffering cannot overturn God's ultimate purpose for those who love Him. The verses that follow, Romans 8:29–39, direct us towards becoming more like Christ and towards the assurance that nothing can separate believers from God's love in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Sometimes we recognise God's mercies only when we look back. Sometimes present circumstances remain painful, and we must continue trusting Him without a clear explanation.

### **🔑 Practical tip.**

Keep a small list of biblical promises that you have read in context and understand. Turn to them when discouraged instead of relying on vague assurances or promises Scripture does not make.

### **📝 Practical to-do — Choose the medicine your heart needs today.**

These are not six problems that you must solve at once. They are six places to turn when a particular struggle arises. The difficulties may come and go; return to the relevant passage when you need its guidance.

- **If you need repentance:** Read Hosea 14:1–4.
- **If you need encouragement in trouble:** Read Psalm 91:14–16.
- **If you need to remember God's strength:** Read Psalm 37:39–40.
- **If you feel overwhelmed by affliction:** Read Psalm 119:92.
- **If you fear that you have been abandoned:** Read John 10:27–30.
- **If you need to remember God's greater purpose:** Read Romans 8:28–39.

Read your chosen passage slowly. Write down what it actually promises, what it teaches you about God's character, and how you can respond faithfully today.

### **☁️ Heavenly view — God's perspective.**

God's goodness is not measured solely by financial circumstances, physical comfort, or immediate success. He cares about our spiritual growth, character, relationship with Him, and ultimate hope in Christ. Material needs remain real and may be brought honestly before Him while we seek practical solutions.

His promises are nourishment for the journey, not a formula for controlling the destination.

**Question for reflection:** Which promise of God do I need to return to today, and what faithful action follows from it?

## **📜 Church History — Thomas Watson and the Divine Cordial.**

Thomas Watson (c. 1620–1686) was an English Puritan preacher and Christian author who studied at Emmanuel College, Cambridge, and served as a minister at St. Stephen's, Walbrook, in London.

He became known for clear, memorable, and practical teaching on repentance, contentment, prayer, and the Christian life. His work commonly known as *All Things for Good* was originally published under the title *A Divine Cordial*, drawing its central teaching from Romans 8:28.

A cordial was historically understood as something intended to comfort or invigorate the heart. Watson used this image to describe the spiritual comfort Christians may receive by reflecting on God's providence and His purposes for those who love Him.

In 1662, following the Act of Uniformity, Watson was among the ministers who lost their official positions because they could not conform to the requirements imposed upon the Church of England. He continued to minister privately where possible.

His circumstances provide a sobering background to his teaching. Christian faithfulness does not always lead to immediate earthly comfort. Believers may endure exclusion, uncertainty, and hardship while seeking to remain faithful to Christ.

Watson's message is not that every affliction will be removed in this life. It is that suffering does not erase God's promises or determine the final meaning of the lives of those who belong to Him.

**Further reading:** [Thomas Watson — Christian Classics Ethereal Library](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/watson).

### **🔑 Practical tip.**

Learn from Christians of earlier generations, but do not imagine that their faith made them immune to grief or hardship.

## **✍️ Original Poem — The Cordial in the Furnace.**

\`\`\`
When morning breaks behind the cloud,
And sorrow speaks its words aloud,
When weary hearts can scarcely pray,
And hope seems distant as the day,

Remember, soul, though storms may rise,
God sees beyond our tear-filled eyes.
The night may hide the path ahead,
Yet still His Word gives daily bread.

The fire may burn, the silver ache,
And trembling hearts may bend and shake.
But pain itself is not our friend,
Nor does each wound bring quick amends.

Yet God can work where we see none,
And guide His children, one by one.
Through truth and grace, through prayer and tears,
He gives us strength to face our fears.

His promises, a steady light,
Can keep our hearts through darkest night.
Not every wish will He fulfil,
But He remains our refuge still.

So take this cordial, read and pray,
And trust the Lord for strength today.
For though we cannot see the end,
Our faithful God remains our Friend.

And when our earthly trials cease,
May Christ receive us into peace.
Till then, with faith and courage true,
May grace sustain us all the way through.
\`\`\`

## **🌅 Final Meditation — Take the Cordial With You.**

- **God's power can sustain you** when your own strength is failing.
- **God's wisdom can guide you** when you cannot understand your circumstances.
- **God's goodness can lead you** towards repentance, growth, and deeper dependence upon Him.
- **God's promises can nourish your faith** when the waters of distress threaten to overwhelm you.

Do not call suffering good merely because you are a Christian. Do not condemn yourself because you are weary. Do not assume that a delayed answer means that God has abandoned you.

Bring your pain honestly before Him. Receive the help He provides through His Word and His people. Take the next faithful step, even if you cannot yet see the destination.

The divine cordial is not a promise that the furnace will never burn. It is a reminder that the furnace does not place God's power, wisdom, goodness, or promises beyond our reach.

*Reflection — The Promises Are Food for Faith.*

Thomas Watson teaches that God's goodness works for the good of His people. Romans 2:4 reminds us that God's goodness and patience lead us towards repentance. His mercy is not an excuse to continue carelessly in sin; it is an invitation to turn towards Him.

Watson also describes God's promises as nourishment for faith. Faith draws strength from what God has revealed about His character and purposes.

When we struggle with sin, Hosea 14:4 speaks of God's willingness to heal the backsliding of His people. Isaiah 44:3 speaks of God pouring out His Spirit. Psalm 91:15 records the assurance of God's presence in trouble, while Psalm 37:39 identifies the Lord as the strength of His people in the time of trouble.

Consider Jacob in Genesis 32. He was afraid and distressed as he prepared to meet Esau. In verse 12, Jacob recalled God's promise to do him good. His circumstances had not yet become easy, but he brought God's promise into his prayer.

The promises are nourishment for faith, not a means of controlling God. They do not guarantee that every earthly difficulty will disappear. They remind us of what God has actually revealed, so that we can pray, obey, and endure with hope.

John 10:29 reminds believers of the security of Christ's sheep in the Father's hand. Psalm 119:92 records the psalmist's testimony that delight in God's law sustained him in affliction.

Romans 8:28 does not ask us to call evil good. It assures us that suffering cannot overturn God's ultimate purpose for those who love Him. The verses that follow, Romans 8:29–39, direct us towards becoming more like Christ and towards the assurance that nothing can separate believers from God's love in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Sometimes we recognise God's mercies only when we look back. Sometimes present circumstances remain painful, and we must continue trusting Him without a clear explanation.

📜 *CHURCH HISTORY — THOMAS WATSON AND THE DIVINE CORDIAL*

Thomas Watson (c. 1620–1686) was an English Puritan preacher and Christian author who studied at Emmanuel College, Cambridge, and served as a minister at St. Stephen's, Walbrook, in London.

He became known for clear, memorable, and practical teaching on repentance, contentment, prayer, and the Christian life. His work commonly known as *All Things for Good* was originally published under the title *A Divine Cordial*, drawing its central teaching from Romans 8:28.

A cordial was historically understood as something intended to comfort or invigorate the heart. Watson used this image to describe the spiritual comfort Christians may receive by reflecting on God's providence and His purposes for those who love Him.

In 1662, following the Act of Uniformity, Watson was among the ministers who lost their official positions because they could not conform to the requirements imposed upon the Church of England. He continued to minister privately where possible.

His circumstances provide a sobering background to his teaching. Christian faithfulness does not always lead to immediate earthly comfort. Believers may endure exclusion, uncertainty, and hardship while seeking to remain faithful to Christ.

Watson's message is not that every affliction will be removed in this life. It is that suffering does not erase God's promises or determine the final meaning of the lives of those who belong to Him.

✍️ *ORIGINAL POEM — THE CORDIAL IN THE FURNACE*

When morning breaks behind the cloud,
And sorrow speaks its words aloud,
When weary hearts can scarcely pray,
And hope seems distant as the day,

Remember, soul, though storms may rise,
God sees beyond our tear-filled eyes.
The night may hide the path ahead,
Yet still His Word gives daily bread.

The fire may burn, the silver ache,
And trembling hearts may bend and shake.
But pain itself is not our friend,
Nor does each wound bring quick amends.

Yet God can work where we see none,
And guide His children, one by one.
Through truth and grace, through prayer and tears,
He gives us strength to face our fears.

His promises, a steady light,
Can keep our hearts through darkest night.
Not every wish will He fulfil,
But He remains our refuge still.

So take this cordial, read and pray,
And trust the Lord for strength today.
For though we cannot see the end,
Our faithful God remains our Friend.

And when our earthly trials cease,
May Christ receive us into peace.
Till then, with faith and courage true,
May grace sustain us all the way through.

🌅 *FINAL MEDITATION — TAKE THE CORDIAL WITH YOU*

- God's power can sustain you when your own strength is failing.
- God's wisdom can guide you when you cannot understand your circumstances.
- God's goodness can lead you towards repentance, growth, and deeper dependence upon Him.
- God's promises can nourish your faith when the waters of distress threaten to overwhelm you.

Do not call suffering good merely because you are a Christian. Do not condemn yourself because you are weary. Do not assume that a delayed answer means that God has abandoned you.

Bring your pain honestly before Him. Receive the help He provides through His Word and His people. Take the next faithful step, even if you cannot yet see the destination.

The divine cordial is not a promise that the furnace will never burn. It is a reminder that the furnace does not place God's power, wisdom, goodness, or promises beyond our reach.
