# **🩸 When We Fail to Be Faithful.**

### **Part 2 — Recognising Our Failures and Finding Our Way Back to God.**

**By God's Journalist — Cody, Dei Scriptor.**

**Today's theme:** Faithfulness is not measured only by what we do when life is easy. It is also revealed in how we respond when life becomes difficult.

**Key Scripture:** Luke 16:10.

## **🙏 1. Opening Prayer.**

_Heavenly Father,

We come before You today with humble hearts. Lord, we so dearly want to be faithful to You, yet time and again we find ourselves falling short.

Sometimes we fail in the little things. Sometimes fear gets the better of us. Sometimes the distractions of this world take our attention away from what You have called us to do.

It is distressing when we recognise our failures, particularly when we find ourselves repeating the same mistakes. We ask for Your forgiveness, but we also ask for wisdom. Teach us to recognise our shortcomings and to take practical steps towards overcoming them.

Help us to keep short accounts with You. When we stumble, help us to return quickly rather than allowing guilt and condemnation to keep us away from Your presence.

Father, teach us what genuine faithfulness looks like. Give us courage when we are afraid, discernment when we are confused, and strength when we are tempted to turn away.

We ask this in the precious name of Jesus Christ.

Amen._

## **🌅 2. Today's Thought — When Good Intentions Are Not Enough.**

Good morning, brothers and sisters.

Just recently, we explored why faithfulness is so important in our Christian walk. Today, I want us to examine the other side of that subject.

What happens when we fail to be faithful?

It is relatively easy to talk about faithfulness when everything is going well. It is easy to promise God that we will serve Him, help others, read His Word and remain obedient.

But life has a way of testing those promises.

Sometimes we recognise somebody who needs help, but because we have seen the same situation repeatedly, we become indifferent.

Sometimes we know what is right, but fear prevents us from doing it.

And sometimes we become so distracted by everyday life that we gradually lose sight of the very things God has asked us to prioritise.

The uncomfortable truth is that we can genuinely love God and still struggle with faithfulness.

Recognising that struggle is not the end of our journey. It can be the beginning of repentance, correction and renewed commitment.

Today, let us examine three areas where unfaithfulness can quietly enter our lives: the small things, fear, and distractions.

## **❤️ 3. Part One — Unfaithfulness in the Small Things.**

Have you ever noticed how easy it is to be faithful when something is important, urgent or extraordinary?

But what about the little things?

Imagine walking down the same street and seeing the same person lying intoxicated on the pavement.

The first time, you might stop and ask whether they need assistance. The second time, you might feel compassion. But by the tenth time, something can change.

You become accustomed to the situation.

You begin thinking:

"Here we go again."

"Somebody else can help."

"There's nothing I can do."

And eventually, you simply walk past.

This is where we need to examine our hearts.

It is easy to show compassion once. It is considerably harder to maintain compassion when the situation becomes repetitive.

Yet God does not measure our faithfulness only by the extraordinary things we accomplish. He also sees what we do when nobody is watching, when there is no recognition, and when the opportunity to help seems insignificant.

We must remember that compassion does not require us to solve every problem. If somebody is unconscious or seriously unwell, the right response may be to contact emergency services. If a situation is unsafe, we should seek appropriate assistance rather than putting ourselves in danger.

But we should not allow familiarity with somebody's suffering to destroy our humanity.

**Practical Tip. 🔑**

Do not allow repeated exposure to another person's suffering to make you indifferent. You may not be able to change their circumstances, but you can still treat them with dignity.

**Practical To-Do. 📝**

The next time you encounter somebody in need:

- Stop and assess whether they require immediate assistance.
- Offer practical help where it is safe and appropriate.
- Contact emergency services if someone is unconscious or seriously unwell.
- If you cannot help personally, consider who might be able to.
- Pray for that person, even when you cannot change their circumstances.

Helping somebody does not always mean giving them money. Sometimes it means making a telephone call, finding assistance, offering food, or simply treating them as a human being.

**How Does God See This? 👑**

God sees the heart behind our actions. He knows when we are genuinely unable to help, and He knows when we simply cannot be bothered.

We should never confuse our limitations with a lack of faithfulness.

God does not expect us to solve every problem in the world. He does call us to remain compassionate.

**Faithfulness is not about doing everything. It is about not neglecting what God has placed within our reach.**

## **🛡️ 4. Part Two — When Fear Makes Us Unfaithful.**

This is perhaps one of the most difficult areas of faithfulness.

Sometimes we know what we should do, but we are frightened of the consequences.

Look at the circumstances surrounding many communities today.

We hear about young women being kidnapped, abused or threatened. Some people find the courage to report what has happened, only to face further intimidation and uncertainty.

We see marriages deteriorating into verbal, emotional and psychological abuse.

We hear gunshots during the night. People go missing. Drugs are sold openly. Violence becomes so familiar that we almost begin accepting it as part of everyday life.

And what happens to us?

We become frightened.

We begin thinking:

"I don't want to get involved."

"I don't want these people coming after me."

"What if I become the next victim?"

And so we remain silent.

Now, let us be clear about something: there is a difference between being fearful and exercising wisdom.

God does not require us to confront dangerous people, challenge gang members or put our families at risk to prove our faithfulness.

Sometimes the wisest and most faithful action is to report something through the proper channels, seek assistance, protect a vulnerable person or allow trained people to intervene.

But we must be careful that fear does not become an excuse for abandoning compassion altogether.

We cannot allow the darkness around us to convince us that doing nothing is always the safest or easiest answer.

**Practical Tip. 🔑**

Do not mistake courage for confrontation. You can be faithful without placing yourself in unnecessary danger.

**Practical To-Do. 📝**

When confronted with a dangerous or distressing situation:

- Pray for wisdom before acting.
- Do not confront violent or potentially dangerous individuals.
- Report serious crimes through appropriate authorities.
- Support victims without judging or blaming them.
- Help people identify safe places and appropriate support services.
- Remember that protecting your own family is also a responsibility God has entrusted to you.

**How Does God See This? 👑**

God understands our fears.

Remember that even the disciples became frightened. Peter denied Jesus three times because he was afraid.

Yet Jesus did not abandon Peter. He restored him and entrusted him with responsibility again.

Our fear does not have to become our permanent identity. We can acknowledge it, bring it before God and ask Him to teach us how to respond differently.

**God does not ask us to be reckless. He asks us to trust Him enough not to let fear become our master.**

## **📵 5. Part Three — Unfaithfulness Through Distractions.**

We live in a world that constantly competes for our attention.

There is always something demanding our time.

Work. Money. Entertainment. Social media. Television. YouTube. Games. News. Arguments. Endless conversations.

Some of these things are perfectly harmless in themselves. The problem begins when they take priority over the things that matter most.

We can become so occupied with making a living that we forget to make time for God.

We can spend hours watching videos, following controversies and absorbing information that does nothing to strengthen our relationship with Christ.

And there is another danger.

We live in an age where artificial intelligence can create convincing images, videos and voices. Deepfakes can make something completely false appear genuine.

If we are not careful, we can spend our time consuming misinformation, becoming angry about things that never happened and believing stories that have no foundation in truth.

Our spiritual discernment becomes clouded.

The answer is not to reject technology or entertainment altogether. It is to recognise when these things are taking us away from God.

We need to return to His Word.

And I would encourage us not merely to read the Bible once in the morning and then forget about it for the remainder of the day.

Why not return to Scripture throughout the day?

When you are worried, read.

When you are discouraged, read.

When you are confused, read.

When you are tempted, read.

When you feel spiritually empty, return to the Word of God.

**Practical Tip. 🔑**

Do not allow your Bible to become something you only open when you attend church. Make God's Word part of your everyday decision-making.

**Practical To-Do. 📝**

Try establishing three moments of spiritual reflection each day:

- **Morning:** Begin with Scripture and prayer before allowing the day's demands to take over.
- **Midday:** Take a few minutes to revisit a passage or reflect on something you have read.
- **Evening:** Examine your day. Where were you faithful? Where did you fall short? What do you need to bring before God?

Also, become more discerning about what you watch and share. Ask yourself:

"Is this helping me grow in wisdom, or is it simply consuming my attention?"

**How Does God See This? 👑**

God wants our hearts, not merely the spare minutes left over after everything else has received our attention.

He knows we have responsibilities. He knows we need rest. He knows we live in a demanding world.

But He also knows when we are gradually allowing everything else to become more important than Him.

**Faithfulness requires us to make room for God, rather than expecting God to fit into whatever time we have left.**

## **🏛️ 6. Church History — Peter: From Denial to Restoration.**

There is a particularly powerful example of human unfaithfulness in the biblical history of the early Church.

It is the story of the Apostle Peter.

Peter was one of the closest disciples of Jesus. He had witnessed miracles, walked with Christ and openly declared his willingness to follow Him, even if it meant dying.

Yet when Jesus was arrested, Peter became frightened.

Three times he denied knowing Jesus.

Think about that for a moment.

This was not somebody who had never known Christ. This was a disciple who loved Jesus, but whose courage failed him when he faced the possibility of suffering.

Afterwards, Peter wept bitterly.

But his story did not end with his failure.

After the resurrection, Jesus met Peter and restored him. In John 21, Jesus asked Peter three times whether he loved Him, and entrusted him with the responsibility to care for His people.

Peter went on to become one of the prominent leaders of the early Christian Church. In Acts 2, we read of Peter standing and proclaiming the Gospel at Pentecost.

His failure became part of his testimony, not the end of his calling.

**The Lesson. 🕊️**

Read John 18:15–27 and John 21:15–19.

Peter teaches us something extraordinarily important: we can fail God without being beyond restoration.

We can become frightened, make mistakes, deny our convictions and disappoint ourselves. But genuine repentance opens the way to restoration.

**Our past failures do not have to determine our future faithfulness.**

>
> **Luke 16:10 (KJV).**

Jesus reminds us that faithfulness is revealed in the small responsibilities entrusted to us.

It is easy to make grand promises. It is more difficult to remain patient, honest, compassionate and dependable in the ordinary moments of life.

The little things are not insignificant to God. They reveal the condition of our hearts and help shape the people we become.

Am I overlooking a small responsibility or opportunity to show kindness because I have convinced myself that it does not matter?

## **🛡️ 9. Scripture Two — Courage, Love and a Sound Mind.**

> "For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind."
>
> **2 Timothy 1:7 (KJV).**

**Reflection.**

Paul's words to Timothy remind us that Christian courage is not recklessness.

Notice the three qualities in this verse: power, love and a sound mind.

We need courage to do what is right, love to keep our hearts compassionate, and a sound mind to recognise the safest and wisest way to act.

Fear may be a genuine human emotion, but it need not control every decision we make.

**Question for Today.**

Is fear preventing me from doing something good that I could safely and wisely do?

## **🔑 11. Practical Application — Keep Short Accounts With God.**

What happens when we repeatedly fail?

Sometimes we do something we know is wrong. We feel guilty. We ask God for forgiveness. Then, perhaps a few days later, we find ourselves making the same mistake again.

Eventually, we begin wondering whether God will ever forgive us.

This is where the enemy can use our guilt against us. He wants us to believe that because we have failed repeatedly, there is no point in trying anymore.

But Scripture does not teach that a repentant person should surrender to despair.

First John 1:9 reminds us that God is faithful and just to forgive those who confess their sins. Romans 8:1 reminds believers that there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.

This does not mean that forgiveness is permission to continue deliberately doing what is wrong. Genuine repentance means turning back to God, learning from our failures and seeking His help to change.

Keeping short accounts with God means that when we recognise a failure, we do not hide from Him.

We confess it.

We seek forgiveness.

We examine what led to it.

And we ask for help to respond differently next time.

## **📝 12. A Daily Exercise — Three Questions Before God.**

At the end of each day, take a few quiet minutes and ask yourself:

1. Where was I faithful to God today?
2. Where did I fall short?
3. What can I do differently tomorrow?

Do not use these questions to condemn yourself.

Use them to grow.

If you recognise a failure, confess it honestly. If somebody has been hurt, consider what responsible steps you can take to make things right. If you need help changing a habit, ask a trusted Christian, counsellor or appropriate professional for support.

God is interested in the transformation of your heart, not in watching you drown in yesterday's mistakes.

## **👑 13. The Heavenly View — What Does God See?**

God sees more than the outward action. He sees the heart, the intention, the struggle and the circumstances surrounding it.

He knows when we are genuinely unable to help. He knows when we are frightened. He knows when we are exhausted and when our attention has wandered.

That does not make faithfulness unimportant. It reminds us that God calls us to grow through grace, truth and obedience rather than pretending to be perfect.

We are not the Saviour of the world. Jesus Christ is.

We are His servants. We are called to love, encourage, pray, support, speak truth and help where we can.

We cannot rescue everyone. We cannot solve every broken marriage, stop every act of violence or provide for every hungry family.

But we can refuse to become indifferent.

We can refuse to allow fear to destroy our compassion.

We can refuse to let distractions take God's place.

And when we fail, we can return to Him.

**Do not measure your faithfulness only by the big things you do for God. Examine the little things you repeatedly overlook.**

Sometimes the greatest opportunity to demonstrate God's love is standing right in front of us.

And sometimes, all God asks is that we do not walk past.

"Is this helping me grow in wisdom, or is it simply consuming my attention?"

*How Does God See This? 👑*

God wants our hearts, not merely the spare minutes left over after everything else has received our attention.

He knows we have responsibilities. He knows we need rest. He knows we live in a demanding world.

But He also knows when we are gradually allowing everything else to become more important than Him.

*Faithfulness requires us to make room for God, rather than expecting God to fit into whatever time we have left.*

🏛️ *6. CHURCH HISTORY — PETER: FROM DENIAL TO RESTORATION.*

There is a particularly powerful example of human unfaithfulness in the biblical history of the early Church.

It is the story of the Apostle Peter.

Peter was one of the closest disciples of Jesus. He had witnessed miracles, walked with Christ and openly declared his willingness to follow Him, even if it meant dying.

Yet when Jesus was arrested, Peter became frightened.

Three times he denied knowing Jesus.

Think about that for a moment.

This was not somebody who had never known Christ. This was a disciple who loved Jesus, but whose courage failed him when he faced the possibility of suffering.

Afterwards, Peter wept bitterly.

But his story did not end with his failure.

After the resurrection, Jesus met Peter and restored him. In John 21, Jesus asked Peter three times whether he loved Him, and entrusted him with the responsibility to care for His people.

Peter went on to become one of the prominent leaders of the early Christian Church. In Acts 2, we read of Peter standing and proclaiming the Gospel at Pentecost.

His failure became part of his testimony, not the end of his calling.

*The Lesson. 🕊️*

Read John 18:15–27 and John 21:15–19.

Peter teaches us something extraordinarily important: we can fail God without being beyond restoration.

We can become frightened, make mistakes, deny our convictions and disappoint ourselves. But genuine repentance opens the way to restoration.

*Our past failures do not have to determine our future faithfulness.*

🔑 *11. PRACTICAL APPLICATION — KEEP SHORT ACCOUNTS WITH GOD.*

What happens when we repeatedly fail?

Sometimes we do something we know is wrong. We feel guilty. We ask God for forgiveness. Then, perhaps a few days later, we find ourselves making the same mistake again.

Eventually, we begin wondering whether God will ever forgive us.

This is where the enemy can use our guilt against us. He wants us to believe that because we have failed repeatedly, there is no point in trying anymore.

But Scripture does not teach that a repentant person should surrender to despair.

First John 1:9 reminds us that God is faithful and just to forgive those who confess their sins. Romans 8:1 reminds believers that there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.

This does not mean that forgiveness is permission to continue deliberately doing what is wrong. Genuine repentance means turning back to God, learning from our failures and seeking His help to change.

Keeping short accounts with God means that when we recognise a failure, we do not hide from Him.

We confess it.

We seek forgiveness.

We examine what led to it.

And we ask for help to respond differently next time.

At the end of each day, take a few quiet minutes and ask yourself:

1. Where was I faithful to God today?
2. Where did I fall short?
3. What can I do differently tomorrow?

👑 *13. THE HEAVENLY VIEW — WHAT DOES GOD SEE?*

God sees more than the outward action. He sees the heart, the intention, the struggle and the circumstances surrounding it.

He knows when we are genuinely unable to help. He knows when we are frightened. He knows when we are exhausted and when our attention has wandered.

That does not make faithfulness unimportant. It reminds us that God calls us to grow through grace, truth and obedience rather than pretending to be perfect.

We are not the Saviour of the world. Jesus Christ is.

We are His servants. We are called to love, encourage, pray, support, speak truth and help where we can.

We cannot rescue everyone. We cannot solve every broken marriage, stop every act of violence or provide for every hungry family.

But we can refuse to become indifferent.

We can refuse to allow fear to destroy our compassion.

We can refuse to let distractions take God's place.

And when we fail, we can return to Him.

*Do not measure your faithfulness only by the big things you do for God. Examine the little things you repeatedly overlook.*

Sometimes the greatest opportunity to demonstrate God's love is standing right in front of us.

And sometimes, all God asks is that we do not walk past.
