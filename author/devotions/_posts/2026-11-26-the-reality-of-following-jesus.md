**❤️ 2. Today's Thought: Knowing Jesus and Making Him Known!**

The reality of following Jesus is about two fundamental things: **knowing Jesus and making Him known to others.**

But before we can genuinely understand what it means to follow Jesus, we need to understand the cost of discipleship.

Jesus never promised that following Him would make our lives free from difficulties, criticism or opposition. In fact, He warned His disciples that the world would not always welcome them.

When we become Christians, we may experience persecution. Not necessarily physical persecution or persecution unto death, but sometimes something equally difficult to deal with emotionally.

People may ridicule us, mock our beliefs, question our integrity, accuse us of being dishonest, or tell us that we do not know what we are talking about. Some may even reject us simply because we choose to live according to the teachings of Jesus.

That is part of the reality of discipleship.

### 🪢 Understanding the Yoke of Jesus.

Jesus gave us a wonderful analogy when He spoke about His yoke.

> "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."
>
> "Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls."
>
> "For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."
>
> — Matthew 11:28–30 (KJV).

This does not mean that following Jesus is without responsibility, sacrifice or hardship.

A yoke represents submission, partnership and direction. When we take the yoke of Christ upon ourselves, we willingly submit to His authority and allow Him to guide our lives.

The burden is light because we are not expected to carry life's difficulties alone. We have His grace, His guidance and His presence.

We are not promised an easy road, but we are promised that we do not have to walk it alone.

### ✝️ Assurance of Salvation.

Before we can confidently profess our faith, we must understand what salvation means.

Have we genuinely accepted Jesus Christ as our Lord and Saviour? Do we understand that salvation is a gift of God's grace, received through faith?

Romans 10 reminds us of the importance of believing in our hearts and confessing Jesus as Lord.

For someone who is uncertain about their salvation, revisiting the sinner's prayer can be a meaningful opportunity to reaffirm their faith and commitment to Christ.

However, salvation does not depend on repeating particular words or performing a religious ritual. Our assurance rests in Jesus Christ, His sacrifice and the promises of God's Word.

### 💧 Understanding Water Baptism.

Water baptism is another important part of our Christian journey.

It is not simply a religious ceremony where somebody plunges us into water, brings us out again, and declares that we are suddenly a different person.

There is a much deeper meaning.

Baptism represents our identification with the death, burial and resurrection of Jesus Christ.

As we go beneath the water, we symbolically identify with the death and burial of our old life. As we emerge from the water, we proclaim our identification with Christ's resurrection and our new life in Him.

It is a public declaration of faith and commitment.

Baptism is not a substitute for faith, nor is the physical act of entering water a guarantee of salvation. It points us towards the spiritual reality of belonging to Christ.

### 👣 Walking the Talk!

Perhaps one of the greatest responsibilities of being a Christian is remaining faithful to the teachings of Jesus.

It is pointless calling ourselves Christians if we cannot love our neighbours.

It is pointless attending church every Sunday, singing worship songs, raising our hands in praise and appearing to be wonderful Christians, only to walk out of the church doors and return to living entirely according to worldly attitudes.

Our Christianity must extend beyond the church building.

We need to become responsible examples of the faith we profess.

Our salvation is not based upon our works. We are saved by grace through faith. But our works are among the most powerful testimonies of what God is doing in our lives.

People watch how we conduct ourselves. They notice whether we are honest, whether we forgive, whether we show compassion, whether we keep our promises and whether we treat others with dignity.

We must be good stewards of the things God entrusts to us. We must be obedient servants, willing to make sacrifices and prepared to put the needs of others before our own comfort.

And perhaps two of the most important characteristics we need to develop are these:

**Be quick to forgive and slow to anger.**

Forgiveness does not mean approving of wrongdoing, tolerating abuse or abandoning healthy boundaries. It means refusing to allow bitterness and hatred to rule our hearts.

We must also remember those who are less fortunate than ourselves and pray for the needy.

### 🤝 A Lesson From an Unexpected Encounter.

While preparing this devotion, a gentleman named Glenn approached me.

He explained that he was living on the streets and asked whether I could help him with some money for food.

Now, I am not exactly in a position of financial abundance myself. I have my own struggles and responsibilities.

But his situation made me reflect on something.

How often do we encounter people who are struggling and immediately begin judging them? How often do we look at somebody's circumstances without knowing anything about their story?

Being a Christian does not mean we must have money to give everybody who asks for help. Neither does it mean we can solve every person's problems.

But we can still show compassion.

We can say:

"Brother, I am struggling financially myself, and I don't have cash to give you. But I will pray for you. Don't lose hope. Keep believing that things can change."

We can listen. We can encourage. We can treat somebody with dignity.

Sometimes a kind word, a little encouragement or a sincere prayer can mean more than we realise.

And when we are able to provide practical assistance, we should do so responsibly and with compassion.

**The point is not how much we have to give. The point is whether we have a heart willing to care.**

### 🔥 The Reality of Discipleship.

Following Jesus is a daily commitment.

It means choosing truth when dishonesty would be easier. It means choosing forgiveness when anger feels justified. It means showing kindness when others show hostility.

It means being faithful when nobody is watching.

It means living our Christianity rather than merely advertising it.

We must never forget that we are representatives of Christ in this world.

Our words should reflect His teachings. Our conduct should demonstrate His love. Our decisions should reflect our commitment to His Kingdom.

**We need to walk the talk, not talk the walk.**

That is the reality of following Jesus.

**⛪ 3. A Little Church History: The Cost of Following Christ!**

The early Christian Church was not built upon popularity, wealth or social acceptance.

It was built upon the testimony of ordinary men and women who believed in Jesus Christ and were prepared to stand firm in their faith.

In the first century, Christians faced suspicion, ridicule, discrimination and, at different times and places, severe persecution under Roman authorities.

The early believers gathered together, shared what they had, supported one another and continued proclaiming the Gospel despite opposition.

The disciples themselves understood the cost of following Jesus. Many suffered imprisonment, rejection and persecution. Some early Christians were put to death for refusing to abandon their faith.

Yet the message continued spreading.

One of the most significant historical developments was the growth of the Christian community in Antioch, where Acts 11:26 tells us that the disciples were first called Christians.

The name Christian became associated with people who followed the teachings of Jesus Christ.

As the Church developed, baptism became an important public expression of Christian identity and commitment. New believers were taught the faith, prepared for baptism and welcomed into the Christian community.

Throughout Church history, different Christian traditions have understood the sacramental meaning of baptism in somewhat different ways, but its connection with Christ and the Christian life has remained central.

The Methodist tradition emphasises God's grace, personal faith, spiritual growth and the importance of living out our faith through love and service.

John Wesley, one of the principal founders of Methodism, strongly emphasised practical holiness. He believed that genuine faith should produce visible changes in how we live, how we treat others and how we serve God.

This is an important lesson for Christians today.

We have inherited a faith that was preserved through the courage, sacrifice and commitment of generations before us.

We should never treat the privilege of following Jesus as something casual or insignificant.

**We are not simply members of a religious organisation. We are disciples of Jesus Christ, called to live according to His teachings.**

**📖 5. Seven Scriptures for a Christ-Centred Life!**

### 1. 🪢 The Yoke of Christ — Matthew 11:28–30.

> "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."
>
> "Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls."
>
> "For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."
>
> — Matthew 11:28–30 (KJV).

**Reflection:** Jesus does not promise that our lives will be free from difficulties. He promises that we can find rest in Him.

Following Christ requires submission, obedience and trust. But we are not expected to carry our burdens alone. We must learn to surrender our worries and allow Jesus to guide our decisions.

**Question:** Am I allowing Jesus to guide my life, or am I still trying to carry everything by myself?

### 2. ✝️ The Cost of Discipleship — Luke 9:23–24.

> "If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me."
>
> "For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: but whosoever will lose his life for my sake, the same shall save it."
>
> — Luke 9:23–24 (KJV).

**Reflection:** Following Jesus is not a once-off declaration. It is a daily decision.

Taking up our cross means putting aside selfish ambitions and choosing obedience to Christ, even when doing so requires sacrifice. We must be prepared to stand firm in our faith, even when others disagree with us.

**Question:** Am I prepared to follow Jesus when doing so becomes uncomfortable or unpopular?

### 3. 🛡️ Expecting Opposition — John 15:18–20.

> "If the world hate you, ye know that it hated me before it hated you."
>
> "If ye were of the world, the world would love his own: but because ye are not of the world, but I have chosen you out of the world, therefore the world hateth you."
>
> "Remember the word that I said unto you, The servant is not greater than his lord."
>
> — John 15:18–20 (KJV).

**Reflection:** Jesus warned His disciples that they would encounter opposition.

Not everyone will understand our beliefs or respect our decisions. We should not respond to ridicule with hostility. We must remain respectful, truthful and humble, remembering that our responsibility is to represent Christ faithfully.

**Question:** When people challenge or ridicule my faith, do I respond with Christlike character?

### 4. ❤️ Assurance of Salvation — Romans 10:9–13.

> "That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved."
>
> "For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved."
>
> — Romans 10:9, 13 (KJV).

**Reflection:** Our salvation rests in Jesus Christ.

We cannot earn it through good deeds, religious ceremonies or personal achievements. We receive salvation through faith in Christ and His finished work.

We should examine our hearts, understand what we believe and find confidence in God's promises. Our assurance is not dependent upon how perfectly we perform, but upon the faithfulness of the Saviour in whom we trust.

**Question:** Do I genuinely understand and trust in the salvation that Jesus offers?

### 5. 💧 Dying and Rising With Christ — Romans 6:3–4.

> "Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death?"
>
> "Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life."
>
> — Romans 6:3–4 (KJV).

**Reflection:** Baptism represents our identification with the death and resurrection of Jesus.

It reminds us that our old way of living must give way to a new life in Christ. We are called to leave behind sinful attitudes and pursue a life of obedience, love and spiritual growth.

Baptism is a declaration of our faith, not a substitute for the grace of God.

**Question:** Does my daily life reflect the newness of life that I profess through Christ?

### 6. 🤲 Faith Demonstrated Through Works — James 2:17–18.

> "Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone."
>
> "Yea, a man may say, Thou hast faith, and I have works: shew me thy faith without thy works, and I will shew thee my faith by my works."
>
> — James 2:17–18 (KJV).

**Reflection:** James reminds us that genuine faith should produce genuine action.

We are not saved by our works, but our actions should demonstrate the faith we profess. A Christian who speaks about love but continually mistreats others is not demonstrating the character of Christ.

Our honesty, compassion, generosity, forgiveness and willingness to serve should reflect our relationship with God.

**Question:** If somebody examined my actions rather than listened to my words, would they recognise my Christian faith?

### 7. 🕊️ Forgiveness and Mercy — Matthew 18:21–22.

> "Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times?"
>
> "Jesus saith unto him, I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven."
>
> — Matthew 18:21–22 (KJV).

**Reflection:** Jesus teaches us that forgiveness must not be limited by a convenient number.

We should not keep a record of every offence or allow bitterness to become part of our character.

Forgiveness is not always easy. Some wounds take time to heal, and forgiveness does not require us to remain in harmful situations.

Nevertheless, we must continually seek a heart that is willing to forgive, just as we depend upon God's forgiveness.

**Question:** Is there somebody I am still holding resentment against, whom I need to begin forgiving?

**💡 6. Practical Tip: Let Your Actions Speak!**

One of the greatest mistakes we can make as Christians is believing that our faith is measured by how religious we appear.

Attending church is important. Reading Scripture is important. Praying and worshipping are important.

But our faith must also be visible in our everyday conduct.

Here are five practical ways to demonstrate Christ-centred living:

1. ❤️ **Practise compassion.** Treat people with dignity, regardless of their financial position, background or circumstances.
2. 🤝 **Forgive deliberately.** Do not allow anger, resentment and bitterness to become permanent residents in your heart.
3. 🗣️ **Speak truthfully.** Be honest in your dealings, keep your promises and avoid gossip or unnecessary criticism.
4. 🙏 **Pray for others.** Remember the sick, the homeless, the unemployed, the lonely and those facing difficulties.
5. 🌱 **Be a good steward.** Take responsibility for your time, finances, talents, relationships and the opportunities God has entrusted to you.

Remember, we cannot help everybody, but we can make a difference in somebody's life.

**👣 7. Practical Application: Becoming a Living Testimony!**

Today, I want us to consider something very simple.

Imagine somebody who does not know us personally is observing our behaviour.

They do not know which church we attend. They have never heard us preach. They do not know how many Bible verses we can quote.

All they can see is how we behave.

Would they recognise something different about us?

Would they see honesty, patience, kindness, forgiveness and compassion?

Would they see somebody who is prepared to help another person, even when there is nothing to gain?

Would they see somebody who remains faithful to their principles when nobody is watching?

This is where Christianity becomes real.

**Our lives should be a reflection of the teachings of Jesus.**

We must remember that we are imperfect human beings. We will make mistakes, lose our tempers and sometimes fall short.

But being a Christian means being willing to acknowledge our shortcomings, repent, seek forgiveness and continue growing in Christ.

We do not demonstrate Christianity by pretending to be perfect.

We demonstrate it by allowing God to work in our imperfect lives.

**👑 9. The Heavenly View: What Does God See in Our Lives?**

Imagine looking at your life from the perspective of eternity.

Not through the eyes of people who judge your appearance, financial position, possessions or social standing.

But through the understanding that God sees the heart.

What matters is not how impressive we appear to the world, but whether we are genuinely seeking to live according to His will.

God knows our struggles. He knows our weaknesses. He knows the battles we fight privately and the burdens we carry silently.

He also knows when we choose to forgive instead of retaliate.

He knows when we show kindness to somebody who cannot repay us.

He knows when we stand for truth even though it costs us something.

He knows when we pray for somebody who has hurt us.

And He knows when we serve others without seeking recognition.

These things do not purchase our salvation. Salvation is God's gift through Jesus Christ.

But they are meaningful expressions of a heart being transformed by His grace.

One day, the applause of the world will mean very little.

What will matter is whether we have remained faithful to Christ.

Let us therefore strive to live lives that bring honour to God.

**May our Christianity be recognised not merely by the words we speak, but by the love, integrity and compassion we demonstrate.**

**🌟 11. Final Thought: Be the Christian You Profess to Be!**

Following Jesus is not about pretending to be perfect.

It is about accepting His grace, trusting His promises, learning His teachings and allowing Him to transform the way we live.

We are not saved because we are good people.

We are saved because Jesus Christ is our Saviour.

But having received that gift, let us not live as though it means nothing.

Let us be faithful servants, responsible stewards, compassionate neighbours and examples of Christlike living.

Let us be quick to forgive, slow to anger and always willing to help those in need.

And when somebody asks what it means to be a Christian, may we not have to rely entirely on our words to answer them.

**May they see the answer in the way we live.**

📖 *5. SEVEN SCRIPTURES FOR A CHRIST-CENTRED LIFE!*

*1. 🪢 THE YOKE OF CHRIST — MATTHEW 11:28–30 (KJV).*
> "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."
> "For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."
*Reflection:* Jesus offers rest in Him. Following Christ requires submission, obedience and trust; we do not carry our burdens alone.
*Question:* Am I allowing Jesus to guide my life, or am I trying to carry everything myself?

*2. ✝️ THE COST OF DISCIPLESHIP — LUKE 9:23–24 (KJV).*
> "If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me."
> "For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: but whosoever will lose his life for my sake, the same shall save it."
*Reflection:* Following Jesus is a daily decision. Taking up our cross means choosing obedience, even when it requires sacrifice.
*Question:* Am I prepared to follow Jesus when it becomes uncomfortable or unpopular?

*3. 🛡️ EXPECTING OPPOSITION — JOHN 15:18–20 (KJV).*
> "If the world hate you, ye know that it hated me before it hated you."
> "The servant is not greater than his lord."
*Reflection:* Jesus warned that disciples would face opposition. We must respond to ridicule with humility, truth and respect, not hostility.
*Question:* When my faith is challenged, do I respond with Christlike character?

*4. ❤️ ASSURANCE OF SALVATION — ROMANS 10:9–13 (KJV).*
> "That if thou shalt confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and shalt believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead, thou shalt be saved."
> "For whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved."
*Reflection:* We cannot earn salvation through deeds or ceremonies. We receive it through faith in Christ and His finished work.
*Question:* Do I genuinely understand and trust the salvation Jesus offers?

*5. 💧 DYING AND RISING WITH CHRIST — ROMANS 6:3–4 (KJV).*
> "Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death?"
> "...even so we also should walk in newness of life."
*Reflection:* Baptism represents our identification with Christ's death and resurrection and reminds us to live a new life of obedience, love and spiritual growth.
*Question:* Does my daily life reflect the newness of life I profess through Christ?

*6. 🤲 FAITH DEMONSTRATED THROUGH WORKS — JAMES 2:17–18 (KJV).*
> "Even so faith, if it hath not works, is dead, being alone."
> "...I will shew thee my faith by my works."
*Reflection:* Genuine faith produces action. We are not saved by works, but honesty, compassion, generosity, forgiveness and service demonstrate our faith.
*Question:* If somebody examined my actions, would they recognise my Christian faith?

*7. 🕊️ FORGIVENESS AND MERCY — MATTHEW 18:21–22 (KJV).*
> "Lord, how oft shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times?"
> "I say not unto thee, Until seven times: but, Until seventy times seven."
*Reflection:* Forgiveness must not be limited by a convenient number. It does not require us to remain in harmful situations, but we must resist bitterness.
*Question:* Is there somebody I need to begin forgiving?
