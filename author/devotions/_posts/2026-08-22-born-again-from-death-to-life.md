# Born Again — From Death to Life.

There are moments in life when we look backwards and realise that we should not be where we are.

We should have been broken beyond repair.

We should have remained buried beneath the consequences of our own choices.

We should have remained in despair.

But somehow, inexplicably, we are still standing.

For the Christian, that realisation should lead to only one conclusion:

**Grace.**

My own life has been an ongoing spiritual journey with the Lord, and when I look back upon it, I cannot honestly attribute my survival, my restoration, or my hope to myself.

God has shown me mercy.

God has shown me grace.

Again and again, He has reached into the ruins of my own making and lifted me from the ash heap.

He has brought light into dark places.

He has given hope where I had almost none left.

And above everything else, He gave His Son.

Jesus Christ went to the Cross and died in my place, bearing the penalty that I could never bear myself.

Through Him comes forgiveness.

Through Him comes reconciliation with God.

Through Him comes eternal life.

**To God alone be the glory.**

There truly is no better life than a life lived for Christ.

I am certain of it.

The Christian life is not merely an improvement programme for morally respectable people. It is not simply adopting better habits, attending church, reading the Bible occasionally, or trying harder to become a nicer person.

Christianity begins with something far more radical.

**We must be born again.**

## 🌱 The Christian Life Begins With a New Birth.

One of the great mistakes people can make about Christianity is to think that becoming a Christian simply means becoming a better person.

But Scripture presents something much deeper.

The Christian does not merely become **improved**.

The Christian becomes **new**.

Jesus said:

> _“Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God.”_  
> **John 3:3**

Those words were spoken to Nicodemus, a religious man, a teacher of Israel, and a person who knew the Scriptures.

Yet Jesus told him that his religious knowledge was not enough.

His position was not enough.

His morality was not enough.

His religious accomplishments were not enough.

**He needed to be born again.**

That is an astonishing statement.

Nicodemus was already religious.

He already knew the Scriptures.

He was already regarded as a teacher.

And yet Jesus told him that something fundamental was missing.

The problem was not simply that Nicodemus needed more information.

He needed **new life**.

This is at the heart of the Gospel.

Human beings do not naturally drift towards God.

Sin has separated humanity from its Creator.

We are born into Adam's fallen race, and Scripture describes humanity apart from Christ as spiritually dead.

That is why the Gospel is not simply advice.

It is not merely instruction.

It is the announcement that God has acted to save sinners.

Through faith in Jesus Christ, the spiritually dead sinner receives new life.

The Bible calls this **regeneration**.

It is the new birth.

And it is the work of God.

We respond in faith to the Gospel, but the glory belongs to God.

## 🩸 From Adam to Christ.

The Bible gives us a remarkably clear picture of humanity.

There are many nations.

Many languages.

Many cultures.

Many political systems.

Many economic classes.

Many personalities.

Many denominations.

But spiritually, Scripture presents humanity in two fundamental conditions:

**In Adam, or in Christ.**

The Apostle Paul explains that sin entered the world through one man, Adam, and death through sin.

Humanity therefore inherits a fallen condition.

But Christ comes as the second Adam — the One through whom grace, righteousness, and life are offered.

This is why the Gospel is so much more than self-improvement.

It is a transfer from death to life.

It is a change of spiritual standing before God.

The sinner who trusts Christ is no longer trusting in his own goodness.

He is trusting in Christ's righteousness.

He is no longer looking to his own works as the basis of salvation.

He is resting upon the finished work of Jesus.

This is why salvation is by **grace through faith**.

We do not save ourselves.

God saves us.

We do not manufacture the new birth.

God gives new life.

This leaves no room for spiritual pride.

A Christian cannot look at another sinner and say:

**“I am better than you.”**

The proper response is:

**“I was a sinner too, but God had mercy upon me.”**

That understanding should radically affect the way Christians treat other people.

Grace received should become grace extended.

Mercy received should become mercy demonstrated.

Forgiveness received should become forgiveness offered.

## 📖 Three Scriptures About the New Birth.

### 1. Jesus Says We Must Be Born Again.

> _“Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God.”_  
> **John 3:3**

Jesus does not present the new birth as an optional extra for particularly devoted Christians.

He says it is necessary.

The language is absolute.

**Unless one is born again.**

The Christian life therefore does not begin with what we do for God.

It begins with what God does in us.

We cannot earn the new birth.

We cannot purchase it.

We cannot manufacture it through religious activity.

We must come to Christ in faith.

### 2. The New Birth Gives Us Living Hope.

Peter writes:

> _“Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ! According to his great mercy, he has caused us to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead.”_  
> **1 Peter 1:3**

Notice where Peter places the emphasis.

**God's great mercy.**

The new birth produces a **living hope**.

Christian hope is not wishful thinking.

It rests upon the resurrection of Jesus Christ.

Christ died.

Christ was buried.

Christ rose again.

And because He lives, those who belong to Him have a hope that death itself cannot destroy.

### 3. The New Birth Comes Through the Living Word.

Peter continues:

> _“You have been born again, not of perishable seed but of imperishable, through the living and abiding word of God.”_  
> **1 Peter 1:23**

This is why the Word of God matters so much.

If we want to know God, we must know what God has revealed.

We cannot live according to God's will while deliberately remaining ignorant of God's Word.

The Bible teaches us who God is.

It reveals our sin.

It reveals God's holiness.

It reveals God's mercy.

It reveals Christ.

It teaches us how to live.

It corrects us when we wander.

It encourages us when we are weak.

And it continually points us back to the One who gives eternal life.

**The Christian who neglects Scripture is trying to live a supernatural life while ignoring the very Word through which God has revealed Himself.**

## 🏛️ A Little Church History.

The doctrine of the new birth has stood at the centre of Christian teaching for centuries.

The early Church understood that Christianity was not merely an external affiliation.

The person who came to Christ was understood to be entering a radically transformed life.

The early Christians spoke frequently about conversion, repentance, baptism, resurrection, and new life in Christ.

As Christianity developed through the centuries, the doctrine of salvation was discussed, defended, and sometimes fiercely debated.

During the Protestant Reformation, the question of how a sinner is justified before God became one of the central theological issues of the age.

The Reformers strongly emphasised salvation by grace through faith rather than salvation being earned through human merit.

The great Reformation cries of **sola gratia** — grace alone — and **sola fide** — faith alone — pointed Christians back toward the biblical teaching that salvation ultimately rests upon God's grace and is received through faith.

Later, during the Evangelical revival movements, preachers such as John Wesley and George Whitefield placed enormous emphasis upon conversion and the necessity of the new birth.

The language of being **“born again”** therefore did not originate in modern Christianity.

It comes directly from Jesus.

The Church has been wrestling with the meaning of those words for nearly two thousand years.

And the central truth remains unchanged:

**We need Christ.**

No denomination can regenerate us.

No church building can regenerate us.

No religious title can regenerate us.

No amount of charitable work can regenerate us.

Only God can give the new life that Scripture calls the new birth.

## ❤️ The Life That Follows the New Birth.

If we have truly received new life in Christ, something should begin to change.

Not necessarily overnight.

Not perfectly.

Not without failure.

But genuinely.

The Christian life is a life of surrender.

We exchange the world's harsh yoke for the yoke of Christ.

Jesus Himself said:

> _“Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and lowly in heart.”_  
> **Matthew 11:29**

The world teaches us to seek ourselves.

Christ teaches us to surrender ourselves.

The world says:

**“What can I get?”**

Christ teaches:

**“What can I give?”**

The world celebrates power over others.

Christ teaches servant leadership.

The world often treats forgiveness as weakness.

Christ commands forgiveness.

The world encourages revenge.

Christ teaches mercy.

The world measures success by possessions, status, influence, and recognition.

Christ measures faithfulness differently.

Love.

Honour.

Humility.

Respect.

Sacrifice.

Obedience.

Mercy.

Service.

These are not merely admirable personality traits.

For the mature Christian, they become expressions of a life increasingly surrendered to Christ.

The Christian does not sacrifice in order to purchase salvation.

**The Christian sacrifices because salvation has already been received by grace.**

We do not serve God so that He will love us.

We serve because He has already loved us.

We do not obey so that Christ will save us.

We obey because Christ has saved us.

## 🧭 Practical Applications.

How do we actually live this truth?

Here are some simple ways to put the doctrine of the new birth into practice.

### 🔑 1. Know God's Word.

Do not build your Christian life entirely upon feelings.

Feelings change.

Circumstances change.

God's Word does not.

Read Scripture regularly.

Study it.

Ask questions.

Learn the context.

Compare Scripture with Scripture.

And most importantly, obey what you understand.

### 🔑 2. Examine the direction of your life.

The question is not:

**“Am I perfect?”**

None of us are.

The better question is:

**“Am I being changed?”**

Is Christ becoming more important to me?

Am I becoming more willing to forgive?

Am I becoming more compassionate?

Do I desire God's Word?

Do I desire holiness?

Do I increasingly want God's will rather than merely my own?

These are worthwhile questions.

### 🔑 3. Replace judgement with prayer.

If we genuinely understand grace, we should become slower to condemn people and quicker to pray for them.

We were not saved because we were superior.

We were saved because God was merciful.

Therefore, when we encounter someone who is lost, our first response should not be pride.

It should be compassion.

### 🔑 4. Serve somebody.

Find a practical way to make somebody else's life a little easier.

Encourage someone.

Feed someone.

Listen to someone.

Help someone.

Pray with someone.

Forgive someone.

The Christian life cannot remain entirely theoretical.

Faith should become visible in how we treat people.

### 🔑 5. Keep returning to the Cross.

When you fail, do not run away from Christ.

Run **toward** Him.

Confess your sin.

Receive His forgiveness.

Learn from your failure.

Get back up.

Grace does not give us permission to remain in sin.

Grace gives us the freedom to get back on our feet and continue walking with Christ.

With no strength left to rise;

Beneath dark, empty skies.

I thought my life was finished,  
My hope had disappeared;  
But Christ reached through the darkness,  
And suddenly He was near.

He bore my sin and pain;  
And through His death and rising,  
He made me live again.

Not by my works or goodness,  
Not by the strength of me;  
But by His grace and mercy,  
He set the sinner free.

So now my life belongs to Him,

There are ultimately two spiritual conditions.

**Alive in Christ.**

Or **dead in sin.**

The difference is not wealth.

It is not education.

It is not nationality.

It is not social position.

It is not religious reputation.

It is Christ.

Jesus said:

> _“Unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God.”_

So the most important question any human being can ever answer is not:

**“Am I a good person?”**

**“Have I been born again?”**

Love Him.

Know His Word.

Obey His will.
