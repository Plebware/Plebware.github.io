**🏺 Hedges and Altars.**

**Repairing the Breaches and Returning to God.**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

**🙏 Opening Prayer.**

_Heavenly Father, I come before You this day._

_Thank You for a new day. Thank You for the gifts that You have bestowed upon me. I want to give You all the honour and all the glory. You are a good God. You have always been true and faithful to me and Julian, and You have always answered the prayers that we pray in earnest._

_My prayer today is, Lord, help us to fix the hedges that we have broken through our own ignorance._

_Your prophet says, “My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge.” Your people, Lord, that is us._

_Help us also to come down on our knees and humble ourselves before You and say, “Lord, forgive us for our ignorance, our arrogance, and sometimes our disobedience and rebellion.”_

_Lord, help us to build altars to You and break down the altars that our ancestors have made to the enemy, and the altars that we ourselves have established through rebellion, disobedience, anger, and whatever other mishaps we have had and have not properly dealt with._

_Lord Jesus taught us to pray that we forgive those who trespass against us, so that the Father can forgive us our trespasses._

_Lord, also help us to forgive ourselves._

_I have just been listening to something about people who self-harm — cutting themselves, burning themselves, or otherwise injuring themselves as a form of punishment. Sometimes they punish themselves for things they did not even do wrong, simply because they were ignored or mistreated as children._

_This is so sad._

_Lord, bless everyone who reads this devotion today, and help us to understand it. Let Your Holy Spirit open our hearts, our minds, and our eyes._

_In Jesus' name, I pray._

_Amen._

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

**📖 Introduction — The Holes We Sometimes Make.**

Something that I have known for years is that we often open doors in our lives.

Sometimes we do it through ignorance.

Sometimes through blatant rebellion.

Sometimes through disappointment.

We feel that God should be doing something for us, but it does not happen in our time, or according to our schedule. We become disappointed with God, become angry, and then we do stupid things.

Sometimes we do not even repent.

The Lord tells us not to let the sun go down on our anger. Jesus tells us that if we remember that our brother has something against us, we should first go and be reconciled before bringing our offering to God.

These are important instructions.

Yet we often overlook them.

We carry on with our lives, leaving the breach unrepaired.

And when there is a hole in a hedge, it is no longer functioning as a complete hedge.

The Bible gives us a striking picture:

> “Whoso breaketh an hedge, a serpent shall bite him.”
>
> — Ecclesiastes 10:8, KJV.

There is a lesson here that reaches beyond the physical picture.

A broken hedge represents vulnerability.

When we knowingly leave things unresolved — anger, bitterness, unforgiveness, rebellion, pride, disobedience, or other sinful behaviour — we should not be surprised when those things begin causing damage in our lives.

We sometimes provide the opportunity ourselves.

It is almost as though we provide the rope, and then discover that the enemy knows exactly how to use it.

This is a difficult topic.

And it is something we are all guilty of in one degree or another.

So please do not read this devotion and become angry with me.

Do not immediately say, “This does not apply to me.”

Look at it thoughtfully.

Search your own heart.

Because I would go so far as to say that every one of us has had moments in our lives when we have created a breach that needed repairing.

The question is not whether we have ever made a mistake.

The question is:

**What are we going to do about the breach?**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

**🏛️ Church History — Repairing the Altar.**

Throughout Scripture and Christian history, the altar has represented a place of sacrifice, surrender, remembrance, and worship.

In the Old Testament, people built altars to worship God and to remember significant encounters with Him. Noah built an altar after the Flood. Abraham built altars as he journeyed with God. Elijah repaired the broken altar of the Lord before calling Israel back to true worship.

That last example is particularly powerful.

Before Elijah called down fire from heaven, he **repaired the altar of the Lord that had been broken down**.

There is something here that we should not overlook.

Sometimes restoration begins with repairing what has been broken.

We cannot undo yesterday.

We cannot go back and change every decision we made.

We cannot erase every angry word, every act of rebellion, every moment of unforgiveness, or every foolish decision.

But we can humble ourselves before God.

We can repent.

We can ask forgiveness.

We can seek reconciliation where reconciliation is appropriate.

And we can begin rebuilding what has been neglected.

Christianity has always placed repentance and reconciliation at the heart of returning to God.

The Gospel does not tell us to pretend that our failures never happened.

It tells us to bring them into the light.

The broken place can become the place where restoration begins.

**✍️ An Original Poem — Repair the Hedge.**

```
A hedge once stood around the field,
A place of safety, strong and sealed.
But stone by stone and branch by branch,
Our careless hands left open gaps.

Through anger, pride, and foolish ways,
Through wasted nights and disobedient days,
We sometimes opened what should close,
Then wondered why the danger grows.

But God does not abandon ground
Because a broken place is found.
He calls us back, He calls us near,
And says, “My child, I brought you here.”

Repair the hedge, rebuild the wall,
Return to Me and hear My call.
Tear down the altars built in pride,
And place your heart at My side.

Forgive the one who caused the pain,
Release the bitterness and chain.
And when you stumble, do not flee;
Bring even that mistake to Me.

For mercy waits where hearts repent,
And grace restores what sin has bent.
The breach may show the scars of years,
But God can work through all our tears.

So kneel today and simply say,
“Lord, help me choose Your righteous way.”
Then build Your altar, stone by stone,
And let your heart become God's home.
```

**📜 Scripture One — Hosea 4:6.**

> **“My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge.”**
>
> — Hosea 4:6, KJV.

Ignorance does not always mean that someone deliberately rejected God.

Sometimes we simply do not know.

Sometimes we were never taught.

Sometimes we misunderstood.

And sometimes we knew better but chose differently.

That is why knowledge matters.

God's Word teaches us what the hedges are supposed to look like. It teaches us where the breaches are.

And when the Holy Spirit reveals something that needs fixing, we should not ignore it.

**💭 Reflection Question.**

_Is there something in your life that you have never dealt with simply because you did not understand that it needed attention?_

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

**📜 Scripture Two — Ephesians 4:26–27.**

> **“Be ye angry, and sin not: let not the sun go down upon your wrath: Neither give place to the devil.”**
>
> — Ephesians 4:26–27, KJV.

Notice the progression.

Anger itself is not presented as the entire problem.

The warning is about what we do with that anger.

Unresolved anger can become bitterness.

Bitterness can become unforgiveness.

Unforgiveness can become a permanent wound.

And a wound that we refuse to deal with can influence everything around it.

Paul's instruction is therefore practical:

**Do not allow anger to become a foothold.**

Deal with it.

Pray about it.

Seek reconciliation where possible.

Forgive where forgiveness is required.

And do not allow yesterday's anger to become tomorrow's prison.

**💭 Reflection Question.**

_Is there an anger, offence, or bitterness that you have carried longer than God is asking you to carry it?_

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

**📜 Scripture Three — Matthew 5:23–24.**

> **“First be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.”**
>
> — Matthew 5:24, KJV.

Jesus places reconciliation alongside worship.

That is powerful.

We can sing.

We can pray.

We can give.

We can attend church.

We can read our Bibles.

But if the Holy Spirit is convicting us about something that we need to put right with another person, we should not simply ignore that conviction.

Sometimes the altar we need to rebuild begins with a conversation.

Sometimes it begins with an apology.

Sometimes it begins with forgiveness.

And sometimes it begins with humbly admitting:

**“Lord, I was wrong.”**

**💭 Reflection Question.**

_Is there someone with whom you need to seek peace, offer an apology, or release an offence?_

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

**🏺 The Altar We Build.**

An altar is not merely a physical structure.

In our lives, an altar can represent the place where we surrender something to God.

We can build an altar of prayer.

An altar of obedience.

An altar of repentance.

An altar of thanksgiving.

An altar of forgiveness.

And sometimes we must tear down the things that have occupied those places instead.

Pride cannot remain on the throne while humility is trying to take its place.

Bitterness cannot remain while forgiveness is being cultivated.

Rebellion cannot remain while obedience is being chosen.

We cannot serve yesterday's wounds forever and expect today's relationship with God to remain unaffected.

This does not mean that every difficulty is caused by some hidden sin.

It does not mean that every hardship is punishment.

And it certainly does not mean that someone who suffers has necessarily opened some spiritual door.

Life is more complicated than that.

But when the Holy Spirit reveals something that **we genuinely need to put right**, we should not run from it.

We should bring it to God.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

**🛠️ Practical Tips — Repair the Breaches.**

**🔑 1. Ask God to Show You the Breaches.**

Pray honestly:

**“Lord, show me anything in my life that I need to put right.”**

Then listen.

Do not manufacture guilt.

Do not search for sins that are not there.

Simply allow God to reveal what genuinely needs attention.

**🔑 2. Deal With Anger Quickly.**

If something has made you angry, do not allow it to become a permanent resident in your heart.

Pray about it.

Think before speaking.

And where necessary, have the difficult conversation.

**🔑 3. Learn to Say, “I Was Wrong.”**

Those four words can be incredibly difficult.

They can also be incredibly liberating.

Humility does not make us smaller.

It puts us in a position where God can work on us.

**🔑 4. Forgive Others.**

Forgiveness does not mean pretending that something never happened.

It does not necessarily mean restoring someone's previous access to your life.

It means refusing to remain chained to the offence.

There may still need to be boundaries.

There may still need to be consequences.

But bitterness does not have to become your master.

**🔑 5. Learn to Forgive Yourself.**

There is a difference between repentance and self-punishment.

If God has forgiven you, repeatedly punishing yourself does not improve the sacrifice of Christ.

Bring your failure to God.

Repent.

Learn.

Make amends where appropriate.

Then allow His grace to do what your self-condemnation never could.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

**❤️ Practical Application — What Needs Repair Today?**

Take a few quiet minutes today.

Ask yourself these questions:

**Is there someone I am angry with?**

**Is there someone I need to forgive?**

**Is there someone I need to apologise to?**

**Is there something I have deliberately disobeyed God about?**

**Is there an area where disappointment has caused me to turn away from God?**

**Have I allowed an old wound to become an excuse for present behaviour?**

**Is there something I need to surrender at the altar?**

Do not answer these questions for somebody else.

Answer them for yourself.

Then choose one thing that needs attention.

Not twenty things.

Just one.

Pray about it.

Then take the appropriate step.

Send the message.

Make the call.

Apologise.

Forgive.

Repent.

Ask for help.

Or simply kneel before God and say:

**“Father, this is the breach. Help me repair it.”**

And then begin.

**🏃 Today's Exercise — Repair One Breach.**

Before the day ends, identify **one breach** that God is asking you to address.

Write it down.

Pray over it.

Then take one practical step toward repairing it.

And when you have done that, thank God.

Not because everything has suddenly become perfect.

But because you have taken a step back toward obedience.

Sometimes restoration begins with one small act of obedience.

Ask forgiveness where you need to ask forgiveness.

And where you have genuinely done everything you can, leave the matter in God's hands.

The hedge can be repaired.

And tomorrow does not have to look exactly like yesterday.
