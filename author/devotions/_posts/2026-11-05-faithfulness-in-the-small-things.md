## ❤️ Introduction — What Does It Really Mean to Be Faithful?

Good morning, brothers and sisters.

Today I want us to consider something that sounds simple, but is actually one of the greatest challenges of Christian living.

**Faithfulness.**

What does it mean to be faithful?

Is it attending church on Sunday? Is it helping somebody when they are in trouble? Is it giving someone a helping hand, making a donation, or doing something kind for another person?

Yes, these can all be expressions of faithfulness.

But there is something we need to understand.

**Being faithful is not about doing one good deed once in a while. It is about living faithfully, consistently, every day.**

It is easy to be faithful when everything is going well.

It is easy to be faithful when somebody is watching.

It is easy to be faithful when we are receiving recognition, appreciation, or some benefit in return.

But what happens when nobody is watching?

What happens when being faithful becomes inconvenient?

What happens when we have to keep our word, honour our commitments, do the right thing, or make a sacrifice when nobody will ever know about it?

That is where our faithfulness is truly tested.

And perhaps we should begin by asking ourselves a difficult question:

**Are we faithful only when it suits us?**

## 📖 Part 1 — Faithful to God and His Word.

Jesus gave us two commandments that summarise the foundation of Christian living.

In Matthew 22:37–39, He tells us to love the Lord our God with all our heart, soul, and mind, and to love our neighbour as ourselves.

Notice something important here.

Jesus was not telling us that the Ten Commandments had become irrelevant.

In Matthew 22:40, He explained that all the Law and the Prophets hang on these two commandments.

In other words, genuine love for God and our neighbours should be reflected in the way we live.

Think about it.

If we genuinely love God with all our heart, would we deliberately put other things before Him?

Would we worship idols?

Would we use His name carelessly?

Would we knowingly disregard His instructions?

And if we genuinely love our neighbours, would we steal from them, lie about them, gossip about them, or deliberately cause them harm?

The two great commandments do not give us permission to ignore God's instructions.

They show us the heart behind them.

**Love should produce faithfulness.**

But there is another question we need to consider.

How familiar are we with God's Word?

How often do we actually open our Bibles to improve our understanding?

Do we take the time to understand what we read, or do we simply select the passages that make us feel comfortable?

We enjoy reading about God's promises, His blessings, His protection, and His love.

And rightly so!

But what about the passages that challenge us?

What about the Scriptures that tell us to change our behaviour, control our tongues, stop gossiping, forgive those who have wronged us, and turn away from sin?

Do we give those passages the same attention?

It is easy to love the parts of Scripture that make us feel good.

It takes genuine commitment to accept the parts that show us where we need to change.

Being faithful to God means being faithful to **all His Word**, not merely the portions that suit our preferences.

And that includes putting His teachings into practice.

James 1:22 reminds us that we must be doers of the Word, not hearers only.

We cannot continually read God's instructions and then live as though we never heard them.

Faithfulness begins when we take what we learn and apply it to our daily lives.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 🤝 Part 2 — Faithful to Ourselves and Our Neighbours.

Here is something I want us to think about.

**How can we expect to be faithful to others if we cannot be faithful to ourselves?**

Are we honest with ourselves?

Do we acknowledge our shortcomings?

Do we take responsibility for our actions?

Do we make promises to ourselves that we continually break?

Do we know what needs changing in our lives, yet keep postponing the necessary changes?

Faithfulness also means respecting the person God created us to be.

It means taking responsibility for our conduct, our habits, our words, and our surroundings.

And then there is our responsibility towards our neighbours.

Jesus did not tell us to love our neighbours only when they are pleasant, helpful, or convenient.

He taught us to love them as ourselves.

That means considering how our actions affect the people around us.

Let us look at something very practical.

Here in Johannesburg, we experience problems with rodents and cockroaches in many residential areas.

Now, imagine if every household took responsibility for its own environment.

People picked up food crumbs, disposed of rubbish properly, cleaned their kitchens, maintained their yards, and avoided leaving food waste lying around.

Would that not make a difference?

Of course it would.

But what happens when one household makes every effort to maintain cleanliness while the surrounding properties are neglected?

The problem becomes much harder to control.

This illustrates something important.

**Our individual responsibilities often have consequences for the wider community.**

We cannot control every decision our neighbours make, but we can take responsibility for our own conduct.

We can lead by example.

We can encourage rather than criticise.

We can help rather than complain.

And we can remember that loving our neighbours is not simply something we say.

It is something we demonstrate.

Faithfulness is not about waiting for everybody else to do the right thing before we do ours.

It is about doing what is right, regardless of what others choose.

## 🗳️ Part 3 — Faithful in the Small Things.

Yesterday, we were reminded of our responsibility towards our country through the opportunity to vote.

I will openly admit that I have been guilty of neglecting this responsibility in the past.

I have thought:

"What's the point? My vote isn't going to change anything."

But an analogy I recently considered made me look at this differently.

Imagine a bus travelling towards a cliff.

Five passengers vote that the bus should continue towards the cliff.

Two passengers vote against it.

The remaining passengers decide not to vote at all.

The majority has spoken.

The bus continues towards the cliff.

Now, perhaps that is a rather dramatic illustration, but it makes an important point.

**Not participating is also a decision with consequences.**

We cannot always determine the outcome, but we can take responsibility for the part entrusted to us.

The same principle applies to everyday life.

Luke 16:10 teaches us that whoever is faithful with very little can also be trusted with much.

Think about that.

We often dream of being entrusted with greater responsibilities.

We want bigger opportunities, greater influence, and more significant roles.

But are we faithful with what we already have?

Are we looking after our homes?

Are we taking care of our belongings?

Are we keeping our promises?

Are we completing the small tasks that others depend upon us to do?

Sometimes we want God to entrust us with something bigger while we are neglecting the responsibilities already in our hands.

Faithfulness does not begin when we receive a promotion, become a leader, or acquire greater wealth.

It begins with what we do today.

The small things matter.

The forgotten promise matters.

The uncollected rubbish matters.

The little act of kindness matters.

The honest decision matters.

**Never underestimate the importance of being faithful in the little things.**

## ⏰ Part 4 — Faithful With Our Time.

Now let us consider another aspect of faithfulness.

Our time.

Are we faithful with the time entrusted to us?

When we tell somebody that we will be somewhere at seven in the morning, do we arrive at seven?

When our employer expects us at work at half past six, do we make an effort to be there on time?

When we arrange an appointment, do we respect the other person's time?

Or do we regularly arrive late, offer excuses, and expect everybody else to accommodate us?

Being punctual is not merely about watching the clock.

It is about respecting commitments.

When somebody gives you their time, they are giving you something they can never recover.

And when you promise to be somewhere at a particular time, another person may have organised their entire day around that commitment.

Our time is a responsibility.

Our words are responsibilities.

Our appointments are responsibilities.

And our work is a responsibility.

First Corinthians 4:2 reminds us that those who have been entrusted with something must prove faithful.

That principle reaches into every part of our lives.

We are entrusted with opportunities, relationships, responsibilities, abilities, and time.

How we manage these things says a great deal about our character.

And remember, people notice.

They notice whether we keep our word.

They notice whether we arrive when promised.

They notice whether we do the work we agreed to do.

They notice whether our actions match our words.

**Faithfulness is not merely what we claim to be. It is what our daily conduct demonstrates.**

## 📜 Church History — Faithfulness in the Early Church.

One of the most interesting early Christian writings outside the New Testament is known as the *Didache*, often translated as *The Teaching of the Twelve Apostles*.

This ancient Christian teaching document is generally dated to the late first or early second century.

It gives us a glimpse into how early Christians understood the practical responsibilities of following Jesus.

The Didache begins with a distinction between the Way of Life and the Way of Death.

It discusses matters such as loving God, loving one's neighbour, rejecting wrongdoing, treating others with compassion, and living with integrity.

What makes this particularly relevant to our devotion is that early Christian instruction was not limited to what believers professed with their mouths.

It addressed how they were expected to live.

Faith had to be demonstrated through conduct.

The early Christian community also placed great importance on prayer, sharing, hospitality, generosity, and caring for those in need.

Consider the example of **Polycarp of Smyrna**, an early Christian leader traditionally associated with the generation that followed the apostles.

His letter to the Philippians encouraged believers to remain steadfast, practise righteousness, and live according to the teachings they had received.

Polycarp himself became known for his steadfast commitment to Christ, even in the face of persecution.

His life and writings remind us that faithfulness is not always convenient.

Sometimes it demands courage.

Sometimes it demands sacrifice.

And sometimes it requires us to stand firm when the world expects us to compromise.

The early Church understood something that we should never forget:

**Christianity is not simply a declaration of belief. It is a way of life.**

We may live in a different century, but the principle remains unchanged.

Our faith should be visible in our conduct.

## ☁️ From the Heavenly View — God Sees the Small Things.

We often measure success by the size of our achievements.

We look at promotions, financial rewards, recognition, influence, and the things that other people can see.

But God's perspective is different.

In Matthew 25:21, Jesus gives us the beautiful words:

> "Well done, good and faithful servant!"

The servant was commended because he had been faithful with what had been entrusted to him.

It is a reminder that faithfulness is not measured merely by how much we accomplish.

It is also measured by how responsibly we use what we have been given.

Perhaps nobody notices when you arrive on time.

Perhaps nobody thanks you for keeping your home clean.

Perhaps nobody sees the small act of kindness you performed.

Perhaps nobody knows that you chose not to participate in a dishonest conversation.

But God sees the heart behind our actions.

And here is something else we should remember.

We are not always going to get everything right.

There will be days when we fall short.

There will be moments when we become impatient, forget a commitment, or fail to demonstrate the faithfulness we should.

When that happens, we must not simply give up.

We must acknowledge our shortcomings, seek God's forgiveness, learn from our mistakes, and continue growing.

After all, Lamentations 3:22–23 reminds us that God's mercies are new every morning and that great is His faithfulness.

**God is faithful to us. Let us strive to be faithful to Him.**

Not occasionally.

Not when it suits us.

But every waking moment of our lives.
