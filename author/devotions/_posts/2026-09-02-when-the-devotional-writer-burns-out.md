# 🔥 When The Devotional Writer Burns Out.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🙏 Opening Prayer.

Heavenly Father,

I come before You today tired, weary, and perhaps a little discouraged.

There are times when the body becomes weak, circumstances become difficult, finances become frightening, and even the words that once flowed so freely seem to disappear.

Lord, You know the sickness.  
You know the financial worries.  
You know the sleepless nights.  
You know the unanswered questions.

And yet, Your Word tells us to trust You.

So today I choose to place my understanding aside and place my hand in Yours.

Restore the weary heart. Renew the tired mind. Strengthen the weak body. Provide where there is need, and remind us that our circumstances do not determine Your faithfulness.

Help us to trust You even when we cannot see the road ahead.

In the precious name of Jesus Christ.

**Amen.**

## 🌅 Introduction — When The Well Runs Dry.

There is a strange thing that happens to devotional writers.

We spend our time encouraging other people to trust God, yet sometimes we reach a place where **we ourselves have nothing left to say.**

Illness can do that.

Financial pressure can do that.

Exhaustion can do that.

And sometimes several difficulties arrive at once.

The body says, *“I can't.”*

The mind says, *“How am I going to get through this?”*

The bank account says, *“There isn't enough.”*

And the writer's notebook sits there silently.

But perhaps there is something God wants us to learn in those quiet seasons.

**We don't always have to produce something for God. Sometimes we simply need to rest in God.**

Proverbs 3:5–6 does not tell us to understand everything.

It tells us to **trust Him.**

Faith does not mean pretending everything is fine.

Faith means saying:

**“Lord, everything isn't fine — but You are still God.”**

## ⛪ Church History — The Weary Servant.

Throughout Christian history, God's servants have discovered that spiritual usefulness does not make a person immune to exhaustion.

The great Christian tradition contains countless stories of believers who experienced illness, discouragement, poverty, persecution, loneliness, and seasons when their work seemed fruitless.

One particularly important lesson comes from the ministry of **Charles Spurgeon**, the great nineteenth-century preacher.

Spurgeon openly struggled with physical illness, emotional darkness, and the enormous pressures associated with his ministry. His life reminds us that being greatly used by God does not mean that we will never become weary.

Sometimes the faithful servant needs to stop.

Sometimes the preacher needs to rest.

Sometimes the writer needs to put down the pen.

And sometimes the strongest prayer we can pray is simply:

**“Lord, help me.”**

God does not measure our worth by the number of pages we produce.

Our worth comes from belonging to Him.

## 🔥 Seven Spiritual Points.

### 1. Trust God When You Don't Understand.

Proverbs does not say, *“Understand everything.”*

It says:

**“Lean not unto thine own understanding.”**

Our understanding is limited.

God sees the entire picture.

We see today.

He sees yesterday, today, tomorrow, and eternity.

When circumstances make no sense, trust the One who sees what we cannot.

### 2. Illness Does Not Cancel Your Calling.

Being sick does not mean God has finished with you.

Sometimes the body needs healing.

Sometimes it needs rest.

Sometimes God uses the quiet season to restore what constant activity has depleted.

You are not less valuable because you are tired.

**Rest is not failure.**

### 3. Financial Trouble Does Not Mean God Has Left.

Money problems can produce tremendous fear.

We start calculating.

We worry about tomorrow.

We imagine the worst.

Yet Jesus reminded His followers that our Heavenly Father knows what we need.

That does not mean we become irresponsible.

We work where we can.

We plan wisely.

We ask for help when necessary.

And above everything else, **we trust God.**

### 4. Sometimes God's Answer Is “Wait.”

We often want God to say:

**“Yes.”**

Sometimes He says:

**“No.”**

And sometimes He says:

**“Wait.”**

Waiting can feel like doing nothing.

But waiting on God is not wasted time.

A seed buried in the ground appears inactive.

Yet something is happening beneath the surface.

Perhaps your quiet season is not an ending.

Perhaps it is preparation.

### 5. God Can Use The Broken Writer.

We sometimes think we must be strong before we can encourage anybody else.

But Scripture repeatedly shows God working through imperfect and struggling people.

The wounded person can speak about healing.

The frightened person can speak about courage.

The weary person can speak about rest.

And the person who has struggled to trust God can give a particularly honest testimony about learning to trust Him.

Your weakness does not disqualify you.

**It can become part of your testimony.**

### 6. Prayer Does Not Require Beautiful Words.

A devotional writer can become so accustomed to writing prayers that we forget something very simple.

God doesn't need polished prose.

He wants our heart.

Sometimes:

**“Jesus, help me.”**

is enough.

Sometimes:

**“Father, I don't understand.”**

is enough.

Sometimes:

**“Lord, please provide.”**

is enough.

Prayer is not a writing competition.

It is conversation with our Father.

### 7. God Remains Faithful When We Feel Faithless.

Our feelings change.

Our circumstances change.

Our finances change.

Our health changes.

But God does not change.

That is why Proverbs 3:5–6 is so powerful.

The foundation of our faith is not:

**“I understand everything.”**

It is:

**“I trust the One who does.”**

## ❤️ Worth To Do.

It is worth choosing trust.

It is worth putting down the burden of trying to control everything.

It is worth praying when prayer feels difficult.

It is worth resting when the body demands rest.

It is worth opening the Bible when anxiety tells us there is no hope.

And it is worth writing again when the words eventually return.

Not because we must prove ourselves.

But because the gift God placed inside us is still His gift.

**The pause does not erase the purpose.**

## 🛠️ Practical Tip — The Three-Step Trust.

When sickness, exhaustion, or financial anxiety begins overwhelming you, stop and do three things.

**1. Breathe.**

Slow down.

Take several deep breaths and deliberately interrupt the cycle of panic.

**2. Pray.**

Tell God exactly what is worrying you.

Don't dress it up.

Give Him the real problem.

**3. Do The Next Right Thing.**

Don't try to solve your entire life in one morning.

Ask:

**“What is the next thing I can responsibly do?”**

Then do that one thing.

Afterwards, trust God with the things beyond your control.

Imagine for a moment looking at your life from Heaven.

You see the tired writer sitting with an empty page.

You see the illness.

You see the financial worries.

You see the unanswered questions.

But you also see something the weary believer cannot always see.

**You see God's hand.**

You see doors that have not yet opened.

You see people who have not yet been reached.

You see prayers whose answers are still travelling toward their appointed time.

You see the story from beginning to end.

And perhaps Heaven would whisper:

**“Child, you were looking at the chapter. I was looking at the whole book.”**

That is the heavenly perspective.

We see circumstances.

God sees purpose.

We see the storm.

God sees the destination.

We see the empty page.

God sees what He may yet write upon it.

When I cannot see the way forward, teach me to walk by faith.

When fear tells me that there will never be enough, remind me that You are my Provider.

When discouragement tells me that my usefulness is finished, remind me that my identity is found in You, not in my productivity.

Restore what is tired.

Heal what is wounded.

Strengthen what is weak.

And when the time is right, place the words back into my heart and let them become a blessing to somebody who desperately needs to hear them.

**“In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths.”**

I believe it.

I receive it.

And I place my future into Your hands.

In the mighty and precious name of Jesus Christ.
