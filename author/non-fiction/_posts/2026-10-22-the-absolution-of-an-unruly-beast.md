# 🚙 The Absolution of an Unruly Beast: On Learning to Forgive a Ford Ranger.

There is a particular brand of existential exasperation reserved exclusively for the working man whose livelihood is shackled to the fickle whims of an internal combustion engine.

To drive is to earn; to stand stationary is to watch one's financial security evaporate like steam from a ruptured radiator.

For months, my mechanical nemesis, a venerable Ford Ranger of approximately 2010 vintage, has rendered my income precariously conditional. It has transformed the simple act of turning an ignition key into an exercise in high anxiety.

This is not merely the story of a motor vehicle that refuses to behave itself. It is the chronicle of dwindling patience, disappearing income, bewildering mechanical diagnoses, and the peculiar humour that sometimes keeps a man from losing his sanity.

And, ultimately, it is a story about forgiveness.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## 🔧 Chapter One: The Insurrection Begins.

The saga did not commence with a spectacular mechanical catastrophe, but with the sort of seemingly modest complaint that encourages a man to believe the repair will be mercifully brief.

An oil leak.

The bakkie went into the workshop, the expectation being that the offending leak would be identified, repaired, and consigned to the annals of automotive history.

Alas, the Ranger had other ideas.

Following an early repair, the vehicle began exhibiting symptoms that turned a straightforward maintenance job into an increasingly expensive investigation. Limp mode, intermittent loss of power, warning lights, stalling, and fuel-pressure irregularities gradually transformed an ordinary working vehicle into an unpredictable mechanical adversary.

The experience had already been chronicled in my earlier account, *When One Job Becomes Ten — A Ford Ranger Story*.

What followed would demonstrate that ten jobs might, in fact, have been an optimistic estimate.

## ⚙️ Chapter Two: The Labyrinth of Mechanical Wisdom.

Our journey through the workshops became an odyssey of diagnoses, replacements, adjustments, and renewed hope.

In Turffontein, a mechanic recommended by my colleagues investigated an unpleasant frictional noise associated with the drivetrain. What initially appeared to be a thrust-bearing problem was subsequently identified as a possible synchro-bearing fault within the gearbox.

Parts were replaced, qualifications were offered regarding non-original components, and for a brief period there was reason to hope.

But mechanical tranquillity proved ephemeral.

The discord returned.

The investigation subsequently led us towards Rosettenville, where another mechanic examined the vehicle and pursued several possible remedies. Trembling mudflaps were tightened, a suspect shock absorber received attention, and the excessive play in the propshaft became another item on the list.

The propshaft was removed for rebalancing and refurbishment.

Yet the Ranger continued to manufacture fresh mysteries with the enthusiasm of a magician producing an endless succession of rabbits from an apparently empty hat.

An oil leak persisted despite gasket work. Limp mode returned. Power disappeared without sufficient warning, and the vehicle became capable of transforming an ordinary journey into an impromptu roadside consultation.

Each repair promised an ending.

Each new symptom announced another chapter.

## 💉 Chapter Three: The Fuel-System Enigma.

The investigation subsequently turned towards the Ranger's fuel-delivery and electronic control systems.

Among the faults investigated was the dreaded high fuel-rail-pressure code, P0088. Attention fell upon the fuel-rail pressure sensor and the fuel-pump metering valve, components involved in regulating the precise delivery of diesel required by a modern common-rail engine.

The fuel-system work did not produce the immediate, unambiguous victory we had hoped for. At one stage, the engine would crank without starting, leaving the mechanics with yet another puzzle to solve.

Later investigations involved suspected injector problems and replacement fuel-rail sensors.

It was a sobering lesson in the complexity of modern diesel technology. A vehicle may possess all the apparent ingredients of mechanical life and still refuse to cooperate because one component, electrical signal, or control parameter has fallen outside the expected limits.

The Ranger was no longer merely a bakkie requiring maintenance. It had become a rolling examination in automotive diagnostics, with the workshop fees serving as the examination fees and my lost working days constituting the tuition.

## 🧠 Chapter Four: The Mysterious Electronic Brain.

Then came the revelation that made even the most patient observer raise an eyebrow.

During the investigations at Super Quick, the electronic control unit became a central suspect. The computer fitted to the vehicle was reported to be an unexpected replacement unit, with its age and history raising questions about whether it was the correct unit for the vehicle.

The computer was eventually replaced, and adjustments were made to the turbocharger.

For a glorious, if comparatively brief, period, the Ranger appeared to undergo a miraculous automotive resurrection.

The engine ran with renewed vigour. Power returned. The vehicle became more responsive, and the prospect of dependable work once again seemed attainable.

One could almost imagine the mechanical beast bowing its head in contrition.

Perhaps, we thought, the ordeal was over.

Perhaps the accumulated expense had finally purchased a little peace.

But hope, when invested in an elderly bakkie with a complicated history, should apparently be regarded as a negotiable instrument rather than a guaranteed return.

## 🚨 Chapter Five: When Electronics Join the Rebellion.

A subsequent attempt to improve the vehicle's security by installing an alarm system was followed by another period of electronic and mechanical uncertainty.

The Ranger again became unreliable, and further workshop visits followed.

In the more recent investigations, attention returned to a combination of suspected turbocharger trouble, exhaust restrictions, electronic sensor faults, and other possible mechanical shortcomings.

At Booysens, a diagnostic assessment reportedly raised concerns about a severely restricted exhaust valve, oil passing through the turbocharger where it ought not to be, and several faulty electronic sensors.

These findings added to the already substantial list of concerns, although each diagnosis must be distinguished from a confirmed final explanation for the vehicle's behaviour.

Then came another failed test run.

Yesterday, the engine lost its spirit once more, and the Ranger was left at the workshop.

Another day of work disappeared into the mechanical abyss.

And today, the latest message arrived.

## 🏔️ Chapter Six: The Engine Mountains.

My boss informed us that the Ranger would have to remain in the workshop for another day.

Apparently, the engine mountings were shot.

In other words, the supports intended to secure the engine and restrain its movement had deteriorated to the point that they required attention.

But the finest moment of the day was yet to arrive.

In describing the situation, my boss announced that the **engine mountains were gone**.

Now, I have the greatest respect for anyone who makes an honest effort to communicate in a language that is not his strongest. English can be a treacherous beast, even for those who claim mastery over it.

Nevertheless, when Julian, my neighbour, and I received the news, the expression proved irresistible.

We laughed.

Not because the repair was inexpensive. Not because another day without the vehicle was welcome. And certainly not because the Ranger had suddenly become less troublesome.

We laughed because sometimes the alternative is to sit in solemn silence while one's remaining patience packs its bags and emigrates.

Besides, if the engine mountings have become mountains, perhaps the Ranger has been attempting an expedition of its own.

One can only hope that the mechanic possesses the necessary climbing equipment.

On a more serious note, failed engine mountings can permit excessive engine movement and create additional mechanical problems. Whether they explain the latest loss of power or any electronic limp-mode symptoms remains something the workshop must establish rather than something we should assume.

Still, the expression has earned its place in the family vocabulary.

From now on, whenever the Ranger develops another expensive complaint, we may simply ask whether yet another mountain has collapsed.

## 💰 Chapter Seven: The Price of Standing Still.

For a man who earns his living through driving, a stationary vehicle represents far more than an inconvenience.

It represents lost opportunity.

No wheel turns, no delivery is made. No delivery is made, no income is earned.

The financial consequences of these repeated interruptions have been substantial. By my present reckoning, the lost earnings exceed R5,000, with another R480 disappearing from this week's potential income alone.

These figures represent lost earnings, not the cost of the mechanical repairs themselves.

There have been days when the bakkie could not be trusted to complete a journey, days when it remained in a workshop, and days when another attempt to restore it to working order consumed the time that should have been spent earning a living.

For someone working to make ends meet, those missing days cannot simply be recovered by turning the calendar backwards.

Rent does not become more understanding because a fuel-pressure sensor has developed an attitude.

Groceries do not materialise because a mechanic is waiting for a diagnostic result.

And financial obligations do not enter into sympathetic negotiations with an engine that refuses to start.

The loss is real, the frustration is understandable, and the uncertainty is exhausting.

It would be entirely reasonable to feel resentful.

## 🕊️ Chapter Eight: The Unreasonable Freedom of Forgiveness.

And yet, sitting atop this mountain of lost hours, interrupted work, and empty pockets, I have arrived at a curious conclusion.

I choose to forgive the Ranger.

Not because the vehicle has behaved admirably. Not because the repairs have been inexpensive. Not because the lost earnings are insignificant, or because the situation has somehow become fair.

I forgive it because anger is a heavy cargo to carry, and I have enough burdens without adding a personal vendetta against a collection of steel, rubber, wiring, and silicon.

A motor vehicle possesses no malice.

It does not wake in the morning and plot the destruction of a man's income. It does not deliberately arrange for a turbocharger to misbehave, a sensor to fail, a bearing to complain, or an engine mounting to surrender its structural dignity.

It simply operates within the unforgiving laws of mechanics, heat, friction, electrical resistance, material fatigue, and time.

Its failures may be expensive. The consequences may be deeply frustrating. The decisions of the people who repair it may sometimes be mistaken.

But the machine itself is not an enemy with an agenda.

To hate it would be to direct my resentment towards an object incapable of understanding the accusation.

And what would that accomplish?

Would the gearbox become more cooperative? Would the lost wages return? Would the engine mountings repair themselves out of embarrassment?

Of course not.

Forgiveness does not mean abandoning accountability, ignoring poor workmanship, or refusing to ask questions. Nor does it mean pretending that the financial consequences do not matter.

It means recognising the difference between addressing a problem and allowing that problem to occupy the throne of one's emotions.

I can insist on proper repairs without cultivating bitterness.

I can acknowledge the cost without allowing resentment to consume what peace remains.

And I can laugh at the expression *engine mountains* while still taking the underlying mechanical problem seriously.

Perhaps that is the most useful repair I can undertake today.

## 🔑 Chapter Nine: Absolution, With a Spanner in Hand.

There is something almost absurd about forgiving a motor vehicle.

We are accustomed to thinking of forgiveness as something reserved for relationships between people: the painful misunderstandings, the disappointments, the injuries inflicted by words or actions, and the long process of releasing the resentment that follows.

Yet the principle of relinquishing bitterness can apply even when the immediate object of our frustration is inanimate.

The Ranger has taught me, albeit at considerable expense, that frustration and peace are not mutually exclusive circumstances.

I may be frustrated with the situation and still choose peace.

I may be disappointed with the outcome and still refuse to become embittered.

I may continue to pursue the correct diagnosis, the proper repair, and the return of dependable working transport without allowing every new setback to become a personal insult.

Tomorrow, the workshop may discover another fault. The repair may take longer than expected. Another component may reveal itself to be worn beyond redemption.

If so, the practical work must continue.

The vehicle needs competent attention, sound diagnosis, and whatever repairs are genuinely necessary.

But my soul does not need to accompany every defective component into the scrapyard.

I have no intention of surrendering my peace to an unruly beast of metal and machinery.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 🏁 Final Thoughts: May the Mountains Be Conquered.

And so, I extend my absolution.

I forgive the Ranger—not because it has earned grace, but because I require peace.

May its engine mountings be replaced or secured as necessary. May the turbocharger receive the attention it requires. May the electronic control system finally settle into dependable cooperation. May the underlying faults be correctly identified, and may the vehicle return to the road without another procession of mechanical surprises.

May the next turn of the ignition key produce the reassuring sound of an engine ready to work rather than the opening notes of another automotive tragedy.

And may the engine mountains, once properly dealt with, remain firmly beneath the bonnet rather than becoming obstacles across the road to financial stability.

For now, I shall endeavour to keep my humour, preserve my patience, and remember that peace is worth protecting even when circumstances are determined to test it.

The Ranger and I are not yet finished with one another.

There are still roads to travel, deliveries to make, and a living to earn.

But we shall travel them without bitterness if I can help it.

**Absolution granted. The spanners may proceed.** 🔧
