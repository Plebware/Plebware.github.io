# 🧭 Otto — The Man Behind PlebWare.

*An honest account of curiosity, ambition, setbacks, faith, creativity and the determination to build something that matters.*

A website can tell you what a person has built. It can show you the articles they have written, the software they have developed, the ideas they have explored and the projects they have brought into the world. But it cannot, by itself, tell you the whole story of the person behind that work.

PlebWare is the product of many years of learning, experimenting, working, writing, making mistakes and beginning again. Its history is connected to the life of one South African creator: Otto Brinkmeier.

This series is an attempt to look beyond the website and examine the man behind it.

It is not intended to present a perfect entrepreneur, an all-knowing technologist or a heroic figure who overcame every obstacle. Otto's story includes achievements, missed opportunities, difficult decisions, financial setbacks, changing priorities and lessons that sometimes took years to understand.

The purpose is to preserve that story honestly.

## 🔍 Why Examine the Man Behind the Work?

PlebWare is built around the principle that **technology must serve humanity**. That philosophy did not appear overnight, nor did it begin with a website or a software project.

Its roots reach back to a childhood shaped by technical books, an engineer father's example, practical experimentation and a desire to understand how things work. Later came railway engineering, business ventures, digital publishing, writing, faith, collaboration and the continuing effort to make useful things with the resources available.

Understanding that history helps explain why PlebWare looks beyond technology for its own sake. It also helps explain why independence, accessibility, practical knowledge and human purpose matter so much to the project.

The articles in this series will examine both the work and the experiences that shaped it. They will distinguish between what Otto remembers, what existing records support and what may reasonably be interpreted from the events. Where a detail is uncertain, it should remain identified as uncertain rather than being turned into a convenient certainty.

## 👨‍🔧 The Engineer's Son.

Otto's earliest technical influence was his father, an engineer who held master's degrees in both mechanical and electrical engineering. His father's example, together with the technical books kept in the family home, helped create an environment in which curiosity could grow.

Popular Mechanics was a particular source of fascination. Otto remembers reading about machines and inventions and imagining how he might build them himself. One idea that stayed with him was an electrically powered soapbox car, using a starter motor as its drive.

That curiosity was practical as well as imaginative. He experimented whenever he had the opportunity, sometimes with more enthusiasm than caution. On one occasion, he dismantled one of his father's cameras to discover how it worked. The investigation was successful; getting the camera back together was another matter.

Those early experiences were followed by railway apprenticeship and working life, where technical curiosity met formal training, real equipment and practical responsibility.

Read [Chapter One — The Engineer's Son](https://plebware.github.io/author/non-fiction/2026/11/26/the-engineers-son/) for the full story of these beginnings.

## 🧭 A Life That Did Not Follow a Straight Line.

Otto was born in Johannesburg on 18 January 1963. His working life and interests have crossed several fields, including electrical work, railway engineering, business, journalism, web development, writing, graphic creation, Linux and software experimentation.

He describes his younger self as shy and reserved, sometimes rebellious, ambitious and impatient, but not naturally a risk-taker. He also remembers a young man who wanted to enjoy life: going dancing, visiting nightclubs, watching films and spending time with friends rather than treating every day as a serious exercise in career planning.

Over time, his social confidence and approach to relationships changed. Today he describes himself as easygoing, comfortable making friends and fortunate to have a small core of dependable people, alongside a much wider circle of acquaintances. He also recognises that choosing whom to trust has remained a lesson throughout his life.

This series will not pretend that every stage was carefully planned. Some opportunities were pursued; others were missed. Some projects succeeded; others were interrupted by circumstances, limited resources or decisions made without enough information.

## 💻 From 0tronix to PlebWare.

Otto's early business journey included 0tronix Developments, established in 2003. At the time, he was still learning web design and had much to learn about search engine optimisation and the commercial side of maintaining an online business.

After his mother's death, practical difficulties involving a telephone account registered in her name and the administration of the estate disrupted his ability to maintain the business's online presence. He continued taking smaller administrative and design jobs, but struggled to regain the momentum he had hoped to build in web development.

The PlebWare idea followed a different path. The concept emerged in 2006 and developed into a more formal project in the years that followed. PlebWare.com eventually gained strong search visibility, but the business had not built sufficient financial contingencies to protect the domain when the COVID-19 period brought further financial pressure. In 2020, Otto could not afford its renewal and had to let the domain go.

The loss of that domain did not end the idea. The later GitHub-based PlebWare website gave him another way to pursue many of the ambitions that had originally inspired the project, using accessible publishing tools and a structure he could continue to develop.

The entrepreneurial chapters will examine these events as lessons in experience, preparation, resilience and the importance of protecting creative work.

## ✍️ One Person, Several Creative Voices.

Otto's work appears under more than one name. Othello Cody Verrocchio is his literary identity for creative writing, including science fiction. Cody — Dei Scriptor, or God's Journalist, is associated with his Christian writing. Captain Gemini is used for journalistic work and selected PlebWare reporting.

These identities help distinguish different kinds of writing and creative purpose. They do not represent separate people. Behind the names is one author who remains responsible for the ideas, decisions and work.

His interests are broad, but the intention is not to claim mastery of every subject. It is to keep learning, to explore connections between disciplines and to share useful knowledge where possible.

## ✝️ Faith, Purpose and the Meaning of Success.

Otto identifies his Christian faith as the principal driving force behind his present work. He wants what he creates to glorify God, while recognising that faith, personal history and character are matters to approach with honesty rather than self-congratulation.

He also wants to leave something meaningful behind, particularly for Jullian "Juelz" De Villiers, his long-standing collaborator, and for other people who may benefit from the work.

Financial reward matters too. Otto wants his online activities to become sustainable and to provide a return for years of effort. But he does not define success as wealth or power. He would rather see PlebWare help millions of people while providing him with a modest, dependable living than accumulate great wealth from a project that helps very few.

That distinction is central to understanding the philosophy behind PlebWare: purpose matters, but sustainability helps useful work endure.

## 🛠️ The Work Still in Progress.

Otto considers the present PlebWare website his greatest achievement to date, not simply because it is his newest project, but because it has realised many of the ambitions he once pursued through PlebWare.com.

PlebMachine is another part of that work. Its purpose is to make computing more responsive to the person using it. Otto's immediate ambition is not endless expansion, but a stable, dependable system with a small number of carefully chosen additions.

His five-year goals are substantial: publish all twenty-one planned science-fiction books, grow the PlebWare ecosystem to approximately 5,800 quality articles, establish a stable PlebMachine and make the work financially sustainable.

These are ambitions, not promises that every target is guaranteed. The chapters will explore what they mean and what practical steps might bring them closer.

## 📚 The Twelve Chapters.

This biographical series will be developed as twelve detailed articles. Each chapter will explore its subject in depth, using Otto's recollections, relevant existing material and careful clarification where the historical record is incomplete.

1. **The Engineer's Son.** — The father, the family library, Popular Mechanics, early experiments and the roots of technical curiosity.
2. **The Young Man and His Dreams.** — Youth, personality, friendships, entertainment, ambition and the experiences that shaped the young Otto.
3. **From Railway Engineering to Computing.** — Apprenticeship, electrical work, practical systems thinking and the move into computers and digital technology.
4. **The Entrepreneur and His Setbacks.** — 0tronix Developments, the early PlebWare idea, PlebWare.com, search visibility and the lessons of running a small business.
5. **The Losses That Changed His Direction.** — Bereavement, the loss of the home, interrupted plans and the personal consequences of difficult decisions.
6. **The Many Voices of One Author.** — Othello Cody Verrocchio, God's Journalist, Captain Gemini and the creative purpose behind distinct writing identities.
7. **Faith, Redemption and Purpose.** — The Christian journey and the desire to make faith part of everyday work and service.
8. **Jullian and the Importance of Relationships.** — Collaboration, friendship, trust and the human connections behind the projects.
9. **The Philosophy Behind PlebWare.** — The human element, the Computing Triangle and the principle that technology must serve humanity.
10. **The Persistent Builder.** — PlebWare, PlebMachine, learning, experimentation and the habit of returning to unfinished ideas.
11. **The 2031 Vision.** — Twenty-one books, 5,800 quality articles, a stable PlebMachine and a sustainable future.
12. **The Legacy Yet to Be Written.** — What should endure, what should be passed on and what it means to leave something useful behind.

The chapters will be published progressively. Each will receive its own article, and this introduction will be updated with direct links as the chapters become available.
