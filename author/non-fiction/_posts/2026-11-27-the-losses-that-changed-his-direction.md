# Chapter Five — The Losses That Changed His Direction. 🧭

*Some losses change what a person owns. Others change where a person lives, what they can pursue, and how they approach the future.*

A person's direction in life is not always changed by a single decision or dramatic event. Sometimes it changes through a series of losses and difficult circumstances, each one affecting the choices that follow.

For Otto Brinkmeier, three experiences stand out: the disruption that followed his mother's death, the loss of his home in 2010, and the eventual loss of PlebWare.com during the COVID-19 period.

They were different kinds of losses. One affected his family life and early business activities. Another changed his living circumstances almost overnight. The third brought an end to the original online home of a project into which he had invested years of effort.

This chapter follows those events without pretending that every setback had a hidden benefit. Some things were simply lost. What matters is how these experiences became part of the history of the person who continued to learn, create and build.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## 💔 The First Major Disruption — Losing His Mother.

In 2004, Otto lost his mother. Her death was a significant event in his personal life, but its consequences also reached into his working circumstances.

At the time, Otto was involved in his entrepreneurial and web-development activities through 0tronix Developments.

A practical complication followed the bereavement. The telephone connection on which he relied was registered in his mother's name. As her estate was being administered, the account was dealt with and the telephone service was disconnected. The property and associated estate matters were tied up in the process, making it difficult for Otto to arrange a replacement connection.

Today, losing a telephone connection might sound like a manageable inconvenience. At that time, however, reliable communications were important to running a small business, dealing with clients and maintaining an online presence.

The disruption affected Otto's ability to continue operating as he had before. He gradually moved towards administrative work, and the momentum he had hoped to build in web development became harder to recover.

The business did not simply fade because he had lost interest in technology. Personal circumstances had disrupted some of the practical foundations on which the work depended.

Sometimes a major turning point does not arrive as one dramatic event. It develops through a series of complications that gradually make the original direction more difficult to follow.

## 🏠 2010 — The Loss of a Home.

Of all the setbacks Otto has described, the sale of his home in 2010 stands out as his greatest personal failure.

The situation began with occupants who were not paying rent. Otto wanted to put pressure on them to resolve the matter, and he approached an estate agent. He signed a sole mandate without fully understanding the implications of the document or how the situation might develop.

What began as an attempt to address a difficult property situation became far more consequential than he had intended. The legal position surrounding the occupants made the matter more complicated than he had anticipated, and the eventual outcome was the sale of his home.

But the sale itself was only the beginning of another difficult period.

### ⏳ One Month to Leave.

An important condition of the sale was that Otto had to vacate the property within one month of receiving payment.

That left very little time to organise the next stage of life. He needed transport, somewhere to stay, and a way to move his belongings. There was no comfortable period in which to compare every option or plan a gradual transition.

He had to make major decisions in a hurry.

### 🚐 The Tata Telcoline and the Caravan.

In those circumstances, Otto bought a Tata Telcoline bakkie, footstoots. He needed a vehicle, and the purchase was made under pressure. Unfortunately, the bakkie later let him down, becoming another difficulty rather than the dependable transport he had hoped to secure.

He also needed accommodation.

His original intention was to buy a caravan from a dealer in Pretoria, but the dealer did not have the particular model he wanted. With time running out, Otto eventually bought a second-hand caravan privately from someone known through his family and friends.

The caravan became the immediate answer to a very practical problem: where would he and Jullian live?

For a period, Otto and Jullian stayed in that caravan in a friend's backyard.

The contrast was considerable. They had previously had a home of their own. Now they were living in a second-hand caravan parked on somebody else's property. It was not a lifestyle choice or a planned adventure. It was a temporary arrangement made necessary by the circumstances surrounding the sale and the short time allowed to leave.

The hurried vehicle purchase, the second-hand caravan and the arrangement in the backyard were all connected to the same underlying pressure. Otto had to find a way forward within a month.

Looking back, he recognises that he had not fully understood the implications of the estate agent's mandate and had not adequately prepared for how the situation might unfold. The experience remains a painful reminder that important property and financial decisions deserve careful investigation, especially when the consequences may affect one's home and security.

A home represents more than a building. It is a place of continuity, privacy and stability. Losing it is difficult enough; having to organise a new living arrangement under an immediate deadline adds another layer of strain.

## 🌧️ 2020 — When PlebWare.com Was Lost.

A decade later, another important part of Otto's life and work was affected by circumstances he could not easily overcome.

The COVID-19 period hit Otto and those around him hard financially. When the renewal of PlebWare.com became due, he did not have the money available to keep the domain.

The renewal cost was approximately R2,000, as he recalls it. He tried to find a way to raise the money, but he could not obtain a personal loan from anyone, nor could he secure a donation to cover the renewal.

Without the funds, he had to let the domain go.

It was not a decision made because he no longer believed in PlebWare. He wanted to keep the domain, but the money simply was not available at the time.

Losing PlebWare.com meant losing the original online home of a project that had taken years to develop. The website had built a strong search-engine presence, and Otto had taken considerable pride in seeing an independent project achieve that level of visibility.

The loss was therefore more than the loss of a web address. It meant losing an established digital identity and the online home associated with years of work.

The experience exposed a difficult distinction: building something valuable and having the financial means to protect it are two different responsibilities. Even a project with a strong online presence still depends on practical resources to remain online.

## 🔍 What These Losses Had in Common.

The events of 2004, 2010 and 2020 were not identical, and it would be wrong to force them into one simple explanation.

The death of Otto's mother was a bereavement followed by practical complications that disrupted his early business activities.

The sale of his home involved decisions and circumstances that he now believes he should have approached with more preparation and a fuller understanding of the commitments involved. The one-month deadline then forced him to arrange transport and accommodation in a hurry.

The loss of PlebWare.com came during a period of financial hardship when even a relatively modest renewal fee was beyond his reach, and attempts to obtain help were unsuccessful.

Each experience exposed a different kind of vulnerability: dependence on arrangements that could change, the importance of understanding legal and financial commitments, and the need for resources to protect something built over time.

There are lessons in these experiences, but lessons do not undo consequences. Understanding what might have been done differently does not restore a home, remove the difficulty of a rushed move, or bring back a domain that has been lost.

The purpose of remembering these events is not to assign blame or manufacture a heroic ending. It is to understand the circumstances that helped change Otto's direction.

## 🛠️ Losing the Domain Did Not End the Idea.

Although PlebWare.com was lost, the idea behind PlebWare remained.

The later GitHub-based PlebWare website gave Otto another way to continue developing the project. It became a broader knowledge and publishing platform, connected to other repositories and projects across the ecosystem.

PlebMachine also became part of that wider work, carrying forward Otto's long-standing interest in practical computing and technology that responds to the needs of ordinary people.

The present PlebWare project is not a restoration of the old domain or a claim that nothing was lost. It is a new stage built with the knowledge and experience that remained.

Otto now places particular importance on sustainability, careful development and protecting the work as it grows. Those priorities make more sense when viewed against the earlier experiences.

The modern [PlebWare website and ecosystem overview](https://plebware.github.io/everyday/about-plebware/2026/11/25/plebware-website-and-ecosystem-overview/) explains how the project has developed beyond its original online home.

## 🧭 What the Losses Left Behind.

It would be easy to describe these years as a succession of setbacks. That would be incomplete, but it would also be wrong to pretend that every loss was somehow necessary.

Some experiences were painful. Some decisions could have been approached differently. Some circumstances left Otto with very few practical choices.

What can be said is that he continued to learn, write, experiment and create.

He did not recover the original home through his later work. He could not reverse the circumstances that followed his mother's death. And the new PlebWare platform does not bring back the old domain or its accumulated history.

But he could decide what to do with the experience that remained.

The losses changed his direction. They influenced the way he thought about preparation, financial security, ownership and the importance of building something that could endure.

They did not bring his willingness to create to an end.
