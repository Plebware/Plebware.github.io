# 🍎 Rotten Fruit, Human Hearts, and the Creative Spark.

There are days when life gives you more to think about than you ever intended. Some thoughts arrive quietly, others come knocking at the door, and a few seem determined to sit down, make themselves comfortable, and refuse to leave.

Today was one of those days.

It became a day of reflection — about friendship, disappointment, forgiveness, healing, creativity, and the unexpected ways technology can bring people together.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The Basket of Rotten Fruit.

Imagine carrying a basket of fruit.

Some of the fruit is fresh. Some is bruised. And some has been left so long that it has started to rot.

Now imagine that every time someone hurts you, disappoints you, or lets you down, you place another piece of rotten fruit into that basket.

Eventually, the basket becomes heavy.

But here's the problem: we keep carrying it.

We revisit the memories. We examine what happened. We turn the fruit over, inspect the damage, and sometimes even ask ourselves whether we could have prevented it from rotting.

There comes a point when we have to put the basket down.

Forgiveness is putting the rotten fruit at the foot of the cross. Healing is learning not to return to the basket to count it.

That distinction matters.

Forgiveness does not necessarily mean that the hurt disappears. Nor does it mean that trust must immediately be restored. Sometimes forgiveness is a decision we make long before our emotions have caught up with it.

Healing takes time. It asks us to stop allowing yesterday's disappointment to occupy every corner of today's life.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## When Friendship Leaves Questions Behind.

One of the hardest things about disappointment is that it often comes from people who matter to us.

When expectations are broken, the pain is not always about the event itself. It is also about what the relationship meant, what we believed had been understood, and what we thought we could rely upon.

There are moments when decisions are made and understandings are reached, only for things to unfold differently.

For me, one particularly painful experience involved my son and his brother being excluded from witnessing the committal and scattering of their respective mothers' ashes.

This happened at the same time that the ashes of a man I had known for fifty-one years were being committed to the ocean.

There was a great deal of emotion surrounding those events. There was also disappointment, because what I believed had been understood and agreed upon was not honoured.

I cannot change what happened.

I can, however, decide what I do with the hurt.

That is not always easy. Especially when the people involved have been part of your life for many years.

Friendship is not measured only by the good memories we share. Sometimes it is tested by how we handle the difficult ones.

And sometimes, sadly, we discover that our understanding of a relationship was not quite the same as someone else's.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## The Analytical Mind and the Endless Questions.

There is another complication.

I have an analytical mind. I naturally examine things, look for causes, compare events, and try to understand how one thing led to another.

That ability can be useful. It helps when solving technical problems, developing software, writing, and working through complicated situations.

But when emotions are involved, the same ability can become exhausting.

The questions start.

Where did I go wrong?

Was it my fault?

Could I have done something differently?

Should I have said something?

What should I have done?

And around we go again.

It is almost as if the mind returns to the basket, picks up every piece of rotten fruit, and starts counting it all over again.

The difficulty is that some questions do not have satisfying answers. Some situations cannot be repaired by thinking harder. And some people will never give us the explanation we hoped to receive.

That does not mean we should stop thinking.

It means we must learn when thinking has served its purpose.

Sometimes the best thing we can do is turn our attention towards something constructive. Not to pretend that the hurt never happened, but to prevent it from becoming the only thing happening in our lives.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

## Humanity Has a Heart.

And this brings me to something rather different.

Music.

During our conversations, Julian and I have been exploring creative possibilities with the assistance of artificial intelligence.

One of the interesting things about this process is that it does not have to follow a rigid formula.

A person can bring lyrics, a melody, a mood, a concept, or simply an idea. The other person can add something, change the direction, or take the idea somewhere unexpected.

Sometimes Julian starts something and I add my twist. Sometimes I start something and Julian takes it further.

We can pass ideas back and forth until something begins to take shape.

Gemini has become part of that creative process, helping us turn ideas and direction into musical productions.

But the important thing is that the human creative spark comes first.

The AI can help produce the music, explore arrangements, and bring an idea to life. Yet the inspiration, the choices, the meaning, and the intention belong to the people working together.

One of the themes that has emerged is simple:

**Humanity Has a Heart.**

That is more than a possible musical theme. It is also a reminder of what we believe technology should serve.

## Boy Mist, Crazy Otter, and Scribble and Scratch.

As we talked about the music, some names began to emerge.

Not everything needs to be placed under one umbrella. Sometimes individual creativity deserves its own identity, while collaboration deserves a name of its own.

For now, these are the identities that make sense to us.

**Boy Mist** is Julian's personal creative identity — his own music, ideas, and individual creations.

**Crazy Otter** is mine.

I did briefly consider Crazy Otto, but I rather like Crazy Otter. It has a little more character, a little more mischief, and perhaps a better ring to it.

And then there is **Scribble and Scratch**.

That is the shared creative identity — the space where Julian and I can bring our ideas together, experiment, exchange contributions, and create something jointly.

There is no strict division of labour here. We do not have to decide that one person always writes while the other always produces, or that one person must finish what the other starts.

The whole point is that creativity can move in either direction.

An idea can begin with Boy Mist and acquire a little Crazy Otter along the way. Or Crazy Otter can start something that Boy Mist takes in a completely different direction.

And when the two of us work together, Scribble and Scratch is where the magic might happen.

These names are not announcements of completed albums or a finished music catalogue. They are the beginnings of a creative identity.

There is still plenty of scribbling and scratching to do!

## A Showcase, Not a Squeeze Page.

This conversation has also sparked an idea beyond music.

What if we created a place where our creative work could live together?

Music, videos, writing, graphics, experiments, and other projects could all have a home.

Not necessarily a complicated commercial platform. Not a website designed to pressure people into buying something.

A showcase.

A place where people can discover what we have created, enjoy it, and perhaps support the work if they wish.

The idea of offering some digital creations for a small contribution — perhaps the price of a cup of coffee — is worth exploring.

But there are practical matters to consider before anything is sold. We would need to check the relevant AI service terms, licensing conditions, and copyright arrangements for any music or other material produced with AI assistance.

We must also be honest about attribution and the contributions made by everyone involved.

A ZIP file with a password might make access a little less convenient for casual sharing, but it does not prevent redistribution once somebody has extracted the contents. We should never confuse a small obstacle with genuine protection.

The spirit of the idea is more important than the technical packaging.

**Not a squeeze page. It's our showcase!**

## Technology Must Serve Humanity.

This brings me back to something that has been at the heart of my PlebWare project for many years.

Technology must serve humanity, not the other way around.

A computer should not dictate how a person thinks, works, or lives. It should help people achieve their objectives while leaving the decisions in human hands.

The same principle applies to artificial intelligence.

AI can be an extraordinary tool. It can help us write, compose, research, design, develop software, and explore ideas that might otherwise remain unexplored.

But it should not replace the human being.

The computer does not make the decision. It helps you think through the decision.

That is the philosophy behind PlebWare, and it is also something I see in our musical experiments.

We are not trying to prove that a machine can be human.

We are exploring what human beings can create when they have useful tools at their disposal.

Convenience is the objective. Empowerment is the outcome. Human freedom remains the safeguard.

## When Life Interrupts the Creative Process.

Of course, life does not always respect our plans.

The day had already begun with an interruption: a telephone call at around one in the morning from someone dealing with difficulties in his marriage.

Then there was the electricity outage, which added another layer of uncertainty to an already disrupted day.

These are the things that happen in ordinary life. The phone rings. The power goes off. Someone needs to talk. Plans change.

And yet, somewhere in between all those interruptions, we found time to create.

That is perhaps one of the things I appreciate about creative work.

It gives the mind somewhere constructive to go.

It does not erase disappointment. It does not solve every personal problem. But it reminds us that life is still capable of producing something worthwhile, even when the day has not gone according to plan.

Perhaps that is one way of putting the basket down.

Not by pretending the fruit was never rotten, but by deciding that there are still good things worth growing.

## The Things We Choose to Carry.

I started this reflection thinking about rotten fruit.

I end it thinking about what we choose to carry forward.

We cannot rewrite every conversation, repair every friendship, or undo every disappointment. We cannot make other people honour an understanding they have chosen not to honour.

But we can choose what receives our attention next.

We can forgive without pretending that nothing happened. We can allow ourselves time to heal without making our hurt our permanent identity.

We can continue creating, learning, laughing, and exploring.

And sometimes, when life gives us a particularly difficult day, we can sit down with someone we care about, exchange a few ideas, and make something completely unexpected.

Perhaps a song.

Perhaps a story.

Perhaps a little corner of the internet where people can see what two human beings have made with the help of an artificial intelligence.

And perhaps, somewhere along the way, we will discover that the creative spark is still very much alive.

The basket can stay where we left it.

There is more to life than counting rotten fruit.
