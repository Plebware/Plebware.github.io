# 🌱 Chapter Two — The Young Man and His Dreams.

*Growing up, discovering independence, enjoying life and finding a direction in a world that was becoming increasingly digital.*

The boy from the previous chapter was growing up. The curiosity was still there, but childhood experiments and technical magazines were no longer the whole story.

There was a young man discovering the world, forming friendships, enjoying himself, making mistakes and beginning to imagine what his future might look like.

I was not someone who spent every waking moment planning a career. I had ambitions, certainly, but I also wanted to enjoy being young. There were nightclubs, dancing, movies, friendships and all the ordinary distractions that make up a young person's life.

Yet somewhere beneath all that, an idea was taking shape.

I wanted to become an electrical engineer who worked with computers. I was particularly interested in computer programming, and an important influence on that ambition was the Commodore VIC-20.

💻 Looking back, I can see that this was more than an interest in a new machine. It was the beginning of a dream that would eventually connect many different parts of my life.

## 🧑‍🦱 The Young Man Behind the Ambition.

I would describe my younger self as shy and reserved. I was not necessarily the person who walked into a room and immediately became the centre of attention.

I could also be rebellious at times. I had ambitions, and I could be impatient when things did not move as quickly as I wanted them to.

There was a certain contradiction in my personality. I wanted to achieve things, but I was not always prepared to sacrifice the enjoyment of the present for the sake of some distant future.

I was not particularly inclined to spend all my time worrying about where I would be in five or ten years. I wanted to experience life as it happened.

That did not mean I lacked direction or ambition. It simply meant that I was still finding my feet.

Like many young people, I was learning about myself while trying to work out what I wanted from life.

🔍 The benefit of looking back is that I can now recognise qualities that I did not necessarily understand at the time. Ambition, impatience, curiosity and a desire for independence were all present, even if they did not always work together particularly well.

## 🕺 There Was More to Life Than Engineering.

It would be misleading to portray my younger years as one long technical exercise.

I enjoyed myself.

Nightclubs, dancing, movies and spending time enjoying life were all part of that period. I was interested in more than engineering and computers, and I certainly did not approach every day as though I were preparing for an important examination.

There is a difference between having a dream and being completely consumed by it.

I had a picture of what I wanted to become, but I also wanted to live. I wanted to meet people, experience different things and enjoy the freedom that comes with being young.

Perhaps that balance was not always ideal. There were times when I could have been more focused, more patient or more deliberate about the decisions I made.

But those experiences were part of growing up, too.

🎬 Life was not simply about reaching a destination. There were friendships to make, places to go, music to enjoy and memories to create.

I would not rewrite my younger years as though I had been a perfectly disciplined young engineer. That would not be an honest account of the person I was.

## 💻 The VIC-20 and the Computer Dream.

One of the most important influences on my ambitions was the Commodore VIC-20.

The VIC-20 helped introduce me to the possibilities of personal computing. It gave me a glimpse of a world in which a computer was not merely a machine that performed a fixed task. It could be instructed, programmed and used as a tool for creating something.

That idea appealed to me.

I was interested in computer programming, and I wanted to work with computers as part of an engineering career. I could see a connection between electrical engineering and the emerging world of digital technology.

The ambition was quite specific.

I wanted to become an **electrical engineer who worked with computers and was involved in computer programming**.

That is an important distinction. I was not simply fascinated by owning a computer or using one for entertainment. I was interested in what could be done with it.

The possibility of giving a machine instructions and making it perform a task was particularly attractive to someone who had grown up wondering how mechanical and electrical things worked.

⚡ My father's engineering influence had helped establish the curiosity. The VIC-20 helped give that curiosity a new direction.

The world of computers offered another kind of workshop: one where ideas could be tested through logic, instructions and programming rather than only through physical components.

I was beginning to imagine myself working in that world.

For a more detailed account of those early computer experiences, see [Teenage Dreams! — Before the Idea, It Was There](https://plebware.github.io/everyday/news/2026/09/11/before-idea-it-was-there/). That article explores the VIC-20, Microsoft BASIC and the programming ideas that stayed with me.

## 🧭 A Dream, Not Yet a Roadmap.

Having an ambition and knowing exactly how to achieve it are two very different things.

I knew what I wanted to become, but that does not mean I had a complete plan for getting there.

I was still young, still developing as a person and still discovering the opportunities available to me. I did not have the benefit of looking backwards and knowing which decisions would prove important.

There was also a practical side to life. Opportunities, circumstances and resources influence what a person can pursue. A dream may be clear even when the route towards it is not.

I do not want to manufacture a neat story in which every decision led directly towards the person I am today. That would be convenient, but it would not be truthful.

My ambitions developed alongside ordinary life. I enjoyed myself, made choices, learned from experiences and continued to carry an interest in engineering and computers.

The important point is that the computer dream was there.

It was not something I invented much later to make my biography sound more impressive. The VIC-20 had made an impression, and I wanted a future in which computers and engineering played a central role.

🔑 Sometimes a person's direction begins with a simple idea long before the opportunity to pursue it becomes clear.

## 🤝 Friendships, Trust and the Lessons of Growing Up.

Relationships were another part of growing up.

I tried to make relationships work, but I did not always choose the right people. That is one of those lessons that can take a considerable amount of time to understand.

When you are young, you do not always recognise the difference between someone who is simply present in your life and someone who is genuinely dependable.

You learn through experience.

My personality has changed over the years. Today, I am considerably more easygoing and comfortable meeting people. I can make friends quite easily, although I still value a relatively small circle of dependable friends more than a large collection of superficial relationships.

I have around five close friends whom I regard as reliable, alongside many acquaintances.

And, even now, choosing the right people remains an important lesson.

❤️ Looking back, I can see that growing up was not only about discovering what I wanted to do professionally. It was also about discovering what kind of people I wanted around me, what I valued in a relationship and what trust really meant.

Those lessons would become important in later chapters.

## ⚙️ Ambition, Independence and the Desire to Build.

There was another characteristic that would become increasingly important as I grew older: my desire for independence.

I was comfortable taking instructions in a working environment. I understood that a workplace has responsibilities, procedures and people who must coordinate their efforts.

My objection was never simply that somebody else was giving the instructions.

What I disliked was the idea of working hard, earning mediocre wages and watching somebody else benefit disproportionately from the value of my effort.

I wanted the opportunity to build something of my own.

That does not mean I was naturally a risk-taker. In fact, I would not describe myself as particularly adventurous when it came to taking risks. I could be ambitious and impatient without necessarily being reckless.

I enjoyed learning, and I wanted to put knowledge to practical use whenever circumstances and resources allowed.

That combination would eventually influence my approach to business, technology and the projects I created.

🛠️ The desire was not merely to have a job. It was to develop the ability to create, improve and build something that had value beyond the immediate task.

At the time, I could not have known exactly where that desire would lead.

## 🌄 The Young Man and the Future He Imagined.

When I look back at that younger version of myself, I see someone who had several sides to his personality.

I was shy and reserved, but I had ambitions.

I could be rebellious and impatient, but I was not someone who enjoyed taking unnecessary risks.

I wanted to enjoy life, but I also wanted to achieve something meaningful.

I was interested in engineering, but my imagination was increasingly drawn towards computers and programming.

The Commodore VIC-20 helped make that future seem possible. It connected the technical curiosity I had developed as a boy with a new world of digital possibilities.

I did not yet know about the businesses I would attempt, the websites I would create, the writing I would produce or the software projects that would eventually occupy so much of my attention.

I certainly did not know that one day I would be building an ecosystem called PlebWare.

But the ingredients were beginning to come together.

The influence of my father. The curiosity of childhood. The attraction of electrical engineering. The fascination with computers. The desire to learn and the wish to build something of my own.

🚀 The young man had a dream. The road towards it, however, would not be a straight one.

## 🚂 Looking Ahead — From Dreams to Working Life.

Dreams eventually meet reality.

The ambition to become an electrical engineer working with computers was an important part of my thinking as a young man. But the next stage of my life would involve practical working experience, training and the development of technical skills.

My railway apprenticeship and the work that followed would become an important part of that journey.

It was where technical knowledge would increasingly meet practical responsibility, real equipment and the demands of working life.

The article [From Railways to Linux](https://plebware.github.io/everyday/news/2026/06/10/from-railways-to-linux/) provides another perspective on how those practical engineering principles later informed my approach to computers and Linux.

The next chapter in this biography will explore that transition in greater depth: **from railway engineering to computing**, and how the experiences of those years helped shape the technical abilities and interests that would later find expression in my own projects.

The dream was taking shape. Now life would begin testing it.
