# ✍️ The Human Behind the Machine: Faith, Thought and Artificial Intelligence.

*Can artificial intelligence make us better thinkers without doing our thinking for us?*

As a writer, I have a mandate to **honour God**. I have always believed, and will continue to believe, that technology is a tool. Artificial intelligence, especially, must never become a replacement for faith, wisdom, conscience, or prayer.

Writing has become my number-one passion lately, although, somehow, I think it has always been there.

## 🔥 The Fire That Burns Within Me.

Yes, I love a variety of things: graphic creation and manipulation, developing and testing new software, cooking, gardening, and personal fitness, to mention just a few.

However, the fire that burns brightest in me is my love of technology and my desire to understand systems.

That does not mean I am predicting a dystopian future in which machines take over the world. My technological interests naturally draw me towards science fiction and the scientific world, but that does not make technology taboo for me as a Christian.

On the contrary, I firmly believe that God has given humanity the ability to develop and use technology to improve our lives. What matters is how we apply it, the purposes we pursue, and the responsibility we accept for the consequences.

A tool can accomplish wonderful things in capable hands. The same tool can cause harm when it is used carelessly or without wisdom.

**The question is not simply what technology can do. It is what we choose to do with it.**

## 📖 Looking at Technology Through the Eyes of Faith.

I have been a Christian for more than forty-seven years, and I have spent much of that time exploring theology and drinking deeply from God's Word.

When I consider technology, I sometimes find myself looking at biblical passages through the eyes of a man who has spent much of his working life dealing with complex systems.

Two passages, in particular, have caught my attention.

### 🔥 Elijah and the Chariot of Fire.

**Scripture: 2 Kings 2:11–12.**

The biblical account describes a chariot of fire and horses of fire separating Elijah and Elisha, after which Elijah is taken up into heaven in a whirlwind.

As someone fascinated by technology, I cannot help noticing the imagery of movement, separation, and a dramatic means of transport.

The ancient Near Eastern chariot was an important technological achievement, combining wheels, axles, construction techniques, and a means of propulsion supplied by animals. The biblical account, however, describes a supernatural event, not a technical specification for a machine.

I am not claiming that Elijah's chariot was an ordinary vehicle or that the passage proves the existence of advanced human engineering in biblical times.

Rather, I find myself reflecting on how God can use imagery familiar to people to communicate something far greater than the technology they understand.

### ⚙️ Ezekiel's Wheels Within Wheels.

**Scripture: Ezekiel 1:15–21 and Ezekiel 10:9–13.**

Ezekiel describes extraordinary wheels associated with the living creatures in his vision. Their appearance is striking, and their movement is closely connected with the movement of the living creatures and the Spirit.

The expression *wheels within wheels* naturally catches the attention of anyone interested in mechanical design.

My engineering mind immediately begins thinking about nested wheel assemblies, coordinated movement, articulation, and the ability to move in different directions.

Modern engineering provides examples of specialised wheel systems, including omnidirectional wheels and mecanum wheels, that allow machinery and robots to move in ways that ordinary wheels cannot.

I see an interesting analogy here, but I recognise the distinction: Ezekiel's vision is a biblical description of a divine revelation, not an engineering blueprint for modern robotics.

The important spiritual message is not that Ezekiel secretly designed a robot. It is that the vision communicates God's majesty, authority, and activity.

For me, these passages are reminders that technical curiosity and Christian faith do not have to be enemies.

**I can marvel at the engineering of the world without forgetting the Creator.**

## 🛠️ How Writing Benefits Otto.

So, how does writing benefit Otto?

Personally, I have noticed that the more I write, with the assistance of technology, the better my writing is becoming.

My journey with word processing began long before today's AI assistants. Over the years, I worked with WordPerfect, Lotus Word Pro, Microsoft Word in the earlier stages, and LibreOffice Writer in more recent years.

Collectively, these tools have accompanied me through approximately twenty-seven years of word processing.

For roughly seven years, I also used Grammarly, a writing assistant that employs artificial intelligence techniques, including Natural Language Processing (NLP), alongside Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning technologies.

My journey progressed to ChatGPT, which I was using by August 2026. That marked another step in my exploration of how AI could support my writing, sharpen my thinking, and help me turn ideas into meaningful, practical work.

My biggest takeaway has been a marked improvement in efficiency and productivity.

Has AI been detrimental to my writing? No. My experience has been the opposite.

It has made writing easier, helped me improve my skills, and taught me something through our daily interactions. I am naturally curious, so I enjoy reading the explanations behind suggested changes: why a sentence has been restructured, why a grammatical correction is recommended, or why a different expression might communicate my meaning more clearly.

I do not want a machine simply to correct my words. I want to understand the correction.

That distinction matters.

When I learn why a sentence works better, I can apply that lesson to future writing. The benefit can extend beyond the individual article.

**Otto is getting better, not regressing.**

## ⚡ An Electrical Tradesman's Mind.

I must admit that I am fortunate to have an analytical mind. I love systems, and I believe that inclination was strengthened during my years as an electrical tradesman working for the South African Railways in the field of electromotive power.

We worked with 3,000-volt direct-current systems. There was no room for careless assumptions. Preventative maintenance was paramount, and we had to understand our systems thoroughly.

An electrical fault was not merely an inconvenience to be brushed aside. We had to understand how the system worked, identify the source of a problem, and take the appropriate corrective action.

My work was not limited to electrical circuits, either. As an Electrical-Fitter, I also worked extensively with electro-pneumatic valves used in the braking systems of locomotives.

For a good couple of years, I worked in the valve room at Braamfontein Depot, servicing and testing various complex brake components.

That work required patience, attention to detail, an understanding of interacting components, and respect for the consequences of getting something wrong.

Looking back, I realise that technology and I have walked alongside each other for many years. Much of my working life involved one technological system or another.

Today, when I work with artificial intelligence, I bring some of that same attitude to the table.

I want to understand the system. I want to know why something works, where it might fail, and how the individual components contribute to the final result.

I do not expect AI to be infallible, any more than I would expect a complex mechanical or electrical system to be immune to faults.

**Understanding a system is more valuable than blindly trusting it.**

## 📰 From Tradesman to Student of Journalism.

On 19 August 2020, I completed a diploma course in journalism.

Writing, observation, and curiosity have always been part of who I am. Journalism gave me another opportunity to develop those interests and to think more deliberately about communication.

My goal as an author is to bring the human perspective to the table.

Facts matter. Structure matters. Accuracy matters. But so do the people behind the facts, the experiences that shape our understanding, and the questions that deserve to be asked.

I do not want to write merely to fill a page. I want to communicate something worth considering.

That ambition influences the way I approach both writing and artificial intelligence.

## 🌐 Writing Is Essential to Making PlebWare.

My vision for PlebWare extends far beyond the sum of its present parts.

Yes, it is designed as a human-centred knowledge and technology project, specifically created to empower ordinary people. But it is also a place where I can put my thoughts, experiences, and recollections into words.

In many respects, PlebWare is becoming a living biography: a record of what I have learnt, what I have experienced, what I believe, and what I hope to contribute.

It is also a legacy that I want to leave for Juelz, my friend, spiritual son, and partner in technological development.

Many of my thoughts naturally develop into articles, lessons, reflections, and practical resources. Some even become comics or visual snapshots.

An idea that begins as a passing thought can become something another person reads years later.

That is one of the things I find exciting about writing for PlebWare. I am not merely recording information for the moment. I am building a body of knowledge that people can return to, learn from, and use.

The process begins with a thought, but it does not have to end there.

**Writing gives ideas a home. PlebWare gives those ideas a place where they can be shared.**

## 🤝 Writing That Benefits AI.

Here is another angle I find particularly interesting.

My writing and authoring skills do not merely benefit from artificial intelligence. They can also improve the quality of the work I develop with AI.

The relationship works in both directions.

AI can help me research, organise, challenge, refine, and implement ideas. But the quality of that collaboration depends partly on what I bring to it.

I bring the purpose, the experience, the questions, the context, and the judgement.

### 💡 Original Thought Gives AI Direction.

When I provide an original thought, AI can help me explore and develop it.

That thought might originate in my personal experiences, observations, questions, technical background, or faith. I decide which ideas matter to me and which ones are worth exploring.

The AI can help me discover possible approaches, but it does not possess my personal history or the precise meaning I attach to an experience simply because I have asked a question.

My contribution gives the work its human starting point.

### 🎯 Clear Communication Improves the Collaboration.

I have learnt that vague instructions often produce generic responses.

If I ask an AI to write an article about technology, the result could go in almost any direction.

However, if I explain that I want to explore how technology can empower ordinary people without taking away their independence, the task becomes much clearer.

That distinction changes the direction of the writing.

The more effectively I communicate my intention, audience, and purpose, the better the starting point I can provide.

Humans sometimes need to relearn the art of communication when working with machines. We cannot always assume that a loosely expressed thought will produce the result we imagined.

I do not always get the instructions right myself. That is part of the learning process.

### 🧭 Human Judgement Remains Essential.

AI can produce fluent sentences that sound convincing while missing my intended meaning.

Fluency alone does not make something accurate, relevant, or worthwhile.

This is where my experience as a writer becomes important. I must be able to evaluate the result and ask myself:

- Does it express what I actually mean?
- Is the argument coherent?
- Does the tone suit the subject?
- Is anything exaggerated or misleading?
- Does it sound like something I would genuinely stand behind?

I can then accept, reject, correct, or redirect the output.

My editorial judgement helps determine whether the result meets the standard I want to publish under my name.

This is not a claim that my instructions make an AI system learn permanently or that every interaction improves the underlying model. It is about something more immediate and practical: **I can guide the work in front of me towards a better result.**

### 🏗️ From Conversation to Lasting Knowledge.

My real goal is to help AI-assisted writing become useful to others.

Consider the difference between two outcomes.

The first is an AI response that answers a question during a conversation.

The second is a carefully developed PlebWare article that explains the answer, supplies context, considers limitations, and remains available for future readers.

The first may solve a problem in the moment. The second can become an educational resource.

My authoring process can turn a temporary exchange into something more durable. I can develop the initial answer through revision, research, examples, and practical conclusions.

That is where my writing skills connect directly to the vision for PlebWare: freely shared knowledge that people can learn from and use.

In this sense, my writing contributes direction, context, and editorial standards to AI-assisted work. AI helps me develop the material, and I remain responsible for deciding what is worth sharing.

**The human supplies the purpose. The machine assists with the work. The published result must serve the reader.**

## 🔑 The Message: Better Tools, Better Thinking.

When I work with AI, I try to bring structure to the collaboration. I want to move from an initial thought to a clear explanation, and from that explanation to something useful.

I am still learning. I still make mistakes. Sometimes my instructions are unclear, and sometimes an AI response needs considerable correction.

That is no reason to abandon the process. It is a reason to participate more thoughtfully.

My years in technical work taught me to respect systems without assuming they are perfect. My experience with writing tools has taught me that assistance can improve the way I work. My faith reminds me that technology must not replace conscience, wisdom, or responsibility.

And my work on PlebWare gives these lessons a practical destination.

I believe that writing benefits from AI when the technology helps us express, examine, and develop our thoughts. I also believe AI-assisted work benefits from a human author who can supply meaning, context, and direction, then take responsibility for the final result.

The relationship should not be about surrendering our thinking to a machine.

It should be about learning to think, communicate, and create more effectively with the tools available to us.

**AI can help us express our thoughts, but we remain responsible for what we believe, publish, and do.**

That is the human being behind the machine.
