# When Flu Costs More Than a Day.
<img src="/assets/images/fighting-the-flu.webp"
     alt="Hanging On - Fighting Off Flu, When you old, or at any age"
     style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">

There is nothing glamorous about having the flu.

One moment you are trying to get through an ordinary week, thinking about work, bills and the next job. Then, almost without warning, your body declares war.

The headache arrives first—or perhaps the aching muscles. Your nose begins running as though somebody opened a tap and forgot to close it. Your joints hurt. Your muscles feel stiff. You are exhausted, yet somehow too uncomfortable to sleep properly.

And then comes the irritability.

Everything becomes annoying.

The noise. The light. The phone. The questions. Even the innocent little things that normally wouldn't bother you suddenly feel like personal attacks from the universe.

You know what you should do.

**Rest.**

But there is another voice in your head.

**Work means money.**

For someone paid by the day, staying home isn't simply taking a sick day. It can mean sacrificing the money that would have paid for food, transport, electricity or something else that cannot wait.

That makes the decision brutally simple—and brutally difficult.

Today, the responsible thing may be to stay in bed.

Not because you are lazy.

Not because you don't want to work.

Because your body is already working overtime.

Your immune system is fighting an infection, and forcing yourself through a physically demanding day can leave you feeling considerably worse. Flu can produce a sudden combination of fever, headache, muscle and joint pain, fatigue, cough, sore throat and a runny nose. Most people recover without specific treatment, but some develop serious complications.

So sometimes the wisest investment you can make is to lose one day's income rather than turn one illness into a much longer problem.

It is frustrating.

It is expensive.

And it is unfair.

But there is a difference between **giving up** and **stopping to recover**.

The flu may take today's working hours.

It doesn't get to take tomorrow.
