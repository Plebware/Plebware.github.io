# Jack and Jill — The Hill We Choose.

## Jack and Jill Went Up the Hill.

Once upon a time, Jack and Jill went up the hill with dreams much like everyone else. They wanted a better life, a secure home, enough money to live, and perhaps a family of their own. The hill represented hope, ambition and the belief that tomorrow could be better than today.

But real life is rarely as simple as a nursery rhyme. Sometimes people begin their climb with good intentions and still find themselves taking a wrong turn.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Then Came the Choices.

A drink becomes another drink. A harmless-looking bet becomes gambling. A temporary escape becomes a habit. Alcohol, drugs and addiction can gradually turn something that once seemed like a choice into something that begins making the choices for you.

The danger is that the fall rarely happens all at once. It can happen one decision at a time — until the person looks around and realises that they are much farther down the hill than they ever intended to be.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## And Then They Fell.

The old rhyme says that Jack and Jill went up the hill to fetch a pail of water, and then came tumbling down. In our modern version, the fall represents the consequences that can follow destructive choices: financial ruin, broken relationships, addiction, homelessness, damaged health and the loss of trust.

Sometimes children become caught in the consequences as well. A person's bad choices can create a chain reaction that reaches far beyond the person who made them.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The Crown Is Broken.

Breaking the crown is more than a bump on the head. It represents the moment when the consequences become impossible to ignore. The life that Jack and Jill imagined for themselves has been damaged, and there may be no simple way to put everything back together.

Yet this is where another question begins: **Does a person who has made terrible choices become a terrible person forever?**

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The Climb Back.

Recovery does not erase the past. It does not immediately restore trust, repair families, remove debt or provide a home. The consequences remain, and the climb back up the hill can be painfully slow.

But wanting to change still matters. Asking for help matters. Getting through one more day matters. Sometimes the first victory is simply deciding not to take the next step downward.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Not Everyone at the Bottom Is a Bad Person.

This comic is not an excuse for destructive behaviour. Bad choices can cause real damage, and people must accept responsibility for the things they have done. Addiction does not make consequences disappear.

But neither should we assume that everyone who has fallen is beyond redemption. Some people reach the bottom and finally realise that they do not want to remain there. They may need help, patience, accountability and someone willing to remind them that another direction is still possible.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The Hill Is Still There.

Jack and Jill may have fallen, but the hill has not disappeared. The road back is steep, uncertain and sometimes frightening. There are no guarantees that everything will return to the way it was.

But there is also no requirement that the story end where the fall happened.

**Bad choices can break a life. They do not necessarily have to define the rest of it.**

Sometimes the most important thing we can do is help someone find the strength to take the next step upward.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## The Moral of Jack and Jill.

Perhaps the old nursery rhyme was never really about a hill, a bucket and a broken crown.

Perhaps the modern moral is this: **we should think carefully about the choices that take us up the hill, because some roads lead much farther down than we expect. But when someone has fallen, we should remember that a human being is more than their worst decision.**

The hill is still there.

So is the possibility of climbing.

<!-- PLEBVOX:END -->

<!-- PLEBVOX:START -->

## Author's Note.

This story is also personal. I have made bad choices in my own life. I have dabbled with drugs and alcohol, spent time in nightclubs, been disobedient to my parents, been rebellious, and even dabbled in Satanism for a period of my life. I am not writing about falling from some position of moral perfection. I know what it is to make choices that take a person in the wrong direction.

I have also made a turnaround in my life, and I am still walking the road of recovery. In my own understanding of addiction, once an addict, always an addict; the safest road is therefore not to pretend that the old dangers have ceased to exist, but to keep choosing a different path. From the Christian perspective, the story goes even deeper: **all men are sinners, and none of us stands before God because we are good enough. We stand by God's grace.**

That is why this comic is not intended to point a finger at people who have fallen. It is intended to make us think about our own choices, the consequences those choices can bring, and the possibility of change. We can acknowledge the wrong without declaring the person worthless.

The hill can be steep. The climb can be painful. But by God's grace, the story does not have to end at the bottom.
